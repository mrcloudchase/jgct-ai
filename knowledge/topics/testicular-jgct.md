# Testicular JGCT: a benign tumor of baby boys

Juvenile granulosa cell tumor of the testis looks like its ovarian namesake under the microscope but behaves nothing like it. It appears in newborns and infants, makes no hormones, and has never been reported to spread. Surgery, ideally sparing the testis, is the whole treatment, and pathology groups now call it "benign juvenile granulosa cell tumour" so that nobody over-treats it.

## Who gets it

It is the most common testicular tumor in the first six months of life and about 1 to 3% of all prepubertal testis tumors [pmid-31871849, pmid-16985566]. In STEP, 24 boys had a median age of 16 days (day 1 to 12 months), 17 diagnosed in the first month [pmid-40272823]. More than 90% occur in the first year [pmid-42421431]. In a systematic review of 239 testicular granulosa cell tumors, the 166 juvenile cases had a mean age of 1.5 years [pmid-32719989]. The primary series are known here from abstracts only: 14 infants under 6 months, seven diagnosed in the first days of life, in the 1985 description [pmid-3976984]; 70 cases with 90% aged 6 months or younger, tumors 0.5 to 5 cm, six in undescended and two in dysgenetic testes [pmid-26076062]; 11 of 198 childhood testicular tumours in the Kiel registry, average age 0.4 months [pmid-9134041]; 16 in the German MAKEI/MAHO studies, median age 6 days [pmid-23733594]; 6 of 22 neonatal tumors in the Prepubertal Testis Tumor Registry [pmid-8308995]; 4 of 11 in the Italian TREP project [pmid-20850634]; 5 of 15 in the French TGM13 registry [pmid-38259036]. Prenatal diagnosis by ultrasound at 36 to 38 weeks is recorded three times [pmid-23217189, pmid-31871849]. Some arise in undescended or dysgenetic testes, and sex-chromosome mosaicism with ambiguous genitalia is reported, but karyotyping is advised only when the genitalia are abnormal [pmid-16985566, pmid-33201299, pmid-40528632].

## How it presents

A painless swollen testis or scrotal mass [pmid-40272823, pmid-32719989]. Ultrasound shows a multiseptated cystic "Swiss cheese" mass, often replacing the testis, with a vascular rim [pmid-33209713, pmid-25715370]. It can mimic torsion [pmid-33201299].

## The AFP trap

Alpha-fetoprotein, the yolk sac tumor marker, is physiologically very high in newborns (about 50,000 ng/mL at birth, 12 ng/mL by 6 months) and so cannot distinguish JGCT from yolk sac tumor in young infants; reliable interpretation is possible only after about 6 months [pmid-16985566, pmid-33201299]. Values of 48,370 and 113,200 ng/mL have been recorded in proven JGCT [pmid-33201299, pmid-28331810]; the registry's neonatal tumors had AFP of 23 to 61,700 ng/mL, all normal for age, and a 1987 newborn with a Swiss-cheese mass and AFP above 450 ng/mL was thought to have a teratoma (abstracts only) [pmid-8308995, pmid-2439713]. Yolk sac tumor, the tumor to exclude, occurs slightly later (mean 20.7 months in a 33-case series) and is separated by SALL4, glypican-3 and AFP staining (abstract only) [pmid-25828390]. One tumor even stained for AFP on immunohistochemistry [pmid-28331810]. Guidance holds that no boy over 6 months with AFP under 100 ng/mL has a malignant testicular tumor [pmid-41762432].

## Pathology and genetics

Follicles with mucinous fluid and small round cells without grooves, positive for inhibin, calretinin, SF-1 and FOXL2 protein; mitoses can be plentiful without any clinical meaning [pmid-40528632, pmid-31871849]. Unlike ovarian JGCT, testicular tumors lack AKT1 and GNAS alterations and show monosomy 10 in about 60% [pmid-42421431]; the sequencing study behind that statement, 18 tumors with monosomy 10 in 8 of 14 and no recurrent point mutations or fusions, is known here from its abstract [pmid-36813116]. Ultrastructure shows a dual epithelial and smooth-muscle differentiation, and 4 of 4 Goswitz tumors with high mitotic rates or sarcomatoid areas behaved benignly (abstracts only) [pmid-8540611, pmid-9025845]. Markers from abstracts: inhibin in 16 of 18 and 4 of 5, FOXL2 and SF-1 in 12 of 12, SALL4 and glypican-3 negative, nuclear beta-catenin negative in 7 of 7 [pmid-26076062, pmid-11014571, pmid-26034868]. In two siblings with a biallelic MAD2L1BP mutation, one ovarian and one testicular JGCT occurred [pmid-37796616].

## Treatment

Inguinal orchiectomy has been standard; testis-sparing enucleation is recommended when a rim of normal testis remains, AFP is normal for age and frozen section is reassuring [pmid-33201299, pmid-33036134, pmid-33209713, pmid-41762432]. In the systematic review, 20 of 231 patients had testis-sparing surgery with one local recurrence (5%) [pmid-32719989]. One neonate with bilateral diffuse disease was tumor-free after 14 months of buserelin (LH-RH blockade) alone, which led the STEP authors to speculate that the tumor may be a developmental lesion that regresses [pmid-40272823]. No chemotherapy, staging scans or lymph node surgery are needed [pmid-33201299, pmid-16985566].

## Outcome

No relapse in 24 STEP boys at a median 95 months [pmid-40272823]; no metastasis in 166 reviewed juvenile cases [pmid-32719989]; none in 4 TGM-95 boys [url-039c1963d955, pmid-26206391]. Every registry known from abstracts says the same: no recurrence or metastasis in the Kiel, MAKEI, Prepubertal Testis Tumor Registry, TREP and TGM13 cases, with surgery the only treatment and no chemotherapy or lymphadenectomy indicated [pmid-9134041, pmid-23733594, pmid-11696779, pmid-20850634, pmid-38259036]; a pathology review calls the outcome uniformly benign to date [pmid-25440720]. Testis-sparing tumorectomy was used for a JGCT among 15 childhood testicular tumors in Spain (abstract only) [pmid-21040368]. Annual clinical and ultrasound follow-up suffices [pmid-33201299]; the TESST classification states no long-term follow-up is needed after complete resection [pmid-42421431].

## Where sources disagree

Older reviews advised karyotyping every boy with JGCT [pmid-16985566, pmid-25512812]; recent German authors restrict it to boys with genital anomalies [pmid-33201299]. Whether to spare the testis or remove it when little parenchyma remains is decided case by case [pmid-31871849, pmid-33201299].
