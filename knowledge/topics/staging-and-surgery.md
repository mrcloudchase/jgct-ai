# Staging and surgery

Surgery is both the diagnosis and, for most children, the whole treatment. What matters is that the tumor comes out intact, that the surgeon looks for spread and records what was found, and that the other ovary and the uterus are left in place.

## Staging

JGCT is staged with the FIGO system for ovarian cancer [pmid-42504512, pmid-36184758]. Stage I means the tumor is confined to the ovary; stage IC is subdivided into IC1 (the capsule broke during surgery), IC2 (the capsule had already broken, or tumor is on the ovarian surface) and IC3 (tumor cells in the peritoneal fluid) [url-3b3cdea64edb]. The distinction decides chemotherapy, so operative reports must say when any rupture occurred [pmid-27858560]. Four in five tumors are stage I: 81% in OTST (36% IA, 29% IC1, 17% IC2/IC3/ICX, 14% II-IV) [pmid-40267023] and 84 of 89 in STEP (52 IA, 14 IC1, 14 IC2, 4 IC3, 5 II/III) [pmid-40272823]. Complete FIGO staging was documented in only 20% of OTST patients [pmid-40267023].

## The operation

The 2026 European recommendations: an open approach that avoids rupture, with laparoscopy only when rupture can be safely avoided; peritoneal fluid or washings for cytology; biopsy of suspicious peritoneal areas; lymph node sampling only if nodes look abnormal; unilateral oophorectomy for an encapsulated tumor; no biopsy of the other ovary, no hysterectomy, no routine omentectomy [pmid-42504512]. Earlier guidance is the same in substance [pmid-22525408, pmid-27858560]. Lymph node metastases have not been found in any series that looked: none in the OTST registry [pmid-40267023], none in 8 patients with lymphadenectomy in a Chinese cohort [doi-10.21037_gpm-22-45], none in 54 nodes per patient in a Turkish series [pmid-25788847]; routine lymphadenectomy is therefore not recommended [pmid-42504512]. Malignant peritoneal cells in OTST occurred only when the tumor had ruptured or spread [pmid-40267023].

## Cystectomy and enucleation: a warning

Three of the four STEP relapses followed organ-sparing surgery (enucleation or partial ovariectomy), and the registry authors recommend second-look surgery with oophorectomy when an organ-sparing operation turns out to have removed a JGCT with high mitotic activity [pmid-40272823]; the European recommendations say the same (level III, grade B) [pmid-42504512]. A review found a 25% recurrence rate after cystectomy in JGCT [pmid-35437273]. Many tumors are first removed as presumed benign cysts and need a completion operation, which can be done laparoscopically [doi-10.5180_jsgoe.32.285, pmid-36748172].

## Fertility-sparing surgery works

Keeping the uterus and the other ovary is the norm and does not worsen outcome in early stage. In a Korean series, 4 of 11 JGCT patients had seven pregnancies [pmid-21998762]; in a Turkish series 4 of 5 who wished to conceive had five term births, one after BEP chemotherapy [pmid-25788847]; a review found pregnancy in 11 of 26 JGCT patients (42%) and in 23 of 26 who tried [pmid-35437273]; in a Fudan cohort of 278 malignant sex cord-stromal tumors, surgical extent did not change recurrence or survival [pmid-39349505]. Fertility-sparing surgery with complete staging was also used in stage IIIB disease with long survival in one patient [pmid-36992983].

## Advanced disease

For stage II-III the European recommendations advise removing the adnexa and metastases, then chemotherapy; for disease that cannot be resected, biopsy, two neoadjuvant cycles and delayed resection; debulking has no role except for palliation [pmid-42504512, pmid-36980387]. Ovarian tissue cryopreservation is not recommended because of the risk of reimplanting tumor [pmid-35437273, pmid-41367860].

## Where sources disagree

Whether laparoscopy is acceptable: European guidance allows it only if rupture can be avoided [pmid-42504512]; 14% of STEP and 18% of OTST patients had laparoscopic surgery [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023], and small series report it as safe [doi-10.5180_jsgoe.32.285]. Whether omentectomy adds anything is unsettled [pmid-42504512, doi-10.5180_jsgoe.32.285].
