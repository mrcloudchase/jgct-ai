# Relapse and salvage treatment

When JGCT comes back it usually does so within two years, in the abdomen or pelvis, and it is hard to treat. Roughly one in ten tumors behaves this way. There is no agreed treatment for relapse; what exists is a list of things that have been tried.

## How often, where and when

In OTST, 14 of 70 patients relapsed (median 8.5 months, range 3 to 32) and 8 died, all of recurrent JGCT, at a median of 35 months from diagnosis; of 6 survivors, 4 were still on treatment [pmid-40267023]. In STEP, 4 of 89 relapsed and 2 died [pmid-40272823]. In 94 published pediatric cases, 17% relapsed and 44% of those died; relapse sites were the pelvis and abdomen (8), lymph nodes (4), bone (2), liver (2), lung, omentum and spleen region [pmid-41367860]. Relapse is most often peritoneal, and long-term survival after relapse is about 20% or lower even without prior chemotherapy [pmid-42504512]. In the MD Anderson registry, median overall survival after first recurrence was 32 months for 14 pure JGCT versus 182 months for adult-type tumors [pmid-40815979]. Rapid courses are recorded: liver and splenic metastases three months after a stage IC resection [pmid-27843515]; stage IA to IV in 8 months in a 4-year-old [pmid-23024526]; death 4 months after surgery for a stage III tumor with hypercalcemia [pmid-24371683]; and, from abstracts only, bone relapses in two of four Gustave-Roussy children [pmid-3373268], a 4-year-old with a mitotically active tumor dead 18 months after diagnosis [pmid-14674109], a stage IA tumor with massive ascites recurring at 18 months after surgery alone [doi-10.22730_jmls.2014.11.1.8], and a 25-year-old whose high-mitotic-index tumor recurred within 6 months of BEP and killed her within weeks [doi-10.1155_crog_7348622]. Further single cases, read in full, add to that list: a 14-year-old in Rabat who relapsed 5 months after surgery and, despite three courses of VIP, developed sacral epidural, nodal and liver disease and received palliative care [doi-10.83129_toubkal-6511]; a Japanese 25-year-old dead about three months after surgery [doi-10.5795_jjscc.31.74]; a Japanese 3-year-old with stage IV hormone-silent disease dead 4.5 months after presentation despite two regimens [doi-10.11164_jjsps.26.5_987]; and a Turkish 23-year-old with stage IIIC intra-abdominal recurrence two months after surgery for a tumor of doubtful classification [doi-10.5505_aot.2012.33042]. A 2010 Rabat thesis, which may describe some of the same patients as the 2016 one, reports one relapse treated with adjuvant then palliative chemotherapy (abstract only) [doi-10.83129_toubkal-9338, doi-10.83129_toubkal-11437]. Self-report points the same way: in a survivor survey, 13 of 52 respondents with the juvenile type reported recurrence, and half of those recurred within 12 months of diagnosis against 11% of recurrent adult-type tumors [pmid-37011030] (its conference abstract reports the same survey [doi-10.1210_jendso_bvad114.2137]).

Late and unusual relapses also occur. A 12-year-old whose ruptured tumor was removed (stage IC2) found a mass in her surgical scar five years later; it was JGCT, which the authors call a cutaneous metastasis, though implantation from the ruptured tumor is also possible [doi-10.52768_2766-7820_1964]. A Japanese woman had an adult-type granulosa cell tumor at 40, 28 years after a tumor called juvenile at 12; neither tumor carried FOXL2 C134W, and the authors cannot say whether the second was a recurrence or a new tumor [doi-10.57291_jsgo.44.2_161]. A Spanish 36-year-old's recurrence is known only from a brief abstract [doi-10.4321_s0378-48352006000700004].

In older adult-dominated series, first recurrences were pelvic in 70% and involved the retroperitoneum in about 15%, and nodal recurrence occurred in 5% (abstracts only) [pmid-16537089, pmid-19162310].

## Surgery first

Salvage surgery is indicated for localized relapse when complete resection is feasible [pmid-41367860]. Both STEP survivors of relapse had resection plus chemotherapy, one reaching a second remission after PEI and the other a third remission after BEP, radiotherapy, carboplatin-etoposide-bleomycin and autologous stem cell transplant [pmid-40272823]. Isolated 10 cm recurrences were resected without residual disease in two Turkish patients [pmid-36748172]; the scar recurrence above was excised and further chemotherapy planned, with no recurrence reported afterward [doi-10.52768_2766-7820_1964]. The Gynecologic Cancer InterGroup, writing about sex cord-stromal tumors generally without separating JGCT, offers repeat resection when feasible and notes no standard for unresectable metastatic disease [pmid-27993794].

## Chemotherapy and other systemic options

Platinum regimens (PEI, BEP) are first choice; paclitaxel with carboplatin, bevacizumab, gemcitabine and thalidomide are listed as later lines [pmid-41367860, pmid-40272823]. One STEP patient received carboplatin, paclitaxel, bevacizumab and regional hyperthermia and died 2.5 months after relapse [pmid-40272823]. A Czech patient received bevacizumab with limited response and died at 21 months [pmid-39773640]. Weekly then three-weekly paclitaxel was used in a xeroderma pigmentosum patient whose relapse progressed after four cycles [pmid-37567969]. Anastrozole did not hold a late recurrence in a 40-year-old [pmid-32341591]. The Turkish early relapse shrank in symptoms and CA-125 on four cycles of BEP, was debulked, and was followed with an LHRH analogue; its outcome is not reported [doi-10.5505_aot.2012.33042]. Dose-intensified chemotherapy with stem cell rescue, radiotherapy, and GnRH blockade as an add-on (level V) are listed in the European recommendations, which stress that no harmonized relapse strategy exists [pmid-42504512]. From abstracts only: one of five Chinese patients given high-dose chemotherapy with stem cell rescue died of recurrent JGCT [pmid-11776205]; a stage IIIB recurrence at 55 months was controlled by reoperation and chemotherapy for at least 30 months [pmid-30728165]; a stage IIIB patient whose relapse was detected by rising inhibin died despite several regimens [pmid-27702598]; in the MAKEI advanced-stage series one patient reached a second remission at 12 months and one died of diffuse peritoneal recurrence [pmid-12165898]; the 1996 BEP trial saw responses in most but durable remission in only 1 of 7 metastatic patients [pmid-8602303].

## Cytoreduction with HIPEC

Cytoreductive surgery with heated intraperitoneal chemotherapy has been reported in two JGCT patients: one in an adult series (outcome not individually reported) and an 18-year-old at her fourth relapse after abdominal radiotherapy and transplant, who developed a fistula and died at 12 months; prior abdominal radiotherapy appears to be a contraindication [pmid-30911605, pmid-26500075].

## Mixed tumors

Recurrent mixed adult-plus-juvenile granulosa cell tumors behaved like pure JGCT (median survival after recurrence 84 months, progression-free 8.7 months), not like adult-type tumors [pmid-40815979]. A DICER1-mutant mixed JGCT and Sertoli-Leydig tumor recurred at 6 months and was controlled by second surgery and eight cycles of paclitaxel-carboplatin [pmid-40901169].

## Targeted therapy

AKT1 duplications activate the kinase constitutively and AKT inhibitors are the natural candidates; OTST authors recommend tumor sequencing for targetable alterations in recurrent disease [pmid-26137586, pmid-40267023]. Commercial AKT inhibitors modulated mutant AKT1 activity in vitro, and MYC inhibitors have been suggested for anaplastic tumors with MYC-family amplification (abstracts only) [pmid-26362254, pmid-41036825]; sorafenib inhibited COV434 cells, though that line is no longer accepted as JGCT (abstract only) [pmid-26067856, pmid-33328126]. Adult-type trials of bevacizumab, aromatase inhibitors, leuprolide and others have not included JGCT [pmid-32485873, url-68575db179a7]. Immunotherapy looks unpromising: in a Belgian-Spanish study, all sex cord-stromal tumors including 9 JGCT had near-zero PD-L1, and single-cell profiling of one juvenile tumor showed the same T-cell-poor "immune desert" as adult-type tumors [pmid-40269311].

## What is unknown

Which second-line regimen is best, whether AKT-directed drugs work, and whether metachronous contralateral tumors (up to 10% of patients in some series, median 2.3 years later) are relapses or new tumors; they carry a prognosis like a first tumor [pmid-41367860, pmid-42504512].
