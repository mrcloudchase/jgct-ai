# Syndromes, inherited risk and when to ask for genetic testing

Almost every child with JGCT has no family history and no underlying condition. A small number do, and the links are worth knowing because they change follow-up for the child and sometimes testing for relatives. In a 2025 review of 94 published pediatric cases, 21 (22%) had a recognized condition, 14 of them Ollier disease [pmid-41367860]; that figure is inflated by the tendency to publish unusual cases.

## Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome

Multiple enchondromatosis (Ollier disease; with hemangiomas, Maffucci syndrome) is the oldest and strongest association: two Ollier and two Maffucci patients were among the 125 in the classic series as quoted by later sources, and reviews now count at least twelve to fifteen cases [pmid-39601780, pmid-32030011, pmid-37324278]. The link is biological: a 4-year-old's tumor and her enchondroma carried the same somatic IDH1 R132C mutation, absent from her blood, skin and parents [pmid-37324278]. Enchondromas are usually on the same side as the tumor, with one contralateral exception [pmid-32030011]. Bone lesions seen on staging scans have been mistaken for metastases [pmid-28144098]. The AACR surveillance consensus for these syndromes advises a high index of suspicion for lower abdominal pain in girls but proposes no routine ovarian screening [pmid-39601780]; the European recommendations advise germline testing and IDH1/IDH2 tumor analysis when enchondromatosis and JGCT coincide [pmid-42504512]. A triple-X girl with Ollier disease and JGCT, and a transgender man with Ollier disease on testosterone who developed a 3.3 kg JGCT, are also recorded [pmid-35968204, pmid-37840846].

## McCune-Albright syndrome

The GNAS R201C mutation that causes fibrous dysplasia and peripheral puberty in McCune-Albright syndrome also drives about 30% of JGCT; one girl had both diseases from the same mosaic mutation [pmid-34286167]. A 2-year-old whose bilateral cystic ovaries progressed to a unilateral JGCT raised the same question, though her tumor had no GNAS, AKT1 exon 3 or FOXL2 mutation [url-b0153a2c356d, pmid-34286167].

## DICER1 tumor predisposition

DICER1 syndrome mainly causes Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors and pleuropulmonary blastoma. JGCT is a rare manifestation: the PPB registry found one JGCT in a relative of a blastoma patient and one in a sister of a gynandroblastoma patient, both with germline DICER1 mutations [pmid-21501861]; among 713 germline carriers, 2 had JGCT [pmid-39400264]; the OTST registry found no germline DICER1 in 19 JGCT patients [pmid-29037807]. Several "JGCT" with DICER1 mutations turned out on review to be gynandroblastoma or Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor [pmid-29037807, pmid-37248399]. Testing is advised when a JGCT is anaplastic or highly mitotic, or when the history suggests DICER1 disease; carriers are offered pelvic ultrasound every six months [url-1b49311e69d4, pmid-39400264, pmid-42504512].

## Other reported associations

- **Biallelic MAD2L1BP** loss causing mosaic variegated aneuploidy: a sister and brother developed ovarian and testicular JGCT at 14 and 11 months; 23 sporadic JGCT patients had no such mutation [pmid-37796616].
- **Xeroderma pigmentosum group C**: a 19-year-old's JGCT and later Sertoli-Leydig tumor both carried DICER1 mutations, with a mutational signature of defective DNA repair; a 14-year-old had an incidental stage IA tumor [pmid-37567969, pmid-41035579].
- **Li-Fraumeni plus PTEN hamartoma syndrome**: one stage I patient later died of liposarcoma [pmid-41367860].
- **Neurofibromatosis type 1**, **Beckwith-Wiedemann**, **tuberous sclerosis**, **ovotesticular DSD**: single cases, no proven increased risk [pmid-35848651, pmid-41367860].
- **Donohue syndrome** (insulin receptor defect): bilateral neonatal JGCT [pmid-25153212].
- **Testicular JGCT** with sex-chromosome mosaicism and ambiguous genitalia; karyotyping is advised only when the genitalia are abnormal [pmid-16985566, pmid-33201299].

## What to do with this

The 2025 multidisciplinary flowchart recommends genetic referral when a syndrome is suspected, when the tumor is bilateral or metachronous, or when histology suggests a Sertoli-Leydig component [pmid-41367860]. For an ordinary unilateral JGCT with no other features, no source recommends routine germline testing; the European recommendations limit DICER1 testing to anaplastic tumors [pmid-42504512].
