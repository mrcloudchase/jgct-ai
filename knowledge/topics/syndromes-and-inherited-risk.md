# Syndromes, inherited risk and when to ask for genetic testing

Almost every child with JGCT has no family history and no underlying condition. A small number do, and the links are worth knowing because they change follow-up for the child and sometimes testing for relatives. In a 2025 review of 94 published pediatric cases, 21 (22%) had a recognized condition, 14 of them Ollier disease [pmid-41367860]; that figure is inflated by the tendency to publish unusual cases.

## Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome

Multiple enchondromatosis (Ollier disease; with hemangiomas, Maffucci syndrome) is the oldest and strongest association: two Ollier and two Maffucci patients were among the 125 in the classic series (abstract only) [pmid-6465418], 3 of 40 in the French multicenter series (abstract only) [pmid-1300967], and reviews now count at least twelve to fifteen cases [pmid-39601780, pmid-32030011, pmid-37324278]. A 1991 French case was the sixth reported and already noted the tumor on the same side as the enchondromas (abstract only) [pmid-2048938]; a 10-year-old with Ollier disease had an IDH1-mutant mixed juvenile granulosa and Sertoli-Leydig tumor, extending the association to gynandroblastoma (abstract only) [pmid-39098546]. The link is biological: a 4-year-old's tumor and her enchondroma carried the same somatic IDH1 R132C mutation, absent from her blood, skin and parents [pmid-37324278]. Enchondromas are usually on the same side as the tumor, with one contralateral exception [pmid-32030011]. Bone lesions seen on staging scans have been mistaken for metastases [pmid-28144098]. The AACR surveillance consensus for these syndromes advises a high index of suspicion for lower abdominal pain in girls but proposes no routine ovarian screening [pmid-39601780]; the European recommendations advise germline testing and IDH1/IDH2 tumor analysis when enchondromatosis and JGCT coincide [pmid-42504512]. A triple-X girl with Ollier disease and JGCT, and a transgender man with Ollier disease on testosterone who developed a 3.3 kg JGCT, are also recorded [pmid-35968204, pmid-37840846]. Single cases from Japan, Mexico and India widen the picture: a 15-year-old with Maffucci syndrome whose 3.9 kg tumor came with peritoneal deposits and ascites (recurrence-free at only 5 months) [doi-10.11164_jjsps.24.2_472_2]; a 13-year-old with Maffucci syndrome and a stage IA tumor, disease-free a little over 3 years later [url-71fabf45e585]; and a girl of 2 years 8 months with Ollier disease whose enchondromas lay in the hemipelvis on the same side as the tumor, which the authors warn should not be mistaken for metastases and suggest may reflect a generalised mesodermal dysplasia [url-bf94bf8a0b06]. The published counts differ with the date and method of each review: the Indian authors found 11 earlier JGCT with enchondromatosis, the Mexican authors about 10 with Maffucci syndrome, and later reviews more [url-bf94bf8a0b06, url-71fabf45e585, pmid-32030011].

## McCune-Albright syndrome

The GNAS R201C mutation that causes fibrous dysplasia and peripheral puberty in McCune-Albright syndrome also drives about 30% of JGCT; a 15-year-old had both diseases from the same mosaic mutation, which her 2021 report called the first such case [pmid-34286167]. An earlier conference poster (2018, Hamburg) had already described a girl with neonatal McCune-Albright syndrome (Cushing syndrome from 6 weeks, cardiomyopathy, hyperthyroidism) whose large JGCT ruptured at 4 months; GNAS R201H was found in the tumor cells and the adrenal glands, and those authors in turn stated that JGCT had not been described in the syndrome and suggested that androgens raised by metyrapone treatment may have driven the tumor's growth [url-08fbb6ac79f2]. The two reports therefore each claim priority; taken together they show the link in both a teenager and an infant. A 2-year-old whose bilateral cystic ovaries progressed to a unilateral JGCT raised the same question, though her tumor had no GNAS, AKT1 exon 3 or FOXL2 mutation [url-b0153a2c356d, pmid-34286167]. A 2002 screen of children with premature thelarche and of juvenile and adult granulosa cell tumors found no activating Gs-alpha or FSH-receptor mutation in the fragments studied (abstract only) [pmid-12199347].

## DICER1 tumor predisposition

DICER1 syndrome mainly causes Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors and pleuropulmonary blastoma. JGCT is a rare manifestation: the PPB registry found one JGCT in a relative of a blastoma patient and one in a sister of a gynandroblastoma patient, both with germline DICER1 mutations [pmid-21501861]; among 713 germline carriers, 2 had JGCT [pmid-39400264]; the OTST registry found no germline DICER1 in 19 JGCT patients [pmid-29037807]. Several "JGCT" with DICER1 mutations turned out on review to be gynandroblastoma or Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor [pmid-29037807, pmid-37248399]. Testing is advised when a JGCT is anaplastic or highly mitotic, or when the history suggests DICER1 disease; carriers are offered pelvic ultrasound every six months [url-1b49311e69d4, pmid-39400264, pmid-42504512].

## Other reported associations

- **Biallelic MAD2L1BP** loss causing mosaic variegated aneuploidy: a sister and brother developed ovarian and testicular JGCT at 14 and 11 months; 23 sporadic JGCT patients had no such mutation [pmid-37796616].
- **Xeroderma pigmentosum group C**: a 19-year-old's JGCT and later Sertoli-Leydig tumor both carried DICER1 mutations, with a mutational signature of defective DNA repair; a 14-year-old had an incidental stage IA tumor [pmid-37567969, pmid-41035579].
- **Li-Fraumeni syndrome**: a stage I patient with germline TP53 and PTEN mutations later died of liposarcoma (abstract only) [pmid-27702598, pmid-41367860], and a woman with Li-Fraumeni syndrome had JGCT before a phyllodes tumor and bilateral breast cancer (abstract only) [pmid-36420590].
- **Peutz-Jeghers syndrome**: a daughter in a Peutz-Jeghers family died of JGCT without a confirmed STK11 diagnosis (abstract only) [pmid-35466733].
- **Proteus syndrome**: a 7-month-old with JGCT and precocity (abstract only) [pmid-11938428].
- **Denys-Drash syndrome and WT1**: a JGCT with loss of the wild-type WT1 allele in a child with germline WT1 mutation, and a JGCT beside gonadoblastoma in dysgenetic gonads of Drash syndrome (abstracts only) [pmid-1655284, pmid-3028928, pmid-9090524].
- **Disorders of sex development**: JGCT among gonadal tumors in gonadal dysgenesis, an incipient JGCT in an ovotestis of a 45,X/46,X,idic(Y) infant, a congenital tumor with 45,X/46,X,dic(Y) mosaicism, and juvenile-type granulosa tumors in androgen insensitivity syndrome (abstracts only) [pmid-8154078, pmid-19546054, pmid-10925635, pmid-23552209, pmid-2332212]. A German fetus with a complicated ovarian cyst at 32 weeks was born with clitoral hypertrophy and a large JGCT; child and tumor were 45,X/46,X mar(Y), a second gonad held dysplastic ovarian and testicular tissue, and the tumor was thought to make the child's high androgens (two abstracts of probably the same case) [doi-10.1055_s-2006-952443, doi-10.1055_s-2006-953888]. A French thesis described dysregulated SOX9 and FOXL2 in three XY patients with testicular granulosa cell tumors (abstract only) [url-670c73814ffc].
- **Neurofibromatosis type 1**, **Beckwith-Wiedemann**, **tuberous sclerosis**, **ovotesticular DSD**: single cases, no proven increased risk [pmid-35848651, pmid-41367860].
- **Donohue syndrome** (insulin receptor defect): bilateral neonatal JGCT, now reported twice; the second infant died of necrotizing enterocolitis at 30 days (second case abstract only) [pmid-25153212, doi-10.1186_s43054-025-00368-5].
- **Hypothalamic hamartoma** coexisting with an ovarian JGCT in an 18-month-old (abstract only) [pmid-10925635].
- **Testicular JGCT** with sex-chromosome mosaicism and ambiguous genitalia; karyotyping is advised only when the genitalia are abnormal [pmid-16985566, pmid-33201299].

## What to do with this

The 2025 multidisciplinary flowchart recommends genetic referral when a syndrome is suspected, when the tumor is bilateral or metachronous, or when histology suggests a Sertoli-Leydig component [pmid-41367860]. For an ordinary unilateral JGCT with no other features, no source recommends routine germline testing; the European recommendations limit DICER1 testing to anaplastic tumors [pmid-42504512], and the 2021 EXPeRT/PARTNER consensus, known here from its abstract, advises genetic testing for predisposition syndromes such as DICER1 in selected cases [pmid-33760357]. Reviews of pediatric ovarian tumors and their syndromes (abstracts only) give the same map: Ollier and Maffucci with postzygotic IDH1/IDH2, DICER1 with Sertoli-Leydig tumors, Peutz-Jeghers with sex cord tumors with annular tubules, SMARCA4 with small cell carcinoma [pmid-29175835, pmid-31409083, pmid-35778792].
