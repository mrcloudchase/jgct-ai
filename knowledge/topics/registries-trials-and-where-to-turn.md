# Registries, trials and where to turn

There is no clinical trial for JGCT and no charity devoted to it. What exists is two registries that have produced nearly everything known about modern outcomes, a European consensus group that writes the recommendations, and a DICER1 registry for the families it concerns. Joining a registry is the one concrete way a family can add to what is known.

## The registries that produce the evidence

- **International Ovarian and Testicular Stromal Tumor (OTST) Registry**, Children's Minnesota (NCT01970696). Open since 2011 to anyone of any age with a suspected gonadal stromal tumor; collects records, offers central pathology review, and banks blood and tumor; families can self-refer [url-599767ff9c45]. Its 2025 report on 70 ovarian JGCT patients established the mitotic index as the main prognostic factor [pmid-40267023]; earlier reports covered DICER1 findings [pmid-29037807, url-0a529aa0ceb0].
- **German Registry for Rare Pediatric Tumors (STEP)**, now STEP 2.0 (NCT05773651), University of Tübingen, with central reference pathology and a consultation board whose written recommendations treating centers follow [url-00efe8fac0ba, pmid-40272823]. Its 2025 analysis of 113 patients is the largest prospective pediatric JGCT series [pmid-40272823].
- **International Pleuropulmonary Blastoma/DICER1 Registry** (NCT03382158), for anyone with a DICER1-related tumor or variant; it does not list JGCT by name [url-f623903cbc28], but its 2024 surveillance paper lists JGCT with anaplasia or high mitotic index as a reason to consider germline DICER1 testing [pmid-39400264].
- **European Cooperative Study Group for Pediatric Rare Tumors (EXPeRT)**, within the PARTNER project, wrote the 2021 consensus and the 2026 European Standard Clinical Practice recommendations [pmid-42504512, url-3b3cdea64edb]. The NCI PDQ summary points US clinicians to the OTST registry and European groups and notes that work-up, staging and therapy have been harmonized between them [url-3b3cdea64edb].

## Trials

No JGCT-specific interventional trial exists [url-599767ff9c45, pmid-32485873]. Trials in ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors enroll adults: GOG-0264 compared carboplatin-paclitaxel with BEP in 63 patients aged 20 and over and closed early with no difference shown [url-68575db179a7]; a Chinese phase III trial (NCT02429700) compares the same regimens from age 14 but names only adult-type histologies [url-c7a8cfb06452]; a 2000 phase II of second-line paclitaxel was for adults [url-809f17ade28d]. A review counted only 12 interventional trials involving sex cord-stromal tumors on ClinicalTrials.gov, none specific to JGCT [pmid-32485873]. A study of DICER1 and microRNA in pediatric stromal tumors (NCT01572467) is the one child-focused research protocol recorded [url-ad760335194f].

## Data resources

cBioPortal's 551 public studies hold exactly one sample labelled JGCT; most "granulosa cell tumor" samples carry FOXL2 C134W and are adult-type, though a 7-year-old's tumor with an AKT1 insertion is there [url-6657783a29d4]. Orphanet has no JGCT entry, only adult-type granulosa cell tumor [url-7c7ae0b05976]. GeneReviews' DICER1 chapter is the reference for that syndrome [url-1b49311e69d4].

## For families

- Ask the care team whether the tumor was reviewed by a pathologist experienced in sex cord-stromal tumors; the European recommendations require reference pathology [pmid-42504512].
- Ask for the FIGO stage (IA, IC1, IC2, IC3, II or higher) and the mitotic index, the two findings that decide treatment and follow-up [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823, pmid-42504512].
- Ask about enrolling in the OTST Registry, which accepts families worldwide and funds its work partly through the Pine Tree Apple Classic Fund [url-599767ff9c45, pmid-40272823].
- Ask whether genetic counselling is advised, which depends on the histology and any syndromic features [pmid-41367860, pmid-42504512].

## What is unknown

How many JGCTs occur each year is not reliably counted, because cancer registries code the tumor as benign or uncertain and miss cases [pmid-27858560]. Reviews give figures of about 2 per million children per year and 15% of pediatric ovarian neoplasms [pmid-41367860, pmid-42927143], but these rest on small series.
