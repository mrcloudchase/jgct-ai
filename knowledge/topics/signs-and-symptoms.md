# Signs and symptoms

Because JGCT usually makes hormones, it tends to announce itself early. In young girls the first sign is often puberty arriving years too soon; in older girls and women it is a swollen or painful belly or a change in periods. A few tumors make no hormones and are found only when they are large, twist, or burst.

## Prepubertal girls

Isosexual precocious puberty (breast development, pubic hair, vaginal bleeding, growth spurt and advanced bone age, without true activation of the brain's puberty axis) is the leading presentation: 27 of 38 girls under 8 in STEP (71%) [pmid-40272823], 82% of prepubertal patients in the classic 125-case series (abstract only) [pmid-6465418, pmid-38136408], 15 of 20 premenarcheal girls in a 1982 series, 23 of 40 in the French multicenter series, 14 of 33 in the first German prospective cohort and 70% of 17 children in the Novak registry (all abstract only) [pmid-7149091, pmid-1300967, pmid-9190974, pmid-9560834], and 43 of 57 (75%) in a 2025 review of 94 published pediatric cases [pmid-41367860]. Signs of androgen excess (pubarche, clitoromegaly, acne, hirsutism) occur in about a third of prepubertal cases, sometimes alongside estrogen effects [pmid-41367860]. From abstracts only: a nationwide French study found clinical hyperandrogenism with high testosterone in 6 of 30 JGCT patients and traced it to absent or greatly reduced aromatase in the tumor [pmid-20395670]; six of 17 androgenic granulosa cell tumors in a classic series were juvenile [pmid-6548120]; 16 of 56 cystic JGCT had androgenic manifestations [pmid-35353158]; and a 2026 systematic review of 117 pediatric virilizing ovarian tumors found JGCT the commonest type under age 8, where hyperandrogenic signs were less frequent and the diagnosis quicker [pmid-41340166]. Infants are not spared: cases at 5, 6 and 9 months are recorded, presenting with bleeding, breast buds or a bursting tumor [pmid-37483405, pmid-34285730, pmid-34487069], and a 10-month-old presented with both breast growth and clitoromegaly [pmid-41367860]. Further infants: a Moroccan girl of nearly 4 months with genital swelling and large ascites and another of 8 months with vulvar bleeding and breast development [doi-10.83129_toubkal-6511], a German girl of 14 months with breast swelling (abstract only) [doi-10.1055_s-2006-952778], and a Russian girl of 18 months with pain and breast swelling [doi-10.17116_repro20212705148]. The same pattern of breast development, vaginal bleeding or discharge and a growth spurt in girls of 3 to 7 years is described from Japan, Morocco and Burkina Faso and in a Spanish-language report known from its abstract [doi-10.11164_jjsps.29.6_1141, doi-10.11164_jjsps.52.6_1241, doi-10.11437_sanpunosinpo1949.41.783, doi-10.83129_toubkal-6511, doi-10.4236_ojped.2023.132019, url-25110c0836bc]. Delay is common where signs are not recognized: a Brazilian 2-year-old's discharge and breast buds were treated repeatedly as candidiasis, and about five months passed between the first primary-care visit and surgery [doi-10.5712_rbmfc1(2)136]; a 5-year-old in Burkina Faso had breast enlargement noticed but not brought to a doctor until an abdominal mass appeared [doi-10.4236_ojped.2023.132019].

## Adolescents and adults

Abdominal pain (54%) and distension (45%) led in the OTST registry, with precocious puberty in 26%, secondary amenorrhea in 21% of those old enough and virilization in 12% [pmid-40267023]. In STEP, 74% of girls aged 8 and over had abdominal pain and 15% secondary amenorrhea [pmid-40272823]. Menstrual irregularity was reported in 41% of post-pubertal patients in the 94-case review [pmid-41367860]; a 15-year-old in Mexico presented with heavy bleeding with clots, her mass first reported as fibroids [doi-10.62954_t96nbw45]. Some children present only with pain or a growing belly and no hormonal signs are described, as in two Brazilian 6-year-olds with a month of pain (one known from an abstract) and a 14-year-old in Cuba whose abdomen enlarged over a year [doi-10.54265_ucdu5860, doi-10.34119_bjhrv5n5-222, doi-10.5281_zenodo.6564246]. Tumors are large, median 11 to 12 cm, and larger in pubertal than prepubertal girls (12.6 vs 7.8 cm) [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023].

## Emergencies

Ovarian torsion was the presenting event in 6% of STEP girls [pmid-40272823] and is described in children of 5 and 7 years and a 17-year-old with a 15 cm tumor twisted 720 degrees [pmid-41112815, pmid-23776871, pmid-36247268]; also in a Japanese 7-year-old referred as appendicitis and a Welsh 17-year-old with polycystic ovaries whose 9 cm tumor had twisted twice [doi-10.11164_jjsps.23.6_1133_1, doi-10.3126_njog.v6i2.6764]. A Japanese 4-year-old went to laparotomy for presumed appendicitis and was found to have a huge tumor with bloody ascites [doi-10.11164_jjsps.22.6_1102]. Rupture with bleeding into the abdomen caused an acute abdomen in 5% of reviewed cases [pmid-41367860] and in a 5-month-old who needed emergency surgery and transfusion [pmid-37483405]. Other ruptures: a 12-year-old in Algeria in shock with 2.7 liters of blood in the abdomen, well a year after salpingo-oophorectomy without chemotherapy [doi-10.33425_2689-1093.1008]; a 6-year-old in Brazil with an actively bleeding 12 cm tumor [doi-10.54265_ucdu5860]; a 12-year-old in China with about 8 liters of ascites around a ruptured tumor [doi-10.52768_2766-7820_1964]; and a 4-month-old with McCune-Albright syndrome [url-08fbb6ac79f2]. In one Chinese cohort 10 of 15 early-stage tumors had ruptured [doi-10.21037_gpm-22-45]. Massive ascites can be the only sign [pmid-23424695, pmid-29923976], including in young women whose 5 liters or more of ascites came without peritoneal spread (abstracts only) [doi-10.1055_s-2006-952364, doi-10.1055_s-0036-1592834]; the classic series recorded ascites in 9% and rupture in 11% (abstract only) [pmid-6465418]. A pediatric torsion series found JGCT the commonest malignancy presenting as torsion, yet only 1.8% of 707 torsions involved any malignancy, so torsion itself rarely signals a tumor (abstract only) [pmid-20105593].

## Unusual presentations

- Galactorrhea and amenorrhea from estrogen-driven hyperprolactinemia, with no pituitary tumor [pmid-27047664, pmid-36647328]; the first childhood case, an 11-year-old, is known from its abstract [pmid-15160508].
- Primary amenorrhea from a 1.6 cm inhibin-producing tumor that looked like a follicle on imaging (abstract only) [pmid-39993522].
- Paraneoplastic hypercalcemia, three cases in the literature [pmid-24371683].
- Five months of fever and delayed puberty with very high interleukin-6 that fell after surgery [pmid-31582893].
- Virilization with acanthosis nigricans and hyperinsulinism in a 4-year-old, mimicking HAIR-AN syndrome [pmid-36112066].
- Hirsutism treated as polycystic ovary syndrome for 18 months before a 30 cm tumor was found [pmid-24596770]; a prior polycystic ovary diagnosis also preceded tumors in women of 25 and 28 (abstracts only) [doi-10.1055_s-0036-1592834, doi-10.1055_s-0044-1790967] and a 17-year-old taking a combined pill [doi-10.3126_njog.v6i2.6764].
- Hirsutism with high testosterone in a Brazilian patient of unstated age and a 35-year-old in Serbia, both with doubtful age-typing [doi-10.5935_2965-1980.2022v1n3a25, doi-10.1515_sjecr-2017-0016].
- Hormone-silent bilateral tumors with an adrenal-region mass in a 3-year-old, fatal within five months [doi-10.11164_jjsps.26.5_987].
- Secondary amenorrhea with isolated FSH suppression, the clue being a very high inhibin B [pmid-33241170].
- Vaginal bleeding in a 6-year-old first investigated as possible abuse [pmid-38513413].
- Central precocious puberty diagnosed four months before the tumor declared itself [pmid-24198925].
- Sclerosing peritonitis mimicking peritoneal metastases [pmid-24783102].

## Where sources disagree

Most series describe estrogen effects as the rule, but one Karachi pathology series listed virilization as the most common JGCT presentation [pmid-24606464], and a five-patient Istanbul series found no raised estradiol in any patient [pmid-36748172]. Adolescent presentations overlap with ordinary menstrual irregularity, which delayed diagnosis in several reports [pmid-36647328, pmid-33241170].
