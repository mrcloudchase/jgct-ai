# Signs and symptoms

Because JGCT usually makes hormones, it tends to announce itself early. In young girls the first sign is often puberty arriving years too soon; in older girls and women it is a swollen or painful belly or a change in periods. A few tumors make no hormones and are found only when they are large, twist, or burst.

## Prepubertal girls

Isosexual precocious puberty (breast development, pubic hair, vaginal bleeding, growth spurt and advanced bone age, without true activation of the brain's puberty axis) is the leading presentation: 27 of 38 girls under 8 in STEP (71%) [pmid-40272823], 82% of prepubertal patients in the classic 125-case series (abstract only) [pmid-6465418, pmid-38136408], 15 of 20 premenarcheal girls in a 1982 series, 23 of 40 in the French multicenter series, 14 of 33 in the first German prospective cohort and 70% of 17 children in the Novak registry (all abstract only) [pmid-7149091, pmid-1300967, pmid-9190974, pmid-9560834], and 43 of 57 (75%) in a 2025 review of 94 published pediatric cases [pmid-41367860]. Signs of androgen excess (pubarche, clitoromegaly, acne, hirsutism) occur in about a third of prepubertal cases, sometimes alongside estrogen effects [pmid-41367860]. From abstracts only: a nationwide French study found clinical hyperandrogenism with high testosterone in 6 of 30 JGCT patients and traced it to absent or greatly reduced aromatase in the tumor [pmid-20395670]; six of 17 androgenic granulosa cell tumors in a classic series were juvenile [pmid-6548120]; 16 of 56 cystic JGCT had androgenic manifestations [pmid-35353158]; and a 2026 systematic review of 117 pediatric virilizing ovarian tumors found JGCT the commonest type under age 8, where hyperandrogenic signs were less frequent and the diagnosis quicker [pmid-41340166]. Infants are not spared: cases at 5, 6 and 9 months are recorded, presenting with bleeding, breast buds or a bursting tumor [pmid-37483405, pmid-34285730, pmid-34487069], and a 10-month-old presented with both breast growth and clitoromegaly [pmid-41367860].

## Adolescents and adults

Abdominal pain (54%) and distension (45%) led in the OTST registry, with precocious puberty in 26%, secondary amenorrhea in 21% of those old enough and virilization in 12% [pmid-40267023]. In STEP, 74% of girls aged 8 and over had abdominal pain and 15% secondary amenorrhea [pmid-40272823]. Menstrual irregularity was reported in 41% of post-pubertal patients in the 94-case review [pmid-41367860]. Tumors are large, median 11 to 12 cm, and larger in pubertal than prepubertal girls (12.6 vs 7.8 cm) [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023].

## Emergencies

Ovarian torsion was the presenting event in 6% of STEP girls [pmid-40272823] and is described in children of 5 and 7 years and a 17-year-old with a 15 cm tumor twisted 720 degrees [pmid-41112815, pmid-23776871, pmid-36247268]. Rupture with bleeding into the abdomen caused an acute abdomen in 5% of reviewed cases [pmid-41367860] and in a 5-month-old who needed emergency surgery and transfusion [pmid-37483405]. Massive ascites can be the only sign [pmid-23424695, pmid-29923976]; the classic series recorded ascites in 9% and rupture in 11% (abstract only) [pmid-6465418]. A pediatric torsion series found JGCT the commonest malignancy presenting as torsion, yet only 1.8% of 707 torsions involved any malignancy, so torsion itself rarely signals a tumor (abstract only) [pmid-20105593].

## Unusual presentations

- Galactorrhea and amenorrhea from estrogen-driven hyperprolactinemia, with no pituitary tumor [pmid-27047664, pmid-36647328]; the first childhood case, an 11-year-old, is known from its abstract [pmid-15160508].
- Primary amenorrhea from a 1.6 cm inhibin-producing tumor that looked like a follicle on imaging (abstract only) [pmid-39993522].
- Paraneoplastic hypercalcemia, three cases in the literature [pmid-24371683].
- Five months of fever and delayed puberty with very high interleukin-6 that fell after surgery [pmid-31582893].
- Virilization with acanthosis nigricans and hyperinsulinism in a 4-year-old, mimicking HAIR-AN syndrome [pmid-36112066].
- Hirsutism treated as polycystic ovary syndrome for 18 months before a 30 cm tumor was found [pmid-24596770].
- Secondary amenorrhea with isolated FSH suppression, the clue being a very high inhibin B [pmid-33241170].
- Vaginal bleeding in a 6-year-old first investigated as possible abuse [pmid-38513413].
- Central precocious puberty diagnosed four months before the tumor declared itself [pmid-24198925].
- Sclerosing peritonitis mimicking peritoneal metastases [pmid-24783102].

## Where sources disagree

Most series describe estrogen effects as the rule, but one Karachi pathology series listed virilization as the most common JGCT presentation [pmid-24606464], and a five-patient Istanbul series found no raised estradiol in any patient [pmid-36748172]. Adolescent presentations overlap with ordinary menstrual irregularity, which delayed diagnosis in several reports [pmid-36647328, pmid-33241170].
