# Follow-up and life after treatment

After surgery, the work is watching: for the tumor coming back, which happens early if it happens; for the other ovary; and for the child's hormones, which usually settle but sometimes bring puberty forward for real.

## Surveillance for recurrence

The 2026 European recommendations: physical examination with abdominal and pelvic ultrasound every 3 months for the first 2 years, then at longer intervals; tumor markers (inhibin, estrogen or testosterone, CA-125) only if they were raised at diagnosis; MRI only when ultrasound is unclear; no routine imaging above the diaphragm [pmid-42504512]. The 2025 multidisciplinary review suggests every 3 months for 3 years [pmid-41367860]; OTST authors advise imaging plus markers for up to 5 years [pmid-40267023]; the COG imaging white paper recommends pelvic ultrasound or MRI surveillance in every girl after an ovarian tumor and notes that no formal schedule exists for children [pmid-36184758]. Earlier US guidance proposed MRI or ultrasound with inhibin B about every 3 months for at least 3 years [pmid-22525408].

## Markers during follow-up

Inhibin B falls to normal within days to weeks of complete resection [pmid-34487069, pmid-42927143, pmid-36112066] and a rise can precede visible recurrence. It is not infallible: among OTST patients whose inhibin B was high at diagnosis and who relapsed, it was high again in only 3 of 5 [pmid-40267023]. CA-125 was raised at relapse in all three relapsing patients of one Chinese cohort [doi-10.21037_gpm-22-45]. AMH, estradiol and testosterone can serve the same role when raised at diagnosis [pmid-41367860, pmid-34487069].

## The hormones after surgery

Breast development, bleeding and virilization regress over weeks to months once the tumor is out [pmid-41367860, pmid-39877787, pmid-36112066]. Two things to watch:

- **Central precocious puberty.** Prolonged exposure to tumor estrogen can "prime" the brain's puberty axis, so true puberty starts early after the tumor is gone; a 5-year-old needed GnRH analog treatment 18 months after surgery, and 2 of 57 prepubertal cases in a review developed it [pmid-35371651, pmid-41367860]. Re-appearing pubertal signs therefore need hormone tests and an ultrasound to tell priming from relapse [pmid-41367860].
- **Delayed puberty from the tumor itself** resolved after surgery in a 13-year-old whose interleukin-6 had suppressed her puberty axis [pmid-31582893].

Hormone replacement is avoided right after surgery because sex steroids are tumor markers and the tumor may be hormone-dependent; in girls who lose both ovaries, pubertal induction is planned by an endocrinologist [pmid-41367860]. One guideline comparison allows ovarian stimulation only for stage IA tumors after multidisciplinary discussion and lists estrogen-containing contraceptives as contraindicated for these tumors [pmid-36980387].

## Fertility

Most patients keep one ovary and the uterus. Pregnancy after fertility-sparing surgery occurred in 42% of JGCT patients in one review and in 88% of those who tried [pmid-35437273]; small series report term births, including after BEP chemotherapy [pmid-25788847, pmid-21998762]. Ifosfamide and cyclophosphamide are the most gonadotoxic drugs used; platinum carries moderate risk [pmid-32355841].

## The other ovary

Metachronous tumors in the remaining ovary are reported in about 4% of published pediatric cases (up to 10% in some sources), at a median 2.3 years, some benign (cystadenomas) and some new JGCT [pmid-41367860, pmid-36748172]. New masses in the contralateral ovary are therefore a reason to re-image rather than assume relapse or assume benignity [pmid-41367860].

## Testicular JGCT

Annual clinical and ultrasound review is enough; markers and staging imaging are not needed [pmid-33201299, pmid-40272823].

## What is unknown

How long to follow a stage IA tumor with a low mitotic index, which has not relapsed in either registry [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823]; whether the schedule can be relaxed for them; and long-term endocrine and psychosocial outcomes, which no source reports systematically.
