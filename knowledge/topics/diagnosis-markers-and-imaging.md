# Diagnosis: blood tests, imaging and what they can and cannot tell

A JGCT is usually suspected from the combination of a child's hormonal signs and an ovarian mass on ultrasound, and supported by a blood test for inhibin. Imaging cannot tell JGCT apart from other ovarian tumors; only the pathologist can, after surgery.

## Hormones and tumor markers

- **Pattern of peripheral precocity.** High estradiol with suppressed LH and FSH identifies gonadotropin-independent (peripheral) puberty and points away from the brain and toward the ovary [pmid-41367860, pmid-39877787, pmid-42927143]. Testosterone can also be high: 9 of 21 OTST patients tested [pmid-40267023], and some tumors secrete mainly androgens [pmid-36112066]. Estradiol is not always high: it was normal despite breast development in a 5-year-old in Burkina Faso, whose authors suggest circadian variation or assay limits, and in a Moroccan thesis's two patients with pseudopuberty (abstract only) [doi-10.4236_ojped.2023.132019, doi-10.83129_toubkal-9338]. 17-hydroxyprogesterone was raised in two young girls [url-25110c0836bc, doi-10.17116_repro20212705148].
- **Inhibin B** is the most consistently raised marker: 38 of 42 OTST patients (90%), inhibin A in 22 of 34 (65%) [pmid-40267023]. Values in the thousands of pg/mL are common [pmid-42927143, pmid-34487069, pmid-27047664]; abstracts record 8,497 ng/L in a 14-month-old and 4,496 ng/L in a 25-year-old, falling below 10 ng/L after surgery [doi-10.1055_s-2006-952778, doi-10.1055_s-0036-1592834]. A high inhibin B does not prove JGCT: a girl with a Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor had inhibin B above 5,000 pg/mL and AMH of 30.2 ng/mL [pmid-42889762]. In poorer settings inhibin, or any hormone test, could not be done for lack of means [doi-10.4236_ojped.2023.132019, doi-10.83129_toubkal-6511]. The 2026 European recommendations list inhibin among the work-up tests (level III, grade B) [pmid-42504512]. Across 1,128 young patients with ovarian masses of all kinds, inhibin B predicted malignancy with 70% sensitivity and 85% specificity [pmid-41000187].
- **AMH** is co-secreted and falls after resection [pmid-34487069, pmid-38513413]. Abstracts report AMH 15 times the upper reference limit in a 17-year-old whose CA-125 and HE4 were normal, leading the authors to recommend AMH and inhibin B for solid ovarian masses, and above 23 ug/L in a 28-year-old [doi-10.24061_1727-0847.17.2.2018.16, doi-10.1055_s-0044-1790967].
- **CA-125** is raised in about half (20 of 42) [pmid-40267023] and can be very high with ascites [pmid-31582893, pmid-29923976], reaching 1,700 U/mL in a Japanese woman who died of her tumor [doi-10.5795_jjscc.31.74]; it is not specific. LDH was very high (4,330 IU/L) in a fatal hormone-silent case [doi-10.11164_jjsps.26.5_987].
- **AFP and beta-hCG** should be normal; they exclude germ cell tumors [pmid-42504512, pmid-22525408]. AFP was raised in only 2 of 46 OTST patients [pmid-40267023]. In infants AFP is physiologically high and useless: neonatal testis tumors in the Prepubertal Testis Tumor Registry had AFP of 23 to 61,700 ng/mL, all within limits for age, and 6 of 14 testicular JGCT in the largest series had raised AFP (abstracts only) [pmid-8308995, pmid-26076062]; a 7-month-old girl's JGCT came with marginally raised AFP (abstract only) [pmid-9343289], and a Moroccan girl of nearly 4 months had AFP printed as 134.4 [doi-10.83129_toubkal-6511]. See [Testicular JGCT](testicular-jgct.md) for boys.
- **Calcium** belongs in the work-up because hypercalcemia suggests small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type, the most important mimic [pmid-42504512]; paraneoplastic hypercalcemia in true JGCT is exceptional [pmid-24371683].

## Imaging

Ultrasound is the first test; MRI of the abdomen and pelvis characterizes the mass and stages it, with CT acceptable if MRI is unavailable; chest imaging only when symptoms, abdominal metastases or DICER1 surveillance call for it [pmid-36184758, pmid-42504512]. Typical findings are a large unilateral mass, solid and cystic with septa, often with a "sponge-like" multilocular pattern and blood products on MRI; purely solid and purely cystic tumors also occur [pmid-40723200, pmid-37508611, pmid-26054417]. Indirect clues in a child are an enlarged, pubertal-looking uterus and a thickened endometrium [pmid-34285730, pmid-42927143]. Imaging cannot distinguish juvenile from adult-type granulosa cell tumor or from other ovarian neoplasms [pmid-26054417]. Signs of torsion may be hidden by a large solid mass [pmid-41112815]. Older imaging work, known from abstracts, describes the JGCT of childhood as a large hemorrhagic multicystic mass with pseudoprecocity [pmid-9821198], sonographic patterns of solid-cystic, sponge-like and solid masses that match follicular, hemorrhagic and diffuse histology [pmid-20103788], and relapses that look like the primary on transvaginal ultrasound [pmid-32474449]. A practical rule from the endocrine literature: an ovarian cyst that persists beyond three months, especially with a solid component, should raise the possibility of JGCT (abstract only) [pmid-9777570]. In testicular JGCT the ultrasound shows a multiseptated "Swiss cheese" cystic mass [pmid-33209713, pmid-25715370].

## Pitfalls

- A unilocular, purely cystic JGCT can look benign on imaging [pmid-29923976].
- Bony lesions of Ollier disease seen on staging MRI have been mistaken for metastases; biopsy showed enchondromas [pmid-28144098, pmid-32030011, url-bf94bf8a0b06]. Polyostotic fibrous dysplasia in McCune-Albright syndrome was likewise first concerning for metastases [pmid-34286167].
- A 10-year-old's second, contralateral JGCT two years after the first was a smooth unilocular cyst with normal hormones, followed as a physiological cyst until it reached 21 cm within six months (preprint) [doi-10.21203_rs.3.rs-143384_v1].
- Fetal ovarian and testicular tumors can be seen before birth, but their nature is settled only after delivery; one was first read histologically as immature teratoma (abstracts only) [doi-10.1055_s-2006-953888, doi-10.1055_s-2005-923143]. In pregnancy, ultrasound changes delayed suspicion of a 17 cm tumor (abstract only) [doi-10.1055_s-0043-1769866].
- An ovarian JGCT in a woman with gastric and rectal cancers was taken for a Krukenberg metastasis until removed (abstract only) [pmid-35944993].
- Peritoneal nodules of sclerosing peritonitis mimicked spread in one patient [pmid-24783102].
- Frozen section at surgery is often inconclusive: diagnostic for JGCT in only 2 of 5 cases in one series, with lymphoma suspected in one [pmid-25788847].
- McCune-Albright syndrome can mimic JGCT exactly (precocity, high estradiol, an ovarian cyst): one 3-year-old had an unnecessary oophorectomy before cafe-au-lait spots and fibrous dysplasia appeared (abstract only) [doi-10.36013_ijipem.v6i.85]. A neonate's bilateral ovarian tumors behaved partly like a developmental process, one side resolving without surgery (abstract only) [pmid-19058207].

## What is unknown

No marker threshold diagnoses JGCT; inhibin B is a strong indicator of a granulosa cell tumor but is also raised in other sex cord-stromal tumors, and recurrences do not always raise it again [pmid-40267023]. No imaging score (O-RADS, IOTA) is validated in children [pmid-40723200].
