# Pathology and the tumors that mimic JGCT

The diagnosis of JGCT is made under the microscope. The pattern is distinctive once seen, but several other tumors of young women look similar, and two of them (small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type, and Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor) carry very different outlooks. Every guideline therefore asks for review by an expert pathologist.

## What JGCT looks like

Tumors are large (average about 12.5 cm), solid and cystic, with hemorrhage and necrosis in a minority [pmid-38136408]. Microscopically the growth is nodular or diffuse, punctuated by follicles of irregular size and shape filled with basophilic or eosinophilic, often mucin-positive, fluid; the cells have abundant eosinophilic (luteinized) cytoplasm and round, dark nuclei that usually lack grooves, though grooves are seen focally in about half; mitoses run from under 1 to more than 30 per 10 high-power fields (mean 7) and nuclear atypia can be striking [pmid-38136408, pmid-27858560]. Call-Exner bodies, typical of the adult type, are rare [pmid-23641362]. Sclerosis is common [pmid-38136408].

## Immunohistochemistry

Inhibin, calretinin, SF-1, FOXL2 protein, CD99 and WT1 are the usual positives, as in other sex cord tumors; cytokeratin may be expressed and EMA is typically negative [pmid-38136408, pmid-39773640]. A five-case study found SF-1, inhibin A, calretinin, FOXL2, ER, PR, AR, CK AE1/AE3 and CD99 positive in all, p53 wild-type pattern in all, SMARCA4 (BRG1) and INI1 retained, mismatch repair proteins intact, and HER2 and PD-L1 negative [pmid-39773640]. Inhibin can be negative in a genuine JGCT, when calretinin carries the diagnosis [pmid-24455600]. Immunohistochemistry cannot separate juvenile from adult-type tumors; molecular testing can [pmid-38136408].

## Mitotic index

Mitotic index is now the most important thing a pathologist reports. In OTST, 62% of tumors had more than 19 mitoses per 10 high-power fields, and every recurrence with a known index was in that group [pmid-40267023]; in STEP all four relapses had high mitotic rates [pmid-40272823]. The European recommendations rate proliferation and mitotic index as prognostic (level III, grade B) [pmid-42504512]. See [Prognosis and the mitotic index](prognosis-and-mitotic-index.md).

## Anaplastic JGCT

Rare JGCTs lose the orderly solid-and-follicular architecture, with diffuse growth and marked atypia; OTST held one such tumor, with a mitotic index of 73 and a somatic DICER1 hotspot variant, and the patient died [pmid-40267023]. The European recommendations advise DICER1 testing in anaplastic JGCT [pmid-42504512].

## Mimics

- **Small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type (SCCOHT).** Young patients, brisk mitoses and pseudofollicles make it the key mimic; of 16 SCCOHT first misdiagnosed locally, 6 had been called JGCT [pmid-31844183]. SMARCA4 (BRG1) immunohistochemistry separates them: 0 of 29 JGCT and 0 of 221 sex cord-stromal tumors lost SMARCA4 versus 46 of 50 SCCOHT [pmid-26356327]. One SCCOHT with patchy inhibin staining and normal calcium was called JGCT until sequencing found a germline SMARCA4 mutation [pmid-30275002]. The COV434 cell line, used for years as a JGCT model, is in fact SCCOHT [pmid-33328126, pmid-31311113].
- **Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor and gynandroblastoma.** Sertoli-Leydig tumors can have follicular areas that resemble JGCT; the European recommendations ask pathologists to look for the other component in each [pmid-42504512]. In the OTST registry two DICER1-mutant tumors called JGCT locally were gynandroblastomas on central review [pmid-29037807]; a tumor called JGCT at age 5 was reclassified as retiform Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor at 15 [pmid-37248399]. Gynandroblastomas with a JGCT component are recorded from adolescence to age 71 [pmid-41416308, pmid-30011982, pmid-32751726].
- **Adult-type granulosa cell tumor.** Separated by nuclear features and by the FOXL2 C134W mutation, present in nearly all adult-type tumors and absent in JGCT [pmid-38136408]; mixed tumors occur [pmid-34688077].
- **Yolk sac tumor** (in infants and testes), excluded by SALL4, glypican-3 and AFP staining [pmid-39773640, pmid-28331810].

## Where sources disagree

Whether the many labelled "JGCT" in adults really are juvenile-type is questioned; some adult-age reports lack supporting immunohistochemistry or molecular data [pmid-34688077]. The prognostic meaning of FOXL2 protein expression is contested: one group linked loss to aggressive tumors, another linked strong expression to worse survival [pmid-24371683, pmid-36869369].
