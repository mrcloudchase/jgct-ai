# Pathology and the tumors that mimic JGCT

The diagnosis of JGCT is made under the microscope. The pattern is distinctive once seen, but several other tumors of young women look similar, and two of them (small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type, and Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor) carry very different outlooks. Every guideline therefore asks for review by an expert pathologist.

## What JGCT looks like

Tumors are large (average about 12.5 cm), solid and cystic, with hemorrhage and necrosis in a minority [pmid-38136408]. The defining 125-case series, known here from its abstract, measured 3 to 32 cm, 49% solid and cystic, 37% solid and 14% cystic, with mitoses from under 1 to 32 per 10 high-power fields (average 7) [pmid-6465418]. Purely cystic JGCT (56 of 80 cystic granulosa cell tumors in one series) is a recognized trap whose main differential is a follicle cyst; invagination of the lining into the cyst wall is the key clue (abstract only) [pmid-35353158]. Microscopically the growth is nodular or diffuse, punctuated by follicles of irregular size and shape filled with basophilic or eosinophilic, often mucin-positive, fluid; the cells have abundant eosinophilic (luteinized) cytoplasm and round, dark nuclei that usually lack grooves, though grooves are seen focally in about half; mitoses run from under 1 to more than 30 per 10 high-power fields (mean 7) and nuclear atypia can be striking [pmid-38136408, pmid-27858560]. Call-Exner bodies, typical of the adult type, are rare [pmid-23641362]. Sclerosis is common [pmid-38136408]: 20 of 70 sex cord-stromal tumors with at least 20% sclerosis were JGCT, and the sclerosis often hid the diagnostic areas (abstract only) [pmid-37184091]. Variants that mislead, from abstracts only: a pseudopapillary pattern mistaken for transitional cell or retiform Sertoli-Leydig tumor (10 of 14 such cases were juvenile) [pmid-18301054]; macronodular tumors with scant follicles, heavily sclerotic tumors and unusually pleomorphic tumors [pmid-29132723]; reticular-cystic areas mimicking yolk sac tumor, spindle, microcystic, hobnail and rhabdoid cells [pmid-32561911]. Flow cytometry shows aneuploidy in about 45% without prognostic meaning at stage I (abstracts only) [pmid-1634146, pmid-2332268].

## Immunohistochemistry

Inhibin, calretinin, SF-1, FOXL2 protein, CD99 and WT1 are the usual positives, as in other sex cord tumors; cytokeratin may be expressed and EMA is typically negative [pmid-38136408, pmid-39773640]. A five-case study found SF-1, inhibin A, calretinin, FOXL2, ER, PR, AR, CK AE1/AE3 and CD99 positive in all, p53 wild-type pattern in all, SMARCA4 (BRG1) and INI1 retained, mismatch repair proteins intact, and HER2 and PD-L1 negative [pmid-39773640]. Inhibin can be negative in a genuine JGCT, when calretinin carries the diagnosis [pmid-24455600]. Immunohistochemistry cannot separate juvenile from adult-type tumors; molecular testing can [pmid-38136408]. The marker literature, known here from abstracts only, is consistent: inhibin positive in 7 of 7, 10 of 10 and 33 of 35 granulosa cell tumors and absent from small cell carcinoma, germ cell tumors and carcinomas [pmid-9158684, pmid-9688187, pmid-9416696]; calretinin at least as sensitive as inhibin [pmid-12808064]; FOXL2 protein in more than 95% of JGCT despite the absent mutation [pmid-21378549]; CD56 in 8 of 8 [pmid-17581419]; CD99 and Fli-1 in 11 of 11 with no EWSR1 rearrangement [pmid-24300529]; AMH in all 7 tested [pmid-11070112]; 14-3-3 sigma in all [pmid-23370648]; smooth muscle actin in nearly all and EMA in none, the EMA negativity separating JGCT from carcinomas [pmid-7731938, pmid-8314261, pmid-2471350]. Testicular JGCT stain for inhibin, calretinin, WT1, FOXL2 and SF-1, and are negative for SALL4 and glypican-3 (abstract only) [pmid-26076062].

## Mitotic index

Mitotic index is now the most important thing a pathologist reports. In OTST, 62% of tumors had more than 19 mitoses per 10 high-power fields, and every recurrence with a known index was in that group [pmid-40267023]; in STEP all four relapses had high mitotic rates [pmid-40272823]. The European recommendations rate proliferation and mitotic index as prognostic (level III, grade B) [pmid-42504512]. See [Prognosis and the mitotic index](prognosis-and-mitotic-index.md).

## Anaplastic JGCT

Rare JGCTs lose the orderly solid-and-follicular architecture, with diffuse growth and marked atypia; OTST held one such tumor, with a mitotic index of 73 and a somatic DICER1 hotspot variant, and the patient died [pmid-40267023]. The European recommendations advise DICER1 testing in anaplastic JGCT [pmid-42504512]. A 2026 series of 10 anaplastic JGCT (ages 7 to 44, tumors up to 28 cm), known here from its abstract, found TP53 mutations in all 7 sequenced and aberrant p53 staining in the rest, MYC or MYCN amplification in 4, AKT1 duplications in 4 and DICER1 mutations in 2; 4 of 9 died, all with stage II-III disease and MYC-family amplification, and the authors argue the variant should be named separately [pmid-41036825]. A recurrent Chinese case with TP53 and CDKN2A mutations was likewise classed as anaplastic (abstract only) [pmid-42706059].

## Mimics

- **Small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type (SCCOHT).** Young patients, brisk mitoses and pseudofollicles make it the key mimic; of 16 SCCOHT first misdiagnosed locally, 6 had been called JGCT [pmid-31844183]. SMARCA4 (BRG1) immunohistochemistry separates them: 0 of 29 JGCT and 0 of 221 sex cord-stromal tumors lost SMARCA4 versus 46 of 50 SCCOHT [pmid-26356327]. One SCCOHT with patchy inhibin staining and normal calcium was called JGCT until sequencing found a germline SMARCA4 mutation [pmid-30275002]. In the German pediatric SCCOHT series, 8 of 11 had first been called germ cell tumor or JGCT, and the untreated ones all relapsed within a year (abstract only) [pmid-16998935]; 9 of 9 JGCT retained BRG1 in another SMARCA4 study (abstract only) [pmid-26135561]; WT1 does not help with this distinction (abstract only) [pmid-21993272]. The COV434 cell line, used for years as a JGCT model, is in fact SCCOHT [pmid-33328126, pmid-31311113].
- **Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor and gynandroblastoma.** Sertoli-Leydig tumors can have follicular areas that resemble JGCT; the European recommendations ask pathologists to look for the other component in each [pmid-42504512]. In the OTST registry two DICER1-mutant tumors called JGCT locally were gynandroblastomas on central review [pmid-29037807]; a tumor called JGCT at age 5 was reclassified as retiform Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor at 15 [pmid-37248399]. Gynandroblastomas with a JGCT component are recorded from adolescence to age 71 [pmid-41416308, pmid-30011982, pmid-32751726].
- **Adult-type granulosa cell tumor.** Separated by nuclear features and by the FOXL2 C134W mutation, present in nearly all adult-type tumors and absent in JGCT [pmid-38136408]; mixed tumors occur [pmid-34688077]. Two traps known from abstracts: adult-type tumors with high-grade transformation whose high-grade areas look like JGCT, resolved by finding FOXL2 C134W in both components [pmid-31162286], and a FOXL2-mutant sex cord-stromal tumor NOS in a 46-year-old composed mostly of JGCT-like tissue [pmid-31789679]. DICER1-mutant gynandroblastomas with JGCT components lack AKT1 mutations and may be variants of Sertoli-Leydig tumor (abstract only) [pmid-29660837].
- **Benign mimics in pregnancy and infancy.** Hyperreactio luteinalis and multiple luteinized follicle cysts share cystic follicles with JGCT; the absence of pale vacuolated cytoplasm, solid granulosa growth and immature mitotically active nuclei tells them apart (abstracts only) [pmid-33323862, pmid-30358638].
- **Others, from abstracts only.** Metastatic melanoma and chordoma with follicle-like spaces [pmid-1951843, pmid-2302039]; an epithelioid malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor with EWSR1-CREM fusion treated as JGCT through two consultations until it failed to respond [pmid-37151162]; embryonal carcinoma misdiagnoses [pmid-41887253]; benign histiocytic nodules of the omentum mistaken for spread [pmid-22271884].
- **Cytology.** JGCT in fluids or aspirates shows sheets and single cells without Call-Exner bodies or grooves, pleomorphic cells with nuclear protrusions and frequent mitoses, and can be misread as adenocarcinoma (abstracts only) [pmid-35942608, pmid-20715655, pmid-15192945, pmid-20715666].
- **Yolk sac tumor** (in infants and testes), excluded by SALL4, glypican-3 and AFP staining [pmid-39773640, pmid-28331810].

## Where sources disagree

Whether the many labelled "JGCT" in adults really are juvenile-type is questioned; some adult-age reports lack supporting immunohistochemistry or molecular data [pmid-34688077]. The prognostic meaning of FOXL2 protein expression is contested: a French group found FOXL2 absent or reduced in 14 of 26 childhood tumors and linked loss to ascites, high mitotic activity, advanced stage and all three recurrences, while another group linked higher FOXL2 expression to worse survival (both abstract only) [pmid-17430735, pmid-21623383, pmid-36869369].
