# Genetics of JGCT

Almost all JGCTs are sporadic: the gene changes are in the tumor, not inherited. The two best-established drivers both switch on growth signalling inside granulosa cells. Knowing them matters for diagnosis, for separating JGCT from look-alikes, and as possible targets for future drugs.

## AKT1

In 16 tumors from girls under 15, 10 (more than 60%) carried in-frame tandem duplications in exon 3 of AKT1, in the pleckstrin-homology domain; the duplicated proteins sat at the cell membrane, were strongly phosphorylated and were active even without serum, and 14 of 16 tumors had some putative AKT1 driver change [pmid-26137586]. A 2025 Czech series found AKT1 duplications in 2 of 5 tumors and loss of PTEN protein in the 3 AKT1-wild-type tumors, so the authors propose that most JGCTs activate the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway one way or another [pmid-39773640]. OTST sequencing found AKT1 duplications in 3 patients [pmid-40267023], and a 7-year-old's tumor in cBioPortal carries an AKT1 insertion [url-6657783a29d4]. In flies, the mutant human AKT1 enlarges cells and disturbs tissue polarity [pmid-41237900]. Reviews quote AKT1 alterations in 29 to 60% of JGCT [pmid-38136408].

## GNAS

Activating GNAS R201 mutations (the same mutation that causes McCune-Albright syndrome) are reported in about 30% of JGCT in reviews [pmid-38136408, pmid-32485873]; OTST found GNAS p.R201C in 2 tumors [pmid-40267023], and one 15-year-old had the identical GNAS R201C in her tumor and in her fibrous dysplasia bone, with normal blood, proving mosaicism [pmid-34286167]. The AKT1 discovery cohort had no GNAS mutations [pmid-26137586], so the two drivers may be largely separate groups.

## Other recurrent changes

A review of the genomic landscape lists KMT2C (52%), ARID1A (39%), KMT2D (35%), TERT promoter variants (19%) and TERT rearrangements (13%), TP53 (up to 16%), and DNA-repair gene variants [pmid-38136408]. OTST found TERT promoter variants in 2 and TP53 variants in 4 patients [pmid-40267023]; the Czech series found a TERT promoter mutation in one recurrent tumor and an EP400 mutation in another [pmid-39773640].

## FOXL2: the absent marker

FOXL2 C134W defines adult-type granulosa cell tumor and is essentially absent from JGCT: 1 of 10 in one early series [pmid-23641362], 0 of 5 [pmid-39773640], 0 of 1 in a late-recurring case [pmid-32341591]. FOXL2 protein is nevertheless expressed [pmid-39773640].

## DICER1: rare, and often a sign the diagnosis is something else

DICER1 RNase IIIb hotspot mutations were found in 1 of 14 JGCT in the NEJM discovery study [pmid-22187960] and 0 of 4 in another [pmid-24136150]; the OTST registry found no germline DICER1 mutation in 19 JGCT patients and none in 2 sequenced tumors [pmid-29037807]; reviews quote 6 to 23% [pmid-38136408]. Two DICER1-mutant tumors called JGCT locally were gynandroblastomas on central review [pmid-29037807], and a JGCT in an xeroderma pigmentosum patient carried DICER1 mutations [pmid-37567969]. The European recommendations and GeneReviews advise DICER1 testing when a JGCT is anaplastic or has a high mitotic index [pmid-42504512, url-1b49311e69d4, pmid-39400264].

## Models and mechanisms

Smad1/Smad5 knockout mice develop metastatic tumors resembling JGCT; TGF-beta signalling protects the tumor cells from apoptosis, and a soy diet accelerated tumors in the mice [pmid-27104151, pmid-25243859, pmid-25165122]. Results from the COV434 cell line must be read knowing it is now classed as small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type [pmid-33328126].

## What this means and what is unknown

No targeted drug has been tested in JGCT; AKT inhibitors are the obvious candidates and OTST authors call for tumor sequencing in high-risk or recurrent disease [pmid-40267023, pmid-26137586]. Which combination of changes makes the roughly 10% of tumors that behave malignantly is not known [pmid-40528632].
