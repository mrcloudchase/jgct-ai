### What JGCT is

Juvenile granulosa cell tumor (JGCT, also written juvGCT) is a sex cord-stromal tumor that arises mostly in the ovary of children and young women and, separately, in the testis of newborn and young infant boys [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823, pmid-26076062]. It was distinguished from the far more common adult granulosa cell tumor by Scully, and the defining 125-case series by Young and colleagues described its presentation, histology, and generally favorable outcome [pmid-40267023, pmid-6465418]. About 95% of granulosa cell tumors are adult-type; JGCT accounts for about 5% [pmid-40267023, pmid-40350706]. The US National Cancer Institute summary describes JGCT as the most common sex cord-stromal subtype in girls under 18 and about 5% of ovarian tumors in children and adolescents [url-3b3cdea64edb]. In a Kiel pediatric registry series of 72 ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors, 48 were JGCT [pmid-12910419]. The main rare-disease nomenclature, Orphanet, has no separate JGCT entry; its only granulosa cell entry defines the adult type [url-7c7ae0b05976].

### Who gets it and how it presents

#### Ovarian JGCT

Age at diagnosis spans newborn to 67 years in the Young series (average 13), with 44% aged 10 or younger [pmid-6465418]. Pediatric registries report median ages of 7.6 years [pmid-9190974], 9.8 years [pmid-40272823], and 10 years [pmid-40267023], while adult-gynecology series report median ages of 17 to 21.5 years [pmid-34902562, pmid-39561630, pmid-25788847].

In prepubertal girls the leading sign is isosexual (peripheral) precocious puberty: 82% of prepubertal patients in the Young series [pmid-6465418] and 71% of girls under 8 in the German STEP registry [pmid-40272823]. Across ages, abdominal pain (54-56%) and abdominal swelling or distention (42-45%) are most common [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823]. Abnormal vaginal bleeding, secondary amenorrhea, and acute abdomen from torsion or rupture also occur [pmid-40272823, pmid-9190974, pmid-9165474]. Virilization was reported in 12% of an international registry cohort [pmid-40267023] and in 6 of 30 French patients, where it was linked to absent intratumoral aromatase [pmid-20395670]. Purely cystic JGCTs are often androgenic and can be mistaken for follicle cysts [pmid-35353158]. One case developed central precocious puberty after tumor removal [pmid-19539204].

Tumors are large, with median or average sizes of 11 to 12.5 cm [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023, pmid-6465418], and almost always unilateral (97% in one registry) [pmid-40267023].

In the international registry, inhibin B was elevated in 90% (38/42), inhibin A in 65% (22/34), CA125 in 48% (20/42), total testosterone in 43% (9/21), and AFP in 4% (2/46) [pmid-40267023]. Inhibin B was elevated at recurrence in only 3 of 5 patients who had it elevated at diagnosis [pmid-40267023].

#### Testicular JGCT

Testicular JGCT is a tumor of the first months of life: 90% of 70 patients were 6 months old or younger [pmid-26076062], the median age was 16 days in STEP [pmid-40272823] and 6 days in MAKEI [pmid-23733594], and it has been found at 30 weeks' gestation [pmid-3976984]. It presents as a painless scrotal mass or enlarging testis [pmid-26076062, pmid-40272823]. Some arise in undescended testes or dysgenetic gonads, and JGCT has been reported in infants with abnormal karyotypes and ambiguous genitalia [pmid-26076062, pmid-4061731, pmid-18008216].

#### Associated conditions

Ovarian JGCT occurs with Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome [pmid-6465418, url-3b3cdea64edb]; two cases with Ollier disease carried IDH1 R132C in the tumor [pmid-37324278, pmid-42896236]. A homozygous germline MAD2L1BP nonsense variant caused mosaic aneuploidy and JGCT of ovary and testis in two siblings [pmid-37796616]. JGCT appears occasionally in DICER1/pleuropulmonary blastoma families [pmid-21501861]. JGCT occurring during pregnancy has been managed with good maternal and newborn outcomes in small series [pmid-34694430, pmid-10509309].

### Pathology and diagnosis

Microscopically JGCT shows solid and follicular growth, follicles with basophilic or eosinophilic secretion, luteinized cells with round nuclei lacking grooves, and variable mitotic activity [pmid-6465418]. Mitotic rates are higher than in other pediatric sex cord-stromal tumors (mean 9.8 vs 2.7 per 10 HPF) [pmid-12910419]. Bizarre nuclear atypia occurs and, by itself, has no proven adverse impact when typical architecture is preserved [pmid-40267023, pmid-35353158].

Immunohistochemistry is typically positive for inhibin, calretinin, SF-1, and often FOXL2, CD99, and keratin [pmid-26076062, pmid-39773640, pmid-36813116]. JGCTs express CD99 and Fli-1 but lack EWSR1 rearrangement, which helps separate them from Ewing sarcoma [pmid-24300529]. Mimics include yolk sac tumor [pmid-32561911], follicle cyst [pmid-35353158], and Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors with JGCT-like follicles, which an expert series argues should be classified as Sertoli-Leydig rather than gynandroblastoma [pmid-32815828, pmid-25844550]. The international registry authors recommend DICER1 testing for JGCTs with high mitotic index, anaplastic features, or possible Sertoli components [pmid-40267023].

An anaplastic variant has been described: 10 tumors with areas of diffuse growth, marked atypia, and brisk mitoses, all with TP53 inactivation and four with MYC or MYCN amplification; four patients, all stage II/III with MYC-family amplification, died [pmid-41036825]. Single anaplastic or TP53-positive fatal cases appear in other series [pmid-40267023, pmid-42706059, pmid-32561911].

### Molecular biology

**AKT1.** In-frame tandem duplications in the AKT1 pleckstrin-homology domain were found in more than 60% of 16 JGCTs in girls under 15, and 14 of 16 tumors carried a putative AKT1 driver alteration [pmid-26137586]. The duplicated proteins move to the plasma membrane and are constitutively active; AKT inhibitors modulated mutant activity in vitro [pmid-26137586, pmid-26362254]. Fruit-fly models confirm gain of function with added complexity [pmid-41237900]. AKT1 duplications also appear in later cohorts [pmid-35031544, pmid-39773640, pmid-41036825, pmid-40267023]. AKT1-wild-type tumors in one small series showed PTEN protein loss, supporting PI3K/AKT/mTOR activation as a common thread [pmid-39773640].

**GNAS.** Activating GNAS R201 mutations were found in 30% (9/30) of French pediatric JGCTs and were associated with stage Ic or recurrence [pmid-16507630]. A later French AKT1 cohort of 16 had none [pmid-26137586], and the international registry found GNAS R201C in 2 sequenced tumors [pmid-40267023].

**Other alterations.** The largest multi-omic study (31 JGCTs) found frequent KMT2C-truncating mutations, SWI/SNF gene mutations, TERT rearrangements in 13%, and a DNA methylation profile that clearly separates JGCT from adult GCT [pmid-35031544]. DICER1 RNase IIIb mutations are uncommon: 3/47 (6%) in the largest cohort [pmid-32910017] and 2/8 in another [pmid-26033501]. TP53 alterations concentrate in anaplastic and aggressive tumors [pmid-41036825, pmid-40267023]. Trisomy 12 has been reported [pmid-10509309]. The FSH receptor showed no activating mutations in four children [pmid-19539204].

**FOXL2.** The FOXL2 C134W mutation that defines adult GCT was absent in JGCT in three cohorts (0/47 after review, 0/18, 0/7) [pmid-32910017, pmid-21623383, pmid-32561911], though one mixed cohort reported 1 of 8 [pmid-26033501].

**Testicular JGCT is molecularly different.** Testicular tumors lacked AKT1 and GNAS variants and showed recurrent monosomy 10 (8/14) [pmid-36813116]; they express FOXL2 protein [pmid-18721930, pmid-26076062].

### Staging and treatment as reported

Surgery is the primary treatment in every series [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023, pmid-33760357]. European consensus recommendations stress complete resection while avoiding tumor spillage, central pathology review, genetic testing where indicated, chemotherapy stratified by risk, and registry enrollment [pmid-33760357, pmid-42504512]. FIGO staging is used by convention but has not been validated in JGCT, and complete surgical staging is uncommon (20% in one registry) [pmid-40267023]. No lymph node involvement was found in sampled nodes across several series [pmid-40267023, pmid-34902562, pmid-25788847].

Fertility-sparing surgery (removal of one ovary and tube) is reported as the usual approach for stage I disease, with pregnancies and live births afterward [pmid-33583630, pmid-34902562, pmid-25788847, pmid-42706059, pmid-30728165]. Organ-sparing operations that remove only the tumor are another matter: in STEP, three of four relapsing patients had tumor enucleation or partial ovariectomy, so the authors advised caution [pmid-40272823]. The MITO-9 group regarded laparoscopy as safe in stage I [pmid-33583630].

The German, French, and European approach gives adjuvant cisplatin-based chemotherapy (PEI, VIP, or BEP) only for preoperative rupture, malignant ascites or washings, or spread beyond the ovary (FIGO IC2 and above), and observes stage IA and IC1 (intraoperative spill) [pmid-40272823, pmid-26206391, pmid-40267023, url-3b3cdea64edb]. Earlier German data supported cisplatin-based chemotherapy in advanced disease, with 6 of 7 stage II-III patients alive at a median 47 months [pmid-12165898].

For testicular JGCT, orchiectomy alone was curative in every registry series, with no relapses [pmid-40272823, pmid-23733594, pmid-26206391, pmid-20850634, pmid-9134041]. Testis-sparing surgery has been used and proposed [pmid-40272823, pmid-38259036, pmid-20850634].

### Outcomes and prognostic factors

Overall outcome in recent pediatric registries is good: 5-year EFS 95% and OS 97% for 89 ovarian patients in STEP [pmid-40272823], and 3-year EFS 73.2% and OS 91.7% for 70 patients in the international registry [pmid-40267023]. Relapses come early: median 8.5 months, 13 of 14 within two years [pmid-40267023]; median 4.5 months in another series [pmid-34902562]. This differs from adult GCT's tendency to late relapse [pmid-40272823]. A historic French pediatric series reported four relapses 6 to 17 months after surgery, all fatal [pmid-3373268].

**Mitotic index is the most consistent prognostic factor.** All recurrences with known mitotic index occurred in tumors with more than 19 mitoses per 10 HPF, in both the international registry (3-year EFS 55.5% vs 100%) and STEP (EFS 0.85 vs 1.0) [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823]. The Kiel registry reported EFS 0.48 vs 1.0 at the 20-mitosis threshold [pmid-12910419]. Even stage IA tumors with high mitotic index relapsed and sometimes caused death [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823, pmid-26165406]. In stage I disease, tumor size was also associated with recurrence [pmid-40267023].

**Stage** matters in most series [pmid-12805338, pmid-40267023, pmid-39561630], though STEP found no significant stage effect once chemotherapy was given for IC2 and above [pmid-40272823].

**Survivorship.** In a French registry, endocrine abnormalities persisted in 15 of 41 patients after treatment [pmid-38259036], and a multidisciplinary follow-up flowchart has been proposed [pmid-41367860].

### Where the literature disagrees

- **Recurrence after preoperative rupture (stage IC2/IC3).** STEP saw no recurrences among 18 IC2/IC3 patients, most of whom got chemotherapy [pmid-40272823]. The international registry reported 3-year EFS of 63.6% for IC2-IC3 [pmid-40267023]. Both agree that high mitotic index drives relapse.
- **Whether adjuvant chemotherapy helps.** STEP and TGM-95 attribute fewer relapses after rupture to chemotherapy [pmid-40272823, pmid-26206391]. A meta-analysis found no benefit in stage IC, including a JGCT subgroup of 33 (4 of 17 relapsed with chemotherapy vs 0 of 16 observed), but cautions that sicker patients are more likely to be given chemotherapy [pmid-40350706]. A Chinese early-stage series also found no association between chemotherapy and DFS [pmid-34902562].
- **Recurrence risk in early-stage disease.** MITO-9 reported no recurrences in 17 stage I patients [pmid-33583630], while a Chinese tertiary series reported 8 recurrences and 6 deaths among 35 early-stage patients [pmid-34902562].
- **FOXL2 protein expression and prognosis.** One French study tied absent or reduced FOXL2 expression to aggressive disease [pmid-17430735]. An Italian study tied higher FOXL2 expression to worse survival [pmid-21623383]. A Czech series found FOXL2 positive in all 5 tumors [pmid-39773640].
- **FOXL2 mutation.** One mixed cohort reported a FOXL2 mutation in 1 of 8 JGCTs [pmid-26033501]. The largest cohort reclassified its FOXL2-mutant cases as adult GCT [pmid-32910017].
- **JGCT versus adult GCT survival.** A SEER-based study reported 59% survival for JGCT, worse than matched adult GCT [pmid-39863551]. That contrasts with 92% disease-free survival in the Young series [pmid-6465418] and 97% OS in STEP [pmid-40272823]. The SEER cohort had more advanced disease (15 of 34 stage II-IV) [pmid-39863551].

### Research and trials

No interventional trial specific to JGCT was found. The international (IOTSTR, NCT01970696) and German (STEP, NCT05773651) registries are enrolling [url-599767ff9c45, url-00efe8fac0ba]. The only randomized chemotherapy trial for ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors (GOG, paclitaxel-carboplatin vs BEP) enrolled adults only [url-68575db179a7]. AKT-directed therapy is described as of particular interest for recurrent disease [pmid-40267023, pmid-26137586].
