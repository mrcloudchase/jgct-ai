# For parents and patients

This is a synthesis of recorded sources, not medical advice, and every record is a draft until a human has checked it. The small bracketed codes point to the sources, so you can show any paragraph to a doctor. Each section is short and ends with a link to a longer article on the same subject.

### The gist

- JGCT is a rare tumor of the hormone-making cells of the ovary. In baby boys it can occur in the testicle [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023].
- Most girls are found because the tumor makes estrogen: early breast growth or bleeding in young girls, a swollen or painful belly in older girls [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023].
- About four in five tumors are still inside one ovary (stage I) when found [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823].
- Treatment is an operation to remove the affected ovary and its tube. The other ovary and the womb stay, so puberty, periods and pregnancy remain possible [pmid-42504512, pmid-35437273].
- Most children with stage I disease need no chemotherapy. Chemotherapy (cisplatin-based, 3 to 4 courses) is advised when the tumor burst before surgery, when tumor cells are found in the belly fluid, or when it has spread [pmid-42504512].
- Experts disagree about chemotherapy in some in-between situations, such as a tumor that burst before surgery but did not spread [pmid-40350706, pmid-41367860, pmid-42504512].
- In the largest modern group of girls, 97 in 100 were alive five years later [pmid-40272823]. In an international group of 70, about 1 in 5 had the tumor come back, almost always within two years [pmid-40267023].
- The microscope finding that best predicts return is a high "mitotic index". Ask for it, and for the exact stage (IA, IC1, IC2, IC3), because the chemotherapy decision rests on them [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823, pmid-42504512].
- Check-ups are an ultrasound and blood tests (inhibin) about every 3 months for the first two years, then less often [pmid-42504512].
- Signs of early puberty fade after surgery. Occasionally real puberty then starts early, so an endocrinologist keeps watching [pmid-41367860, pmid-35371651].
- In baby boys the testicular form is benign: surgery alone, and no spread has ever been reported [pmid-32719989, pmid-40272823].
- Almost no families have an inherited cause. A few conditions (Ollier disease, Maffucci syndrome, McCune-Albright syndrome, DICER1 changes) are linked, and doctors may suggest genetic tests [pmid-42504512, url-1b49311e69d4, pmid-37324278].

### What is a juvenile granulosa cell tumor?

A **tumor** is a lump of cells growing where they should not. **Granulosa cells** are the cells in the ovary that help eggs grow and make the hormone **estrogen**. A juvenile granulosa cell tumor (JGCT) grows from cells like these, mostly in children and young people [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023]. In boys it can grow in the testicle, almost always in babies [pmid-40272823, pmid-32719989]. It is rare: among all ovarian tumors of this family, only about 1 in 20 is the juvenile type, but in children it is the most common one [pmid-38136408, pmid-22525408]. It is a different disease from the "adult" granulosa cell tumor of older women, with different gene changes and a different pattern of coming back [pmid-23641362, pmid-42504512]. Longer article: [What JGCT is](../topics/what-jgct-is.md).

### What signs does it cause?

In girls under about 8, the usual first sign is puberty starting far too early: breast growth, pubic hair, or vaginal bleeding, sometimes in a baby [pmid-40272823, pmid-34487069]. In older girls and teenagers it is a swollen or painful belly, periods that stop or become heavy, or sudden severe pain if the ovary twists or the tumor bursts [pmid-40267023, pmid-36647328, pmid-41112815]. Less often the tumor makes male-type hormones and causes acne, extra hair or a deeper voice [pmid-36112066]. Some children have fluid in the belly, called **ascites** [pmid-23424695]. In baby boys it is a painless swelling of the scrotum [pmid-40272823]. Longer article: [Signs and symptoms](../topics/signs-and-symptoms.md).

### How is it diagnosed?

An ultrasound, sometimes an MRI, shows a large lump on one ovary [pmid-42504512, pmid-36184758]. A blood test for a protein called **inhibin B** is high in most children and falls after surgery, so it is used to follow the tumor [pmid-40267023, pmid-34487069]. The final answer comes from a **pathologist**, a doctor who studies the removed tumor under a microscope. Experts advise a second look by a pathologist who knows these rare tumors, because a few other tumors look similar and are treated differently [pmid-42504512, pmid-31844183, pmid-29037807]. Longer articles: [Diagnosis: markers and imaging](../topics/diagnosis-markers-and-imaging.md), [Pathology and look-alike tumors](../topics/pathology-and-differential-diagnosis.md).

### Is it inherited or linked to other conditions?

Almost always, no. The gene changes that drive the tumor (most often in genes called AKT1 and GNAS) happen in the tumor cells only and are not passed on [pmid-26137586, pmid-38136408]. A few children have a known condition: **Ollier disease** or **Maffucci syndrome** (extra cartilage growths in the bones), **McCune-Albright syndrome**, or an inherited change in a gene called **DICER1**, which runs in some families [pmid-37324278, pmid-34286167, url-1b49311e69d4]. Doctors may suggest a genetic test when the tumor looks very abnormal under the microscope or when there are other signs [pmid-42504512, url-1b49311e69d4]. Longer articles: [Genetics of JGCT](../topics/genetics-of-jgct.md), [Syndromes and inherited risk](../topics/syndromes-and-inherited-risk.md).

### How is it treated?

Surgery is the main treatment. Surgeons remove the affected ovary and its tube, take a sample of the belly fluid, and look at the rest of the belly, leaving the other ovary and the womb in place [pmid-42504512]. Doctors describe how far the tumor has spread with a **stage**: IA means only in the ovary; IC means the tumor's wall broke, either during the operation (IC1) or before it (IC2), or tumor cells were found in the belly fluid (IC3); II and III mean it has spread inside the pelvis or belly [pmid-42504512].

- **Stage IA or IC1:** surgery alone, with close check-ups [pmid-42504512, pmid-36980387].
- **Stage IC2, IC3 or higher:** European guidelines add 3 to 4 courses of **chemotherapy**, medicines that kill tumor cells, and 4 to 6 courses for stage II or III [pmid-42504512, pmid-40272823]. Experts do not fully agree that every child with stage IC2 needs it: one review found no benefit in 33 children with stage IC, while in a French study all 4 children whose tumor had burst and who had no chemotherapy relapsed [pmid-40350706, pmid-41367860, url-039c1963d955].
- **Baby boys:** surgery on the testicle is the only treatment, and surgeons try to save part of it [pmid-40272823, pmid-33201299].

Longer articles: [Staging and surgery](../topics/staging-and-surgery.md), [Chemotherapy and the stage IC question](../topics/chemotherapy-and-the-stage-ic-question.md).

### What are the chances?

Most children are cured. In the largest German group of girls, 97 in 100 were alive after 5 years, and no child with a stage I tumor and a low mitotic index relapsed [pmid-40272823]. In an international group of 70 patients, 1 in 5 had the tumor come back, almost all within 2 years, and that is when it becomes hard to treat [pmid-40267023, pmid-42504512]. The two findings linked to a higher chance of return are a higher stage and a high **mitotic index**, the number of dividing cells the pathologist counts [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823]. Longer articles: [Prognosis and the mitotic index](../topics/prognosis-and-mitotic-index.md), [Relapse and salvage treatment](../topics/relapse-and-salvage.md).

### What happens after treatment?

Check-ups are a physical examination and an ultrasound about every 3 months for the first 2 years, then less often, with blood tests if they were abnormal at the start [pmid-42504512]. Early-puberty signs fade within weeks to months of surgery [pmid-41367860, pmid-36112066]. In a few children real puberty then starts early, because the tumor's hormones primed the brain; this can be treated, and an endocrinologist tells it apart from the tumor returning [pmid-35371651, pmid-41367860]. Many young women have had children after surgery that kept one ovary [pmid-35437273, pmid-25788847]. In baby boys, a yearly check and ultrasound is enough [pmid-33201299]. Longer article: [Follow-up and life after treatment](../topics/follow-up-and-life-after-treatment.md).

### What is uncertain or unknown?

- Whether every girl with stage IC2 needs chemotherapy [pmid-41367860, pmid-42504512].
- The best treatment when the tumor comes back; there are no clinical trials made for JGCT [url-599767ff9c45, pmid-42504512].
- How long check-ups need to continue for a stage IA tumor with a low mitotic index, which has not come back in either large registry [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823].

### Questions families may want to ask the care team

- What stage is the tumor, and what is the mitotic index?
- Has a pathologist experienced in these rare tumors reviewed it?
- Is genetic testing, such as for DICER1, suggested for us?
- What is the plan for check-ups, and which blood tests will be followed?
- How does treatment affect future puberty and fertility?
- Can we join the international registry that collects these cases? See [Registries, trials and where to turn](../topics/registries-trials-and-where-to-turn.md).
