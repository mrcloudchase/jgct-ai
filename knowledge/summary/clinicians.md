# For clinicians and researchers


This is a synthesis of the sources recorded in this knowledge base, not medical advice, and every record is a `draft` until a human reviews it. It reports what sources say and where they disagree. It recommends nothing for any individual patient.

### The gist

- JGCT is a rare sex cord-stromal tumor, about 5% of granulosa cell tumors but the most common ovarian sex cord-stromal tumor of childhood; unlike adult-type tumors it lacks the FOXL2 C134W mutation [pmid-38136408, pmid-23641362, pmid-22525408].
- Ovarian JGCT presents at a median age of about 10 years and also in adolescents and adults. Testicular JGCT is a tumor of infants (median age 16 days in STEP), has never been reported to metastasize, and a 2025 pathology consensus proposes renaming it "benign juvenile granulosa cell tumour" [pmid-40272823, pmid-32719989, pmid-40528632].
- In prepubertal girls isosexual precocity is the leading sign (27 of 38 under 8 years in STEP); older girls present with abdominal pain or distension, menstrual change or an acute abdomen. Inhibin B was elevated in 38 of 42 OTST patients at diagnosis [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023].
- About four in five tumors are FIGO stage I at diagnosis: 81% in OTST, 84 of 89 in STEP [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823].
- The 2026 European recommendations: unilateral oophorectomy by an approach that avoids rupture, with peritoneal cytology and biopsy of suspicious areas and no routine lymphadenectomy; surveillance for stage IA, IB and IC1; 3 to 4 cycles of cisplatin-based chemotherapy (PEI/VIP or BEP) for IC2/IC3; at least 4 to 6 cycles for stage II-III; re-excision with oophorectomy after ovary-sparing surgery when the mitotic count is high [pmid-42504512].
- Whether chemotherapy helps at stage IC is unsettled: a 2025 meta-analysis found no benefit in its 33 JGCT patients, while STEP saw no relapses at IC2/IC3 with chemotherapy given to 13 of 18 [pmid-40350706, pmid-40272823].
- Outcomes for stage I are excellent: 5-year event-free survival 0.95 and overall survival 0.97 for ovarian JGCT in STEP; 3-year event-free survival 73% and overall survival 92% across all stages in OTST, worse from stage IC2 upward [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023].
- Mitotic index is the strongest histological prognostic factor: every OTST recurrence with a known index had more than 19 mitoses per 10 high-power fields, and all four STEP relapses had high mitotic rates, three of them in stage IA tumors [pmid-40267023, pmid-40272823].
- Relapse comes early (13 of 14 within 2 years in OTST) and carries a poor prognosis (long-term survival about 20% or lower per the European recommendations). Follow-up is examination, tumor markers if raised at diagnosis, and ultrasound every 3 months for the first 2 years [pmid-40267023, pmid-42504512].
- Somatic AKT1 pleckstrin-homology-domain duplications (10 of 16 in the index study) and GNAS R201 mutations (about 30% in reviews) are the known drivers; DICER1 variants are uncommon and testing is advised for anaplastic tumors. Small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type, is the key differential and is separated by SMARCA4 immunohistochemistry [pmid-26137586, pmid-38136408, pmid-42504512, pmid-31844183].
- Associations: Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome (a shared somatic IDH1 mutation in one case), McCune-Albright syndrome (shared mosaic GNAS R201C), DICER1 syndrome rarely, and biallelic germline MAD2L1BP in one sibling pair [pmid-37324278, pmid-34286167, pmid-42504512, pmid-37796616].
- No JGCT-specific trial exists, and several of the field's classic series and guidelines remain unread here because their full text is paywalled (see Gaps below).

### What JGCT is

Juvenile granulosa cell tumor is a sex cord-stromal tumor of the ovary or testis. It is cited as about 5% of granulosa cell tumors [pmid-38136408, pmid-23641362, url-3b3cdea64edb], but as the most common ovarian sex cord-stromal tumor of childhood [pmid-22525408, url-3b3cdea64edb]. Reviews put it at about 70% of ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors before age 20 [pmid-32355841, pmid-37508611] and about 75% of paediatric ones [pmid-40723200]. It is distinct from adult-type granulosa cell tumor, which carries the FOXL2 c.402C>G (p.C134W) mutation in most cases. That mutation is rare or absent in JGCT [pmid-23641362, pmid-38136408, pmid-32485873, url-6657783a29d4]. Adult-type tumors may recur after more than 10 years; JGCT relapses mostly within 2 years [pmid-42504512, pmid-36869369].

Ovarian and testicular JGCT behave so differently that recent sources treat them as separate clinical entities [pmid-40272823]. A 2025-2026 international testicular pathology consensus renames the testicular tumor "benign juvenile granulosa cell tumour" [pmid-42421431, pmid-40528632, pmid-41384702].

### Who gets it and how it presents

**Ovary.** The largest prospective paediatric series is the German STEP/MAKEI registry: 89 ovarian JGCT, median age 9.8 years, 44% under 8 years [pmid-40272823]. The International Ovarian and Testicular Stromal Tumor (OTST) Registry reported 70 ovarian JGCT with median age 10 years (1 day to 38 years); 93% were under 20 [pmid-40267023]. JGCT also occurs in adults, including during pregnancy [pmid-36992983, pmid-41797949, pmid-30547828, pmid-29988841]. Pregnancy-associated cases are recorded at 17 to 35 years [doi-10.5350_semb2013470410, doi-10.32677_ijcr.v10i2.4357]. In a pooled review of published pregnancy-associated ovarian cancer, JGCT was the most common sex cord-stromal subtype [pmid-29988841].

In the OTST cohort, presentations were [pmid-40267023]:

- abdominal pain in 54% and distension in 45%;
- precocious puberty in 26% and virilization in 12%;
- median size 12 cm, and 97% unilateral.

In STEP, 27 of 38 girls under 8 years had isosexual precocity, and 6% presented with torsion [pmid-40272823]. Smaller series give similar pictures:

- In a Karachi pathology series of 39 sex cord-stromal tumors under age 21, JGCT was the commonest type (15). All 6 patients under 6 years had JGCT, and mean tumor size was 16.2 cm. Unusually, its table lists virilization as the most common JGCT presentation [pmid-24606464].
- In an Egyptian single-center abstract, all 9 girls (7 months to 6 years) presented with pseudo-precocious puberty and negative AFP and hCG [url-a3204c897af7]. Peripheral precocious puberty with high estradiol and suppressed gonadotropins is the classic picture in infants and toddlers [pmid-34487069, pmid-42927143, pmid-34285730, pmid-29546931, doi-10.56951_medicinus.v33i3.66].

Less typical presentations are recorded:

- virilization from androgen secretion [pmid-36112066, pmid-33362514, doi-10.18544_ep-01.16.01.1664, doi-10.18535_jmscr_v8i4.28, pmid-24959454, pmid-24596770]. In one 15-year-old, hirsutism was first treated as polycystic ovary disease, and a 30 cm tumor was found 18 months later [pmid-24596770];
- massive ascites [pmid-23424695, pmid-29923976, pmid-37483405, doi-10.4103_cjhr.cjhr_39_20, doi-10.15406_jpnc.2015.03.00113], including pseudo-Meigs syndrome with CA-125 of 366 U/mL [doi-10.29333_ejgm_82225];
- secondary amenorrhea [pmid-36647328, pmid-33241170], with hyperprolactinemia in one case [pmid-27047664];
- paraneoplastic hypercalcemia [pmid-24371683];
- fever with interleukin-6 elevation [pmid-31582893];
- torsion [pmid-41112815, pmid-23776871, pmid-36247268];
- rupture with hemoperitoneum at 31 weeks of pregnancy [pmid-37426562];
- a mass found after an aborted IVF stimulation cycle in a 31-year-old, with a "juvenile" label the record flags as unsupported [pmid-30123871].

A girl developed JGCT 4 months after central precocious puberty was diagnosed [pmid-24198925]. Another developed central precocious puberty after JGCT treatment [pmid-35371651].

**Testis.** Testicular JGCT is a tumor of infancy. In the Prepubertal Testis Tumor Registry, 11 of the tumors (3%) were JGCT, and 6 of 22 neonatal tumors were JGCT [pmid-16985566]. In STEP, 24 boys had median age 16 days, all stage I, with no hormone signs; none relapsed in a median 95 months of follow-up [pmid-40272823]. A systematic review of 239 testicular granulosa cell tumors counted 166 juvenile cases. Their mean age was 1.5 years, and none were metastatic or malignant [pmid-32719989]. Prenatal detection by ultrasound is reported [pmid-23217189, pmid-31871849]. Ultrasound shows a multicystic "Swiss cheese" mass [pmid-33209713, pmid-25715370, url-2d2d61fb2c99]. JGCT is linked to sex-chromosome mosaicism and ambiguous genitalia [pmid-25512812, pmid-32462465, pmid-35481403, pmid-27785439]. Serum AFP is physiologically high in neonates and hard to interpret [pmid-33201299, pmid-28331810, pmid-25715370]. One newborn had an AFP of 113,200 ng/mL with a JGCT diagnosis [pmid-28331810]. Further neonatal and infant cases show the multicystic mass with normal markers [url-3730726ce1a3, pmid-22511424]. A 2-month-old in a preprint series of 117 prepubertal testicular tumors had AFP 244 ng/mL and raised testosterone [doi-10.21203_rs.3.rs-9115875_v1]. In the French TGM95 study, 4 boys with testicular JGCT were treated by inguinal orchiectomy alone, and none of the 11 testicular sex cord-stromal tumors relapsed [url-039c1963d955]. A JGCT also occurred in a child with 45,X/46,X,idic(Y) mosaicism in a Hong Kong cohort [pmid-42415556].

### Markers

Inhibin is the most consistently reported marker [pmid-22525408, pmid-36184758, pmid-40218072]. In the OTST cohort, inhibin B was elevated in 38 of 42 patients and inhibin A in 22 of 34. CA-125 was elevated in 20 of 42, testosterone in 9 of 21 and AFP in 2 of 46 [pmid-40267023]. AMH and inhibin can be very high and fall after resection [pmid-34487069, pmid-38513413, url-d9e99f99c3cf]. Inhibin B is used during pregnancy because pregnancy does not raise it [pmid-41797949]. Inhibin-negative JGCT on immunohistochemistry is recorded [pmid-24455600, pmid-38646138, pmid-36247268], though inhibin stained all 15 JGCT of the Karachi series [pmid-24606464]. Serum CA-125 can be raised: 213 U/mL in one 4-year-old with stage IC disease [doi-10.28982_josam.636136]. Among 1,128 paediatric and young-adult ovarian masses, inhibin B predicted malignancy with sensitivity 70% and specificity 85% [pmid-41000187].

### Pathology and differential diagnosis

Typical features recorded:

- cystic tumors with solid areas, no Call-Exner bodies, and mitoses of 7-44 per 10 HPF [pmid-36748172];
- on cytology, no nuclear grooves or microfollicles, unlike most adult-type tumors [pmid-34411449];
- SF1, inhibin A, calretinin and FOXL2 expression in all five tumors of one series [pmid-39773640];
- inhibin and vimentin in all evaluable tumors of another [pmid-23029311].

In the OTST cohort, 37 of 60 tumors (62%) had a high mitotic index, more than 19 per 10 high-power fields [pmid-40267023].

Reported diagnostic pitfalls:

- **Small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type (SCCOHT).** It was misdiagnosed as JGCT in 6 of 16 cases, and SMARCA4 (BRG1) immunohistochemistry separates them [pmid-31844183]. In a large staining study, all 29 JGCT and all 113 adult-type tumors kept SMARCA4 [pmid-26356327]. A 12-year-old first diagnosed with JGCT had SCCOHT with a germline SMARCA4 variant [pmid-30275002]. The European guideline names elevated calcium as a clue to SCCOHT [pmid-42504512]. The COV434 cell line, long used as a JGCT model, has been reassigned to SCCOHT [pmid-33328126, pmid-31311113].
- **Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor and gynandroblastoma.** In the OTST registry, 2 DICER1-mutated tumors locally called JGCT were reclassified as gynandroblastoma [pmid-29037807]. A tumor called JGCT at age 5 was later reclassified as retiform Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor in DICER1 syndrome [pmid-37248399]. Gynandroblastomas with a juvenile granulosa component are recorded [pmid-41416308, pmid-30011982, pmid-32751726, pmid-37493465, pmid-34169133, doi-10.18203_2320-1770.ijrcog20200381, pmid-40390890]. In one adult, such a tumor (70% JGCT) was called granulosa cell tumor and then androblastoma before review found both components. It recurred in the peritoneum after 18 months [doi-10.22038_ijogi.2024.79085.6053].
- **Peritoneal lesions that are not metastases.** Sclerosing peritonitis with a JGCT mimicked peritoneal spread in an 18-year-old, who was well at 24 months without chemotherapy [pmid-24783102].
- **Adult-type granulosa cell tumor.** Mixed or overlapping juvenile and adult morphology occurs [pmid-34688077, url-a3c143927c76, pmid-38513413, pmid-25153212]. Some papers label adult-age tumors "juvenile" without supporting immunohistochemistry or molecular data [doi-10.17352_ijsrhc.000044, doi-10.36346_sarjmcr.2024.v05i02.003].

### Molecular biology

**Somatic changes.** Somatic in-frame duplications in the AKT1 pleckstrin-homology domain were found in 10 of 16 JGCT in girls under 15. Fourteen of 16 carried a putative AKT1 driver; GNAS R201 was absent in that series [pmid-26137586]. In a Czech series, 2 of 5 had an AKT1 internal tandem duplication and 1 a TERT promoter mutation [pmid-39773640]. Activating GNAS R201 mutations, in about a third of cases, are reported in reviews [pmid-38136408, pmid-32485873, pmid-27104151]. One review also lists DICER1 in 6-23%, KMT2C, ARID1A, TERT and TP53 variants [pmid-38136408]. In the OTST registry the findings came from a small subset [pmid-40267023]:

- AKT1 duplications in 3;
- GNAS R201C in 2 and TERT promoter variants in 2;
- TP53 variants in 4;
- one somatic DICER1 RNase IIIb variant, in an anaplastic tumor.

A metastatic JGCT had AKT1 gain and a subclonal MYC amplification [pmid-40985487]. In mice, oocyte PI3K activation and Smad1/5 loss produce JGCT-like tumors [pmid-27197196, pmid-27104151]. In the Smad1/5 model:

- adding Smad4 deletion gave smaller, non-metastatic tumors with more apoptosis, implicating TGFβ-SMAD4 signaling in survival and spread; four human JGCT samples expressed TGFβ ligands and receptors [pmid-25243859];
- a soy diet sped tumor growth and metastasis compared with a soy-free diet, and genistein promoted COV434 survival [pmid-25165122].

Results from COV434 [pmid-25243859, pmid-25165122, pmid-25253739] must be read against its reclassification as SCCOHT (below). Granulosa cell tumors with a juvenile-type histology are also described in horses [pmid-41321570].

**Germline and syndromic associations.**

- **DICER1.** The GeneReviews chapter calls JGCT "rarely associated" with DICER1 and suggests testing when anaplasia or a high mitotic index is present [url-1b49311e69d4]. The European guideline and the DICER1 registry say the same [pmid-42504512, pmid-39400264]. In the NEJM hotspot study, 1 of 14 JGCT carried a DICER1 hotspot mutation [pmid-22187960]. Adults with JGCT and germline DICER1 variants are reported [url-1c23e1dc296b, pmid-40076617].
- **Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome.** In one 4-year-old, the JGCT and an enchondroma shared somatic IDH1 R132C [pmid-37324278]. The AACR consensus counts at least 12 JGCT in Ollier/Maffucci [pmid-39601780]. Cases and reviews are recorded [pmid-28144098, pmid-35968204, pmid-37840846, pmid-30581564, pmid-32144835, pmid-36428825]. A 2020 review tabulates 15 cases, 12 Ollier and 3 Maffucci. In every case with known sides the enchondromas were on the same side as the tumor, except the authors' own [pmid-32030011].
- **McCune-Albright syndrome.** Mosaic GNAS R201C was shared by tumor and bone [pmid-34286167].
- **Other associations:**
  - biallelic germline MAD2L1BP in siblings with ovarian and testicular JGCT [pmid-37796616];
  - xeroderma pigmentosum group C [pmid-37567969, pmid-41035579];
  - neurofibromatosis type 1 [pmid-35848651];
  - Donohue syndrome [pmid-25153212].

### Staging and treatment as reported

In a Polish multicenter series of 595 girls operated on for ovarian lesions, JGCT was the most common malignant tumor, 10 of 44 [pmid-35323325]. Sources stage ovarian JGCT with FIGO [pmid-42504512, pmid-36184758, pmid-40267023]. The 2026 European Standard Clinical Practice recommendations (EXPeRT/ERN PaedCan), at evidence level III, recommend [pmid-42504512]:

- open surgery that avoids rupture, usually unilateral oophorectomy, and reference pathology;
- surveillance for stage IA/IB, and for IC1, with chemotherapy considered when the mitotic count is high;
- 3 to 4 cycles of PEI/VIP or BEP for IC2/IC3, and at least 4 to 6 cycles for stage II-III;
- ultrasound every 3 months for 2 years of follow-up.

A comparison of five guidelines found broad agreement that stage IA-IC1 completely resected may avoid chemotherapy while IC2-IC3 requires it. The guidelines differ on debulking and on chemotherapy at IC1 [pmid-36980387]. An earlier COG/MAKEI review recommended platinum chemotherapy for IC with preoperative rupture or malignant ascites, and for II-IV [pmid-22525408]. Two meeting abstracts bear on this:

- **French TGM95 study** (23 of its 38 ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors were JGCT). Of 15 children with incomplete resection from rupture or malignant ascites, 11 received VIP and none recurred. The 4 who did not get chemotherapy all relapsed within 18 months, and 2 died; both deaths were Sertoli-Leydig tumors [url-039c1963d955].
- **German MAKEI/STEP interim report**, 69 JGCT. Cisplatin-based chemotherapy went to 11 of 12 with preoperative rupture or malignant ascites. Both events were in stage IA patients, and estimated EFS was 0.96 [url-23e3339ee77a]. These patients overlap with the full STEP report [pmid-40272823]. Lymphadenectomy is not recommended: no nodal metastases were found in reported JGCT series [pmid-35849655, doi-10.21037_gpm-22-45, pmid-40267023]. Testicular JGCT is managed by orchiectomy or testis-sparing surgery [pmid-33036134, pmid-33201299, pmid-41762432]. One neonate with bilateral testicular JGCT was tumor free after buserelin alone [pmid-40272823].

Fertility-sparing surgery is the norm in young patients. In a Fudan cohort of 278 malignant ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors, 13 of the 14 JGCT were treated with fertility-sparing surgery [pmid-39349505]. A Japanese 12-year-old with stage IC1 disease had laparoscopic enucleation, then laparoscopic adnexectomy 2 months later, with no recurrence [doi-10.5180_jsgoe.32.285]. Imaging reviews note that juvenile and adult types look alike [pmid-26054417]. A review counted pregnancies after fertility-sparing surgery in 11 of 26 women with JGCT (42.3%) [pmid-35437273]. Small series report term births after such surgery [pmid-25788847, doi-10.4103_tofj.tofj_8_17].

### Outcomes and prognostic factors

- **STEP.** 5-year event-free survival was 0.95 and overall survival 0.97 for ovarian JGCT. Survival by stage was IA 98%, IC1 100%, IC2/IC3 100% and II/III 80%. EFS was 61/61 with a mitotic rate under 20 per 10 HPF versus 24/28 above 19 [pmid-40272823].
- **OTST.** 14 relapsed, 13 of them within 2 years, and there were 8 deaths. Three-year EFS was 80.2% at IA, 87.4% at IC1, 63.6% at IC2-IC3 and 48.0% at II-IV. Every recurrence with a known mitotic index had a high index, and 3-year EFS was 55.5% with a high index versus 100% with a low one [pmid-40267023].
- **Smaller series.** A 2017 OTST report had no deaths at stage IA/IB and 3 deaths among 11 patients at stage IC or above [pmid-29037807]. A Korean series of 11 had no recurrence over a mean 69.8 months [pmid-21998762]. A Chinese series of 8 recorded one death, at stage IIIC [doi-10.4103_2096-2924.255993]. Of 15 early-stage patients, 3 relapsed [doi-10.21037_gpm-22-45]. The South African national series had 2 deaths among 13 JGCT [pmid-33560080]. In the Karachi series, 2 of 15 JGCT recurred, one as a skin metastasis of the forearm at 2.5 years; follow-up existed for only 21 of its 39 patients [pmid-24606464].
- **Relapse.** Recurrence carries a poor prognosis, with about 20% long-term survival per the European guideline [pmid-42504512]. Median overall survival after first recurrence was 32.2 months in 14 pure JGCT, against 181.9 months in adult-type tumors [pmid-40815979]. Rapid distant spread after a stage IC tumor [pmid-27843515] and late fatal relapse [pmid-32341591] are recorded. A 4½-year-old progressed from stage IA to stage IV within 8 months and died [pmid-23024526].
- **Salvage.** Salvage approaches include cytoreduction with HIPEC [pmid-26500075] and carboplatin/paclitaxel [pmid-36748172, pmid-40901169]. A systematic review of HIPEC for recurrent granulosa cell tumor found only 2 JGCT among 19 patients [pmid-30911605]. Trials in adult sex cord-stromal tumors did not include JGCT-specific data [url-68575db179a7, url-809f17ade28d].

### Where the literature disagrees

- **Chemotherapy at stage IC2.** The European guideline recommends 3 to 4 cycles [pmid-42504512]. A 2025 review of 94 paediatric cases found 3 of 5 IC2 patients alive without chemotherapy and questioned it [pmid-41367860]. A stage IC meta-analysis found no recurrence benefit of adjuvant chemotherapy; its JGCT subgroup was only 33 patients [pmid-40350706]. STEP saw no relapses at IC2/IC3 with chemotherapy, while OTST reported a 3-year EFS of 63.6% at IC2-IC3 [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023]. In TGM95, 4 of 4 children with rupture or malignant ascites who went without chemotherapy relapsed, against 0 of 11 given VIP. Histologies were mixed, though, and both deaths were Sertoli-Leydig tumors [url-039c1963d955].
- **DICER1 in JGCT.** The OTST registry found 0 of 19 germline and 0 of 2 tumor DICER1 mutations [pmid-29037807]. A xeroderma pigmentosum C patient's JGCT carried DICER1 mutations [pmid-37567969]. Other sources report DICER1 hotspot mutations in 1 of 14 [pmid-22187960] and 0 of 4 pure JGCT [pmid-24136150], and 6-23% in a review [pmid-38136408].
- **FOXL2 in JGCT.** One review reports the FOXL2 mutation in 1 of 10 JGCT [pmid-23641362]; another calls it absent [pmid-38136408].
- **Drivers.** A case report states that no genetic driver is known [pmid-39877787]. Sequencing series find AKT1 and other alterations [pmid-39773640, pmid-26137586].
- **COV434.** Older work uses COV434 as a JGCT model [pmid-31311113]; it is now classed as SCCOHT [pmid-33328126].

Pooled reviews re-count published cases, so counts across `overlaps_with` records must not be added together.

### Gaps in this base

Several classic series, the full TGM-95 paper, and the 2020 ESGO-SIOPE and 2021 EXPeRT/PARTNER guidelines are not yet recorded because their full text could not be obtained. They are listed in `../sources/INDEX.md` under "Needs full text". About 580 relevant items wait there, most of them paywalled papers with JGCT in the title or abstract. No JGCT-specific interventional trial was found [url-599767ff9c45, url-00efe8fac0ba]. Population cancer registries may undercount JGCT, because ICD-O codes it as benign or of uncertain behavior, so higher-stage cases can be missed [pmid-27858560].
