# JGCT: synthesized overview

_Rewritten by each research sweep from the records in this knowledge base. Every factual sentence cites record IDs in brackets. This is a synthesis of published literature, not medical advice. Nothing here recommends treatment for any person._

## Status

As of 2026-10-29 the knowledge base holds 38 draft records, none yet human-reviewed. Only one record (pmid-40272823) was written from the full text; the rest are drawn from abstracts, so numbers should be checked against the sources before anyone relies on them.

## What JGCT is

Juvenile granulosa cell tumor is a sex cord-stromal tumor of the ovary, and less often the testis, that is distinct from adult granulosa cell tumor in its histology, clinical features and molecular profile [pmid-40272823, pmid-6465418]. The defining clinicopathologic series of 125 ovarian cases described diffuse and follicular growth, follicles with mucin-positive secretions, and luteinized cells with round, dark nuclei that lack grooves. Mitoses ranged from under 1 to 32 per 10 high-power fields [pmid-6465418]. In that series, 44 percent of patients were 10 or younger and 3 percent were over 30, with ages ranging from newborn to 67 years [pmid-6465418]. Recent series report median ages of 9.8 years in a pediatric registry [pmid-40272823], 14 years [pmid-42706059], and 19.5 to 21.5 years in adult-centre and population cohorts [pmid-39863551, pmid-39561630].

## Biology and genetics

JGCT lacks the FOXL2 p.C134W (c.402C>G) mutation that defines adult granulosa cell tumor [pmid-35031544, pmid-32910017, pmid-21623383, pmid-39983368]. In one study, higher FOXL2 protein expression was associated with worse survival in JGCT [pmid-21623383]. In one study, the only FOXL2-mutant tumors in a 50-case JGCT cohort were reclassified as adult tumors on review [pmid-32910017]. Genome-wide DNA methylation separates JGCT from adult granulosa cell tumor [pmid-35031544].

Activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is a recurrent theme. AKT1 in-frame or internal tandem duplications have been reported in ovarian JGCT [pmid-35031544, pmid-39773640, pmid-41036825]. One study found them in over 60 percent of tumors and showed that AKT inhibitors modulated mutant AKT1 activity in vitro [pmid-26362254]. In one small series, every AKT1-wild-type tumor showed loss of PTEN protein [pmid-39773640]. TERT rearrangements with CLPTM1L or DROSHA occur in about 13 percent [pmid-35031544]. KMT2C truncations and SWI/SNF gene mutations are also reported [pmid-35031544]. In one five-case series, the two tumors with TERT and EP400 mutations both recurred [pmid-39773640].

DICER1 mutations are uncommon in JGCT. Reported rates are 3 of 47 (6 percent) [pmid-32910017], 0 of 5 by sequencing and immunohistochemistry [pmid-41839237], 0 of 7 [pmid-42706059], and 2 of 10 in anaplastic JGCT [pmid-41036825]. Reviews list JGCT among the less frequent DICER1-associated gynecologic tumors [pmid-41243833].

Testicular JGCT appears clinically and molecularly distinct. In 70 testicular cases, nearly all in infants, every patient with follow-up was disease-free [pmid-26076062]. In 18 infant tumors there were no recurrent mutations, monosomy 10 in 57 percent, and none of the AKT1 or GNAS variants seen in ovarian tumors [pmid-36813116].

Predisposition contexts reported include:
- Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome [pmid-6465418], with the same somatic IDH1 p.R132C variant found in both the JGCT and the enchondroma [pmid-37324278, pmid-42896236].
- Biallelic germline MAD2L1BP loss, which causes mosaic aneuploidy with ovarian and testicular JGCT [pmid-37796616].
- Xeroderma pigmentosum group C [pmid-37567969].
- Pan-European pediatric consensus recommends genetic testing for predisposition syndromes such as DICER1 in selected cases [pmid-33760357].

## Presentation

In girls younger than about 8 years, isosexual precocious puberty is the leading sign, reported in 71 to 82 percent of prepubertal patients [pmid-40272823, pmid-6465418, pmid-37324278]. Older patients more often present with abdominal pain or swelling, abnormal bleeding or amenorrhea [pmid-40272823, pmid-6465418, pmid-42706059]. Torsion, rupture and ascites also occur [pmid-40272823, pmid-6465418]. JGCT is also reported as the most common virilizing ovarian tumor in children under 8 [pmid-41340166]. Tumors are usually large and unilateral, averaging 10 to 12.5 cm [pmid-6465418, pmid-40272823, pmid-42706059]. Inhibin B can be markedly raised and returns to normal after resection [pmid-40272823]. In the German registry, AFP and beta-hCG were not elevated [pmid-40272823]. JGCT can also present in pregnancy [pmid-34694430]. Testicular JGCT presents as painless scrotal swelling in the first months of life [pmid-40272823, pmid-36813116].

## Diagnosis

Immunohistochemistry shows sex cord markers (calretinin, inhibin, SF1, FOXL2) with EMA negativity [pmid-39773640, pmid-42706059, pmid-36813116]. Stathmin is expressed in all JGCT but does not help separate it from other sex cord-stromal tumors [pmid-39215355]. JGCT is CD99- and Fli-1-positive, which overlaps with Ewing sarcoma. EWSR1 FISH was negative in all 11 tested JGCTs and can help tell the two apart [pmid-24300529]. Testicular JGCT is SALL4- and glypican-3-negative, which helps separate it from yolk sac tumor [pmid-26076062]. JGCT histology can look alarming, with bizarre giant cells and hobnailing, even when the tumor is early stage [pmid-33725665]. Extensive sclerosis and staghorn vessels can mimic sclerosing stromal tumor [pmid-37184091], and purely cystic JGCT can be misdiagnosed [pmid-42706059]. An anaplastic variant has diffuse growth, marked atypia, up to 40 mitoses per 10 high-power fields, TP53 inactivation in all cases, and MYC or MYCN amplification in advanced-stage cases [pmid-41036825, pmid-42706059]. European guidance calls for centralized pathology review [pmid-33760357, pmid-42504512].

## Staging and treatment as reported

Most ovarian JGCTs are FIGO stage I at diagnosis in pathology and pediatric registry series [pmid-6465418, pmid-40272823, pmid-42706059]. The population-based SEER cohort had more advanced disease, with 15 of 34 at stage II to IV [pmid-39863551].

Surgery to remove the tumor completely, avoiding spillage, is the cornerstone of treatment in European guidance [pmid-42504512]. Fertility-sparing surgery is widely reported in stage I disease [pmid-39561630, pmid-33583630, pmid-42706059, pmid-39863551]. One multicentre series reported no relapses after fertility-sparing and minimally invasive surgery at a median of 80 months [pmid-33583630]. In the German STEP registry, however, 3 of 4 relapses followed enucleation or partial ovariectomy of tumors with high mitotic counts, and the authors advise caution with organ-sparing surgery [pmid-40272823].

Approaches to adjuvant chemotherapy differ by group:
- The MAKEI (2003) and STEP (2025) German series observed stage IA tumors without chemotherapy and gave cisplatin-based chemotherapy (PEI) for preoperative rupture, malignant ascites or spread [pmid-12805338, pmid-40272823].
- The 2021 EXPeRT/PARTNER consensus recommends cisplatin-based chemotherapy for spillage or metastases [pmid-33760357].
- The 2026 European recommendations add histological differentiation and mitotic rate to stage [pmid-42504512].
- Adult-centre series used BEP or unspecified platinum regimens [pmid-42706059, pmid-39561630].
- The value of adjuvant chemotherapy in stage I disease remains uncertain [pmid-33583630, pmid-34694430, pmid-39983368].

Testicular JGCT was treated by orchiectomy without relapse. One infant with bilateral disease became tumor-free on LH-RH blockade alone [pmid-40272823].

## Outcomes

In the largest prospective pediatric cohort (89 ovarian JGCT), 5-year event-free survival was 0.95 and overall survival 0.97. All relapses occurred in tumors with at least 20 mitoses per 10 high-power fields [pmid-40272823]. The International OTST and DICER1 registries (70 ovarian JGCT) reported 3-year event-free survival by stage:
- IA: 80.2 percent
- IC1: 87.4 percent
- IC2 to IC3: 63.6 percent
- II to IV: 48 percent

In that cohort, every recurrence with a known mitotic index had more than 19 mitoses per 10 high-power fields [pmid-40267023]. Two registries therefore agree on mitotic index above 19 per 10 high-power fields as an adverse factor [pmid-40272823, pmid-40267023]. Older pediatric series reported event-free survival of 0.75 [pmid-9190974], and 4 of 15 children dying after relapse within 17 months [pmid-3373268]. Small reports describe salvage surgery and platinum or taxane chemotherapy for advanced or recurrent disease, with durable remissions and later pregnancies in some patients [pmid-11277661, pmid-9038278, pmid-30728165]. Compared with the earlier MAKEI cohort, the authors associate restricting chemotherapy to stage IC2 or higher with improved survival [pmid-40272823, pmid-12805338]. In the classic series, 92 percent of 95 patients with follow-up were alive and free of disease, and fatal recurrences happened within 3 years [pmid-6465418]. Adult-centre series report relapse and death concentrated in advanced stage [pmid-39561630, pmid-39863551]. The anaplastic variant with MYC family amplification was associated with death from disease [pmid-41036825].

## Conflicts in the literature

- **Overall prognosis.** A SEER-based cohort reports overall survival of 59 percent and worse survival than adult granulosa cell tumor [pmid-39863551]. Pediatric registry and pathology series report survival above 90 percent [pmid-40272823, pmid-6465418]. The cohorts differ greatly in stage distribution. This is recorded, not resolved.
- **Weight of stage versus mitotic rate.** STEP 2025 found stage was not significantly associated with outcome under its chemotherapy strategy, and 3 of 4 relapses were stage IA tumors with high mitotic counts [pmid-40272823]. Single-centre and SEER series found all or most deaths in advanced stage [pmid-39561630, pmid-39863551]. The earlier MAKEI series found stage associated with event-free survival [pmid-12805338]. The OTST registry found worse outcomes at stage IC2 or higher, and 3-year event-free survival of 80.2 percent in stage IA [pmid-40267023]. In STEP, 48 of 52 stage IA patients remained relapse-free under observation [pmid-40272823].

## Open questions

- Should high mitotic rate, anaplasia or molecular markers (TP53, MYC family, TERT) prompt adjuvant therapy in stage IA disease? The STEP authors call this unresolved [pmid-40272823, pmid-41036825, pmid-39773640].
- Is cystectomy or partial ovariectomy safe given conflicting signals [pmid-33583630, pmid-40272823]?
- Could targetable pathways (PI3K/AKT/mTOR, FGFR1, MYC) be exploited? Authors raise these, but no JGCT trial data exist in this knowledge base [pmid-35031544, pmid-39773640, pmid-41036825].
- What is the true frequency of germline predisposition (DICER1, IDH1 mosaicism, MAD2L1BP, XPC) [pmid-33760357, pmid-37796616, pmid-37567969]?
