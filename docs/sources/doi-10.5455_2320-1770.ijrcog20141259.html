Why it matters for JGCT

Single teen JGCT case with amenorrhea as presentation, stage IA, fertility-sparing unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy and no adjuvant treatment.

Key findings

  • 17-year-old unmarried girl, menarche at 14, with 3 months of irregular cycles then 6 months of amenorrhea; known hypothyroid; abdominal mass of about 18 weeks size.
  • Serum CA-125, CEA, AFP, beta-hCG and LDH were within normal limits; thyroid function, prolactin and testosterone were normal; inhibin was not measured.
  • Ultrasound: 11.2 x 6.2 cm well-defined predominantly cystic mass with increased vascularity, probably from the left ovary, no solid component; CECT: heterogeneously enhancing left lower abdominal/pelvic mass with mild left hydroureteronephrosis, no free fluid; MRI not done.
  • At laparotomy a 20 x 15 cm left ovarian tumor with intact capsule, no free fluid, negative saline-wash cytology and no omental, hepatic or nodal disease; left salpingo-oophorectomy performed because the tube merged with the capsule; omental and peritoneal biopsies were negative.
  • Histopathology: well-differentiated juvenile granulosa cell tumor with hyaline globules; vimentin positive; inhibin and smooth muscle actin could not be done for lack of reagents; staged IA.
  • No adjuvant chemotherapy given on oncologist's advice; at one year after surgery she was doing well under follow-up.
  • The authors discuss (from the literature) that stage is the best prognostic factor, that surgery is the cornerstone, that BEP or taxane-platinum have been used for advanced or recurrent disease, and that radiotherapy has not been shown to confer survival benefit.

Presentation

  • irregular cycles for 3 months followed by 6 months of amenorrhea
  • non-tender firm mobile lower abdominal mass of 18 weeks size
  • hypothyroid on treatment, BMI 30, secondary sexual characters well developed

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy, left salpingo-oophorectomy with intact capsule, omental and peritoneal biopsies; no adjuvant therapy (stage IA primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: Well and under follow-up at 1 year; oncologist advised follow-up for at least 3 years

Notes

The discussion states both that JGCT recurrences typically occur within the first year and within the first three years, and also states a 5-year survival of 95-100% in early-stage disease (cited, not derived from this case). Immunohistochemistry was limited to vimentin. Full text read from the journal PDF.
