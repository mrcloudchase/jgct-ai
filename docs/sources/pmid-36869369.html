Why it matters for JGCT

Adult-type focused review; relevant to JGCT for a few subtype-specific IHC statements (FOXL2, p53, ER-beta) and for documenting that most prognostic marker studies pool or exclude JGCT.

Key findings

  • Systematic PubMed review (409 English full-text results 1965-2021; 54 articles in total) of immunohistochemical prognostic markers in ovarian granulosa cell tumors; the title and focus are adult-type (AGCT), and JGCT is covered only where the primary studies included it.
  • Background statements: GCT are 1-2% of ovarian neoplasms; AGCT is 90-95% of GCT; JGCT mostly affects younger patients (commonly before age 30); AGCT tend to recur late, even more than 10 to 20 years after diagnosis, whereas JGCT generally recur within a few years.
  • JGCT-containing studies cited: King 1996 (2 JGCT among 40 GCT), Sakr 2017 (12 JGCT among 125), Costa 1995 (7 JGCT among 56), Staibano (11 JGCT among 30, ER-beta); the review notes that many studies did not separate AGCT from JGCT.
  • FOXL2: in JGCT, where FOXL2 mutation is rare, strong FOXL2 immunoreactivity correlated with decreased disease-free and overall survival (D'Angelo); in AGCT, the mutation (70% in that study) and FOXL2 mRNA correlated with reduced DFS, but FOXL2 IHC did not.
  • p53: in Staibano, 12/30 GCT overexpressed p53, with correlation to progression predominantly in JGCT; other studies were inconsistent and older p53 IHC interpretation is considered outdated.
  • Estrogen receptor-beta: in 30 GCT (19 AGCT, 11 JGCT) loss of ER-beta was significantly associated with worse prognosis (Staibano); other studies found no ER-prognosis association.
  • Across all markers the review concludes that only CD56, GATA-4 and SMAD3 showed prognostic value for worse outcome; ER, AMH and inhibin IHC were not prognostic; mitotic rate, Ki-67, p53, beta-catenin and HER2 results were inconsistent.
  • The authors call for large multicenter studies with a clear distinction between AGCT and JGCT, and for national and international tumor registries.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • FOXL2: 402C>G (C134W) somatic mutation (97% of adult-type GCT (cited); rare in JGCT)
    strong FOXL2 immunoreactivity correlated with decreased DFS and OS in JGCT (D'Angelo, as summarized by this review)
  • TP53 (p53 IHC): p53 overexpression (12/30 GCT (AGCT and JGCT) in Staibano)
    correlated with progression (metastasis and/or death), predominantly in the JGCT group

Notes

Journal and first author not in cached text (left null). JGCT-specific data are second-hand summaries of cited primary studies. The text says inhibin and AMH IHC are diagnostic but not established as prognostic.
