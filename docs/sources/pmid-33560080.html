Why it matters for JGCT

First African national cohort of paediatric SCST; JGCT is the largest subgroup (13/23), providing stage and outcome data from a resource-limited setting, though survival is reported for the pooled SCST cohort.

Key findings

  • Retrospective national multicentre series (9 South African paediatric oncology units, Jan 1990-Dec 2015) of 23 children up to 16 years with biopsy-proven sex cord stromal tumors (SCST): 3 male, 20 female; 13 JGCT (56.5%), 9 Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors, 1 thecoma. All 13 JGCT were ovarian.
  • From the patient table, the 13 JGCT were stage 1 in 9, stage 3 in 2 and stage 4 in 2; JGCT ages ranged 8 to 162 months; elevated estradiol in 3 JGCT, LDH in 2, AFP in 1; at last report 10 JGCT were alive disease free, 1 was lost to follow-up and 2 (stage 3 and stage 4) died of disease.
  • Across all 23 patients: stage I 14 (60.9%), II 2 (8.7%), III 5 (21.7%), IV 2 (8.7%); upfront resection in 21 (91.3%) with 17 complete resections and 4 incomplete, no surgical morbidity or mortality; no radiotherapy; remission in 21/23 (91.3%).
  • Three patients relapsed and none were salvaged: one stage 2 SLCT, one stage 3 JGCT (primary resection, 6 courses JEb, relapse after chemotherapy, incompletely resectable) and one stage 4 JGCT (good initial response to BEP, recurrence after 6 courses, died before second-line therapy).
  • 5-year OS 82.1% (95% CI 65.5-100%) and 5-year EFS 83.8% (95% CI 68.6-100%) for the whole SCST cohort; no JGCT-specific survival estimate is given. Only underweight-for-age significantly affected OS and EFS (P<0.0001); age, stage, histology, socioeconomic status and chemotherapy type were not predictive; the authors call the nutrition finding tenuous given small numbers.
  • Chemotherapy was not standardized: 12 patients had none; carboplatin-based JEb was used in 6 as an outpatient option because cisplatin was given only as inpatient; 2 of the cisplatin-treated patients had documented hearing loss; GFR and audiology assessment were infrequent and not standardized; DICER1 testing was not available.
  • Authors compare with MAKEI (EFS 87%, OS 88% in 62 non-testicular SCST) and the French TGM-95 study (5-year EFS 85%, OS 94%) and conclude outcomes were similar despite varied treatment; they call for national harmonized protocols and registry reporting of rare tumors.

Presentation

  • abdominopelvic mass 26.1%
  • abdominal pain and distension 17% each
  • precocious puberty 13.0% (all under 5 years)
  • vaginal bleeding 13.0%
  • virilization in one girl

Treatments reported

  • Surgery alone (no chemotherapy) (12 of 23 SCST patients received no chemotherapy, 11 of them FIGO stage 1; n = 12)
    Outcome: one stage 3A patient without chemotherapy was a long-term survivor
  • JEb (carboplatin, etoposide, bleomycin) x6 patients; BEP x3; PEb x1; hybrid PEb/JEb x1; MMT8 vincristine/dactinomycin x1 (platinum-based chemotherapy for stage 1C and above, all SCST; n = 11)
    Outcome: 2 hearing losses from cisplatin; stage 3 JGCT relapsed after 6 courses of JEb; stage 4 JGCT relapsed after 6 courses of BEP and died before second-line therapy

Notes

Tier set to registry because the data come from a national study-group collation (South African Children's Tumour Registry acknowledged). The abstract says 3 patients were lost to follow-up whereas Table 1 shows 2 'Lost' and the text says 2 girls were lost (18 of 23 had documented follow-up). Text says 21 primary resections but 17 + 4 incomplete; the text reports mean estradiol '11,1134 IU/L' (typo). JGCT counts per stage/outcome were counted by this record's author from Table 1. Possible small overlap with other SCST reports cited in the paper (MAKEI, TGM-95) is not expected.
