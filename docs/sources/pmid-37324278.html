Ovarian juvenile granulosa cell tumors with Ollier's disease in children with IDH1 gene somatic mutation.

Why it matters for JGCT

First reported IDH1 R132C somatic mutation shared by an ovarian JGCT and Ollier's enchondroma; links the two entities and provides a review table of 11 cases.

Key findings

  • A 4-year-old girl with Ollier's disease (multiple lytic lesions of the right femur, tibia, fibula, left ulna, radius and phalanges; wide resection of a distal right femur lesion confirmed enchondroma) developed bilateral Tanner III breast development, labial hypertrophy and vulvar discharge about 9 months later.
  • Estradiol was 1,211 pmol/L (reference <=115.6) and prolactin 43.99 ng/ml (3.3-24.1); ultrasound showed an 81 x 76 x 57 mm well-encapsulated multilocular-solid right ovarian tumor and CT showed a right ovarian solid mass; right salpingo-oophorectomy was performed.
  • Histology showed juvenile granulosa cell tumor, FIGO stage IA; IHC: vimentin +, AE1/AE3 weak +, CD117 scattered +, PR +, CD99 +, alpha-inhibin single +, CD56 +, SMA +, Ki67 about 30%; negative for CK7, EMA, ER, CD30, PLAP, SALL4, OCT3/4, hCG, CK8, desmin and S-100.
  • Whole-exome and Sanger sequencing found a somatic IDH1 c.394C>T (p.Arg132Cys) mutation in both the ovarian JGCT and the enchondroma but not in blood, skin, muscle, oral mucosa or the parents' blood.
  • In HeLa cells, IDH1 R132C (3.77-fold overexpression) versus wild type (4.46-fold) reduced phosphorylation of S6 ribosomal protein, interpreted as an effect on the mTOR pathway; the regulatory mechanism was not defined.
  • After surgery estradiol (70.83 pmol/L) and prolactin (20.54 ng/ml) normalized within 1 week, breasts regressed, no recurrence at 3 months, and hormones remained normal to 2 years of follow-up.
  • The authors tabulated 10 prior published cases of JGCT with Ollier's disease (JGCT age 0.5 to 36 years; 8 salpingo-oophorectomies, 2 with postoperative chemotherapy; follow-up 6 months to 7 years without recurrence; 2 cases had chondrosarcoma transformation) and, with their case, 11 cases.
  • The authors propose generalized mesodermal dysplasia with IDH1 somatic mutation as a contributor and recommend regular follow-up; they state this is the first report of gene testing among reported JGCT with Ollier's disease.

Presentation

  • bilateral breast development Tanner III with areolar pigmentation
  • hypertrophy of labia majora and vulvar discharge
  • elevated estradiol 1,211 pmol/L and prolactin 43.99 ng/ml
  • multiple skeletal deformities and enchondromas (Ollier's disease)
  • 81 x 76 x 57 mm multilocular-solid right ovarian tumor

Genetic and molecular findings

  • IDH1: c.394C>T, p.Arg132Cys (exon 4), somatic (1/1 patient; present in ovarian JGCT and enchondroma tissue, absent from peripheral blood, skin, muscle, oral mucosa and parental blood)
    Classified pathogenic/likely pathogenic; ClinVar 375891; gnomAD frequency 0.00001; HeLa transfection showed R132C inhibited phosphorylation of S6 ribosomal protein (p<0.05)

Treatments reported

  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy; no adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy (FIGO stage IA ovarian JGCT with Ollier's disease; n = 1)
    Outcome: Estradiol and prolactin normalized within 1 week; no recurrence at 3 months; hormone levels normal to 2 years

Notes

Abstract says follow-up findings consistent with full text; full text gives follow-up as 3 months for recurrence and 2 years for hormones, while table 2 lists 6 months. Table 1 labels the variant 'hemizygous' though described heterozygous in text. Functional work was in HeLa cells, not in granulosa cells. Table 2 includes one case with JGCT at 36 years (not juvenile age). Possible topical overlap with pmid-35968204 (a different Ollier's/JGCT patient).
