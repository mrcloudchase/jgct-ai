A 12-Year-Old Girl with Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor of the Ovary, Presenting with Adolescent Hyperprolactinemia, Galactorrhea, and Amenorrhea.

Why it matters for JGCT

Unusual JGCT presentation with hyperprolactinemia, galactorrhea and secondary amenorrhea and a very large (1425 g) stage IA tumor.

Key findings

  • 12-year-old obese girl (menarche at 10, no regular menses for 2 years) with intermittent left lower abdominal pain and bilateral galactorrhea; prolactin 70.5 ng/mL (normal 4.8-23.3) with total beta-hCG <0.6; initial working diagnosis was anterior pituitary hyperfunction.
  • Ultrasound showed an incidental large left pelvic mass (heterogeneous, predominantly solid); Doppler showed no torsion. CT: heterogeneously enhancing, partially necrotic cystic-solid mass about 15.0 x 9.0 x 18.8 cm arising from the left adnexa, abutting the uterine fundus without invasion and partially compressing the IVC and common iliac veins.
  • En bloc left salpingo-oophorectomy; resected mass weighed 1425 g, confined to the ovary with intact capsule, no surface tumor or lymphovascular invasion; omental biopsy and peritoneal washings negative.
  • Pathology: JGCT, FIGO stage 1A; immunohistochemistry positive for inhibin and calretinin. No precocious puberty or overt hyperestrogenism.
  • Discharged on hospital day 4 and doing well at 2 weeks; no chemotherapy given.
  • Authors' literature statements: GCT incidence 0.4-1.7 per 100,000 women, JGCT about 5% of GCTs, median JGCT age 8-17 years; stage I 5-year survival 90-95% but GCTs can recur 20-30 years later; tumor rupture in 10%; prognostic factors include stage, size, BMI and failure to resect en bloc.
  • The authors conclude JGCT can present with amenorrhea rather than precocious puberty and should be in the differential for young girls with abdominal pain and hormonal irregularity.

Presentation

  • secondary amenorrhea (menarche at 10, irregular for 2 years)
  • intermittent left lower abdominal pain for 2-3 months
  • galactorrhea with prolactin 70.5 ng/mL
  • incidental large pelvic mass; obesity

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy with en bloc left salpingo-oophorectomy; omental biopsy and peritoneal washings; no chemotherapy (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: Uneventful recovery, discharged hospital day 4, well at 2-week clinic follow-up (no longer follow-up reported)

Notes

Title/abstract call her 12 years old; the conclusion calls her 'pre-pubertal' although she had menarche at 10 years. Follow-up limited to 2 weeks. Galactorrhea-JGCT link is described by authors as underreported; no mechanism established.
