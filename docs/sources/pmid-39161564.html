Unusual radiologic imaging in juvenile granulosa cell tumor with precocious puberty: A unilocular cyst.

Why it matters for JGCT

Rare unilocular cystic JGCT in a toddler, with peripheral precocious puberty and prolactin elevation; reviews imaging patterns.

Key findings

  • 2-year-2-month-old girl with vaginal bleeding (first episode at 4 days, attributed to maternal hormones; recurrence 1 week before admission), breast enlargement from age 1 year and abdominal enlargement; weight 15 kg, height 86 cm; bone age (Greulich and Pyle) 3 years 6 months.
  • Labs: prolactin 54.41 ng/mL, estradiol 120 pg/mL, LDH 328.5 U/L, CA-125 39.6 mIU/mL, low FSH 0.045 mIU/mL; LH, thyroid function, beta-hCG, AFP and CEA normal, indicating peripheral rather than central precocity.
  • Ultrasound: single unilocular anechoic cyst, septa-free, with posterior enhancement and no visible solid component, about 10.1 x 4.76 x 9.8 cm extending from pelvis to abdomen. MRI: right ovarian cyst about 5.2 x 10.9 x 11.8 cm with an enhancing solid mural component up to about 1.01 cm, right hydroureter and mild pelviocaliceal ectasia, and enlarged uterus.
  • Laparoscopic right ovariectomy after decompression; FIGO stage IA; no additional treatment.
  • Pathology: sex cord-stromal tumor with high mitotic activity (20/10 HPF); IHC positive for inhibin, calretinin and WT-1, negative for CK and CD10, consistent with JGCT.
  • Authors conclude imaging of JGCT is nonspecific, unilocular purely cystic presentation is rare, and histopathology with IHC remains mandatory for diagnosis.
  • Authors cite from literature: JGCT about 5% of GCTs, average age 13 years, about 82% of prepubertal patients present with early puberty signs, JGCT cystic 14%, solid 37%, solid and cystic 45%, and about 90% 10-year survival with late recurrence possible 10-20 years later.

Presentation

  • vaginal bleeding (first at age 4 days, again 1 week before admission)
  • breast enlargement from age 1 year
  • progressive abdominal enlargement
  • bone age 3 years 6 months
  • estradiol 120 pg/mL with low FSH

Treatments reported

  • Laparoscopic right ovariectomy after direct cyst decompression (DJ stent planned for right hydroureter) (FIGO stage IA; no additional treatment; n = 1)
    Outcome: No additional treatment needed; no follow-up duration reported

Notes

Hyperprolactinemia is reported but its cause is not established. The note about 'JGCT in males' (Swiss cheese pattern, gubernaculum continuation) is from a cited paper, not this patient. Text calls the lesion a purely cystic unilocular lesion despite a solid mural component seen on MRI. No molecular data.
