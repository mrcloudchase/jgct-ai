Why it matters for JGCT

Marginal: one sentence on neonatal (testicular) JGCT in sex-chromosomal mosaicism with ambiguous genitalia and testis-sparing surgery; the rest is about germ cell tumors in DSD.

Key findings

  • Narrative review of gonadal tumor risk in disorders of sex development (DSD; 1 in 4,500-5,000 live births); DSD patients are at increased risk of testicular carcinoma in situ and germ cell tumors, and the introduction names juvenile granulosa cell tumors (JGCTs) among other tumors of concern.
  • The only JGCT-specific statement: JGCT, a sex cord-stromal tumor, has been noted in rare cases of sex-chromosomal mosaicism and ambiguous genitalia in the neonatal period; the review states that these tumors are benign and hormonally inactive so testis-sparing surgery should be curative (cited to a single source; the review gives no case numbers).
  • Main review conclusions concern other tumors: Y-chromosomal material and gonadal position are risk factors; 46,XX congenital adrenal hyperplasia without Y material is not at higher risk; gonadoblastoma risk in dysgenetic DSD 4.7-25% and overall tumor risk 15-33%; partial AIS with untreated undescended testes up to 50%.
  • Markers discussed for DSD tumors beyond hCG and AFP are OCT3/4, TSPY, stem cell factor and WT1; no JGCT marker data are given.

Notes

The JGCT statement is a brief citation-based claim; its wording ('benign and hormonally inactive') conflicts with the usual description of JGCT as potentially hormone-active in the ovary but is consistent with infantile testicular JGCT, and the review does not specify the site. tumor_site recorded as testis on that inference.
