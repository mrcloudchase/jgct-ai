Why it matters for JGCT

Infant ovarian JGCT presenting as an acute abdomen with massive ascites and tumor rupture, recognized by bedside ultrasound; illustrates the rupture presentation.

Key findings

  • A 5-month-old girl presented to the emergency department with 4 days of worsening abdominal distention and 1 day of postprandial projectile non-bloody non-bilious emesis; outside work-up showed anemia, leukocytosis and hyponatremia.
  • Point-of-care FAST ultrasound showed extensive heterogeneous free fluid (Morison's pouch, subphrenic space, splenorenal recess) and a large heterogeneous pelvic mass.
  • CT showed large-volume ascites with centralized bowel loops and a 6.4 x 6.8 x 10.9 cm midline pelvic mass.
  • At laparotomy 500 mL of serosanguinous fluid was drained and a left salpingo-oophorectomy removed an actively bleeding tumor (described as arising from the left fallopian tube) with a ruptured tumor found; she was transfused and pathology showed juvenile granulosa cell tumor.
  • No tumor markers, hormone levels, immunohistochemistry, stage, or post-discharge follow-up are reported.
  • The discussion states JGCT presents as acute abdomen from rupture in about 6-10% of cases, there is no specific tumor marker though inhibin is elevated in adult and juvenile GCT, early-stage 5-year survival is 90-95%, adjuvant chemotherapy has not been found useful in early stages, and late recurrences require long-term follow-up (authors' literature statements).

Presentation

  • 4 days of progressive abdominal distention
  • postprandial projectile non-bilious emesis
  • pallor, grunting, firm distended tender abdomen
  • anemia, leukocytosis, hyponatremia
  • massive ascites with pelvic mass
  • ruptured tumor

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy, drainage of 500 mL serosanguinous fluid, left salpingo-oophorectomy, packed red cell transfusion (ruptured, actively bleeding tumor; n = 1)
    Outcome: Uncomplicated hospital course; no adjuvant therapy or longer follow-up reported

Notes

Emergency-medicine educational case report; pathology details and outcome are minimal. Text says the tumor was removed from the 'left fallopian tube' with a left salpingo-oophorectomy, while the title and diagnosis are ovarian JGCT. The abstract mentions the ruptured tumor; the full text says the tumor was actively bleeding.
