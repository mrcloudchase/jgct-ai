Why it matters for JGCT

Infant ovarian JGCT with ruptured tumor, massive ascites and precocious pseudopuberty, treated by fertility-sparing surgery alone.

Key findings

  • A 3-month-old girl presented with 10 days of abdominal distension, fever, feeding intolerance and vaginal discharge, with tachypnea, breast development (Tanner B2) and protruding vaginal mucosa (isosexual pseudo-precocious puberty).
  • Hemoglobin 9 g%; AFP and beta-hCG normal; CA-125 160 units/ml; LDH 1000 IU/L. CT showed an enhancing solid-cystic mass of 5.2 x 5.7 x 6.3 cm and free abdominal fluid without lymphadenopathy.
  • Laparotomy drained about 900 cc blood-tinged ascitic fluid and found a 7 x 6 x 6.5 cm solid-cystic left ovarian mass with a small capsular rent (preoperative rupture); uterus, right ovary and other organs normal.
  • Left oophorectomy preserving the fallopian tube was performed (to preserve future fertility); one or two episodes of vaginal bleeding occurred 24 h after surgery, explained by authors as hormone withdrawal.
  • Histology: diffuse and macrofollicular growth of round to oval cells with prominent nucleoli; IHC diffuse inhibin and vimentin, focal calretinin positivity.
  • The authors staged the case FIGO IC although ascitic fluid showed no malignant cells.
  • At 1 year the child was asymptomatic, tumor-free and meeting age-appropriate milestones; authors stress long-term follow-up and IHC for diagnosis, and claim this is probably the youngest JGCT with precocious puberty reported.
  • Discussion cites Young et al. (125 ovarian JGCTs): rupture in 13 (11%), ascites in 11 (9%), isosexual pseudoprecocity in 82% of prepubertal patients.

Presentation

  • abdominal distension (girth 48 cm) with respiratory distress
  • fever, feeding intolerance
  • whitish vaginal discharge, protruding vaginal mucosa
  • breast development Tanner B2
  • massive blood-tinged ascites

Treatments reported

  • Left oophorectomy with preservation of the left fallopian tube after hemodynamic stabilization; ~900 cc ascites drained; no adjuvant therapy reported (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: asymptomatic, tumor-free and meeting milestones at 1 year

Notes

Full text obtained from the Ovid journal page (open access). The 'youngest case' claim is the authors' own. Authors use 'oophorectomy' with tube preserved.
