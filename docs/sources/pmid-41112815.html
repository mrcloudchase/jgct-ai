A rare ovarian tumor in childhood: Juvenile granulosa cell tumor revealed by pelvic pain and ovarian torsion.

Why it matters for JGCT

Illustrates JGCT presenting as ovarian torsion in a 5-year-old with imaging mimicking malignancy and a fertility-sparing partial oophorectomy.

Key findings

  • 5-year-old girl with one month of pelvic heaviness and 24 hours of acute pelvic pain, febrile, diffuse pelvic tenderness, no palpable mass.
  • Ultrasound: large heterogeneous solid pelvic mass without cystic components or torsion signs; MRI: 41 x 57 x 48 mm solid mass, iso-intense T1, intermediate T2, restricted diffusion, heterogeneous enhancement with necrosis, abutting the recto-sigmoid junction suggesting possible infiltration; CT thorax-abdomen-pelvis showed no metastases.
  • Markers: AFP and beta-hCG negative, LDH 269 U/L (normal 135-225), CA-125 20.4 U/mL, CA 15-3 7.2 U/mL; inhibin and AMH not measured (availability limited).
  • Surgery: firm encapsulated 6 cm left ovarian mass, violaceous adnexa with 2 twists; detorsion and monobloc excision with partial oophorectomy; no bowel invasion; histopathology confirmed JGCT (no stage, IHC or mitotic details reported in the case section).
  • Torsion was not recognized on preoperative imaging, which the authors attribute to the size and solid nature of the mass.
  • Authors conclude JGCT should be considered in prepubertal girls with pelvic masses and that early surgical management has an excellent prognosis; discussion (literature-based) notes fertility-sparing surgery is preferred and that inhibin and AMH can be used to monitor recurrence.

Presentation

  • one month of pelvic heaviness
  • acute pelvic pain for 24 hours
  • fever
  • diffuse pelvic tenderness without palpable mass
  • ovarian torsion (2 twists)

Treatments reported

  • Detorsion, monobloc excision with partial oophorectomy of left ovary (surgery within 48 hours of presentation; n = 1)
    Outcome: uneventful postoperative recovery; no adjuvant therapy reported; no long-term follow-up reported

Notes

Case report with sparse pathology detail and no follow-up duration. Size discrepancy: MRI 41 x 57 x 48 mm vs 6 cm at surgery. Authors used ChatGPT for language editing. Statements on prognosis, IHC (inhibin, calretinin, FOXL2) and FOXL2 402C>G are general literature in the discussion, not findings in this patient.
