Why it matters for JGCT

Case of pediatric JGCT presenting with massive ascites and urinary retention rather than precocious puberty, stage IA, with IHC.

Key findings

  • 2-year-old girl (consanguineous parents; two earlier siblings died at 3 months and 2 years of unknown causes) with 2 months of urinary retention and gradual abdominal distention; no signs of precocious puberty.
  • Ultrasound: 11.5 x 6.7 cm mixed solid-cystic pelvic mass with gross ascites; CT: heterogeneous solid-cystic mass (11 x 8.1 x 7.5 cm) arising from the left adnexa without calcification, ascites, no lymphadenopathy. AFP and beta-hCG normal.
  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy with 1 L ascites drained; ascitic fluid cytology negative for malignant cells; tumor 1.3 kg, capsule intact; staged FIGO IA; bone scan and chest radiograph normal.
  • Histology: diffuse sheets and macrofollicular pattern, polygonal cells, atypical nuclei without grooves, mitoses more than 10/10 HPF, focal luteinization, consistent with JGCT; IHC positive for inhibin and CD99, negative for EMA, CD117 and AFP.
  • Outcome: well at 3 months; no adjuvant chemotherapy given; long-term follow-up planned because of late recurrence risk.
  • Authors claim this is the first reported JGCT stage IA with massive ascites and tumor-cell-negative ascitic fluid; they note ascites occurs in about 10% of JGCT, usually with advanced stage or rupture, and cite another case (age 13, stage IC) with massive ascites.
  • Authors state (citing literature) that JGCT is about 5% of granulosa cell tumors, stage IA is treated by unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy alone, 5-year survival is up to 90% for stage IA, and late recurrence requires lifelong follow-up.

Presentation

  • 2 months of urinary retention, painful micturition, abdominal distention with mild pain
  • massive ascites with positive fluid thrill and shifting dullness
  • no precocious puberty (normal breast and genitalia)

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy, drainage of 1 L ascites, left salpingo-oophorectomy; no adjuvant therapy (FIGO stage IA; n = 1)
    Outcome: well at 3 months; urinary retention resolved; long-term follow-up planned

Notes

Follow-up is only 3 months. The 'first case in literature' claim is the authors' own. Cited literature statements (survival, ascites frequency) are background, not data from this patient. Pakistani case; the reference to Ashnagar et al. concerns a separate case (not overlapping).
