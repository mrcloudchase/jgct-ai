Why it matters for JGCT

Series of 14 surgically treated pediatric ovarian lesions; contains exactly one JGCT with precocious puberty and no further JGCT detail. Useful only as a frequency data point.

Key findings

  • Retrospective single-center review (King Abdulaziz Medical City, Riyadh, 1997-2016) of 14 girls aged 14 or younger who had surgical removal of ovarian lesions; mean age 5.7 +/- 5.1 years.
  • Histopathology (Table 3): simple ovarian cyst 7 (50%), mature cystic teratoma 3 (21.4%), paraovarian cyst 1, hemorrhagic cyst 1, juvenile granulosa cell tumor 1 (7.1%), mixed germ cell tumor 1 (7.1%).
  • The single JGCT patient was the only one in the series with endocrine disturbance and precocious puberty; no further individual details (age, stage, surgery, outcome) are reported for her.
  • The only death in the series was a girl with a mixed germ cell tumor, not the JGCT patient; the authors list JGCT and mixed germ cell tumor as the two malignant lesions.
  • Authors conclude most pediatric ovarian lesions were benign; the small single-center sample limits generalization.

Presentation

  • the JGCT patient was the only one with endocrine disturbance and precocious puberty

Notes

JGCT is 1/14 of operated pediatric ovarian lesions in this single center. Journal and author not in fetched text. The text states ovarian lesions had 'very good prognoses' with one death (germ cell tumor).
