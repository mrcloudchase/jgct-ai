Why it matters for JGCT

Small JGCT series (n=8) with outcomes after fertility-sparing surgery in stage I disease and adjuvant chemotherapy in nearly all; supports but cannot prove oncologic safety of fertility sparing.

Key findings

  • Retrospective single-center series (Obstetrics and Gynecology Hospital of Fudan University, China; January 2004 to October 2018) of 8 surgically treated JGCT patients, ages 9 to 32 years; JGCT was 8.7% (8/92) of the center's granulosa cell tumors (AGCT 84/92).
  • Presentation: irregular vaginal bleeding in 6, abdominal mass and pain in 2; 5/8 (62.5%) had a pelvic mass over 10 cm; all unilateral; 7 ruptured tumors; cytology negative in the 5 patients tested.
  • Staging (FIGO 2009): 7 patients stage I (6 IC, 1 IA) had fertility-sparing surgery, median age 17 years (range 9-31); 1 patient with stage IIIC who had completed childbearing had complete surgery.
  • Immunohistochemistry: inhibin A positive in the stage I patients and negative in the stage IIIC patient; calretinin positive in patients 1-3, 6 and 7; CD99 and vimentin positive (patients 1, 2-8 as stated); EMA negative.
  • Mitotic counts available for only 3 patients; one had more than 20 per 10 HPF and no recurrence. Of 4 patients with pelvic lymphadenectomy versus 3 without, no difference in recurrence-free survival was found.
  • 7 of 8 received adjuvant chemotherapy (2-7 courses): bleomycin-etoposide-carboplatin in 4 and bleomycin-vincristine-carboplatin in 3.
  • Median follow-up was 64 months (range 2-117). No recurrence in the fertility-sparing group (overall survival 100%); the stage IIIC patient recurred and died 1 month after initial treatment.
  • Authors' conclusion: fertility-sparing surgery seems safe for stage I JGCT in carefully selected, informed patients treated by gynecologic oncology specialists, with complete surgery considered after childbearing; the small number of cases requires larger or multicenter confirmation.

Presentation

  • irregular vaginal bleeding in 6/8
  • abdominal mass and abdominal pain in 2/8
  • 5/8 had a pelvic mass over 10 cm
  • all tumors unilateral
  • 7/8 tumors ruptured, 1 intact

Treatments reported

  • Fertility-sparing surgery (unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, with or without pelvic lymphadenectomy and omentectomy) (stage I (6 stage IC, 1 stage IA); n = 7)
    Outcome: No recurrence; overall survival 100%; survivors had normal menstruation; none had attempted pregnancy
  • Complete surgery (stage IIIC, childbearing completed; n = 1)
    Outcome: Recurred and died 1 month after initial treatment
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy: bleomycin, etoposide, carboplatin (4); bleomycin, vincristine, carboplatin (3); 2-7 courses (7 of 8 patients (6 of 7 in the fertility-sparing group and the stage IIIC patient); n = 7)
    Outcome: see above
