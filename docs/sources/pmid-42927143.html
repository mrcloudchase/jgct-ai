Peripheral Precocious Puberty as the Initial Manifestation of Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor in Early Childhood.

Why it matters for JGCT

Single well-documented case of FIGO IA JGCT in a toddler with peripheral precocious puberty, very high inhibin B and surgery-only management.

Key findings

  • A 2-year-9-month-old girl had 5 months of progressive breast development, persistent whitish discharge, vaginal bleeding for 15 days and a firm left pelvic mass of about 10 cm.
  • LH <0.10 IU/L and FSH <0.30 IU/L (suppressed) with inhibin B >5000 pg/mL (reference <182); AMH 6.88 ng/mL, DHEAS, androstenedione, AFP 1.3 ng/mL and beta-hCG were normal; preoperative estradiol result was unavailable for review and bone age was not assessed.
  • CT showed a 103 x 63 x 117 mm heterogeneous enhancing suprauterine mass with moderate ascites and endometrial thickening of 5 mm; MRI showed a 110 x 55 x 99 mm multilocular solid-cystic mass with hemorrhagic components, diffusion restriction and left pedicle twisting.
  • Surgery found a well-encapsulated left ovarian tumor (10.5 x 8 x 6 cm); left oophorectomy preserved the ipsilateral tube, contralateral ovary and uterus. Ascitic cytology and omentum were negative; stage FIGO IA.
  • Histology showed lobulated solid areas with cystic spaces, round cells with vesicular nuclei, necrosis foci and 8 mitoses per 10 HPF; IHC was calretinin and androgen receptor positive, focal inhibin positive, and CK19, AFP and CD117 negative.
  • No adjuvant chemotherapy was given; at 12 months estrogenic signs had resolved, inhibin B, LH, FSH and estradiol were normal and there was no clinical, biochemical or radiological recurrence.
  • The authors discuss (citing other studies) that mitotic index >19/10 HPF was associated with recurrences in an international cohort, that inhibin B was elevated in 90% of tested patients at diagnosis but only 3 of 5 evaluable relapsing patients re-elevated at relapse, and that AKT1 duplications rather than FOXL2 C134W characterize JGCT.
  • The authors conclude that progressive estrogenic signs in a prepubertal girl should prompt pelvic imaging for an ovarian tumor and that complete resection of localized disease is generally curative.

Presentation

  • progressive breast development (unilateral then bilateral, Tanner B2)
  • persistent leukorrhea
  • vaginal bleeding
  • left pelvic mass about 10 cm
  • moderate ascites
  • few pubic hairs on labia majora

Treatments reported

  • Fertility-sparing left oophorectomy with staging (ascitic cytology, omental sampling); no adjuvant chemotherapy; active surveillance every 3 months (FIGO stage IA; n = 1)
    Outcome: hormones normalized within 2 months; no recurrence at 12 months

Notes

Abstract wording 'hormonal abnormalities normalized within two months' agrees with text. Discussion statements on Harris and Schneider cohorts are secondhand citations by the authors, not findings of this case.
