Why it matters for JGCT

Only two JGCT specimens; useful for cytologic features and for the AKT1 alteration in an anaplastic JGCT. Mostly an adult GCT cytology series.

Key findings

  • Memorial Sloan Kettering retrospective review of cytology slides (2009-2020) from females with sex cord-stromal tumors: 30 specimens from 24 patients (25 adult GCT specimens from 19 patients, 2 JGCT from 2 patients, 2 Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors, 1 steroid cell tumor); age range 7-90 years.
  • JGCT case 1 (patient aged 7 years, FIGO IC2, no evidence of disease at follow-up per Table 2): pelvic washing with loose clusters and single cells, intermediate N:C ratio, moderate to marked nuclear pleomorphism, coarsely granular chromatin, conspicuous nucleoli, bizarre multinucleated cells, atypical mitoses and necrosis; surgical specimen solid diffuse pattern, designated JGCT with anaplastic features; expressed inhibin and FOXL2.
  • JGCT case 1 targeted sequencing found an AKT1 exon 4 insertion (p.R76_C77insLKTERPRPNTFIIR) and a TP53 exon 8 splicing variant.
  • JGCT case 2 (age 65-66 years per tables, FIGO IIIB, first recurrence at 1.3 years): ascitic/peritoneal fluid with similar cytology but only moderate pleomorphism, no bizarre cells, atypical mitoses or necrosis; inhibin and progesterone receptor positive on cell block; no molecular data.
  • Compared with adult GCT, JGCT cytology showed small clusters and single round-oval cells, moderate to marked pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, and lacked nuclear grooves and rosettes/microfollicles; 8% of adult GCTs also lacked grooves, so distinguishing the two can be hard in paucicellular specimens.
  • The authors state no immunohistochemical marker distinguishes JGCT from adult GCT; JGCTs often express FOXL2 but lack the canonical FOXL2 p.C134W mutation present in all 7/7 adult GCTs tested; they cite AKT1 duplications in 10 of 16 JGCTs from another study.
  • Authors cite JGCT as 5% of granulosa cell tumors, mean age 13 years, 90% before age 30, and a postoperative 5-year disease-free survival over 90% for stage-confined tumors (background statements, cited from other work).

Presentation

  • Cytology from peritoneal washing (case 1, case 2 text also says ascitic fluid)

Genetic and molecular findings

  • AKT1: exon 4 insertion p.R76_C77insLKTERPRPNTFIIR (1/1 JGCT tested (case 1))

  • TP53: exon 8 splice variant (1/1 JGCT tested (case 1))

Notes

Ages for JGCT case 2 differ between Table 1 (66 y) and Table 2 (65 y) and the table stages the 7-year-old as IC2. The text calls case 2 an ascitic fluid specimen while Table 1 lists peritoneal washing. Journal and DOI not stated in the retrieved text.
