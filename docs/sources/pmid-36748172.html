Juvenile granulosa cell tumor: 20 years' experience of a Tertiary Center.

Why it matters for JGCT

Small institutional series showing 2/5 recurrences (12 and 48 months), including one recurrence of stage IA disease, with all patients alive in remission.

Key findings

  • Retrospective series of 5 JGCT patients (2000-2020, one center, Istanbul) after excluding 3 of 8 with missing records or follow-up; ages 9, 16, 16, 15 and 25; one premenarchal girl (age 9) without precocious puberty signs; none had high estradiol and none were obese (BMI 17.8-24).
  • All had abdominal swelling and a unilateral adnexal mass without signs of metastasis; tumor sizes 14, 11, 24, 30 and 19 cm; CA-125 high in 3 (373, 44 and 135 U/mL as tabulated), serum AMH high (23 ng/mL, normal 0.05-11) in one patient; inhibin and calretinin positive in all.
  • FIGO stage: 3 patients IA, 1 IC1 (intraoperative rupture), 1 IC2 (preoperative rupture with free fluid). All underwent fertility-preserving surgery; 2 patients received adjuvant chemotherapy.
  • Histology: multiloculated cystic tumors with solid areas, no Call-Exner bodies in any, mitoses 7-44 per 10 HPF; one patient had focal striking atypia; pseudopapillary and trabecular patterns in one.
  • Median follow-up 60 months (range 12-96); recurrence in 2/5 (40%): Patient 3 (stage IA) recurred at 12 months as a 10 cm mass in the contralateral ovary with final histology gynandroblastoma, treated with surgery plus 3 cycles paclitaxel-carboplatin, no evidence of disease at 49 months; Patient 5 (stage IC2, had 6 cycles cisplatin-etoposide) recurred at 48 months, resected, no evidence of disease at 12 months.
  • All patients were in remission at last follow-up and none died of disease. Four patients had mitotic index above 10/10 HPF and 3 of them did not recur, so the authors saw no correlation between mitotic index and relapse; the only vimentin-positive patient recurred.
  • Authors conclude JGCT should be considered in a young patient with a unilateral adnexal mass with benign features and that recurrence is not uncommon even though stage IA prognosis is good.

Presentation

  • abdominal swelling in all 5
  • menstrual irregularity in 3 (patients 2, 3, 4)
  • urinary incontinence (patient 5)
  • unilateral adnexal mass, 3 right and 2 left
  • preoperative tumor rupture with free fluid in 1

Treatments reported

  • Fertility-preserving surgery (cystectomy, unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy with staging, or tumorectomy) in all 5 (primary; n = 5)
    Outcome: Median follow-up 60 months (range 12-96); 2/5 recurred (at 12 and 48 months); all in remission at last follow-up; no deaths
  • Paclitaxel plus carboplatin, 3 cycles (Patient 3, after recurrence (stage IA primary; recurrent tumor final histology gynandroblastoma); n = 1)
    Outcome: No evidence of disease at 49 months after secondary surgery
  • Cisplatin plus etoposide, 6 cycles (Patient 5, stage IC2 after primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: Recurrence 48 months after primary surgery (isolated 10 cm left ovarian mass), resected without residual disease; no evidence of disease at 12 months after second surgery
  • No adjuvant chemotherapy (3 patients (stage IA x2, and stage IC1 who refused recommended chemotherapy); n = 3)
    Outcome: No recurrence at 96, 12 and 24 months respectively

Notes

Abstract says 5 patients (and 'one of whom was premenarcheal'); methods state 8 were identified and 3 excluded for lost follow-up. Inconsistencies: Patient 3's follow-up appears as 84 months in Table 1 while the text gives 49 months after secondary surgery; recurrent tumor in Patient 3 had final histopathology of gynandroblastoma, not JGCT, which the authors still count as a JGCT recurrence. The text states mean age 17 years, which was not recomputed here; the authors also say only one patient was over 20 years. Discussion statements on stage distribution (88% IA, 2% IB, 8% IC, 3% II), 5-year survival in advanced stage 25-50%, and recurrence timing are literature citations, not data from this series. The mitotic index for Patient 3 is unspecified.
