A Novel Pathogenic Variant of DICER1 Gene in a Young Greek Patient with 2 Different Sex-Cord Ovarian Tumors and Multinodular Goiter.

Why it matters for JGCT

Case of germline DICER1 syndrome with a stage IA JGCT arising 3 years after bilateral SLCT, with JGCT immunohistochemistry and a novel exon 16 DICER1 frameshift variant.

Key findings

  • Young Greek woman with germline DICER1 syndrome: multinodular goiter at 16 (7 nodules, benign FNA), irregular menses with testosterone 3.1 ng/mL and low FSH at 17, bilateral moderately differentiated Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors (SLCT) at 18 (20.6 cm left mass, 5.6 cm right mass, ascites; inhibin 443 pg/mL, CA125 359.7 U/mL; FIGO IB), and a JGCT at 21.
  • JGCT: 13 x 9 x 7.5 cm multilocular cystic lesion in the right parametrial area found on follow-up MRI 3 years after SLCT surgery; no capsular tearing, 7 right pelvic lymph nodes tumor-free, FIGO stage IA; managed by excision and follow-up without adjuvant chemotherapy.
  • JGCT immunohistochemistry: positive for CKAE1/AE3 (focal dot-like juxtanuclear), vimentin, inhibin, CD56, CD99, WT-1, PR and focally calretinin; histology showed a diffuse growth pattern with round hyperchromatic nuclei and rare grooves.
  • WES found a novel heterozygous DICER1 frameshift variant in exon 16 (c.2685dupA, p.Phe896Ilefs*5), pathogenic by ACMG/AMP criteria; her mother carries it and is asymptomatic at 50; a maternal aunt had uterine rhabdomyosarcoma at 11 and goiter requiring thyroidectomy at 21.
  • SLCT treatment: unilateral left oophorectomy and right tumor excision, 3 cycles of BEP; hormones and markers normalized within about 20 days of surgery; no recurrence at about 6 years.
  • Literature statements: JGCT is a very rare DICER1-syndrome phenotype associated mainly with somatic DICER1 mutations; two probable germline-associated JGCT cases cited (age 16, and age 2 in a patient with a relative with pleuropulmonary blastoma); stage I JGCT survival about 97% as cited; about 5% (by age 10) and 19% (by age 50) of germline carriers develop a neoplasm.
  • Authors conclude clinicians should consider germline DICER1 variants when multinodular goiter in young patients coexists with other neoplasms and that genetic testing and family screening are warranted.

Presentation

  • asymptomatic JGCT found on follow-up abdominal MRI 3 years after SLCT surgery
  • earlier: irregular menses, acne, hyperandrogenemia, bilateral SLCT with 20.6 cm mass and ascites

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: germline frameshift, exon 16, c.2685dupA (NM_001195573), p.Phe896Ilefs*5; novel, classified pathogenic (ACMG/AMP) (heterozygous (WES/NGS, >100x reads); mother carries same variant, asymptomatic)
    found after SLCT diagnosis in 2019; JGCT tissue not separately sequenced

Treatments reported

  • Surgical excision of JGCT (right parametrial multilocular cystic lesion), no adjuvant therapy (stage IA JGCT at age 21; n = 1)
    Outcome: referred for follow-up; no recurrence reported (ESMO: stage IA needs no adjuvant therapy)
  • Left oophorectomy plus right ovarian tumor excision, then 3 cycles BEP (earlier bilateral SLCT at age 18, FIGO IB; n = 1)
    Outcome: menses normalized; no recurrence at about 6 years

Notes

JGCT is the second ovarian sex-cord tumor in this patient (after bilateral SLCT); somatic second hits were not assessed. Paper reports JGCT was asymptomatic. Journal and first author not stated in the retrieved full text.
