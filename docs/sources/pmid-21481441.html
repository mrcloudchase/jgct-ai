Patterns of spread and recurrence of sex cord-stromal tumors of the ovary.

Why it matters for JGCT

Mostly adult granulosa cell tumor series; included only because it contains one JGCT patient (a count), with no JGCT-specific outcomes. Its lymph node findings apply to a predominantly adult population (median age 50).

Key findings

  • Retrospective review of 87 ovarian sex cord-stromal tumor patients (Seattle, 1990-2009): 71 adult granulosa cell tumors, 11 Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors, 2 mixed, 2 unclassified and 1 juvenile granulosa cell tumor (1%).
  • No individual clinical, staging, treatment or outcome data are given for the single JGCT patient.
  • Of 59 patients with complete or partial staging, 47 had lymph nodes examined (median 14 nodes, range 3-80) and none had nodal involvement.
  • 18 of 79 patients with follow-up had at least one recurrence (median time to recurrence 46.6 months, range 2.6-186); 2 had isolated retroperitoneal recurrence, neither with nodal sampling at primary surgery.
  • Tumor size predicted survival: median survival 177 months for tumors under 10 cm versus 101 months for 10 cm or larger (p=0.006); no recurrences with tumors under 7 cm; multivariate HR about 1.20 per cm.
  • Authors conclude lymph node metastasis is rare in sex cord-stromal tumors and routine lymphadenectomy can be omitted from staging.

Treatments reported

  • Adjuvant therapy (mostly bleomycin/etoposide/cisplatin; n=10 BEP) in 15 of 87 patients (mostly stage IC or higher or high-risk features, whole series; n = 15)
    Outcome: Not reported separately for the one JGCT patient

Notes

The abstract's HR confidence interval (1.11-1.07) and the text's (1.106-1.065) are misprinted in the source. Table 1 lists stage III as 3 while text lists 2 plus 1 grossly advanced case.
