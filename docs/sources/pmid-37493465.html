Mixed sex cord-stromal tumor (gynandroblastoma) with malignant morphology involving both ovaries: a case report.

Why it matters for JGCT

Documents JGCT morphology within a gynandroblastoma in a DICER1 germline carrier, with metachronous contralateral recurrence as poorly differentiated Sertoli-Leydig tumor, supporting DICER1 testing in JGCT-like tumors with mixed morphology.

Key findings

  • A 15-year-old girl presented with left abdominal pain, prolonged menstrual bleeding and hirsutism; history of left nephrectomy at age 1 year for a reportedly benign mass; a first-degree relative had metastatic breast cancer at age 30.
  • Ultrasound and CT showed a large left ovarian mass; left salpingo-oophorectomy removed an 18 x 14 x 7 cm tumor with nodular/diffuse growth, macro- and microfollicles with eosinophilic secretions; cells positive for SF-1 and inhibin, focally CK positive, SALL4 negative, membranous beta-catenin; diagnosed as juvenile granulosa cell tumor.
  • One year later a 4.5 cm right ovarian mass was found on follow-up ultrasound; serum inhibin and AMH were negative; right salpingo-oophorectomy showed poorly differentiated spindle/polygonal Sertoli cells with fewer Leydig cells, focal tubules, cystic follicles, necrosis and mitotic rate of 20 per 10 HPF (positive SF-1, membranous beta-catenin, alpha-inhibin, calretinin; negative SALL4, CK7, EMA).
  • Comparison with the first tumor showed areas with tubules and cords of Sertoli cells, so the authors concluded both tumors were related and most likely a gynandroblastoma (mixed sex cord-stromal tumor with juvenile GCT component and poorly differentiated Sertoli-Leydig component).
  • The patient had oocyte cryopreservation and carboplatin/paclitaxel chemotherapy with no evidence of recurrence after 2 years; genetic testing found a pathogenic germline DICER1 variant (c.3094-2_3096del), and she developed multinodular goiter during follow-up; the childhood nephrectomy was thought probably DICER1-related.
  • The authors state that juvenile GCT is about 5% of GCT but about 90% of GCT in prepubertal and under-30 patients, secretes estrogen causing precocious puberty in about 80%, and lacks FOXL2 mutation; they found no prior report of bilateral involvement by gynandroblastoma with a JGCT component and suggest the grade of the Sertoli-Leydig component may drive prognosis.

Presentation

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: germline pathogenic variant c.3094-2_3096del (1 of 1 patient)
    splice-site/deletion variant as reported; patient placed on DICER1 surveillance

Treatments reported

  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy, then right salpingo-oophorectomy at 1 year; oocyte cryopreservation; carboplatin/paclitaxel (gynandroblastoma with JGCT component, metachronous bilateral; n = 1)
    Outcome: no evidence of recurrence after 2 years

Notes

Whether the first tumor was a pure JGCT or already part of the gynandroblastoma was a retrospective interpretation after the second tumor. The authors cite 6 of 28 gynandroblastoma cases with JGCT component in the literature. Follow-up is short (2 years). Date of the carboplatin/paclitaxel course relative to surgery is not specified in detail.
