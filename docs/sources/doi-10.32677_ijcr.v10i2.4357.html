Juvenile granulosa cell tumor in a 35-year-old pregnant woman: A rare case report

Why it matters for JGCT

Single case of aggressive recurrent JGCT diagnosed in pregnancy at age 35 (outside the usual age range), with WT1/calretinin IHC; no stage given and limited follow-up.

Key findings

  • Case report from Guwahati, India: 35-year-old woman at 14 weeks of gestation with sudden left abdominal and lower back pain; ultrasound showed an adnexal mass (reported as '90 cm' in diameter in the text); CA 125, CEA, CA 19.9, AFP, LDH and beta-hCG all normal.
  • Left ovarian tumor resection: 13x12x5 cm tumor with ruptured capsule, solid grayish-white cut surface with focal papillaroid and hemorrhagic areas.
  • Histology: round to oval cells with hyperchromatic vesicular nuclei, prominent nucleoli, irregular nuclear contours, occasional nuclear grooves and numerous mitoses; no Call-Exner bodies. IHC: strong diffuse nuclear WT1, patchy panCK, focal calretinin, CK7 negative; diagnosis JGCT.
  • Recurrence 3 months later treated by left ovariotomy (JGCT on histology); she delivered a healthy baby at term by cesarean section 2 months later with heavy vaginal bleeding; hysterectomy with salpingo-oophorectomy again showed JGCT.
  • She developed ascites a few weeks later; adjuvant chemotherapy was advised and she remains under follow-up (duration not stated).
  • Authors' background and discussion (cited, not original): JGCT is 5% of GCT with average age at diagnosis 13 years and only 3% diagnosed after age 30 (Young: 4 of 125 over 30); about 90% are stage I at diagnosis; advanced stages recur or cause death within 3 years; adjuvant chemotherapy recommended for stage IC or higher or mitotic index above 20/10 HPF; pregnancy tumors can show disorderly arrangement and many luteinized cells.
  • Authors' conclusion: JGCT is uncommon in pregnancy and can be dangerous because it often goes unnoticed.

Presentation

  • sudden onset left abdominal and lower back pain for 2 days at 14 weeks of gestation
  • adnexal mass on ultrasound (size given as '90 cm', evidently an error)
  • tumor markers CA 125, CEA, CA 19.9, AFP, LDH, beta-hCG normal

Treatments reported

  • Left ovarian tumor resection; left ovariotomy for recurrence at 3 months; total abdominal hysterectomy and salpingo-oophorectomy after cesarean delivery; adjuvant chemotherapy advised (pregnancy (14 weeks) with ruptured 13x12x5 cm tumor, recurrent disease; n = 1)
    Outcome: Recurrence at 3 months; histology JGCT again at hysterectomy; ascites a few weeks later; under follow-up, chemotherapy advised; healthy baby delivered at term

Notes

The text contains several apparent errors: adnexal mass size '90 cm' (likely 9 cm or 90 mm), the abstract says the patient had a 'left ovarian cystectomy' whereas the text describes tumor resection, and 'Ten adequate follow-ups' appears garbled. FIGO stage is not stated. Because of age 35, JGCT vs adult type was decided on morphology (no Call-Exner bodies, ungrooved hyperchromatic nuclei, numerous mitoses) without molecular testing.
