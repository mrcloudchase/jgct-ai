Why it matters for JGCT

Case report titled as JGCT, but the described features and the discussion are adult-type; limited as evidence for JGCT.

Key findings

  • Case report from Bejaia, Algeria: a 38-year-old woman (four children; mother and aunt with breast cancer) with left-sided abdominopelvic pain and 3 months of amenorrhea; examination showed a firm, regular, painless, mobile abdominopelvic mass.
  • Ultrasound showed a lateral uterine mass with mixed, solid-predominant content; CA125 was 18; MRI showed a solid-cystic mass with multiple fluid compartments and a tissue portion with high signal on b1000 diffusion, homogeneously enhancing, no hemorrhagic component.
  • Laparotomy found a regular, encapsulated solid left ovarian tumor about 10 cm without capsular rupture and two 4 cm uterine fibroids; left adnexectomy and myomectomy were performed.
  • Pathology was reported as 'juvenile granulosa tumor' with diffuse proliferation forming small lacunae/Call-Exner bodies and monomorphic cuboidal cells with grooved nuclei ('coffee bean'), classified pT1Nx, confined to the ovary; the postoperative course was favorable and a reoperation with additional wide excision was decided.
  • The discussion is generic to granulosa cell tumors (95% adult-type, stage I in 78-91%, 5-year survival 95% stage I, late recurrence up to 37 years) and no immunohistochemistry, hormone assays or molecular testing are reported.

Presentation

  • heaviness-type abdominopelvic pain, intermittent, predominantly left
  • amenorrhea of 3 months
  • firm regular mobile abdominopelvic mass
  • no clinical signs of hyperestrogenism

Treatments reported

  • Left adnexectomy without capsular rupture plus myomectomy; reoperation with additional wide excision decided (pT1 left ovarian granulosa tumor; n = 1)
    Outcome: immediate course favorable; no longer follow-up reported

Notes

Internal inconsistencies: the title and pathology line say juvenile granulosa tumor, yet the described morphology (grooved 'coffee bean' nuclei, Call-Exner bodies) is classic adult-type, the abstract and conclusion discuss the adult form, the introduction says the objective was 'to report a series of 17 cases of TGA (adult)', and the discussion refers to 'our three patients'. Ultrasound mass size is printed as '69 x 68 cm' (presumably mm). Follow-up duration not reported. Treat the JGCT diagnosis as unconfirmed (38 years, no IHC).
