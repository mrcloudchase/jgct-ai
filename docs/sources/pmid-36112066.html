Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor Mimicking HAIR-AN in a 4-year-old: A Case Report.

Why it matters for JGCT

Rare androgen-secreting prepubertal JGCT with hyperinsulinemia and acanthosis nigricans reversed by tumor resection; inhibin A and B were markedly elevated and normalized after surgery.

Key findings

  • A 4-year-1-month-old girl presented with virilization (Tanner 3 pubic hair, hirsutism, acne) and extensive acanthosis nigricans mimicking HAIR-AN/type A insulin resistance; height 110.4 cm, weight 29 kg, BMI 23.79 kg/m2; bone age 6 years 4 months.
  • Pre-operative labs: total testosterone 205.6 ng/dL (normal <=20), inhibin B 1715 pg/mL (<73.0), inhibin A 43.7 pg/mL (<7-14), AMH 6.75 ng/mL, 17-OH-progesterone 145 ng/dL, insulin 45.2 uIU/mL (2.6-24.9), LDH 363 IU/L; estradiol 11.8 pg/mL normal, FSH and LH suppressed; beta-hCG, AFP and CA-125 normal.
  • Pelvic ultrasound showed a 6.3 x 3.4 x 7.1 cm heterogeneous solid vascular left adnexal mass; CT showed a 4.4 x 4.6 x 4.2 cm solid left ovarian mass without metastasis or lymphadenopathy.
  • Unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy gave stage 1A JGCT; immunohistochemistry (data not shown) was positive for inhibin and negative for pancytokeratin, WT1 and calretinin; histology showed pleomorphic cells.
  • Within 2 weeks of surgery testosterone fell to <1 ng/dL, inhibin B to 25 pg/mL, inhibin A to 1.4 pg/mL and insulin to 2 uIU/mL; at 6 months-1 year testosterone was 4 ng/dL, inhibin B 15.2, insulin 17 uIU/mL; at age 6 years 3 months acne and hirsutism had resolved, acanthosis nigricans regressed substantially, and no residual mass was seen.
  • The authors suggest the androgen excess caused hyperinsulinemia (which improved after tumor removal despite increasing BMI); they note androgen-secreting JGCTs are uncommon (testosterone secretion 2-3% of GCTs), and cite Kalfa et al. reporting absent or low intratumoral aromatase in six hyperandrogenic JGCTs.
  • They considered DICER1 syndrome but the patient did not fit its clinical picture; no genetic testing is reported; family history included distant ovarian cancer at advanced ages.

Presentation

  • 6 months of pubic, arm and leg hair growth, greasy scalp hair, acne, mood swings
  • extensive acanthosis nigricans
  • Tanner 3 pubic hair without breast buds
  • obesity (BMI 23.79, +3.1 SD)
  • advanced bone age 6 years 4 months
  • no clitoromegaly

Treatments reported

  • Unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy via Pfannenstiel incision (Stage 1A, androgen-secreting JGCT; n = 1)
    Outcome: Inhibin A/B, testosterone, 17-OH-progesterone, AMH and insulin normalized; hirsutism and acne resolved, acanthosis nigricans regressed; normal uterus/right ovary on ultrasound at age 6 years 3 months

Notes

Published 2024 (journal issue) per the existing record; online 2022 identifier in DOI. Literature on hyperinsulinemia in JGCT (Larizza, Kwiatkowska, Brisigotti/Donohue syndrome) is cited secondhand. Age of 4 years is below that typical for HAIR-AN, as authors note. CA-125 unit printed as ug/dL in the table.
