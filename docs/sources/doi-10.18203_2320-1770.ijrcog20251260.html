Why it matters for JGCT

Young-adult (19 y) JGCT with omental micrometastasis treated with fertility-sparing surgery and carboplatin/paclitaxel.

Key findings

  • A 19-year-old nulliparous woman had 8 months of lower abdominal pain and bloating with regular menses; a firm 20-week-sized pelvic mass was palpable.
  • CA-125, AFP, LDH, beta-hCG and CA 19.9 were normal; ultrasound showed an 18 x 9 cm multiseptated cyst and MRI a large multiseptated right abdominopelvic mass read as suggestive of mucinous cystadenoma.
  • Intraoperatively a trilobed 15 x 10 x 18 cm adnexal mass had one lobe ruptured; frozen section suggested a borderline surface epithelial tumor; staging was FIGO IC1 intraoperatively.
  • Final histopathology: JGCT with omental micrometastasis, pT3aN0; IHC positive for inhibin, PR, calretinin, WT1; negative for p63, CK7, CK20, synaptophysin, chromogranin, ER.
  • Tumor board recommended adjuvant paclitaxel and carboplatin; four cycles were completed and the patient was in remission at one year under three-monthly surveillance.
  • Authors conclude for early recognition, comprehensive surgical staging and adjuvant therapy and long-term follow-up; discussion (literature-based) says chemotherapy is reserved for advanced/recurrent cases and late recurrence beyond 4 years has been reported.

Presentation

  • 8 months of intermittent lower abdominal pain and bloating
  • regular menses, no virilization
  • firm 20-week-sized pelvic mass

Treatments reported

  • Fertility-sparing staging laparotomy (infracolic omentectomy, peritoneal biopsies), then 4 cycles adjuvant paclitaxel + carboplatin (primary + adjuvant; n = 1)
    Outcome: In remission at 1 year, 3-monthly surveillance

Notes

Internal stage inconsistency: intraoperative FIGO IC1, final pT3aN0 (omental micrometastasis); discussion says 'stage III'. Frozen section misdiagnosed as borderline epithelial tumor; MRI suggested mucinous cystadenoma. Follow-up only 1 year.
