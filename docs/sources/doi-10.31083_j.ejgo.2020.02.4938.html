Juvenile granulosa cell tumor with massive ascites: A case report and immunohistochemical study of ascites formation

Why it matters for JGCT

Adult-age JGCT presenting only with massive ascites and very high estradiol; adds VEGF and microvessel immunohistochemistry as a possible mechanism for ascites.

Key findings

  • 23-year-old nulliparous woman with two weeks of progressive abdominal distention and no menstrual irregularity; MRI showed a multilocular cystic and solid right ovarian mass with large ascites.
  • Preoperative CA-125 244 U/ml (normal <35), estradiol 2760 pg/ml, FSH and LH both <0.10 U/ml; AFP <0.7 ng/ml, CEA 2.0, CA19-9 0.6 and testosterone 0.76 ng/ml were normal.
  • At laparotomy the dark reddish encapsulated right ovarian tumor was edematous and fragile and ruptured during the procedure; no peritoneal implants; about 5 L of transudate ascites were drained and ascitic cytology was negative; tumor resection with right salpingo-oophorectomy and partial omentectomy was done (stage I).
  • Histology: granulosa cells around microfollicles with hyperchromatic nuclei, frequent mitoses, no Call-Exner bodies; strong calretinin and alpha-inhibin positivity supported JGCT.
  • VEGF staining was diffuse in tumor cell cytoplasm and partly in endothelial cells, with CD34 microvessel density above 60 vessels per visual field; the authors speculate that VEGF-driven angiogenesis and capillary permeability caused the massive ascites.
  • After surgery the ascites disappeared rapidly, CA-125 and estradiol decreased, LH and FSH increased, and there was no recurrence 12 months postoperatively; no adjuvant therapy is described.

Notes

Single case; the VEGF mechanism is the authors' speculation. Tumor rupture occurred intraoperatively although staged as stage I (as stated by the authors in the discussion, FIGO substage not given). Full text read from the journal PDF.
