Pseudoprecocious puberty due to functional cystic juvenile granulosa cell tumor of the ovary in a 5-year-old girl

Why it matters for JGCT

Case report of a purely cystic JGCT that mimicked a functional ovarian cyst on imaging in a 5-year-old with isosexual pseudoprecocity; no follow-up outcome.

Key findings

  • A 5-year-old girl (21.5 kg, 113 cm, 97th percentile) presented with 10 days of scanty-to-moderate vaginal bleeding and bilateral painful breast bud enlargement (Tanner stage 2 breasts, axillary hair Tanner 1, normal external genitalia, no cafe-au-lait spots or bony deformity).
  • Serum estradiol was 39 pg/ml (normal 6-27); CA125 13 U/ml, AFP, beta-hCG, testosterone, FSH, LH and prolactin were normal; wrist X-ray showed bone age of 6 to 7 years; chest X-ray and brain MRI were normal.
  • Ultrasound showed an enlarged uterus and a 36 x 28 mm right ovarian cyst with smaller cysts; pelvic MRI showed a 42 x 31 x 42 mm thin-walled cystic lesion with daughter cysts and no solid component, read as a simple ovarian cyst.
  • Cystectomy and right ovariotomy was performed; aspirated cyst fluid smears showed round hyperchromatic cells without grooving, suggesting JGCT; histology showed cysts lined by 2-10 cell-thick round-to-oval tumor cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm, no nuclear grooving, eosinophilic secretions and 6-8 mitoses per 10 HPF.
  • Immunohistochemistry: calretinin 100% positive and EMA negative; inhibin IHC was advised but not done. Diagnosis was JGCT of the right ovary, confirmed only on histopathology; the patient was lost to follow-up.
  • The authors conclude cystic JGCT must be distinguished from functional follicular cysts in pseudoprecocious puberty, that histology and IHC help, and that long-term follow-up is essential given metastatic potential and late recurrence.

Presentation

  • scanty to moderate vaginal bleeding for 10 days
  • bilateral painful breast bud enlargement (Tanner 2)
  • advanced bone age 6-7 years
  • elevated estradiol

Treatments reported

  • Right ovarian cystectomy and ovariotomy (cystic JGCT, right ovary, 4.5 cm; n = 1)
    Outcome: lost to follow-up

Notes

Stage not stated and tumor size given as 4.5 cm in the discussion versus 4 x 3 x 2.7 cm on gross specimen and 42 mm on MRI. Inhibin staining was not performed, so the diagnosis rests on morphology plus calretinin/EMA. Full text read from the open Ovid-hosted version.
