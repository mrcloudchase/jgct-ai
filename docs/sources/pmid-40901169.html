Rapid recurrence of ovarian mixed sex-cord-stromal tumor associated with DICER1 gene mutation: a case analysis and literature review.

Why it matters for JGCT

Single case of an ovarian mixed sex-cord-stromal tumor containing a 40% juvenile granulosa cell tumor component with DICER1 variants and early recurrence; also cites DICER1 frequency in JGCT (6/75).

Key findings

  • 14-year-old girl with a large cystic-solid abdominopelvic mass on CT (suspected left adnexal), testosterone 457.00 ng/ml (reference 9.8-82.1), prolactin 40.6 ng/ml and AFP 41.0 ng/ml (reference 0-10); treated by exploratory laparotomy and excision.
  • Gross tumor 17 x 14 x 4.5 cm. Histology: ovarian mixed sex-cord-stromal tumor with a juvenile granulosa cell tumor component (40%) and moderately differentiated Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor (60%).
  • Immunohistochemistry: CK +, WT-1 +, p53 + (60%), inhibin partial +, ER partial +, PR few +, synaptophysin few +, CD56 +, BRG1 +, SF-1 +, Ki-67 40%; PAX-8, NapsinA, CD99, S-100, CgA, CD117, PLAP, CD30 and calretinin negative.
  • NGS found DICER1 exon 16 frameshift c.2517delT p.L840Yfs*6 (abundance 39.19%) and exon 25 missense c.5439G>T p.E1813D (42.47%), among 12 mutations in total; the authors state the E1813 hotspot supports the diagnosis.
  • No standardized chemotherapy was given after the first surgery; markers and imaging were normal at 1 month, but CT at 6 months showed recurrence (19.8 x 19.1 x 11.0 cm) with pathology matching the original. After second surgery and 8 cycles of paclitaxel plus carboplatin there was no recurrence at 1 year.
  • Literature statements cited by the authors: sequencing of 75 JGCT cases confirmed somatic DICER1 mutations in 6 (8%) with two possibly germline DICER1-syndrome JGCT; mixed sex-cord-stromal tumors have DICER1 mutations in 19-80%; only two prior recurrences of mixed tumors reported (this being the third); JGCT is about 5% of granulosa cell tumors and about 97% of JGCT patients are under 30.
  • Authors recommend DICER1 testing (exon 16 and 24-25) for mixed tumors and consideration of adjuvant chemotherapy (BEP or paclitaxel/carboplatin) even after R0 resection when JGCT component exceeds 30% or DICER1 biallelic mutations are present; these are the authors' proposals from a single case, not evidence-graded guidance.
  • Limitations stated: single case, 1-year follow-up, no exome or single-cell sequencing.

Presentation

  • large cystic-solid pelvic/abdominal mass on CT
  • testosterone 457.00 ng/ml (reference 9.8-82.1)
  • prolactin 40.6 ng/ml
  • AFP 41.0 ng/ml (reference 0-10)

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: exon 16 frameshift c.2517delT p.L840Yfs*6 (variant abundance 39.19% (NGS))
    germline status not stated
  • DICER1: exon 25 missense c.5439G>T p.E1813D (RNase IIIb hotspot) (variant abundance 42.47% (NGS))
    12 mutations detected in total; others (MKNK1, POLE, RET x2, RTEL1, RUNX1T1, TAP1, DAXX, MSI1, PAK1) class III, unclear significance

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy and excision of pelvic tumor, no adjuvant chemotherapy (primary; n = 1)
    Outcome: CT recurrence at 6 months (19.8 x 19.1 x 11.0 cm)
  • Second surgery plus paclitaxel 240 mg and carboplatin 550 mg, 8 cycles (16 October 2023 to 19 March 2024) (recurrence; n = 1)
    Outcome: no recurrence at 1 year of follow-up

Notes

The tumor is a mixed JGCT/Sertoli-Leydig tumor, not pure JGCT; n_jgct_patients=1 counts it for the JGCT component. The paper sometimes calls these 'mixed germ cell tumors' in its limitations and recommendations, apparently meaning mixed sex-cord-stromal tumors. Germline testing not reported. Table lists abundance as fractions (0.3919); percentages given here. Journal and first author not stated in the retrieved full text.
