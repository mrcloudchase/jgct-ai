Why it matters for JGCT

Minimal: reports a single H&E-diagnosed JGCT in a general hospital series of ovarian tumors in India; useful only as an epidemiologic count.

Key findings

  • Single-center retrospective histopathology series (Rajendra Institute of Medical Sciences, Ranchi, India; March 2020-March 2024) of 190 ovarian tumors classified by WHO 2020; age range 8-72 years, mean 36 years.
  • One juvenile granulosa cell tumor (0.5% of 190) was found; it was counted among the malignant tumors (JGCT 1 of 28 malignant) and among 3 pure sex cord tumors (2 adult GCT, 1 JGCT).
  • Sex cord-stromal tumors were 11/190 (5.7%): 7 pure stromal (5 fibromas, 1 thecoma, 1 sclerosing stromal), 3 pure sex cord (2 AGCT, 1 JGCT) and 1 Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor.
  • No age, presentation, stage, marker, treatment or outcome data are given for the JGCT; diagnosis was by H&E morphology only, without immunohistochemistry or molecular testing (acknowledged by authors as a limitation).
  • Overall: 154 benign (81.1%), 8 borderline (4.2%), 28 malignant (14.7%); surface epithelial tumors 73.2%, germ cell tumors 20%.

Notes

JGCT diagnosed on H&E only; no demographic data for the case. Journal and first author not in retrieved text.
