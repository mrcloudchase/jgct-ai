Prenatal diagnosis and postnatal management of perinatal thoracoabdominopelvic tumors: multicenter experience.

Why it matters for JGCT

Small JGCT contribution: documents 2 congenital/early-infancy JGCT (one ovarian, one testicular) in a regional perinatal tumor series; useful for infantile onset and for the finding that they were not prenatally detected and were localized.

Key findings

  • Retrospective multicenter study (Ile-de-France, January 2010-May 2021) of 153 perinatal thoracic, abdominal or pelvic solid tumors detected prenatally or within 3 months after birth; sacrococcygeal teratomas excluded.
  • Two juvenile granulosa cell tumors were identified (1.3% of 153), both within the 'gonadal tumor' group of 6 (the others were 4 teratomas).
  • Neither JGCT was detected on prenatal imaging; for both, the first sign or symptom occurred between 29 days and 3 months after birth.
  • Imaging (Table 3): one ovarian JGCT was multicystic, maximum size 17.0 cm; one testicular JGCT was solid, 2.6 cm.
  • Extent at postnatal diagnosis (Table 4): both JGCT had local extension only; no adjacent organ, nodal or distant spread. Gonadal tumors overall: 6/6 local only.
  • Per-tumor treatment and outcome for the two JGCT are not reported; deaths in the cohort (6 prenatal, 11 postnatal) were attributed to other tumors (rhabdoid tumor 6/6, neuroblastoma 3/93, nephroblastoma 1/8, hemangioma-to-angiosarcoma 1). Overall 136 (89%) alive at median 5 years (range 0-14).
  • Overall, only 35% (53/153) of perinatal thoracoabdominopelvic tumors were detected prenatally; authors conclude prognosis is largely favorable.

Presentation

  • not detected prenatally (first sign 29 days to 3 months after birth in both)

Notes

Series covers all perinatal trunk tumors; JGCT outcome data not separately reported. Table 3 lists the testicular JGCT under 'Testis' and the ovarian one under 'Ovary'.
