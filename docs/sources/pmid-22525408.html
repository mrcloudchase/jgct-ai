Why it matters for JGCT

Core pediatric guidance document for ovarian and testicular JGCT covering presentation, markers, IHC, genetic associations, staging, risk-adapted adjuvant therapy and follow-up; most outcome data are cited from the German MAKEI studies.

Key findings

  • Consensus/review guidance (Children's Oncology Group, MAKEI authors) on pediatric ovarian and testicular sex cord-stromal tumors (SCST): SCST are about 7% of ovarian tumors overall and about 15% of ovarian tumors in children; JGCT is the most common pediatric ovarian SCST, followed by Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors.
  • Ovarian JGCT is seen in young children and may present with precocious puberty; the vast majority are stage I at diagnosis with favorable prognosis; recurrence is rare and generally within 2-3 years (median 2.8 years for stage I ovarian SCST recurrences, mostly abdominopelvic or regional nodes). Adult-type GCT is very rare in children and has late relapse (>10 years).
  • Both GCT types may secrete inhibin; serum inhibin A/B (B more specific), AFP, beta-hCG, CEA, CA-125, LDH and calcium are advised in suspected ovarian tumors; inhibin IHC and calcium help distinguish JGCT from small cell carcinoma of the hypercalcemic type, which can mimic pseudofollicular growth.
  • Associations: JGCT, Sertoli-Leydig tumors and gynandroblastoma occur in families with pleuropulmonary blastoma and DICER1 mutations; enchondromatosis (Ollier) and Peutz-Jeghers syndrome are linked to granulosa cell tumors; no association with BRCA1/2. 25% of JGCT in one series had chromosomal imbalance, usually gain of chromosome 12.
  • Prognosis (Schneider et al, cited): 10-year EFS 0.88+/-0.05 for JGCT (n=34) vs 0.67+/-0.19 for Sertoli-Leydig tumors (n=6). Mitoses <20/10 HPF (n=35/35) had 100% 10-year EFS vs 53% for >=20/10 HPF (n=7/13), significant also within stage Ic (100% vs 48%). Marked nuclear pleomorphism (about 15% of stage I JGCT) was not associated with adverse prognosis.
  • Stage Ic subgroups (cited): intraoperative rupture (n=12; 9 had no adjuvant therapy, all survived relapse-free) did better than preoperative rupture or malignant ascites (n=9; EFS 0.44+/-0.17 vs 1.0, log-rank 0.003).
  • Staging by FIGO ovarian carcinoma criteria with detailed description of stage Ic spillage; stage I treated by unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy without hysterectomy or routine lymphadenectomy. Recommended: adjuvant platinum chemotherapy (4 or 6 cycles PEB) for JGCT stage Ic with preoperative rupture/malignant ascites and stages II-IV; consider for stage Ic intraoperative rupture with >=20 mitoses/10 HPF; observation may be considered for intraoperative rupture with low mitotic count. Stage IV is rare with poor prognosis.
  • Follow-up advised: abdominopelvic MRI or ultrasound plus inhibin B, hormonal markers, CA-125 about every 3 months for at least the first 3 years; relapse carries poor prognosis and no regimen for relapse is established.
  • Testicular JGCT: usually presents under 6 months of age, inhibin positive, benign with no evidence of recurrence or metastases; associated with Y-chromosome structural abnormalities or ambiguous genitalia; testis-sparing surgery reasonable if normal AFP for age; no adjuvant therapy for stage I; serial imaging generally not required.
  • Registries cited: Prepubertal Testis Tumor Registry had 43 stromal tumors including 9 JGCT, none metastatic at diagnosis; unpublished MAKEI analysis of 40 testicular SCST found no metastases, all in continuous remission; Harms and Kock described 29 testicular SCST with no relapses.

Presentation

  • ovarian JGCT: abdominal pain/distension, mass, isosexual precocity (breast swelling, vaginal bleeding), may secrete inhibin
  • testicular JGCT: painless mass, usually first 6 months of life, usually no hormone-related symptoms

Genetic and molecular findings

  • chromosome 12: gain / chromosomal imbalance (25% of juvenile granulosa cell tumors in 1 series)
    prognostic significance unknown
  • DICER1: germline mutations seen in children with ovarian JGCT, Sertoli-Leydig tumor and/or gynandroblastoma with personal or family pleuropulmonary blastoma history
    clinical testing available

Treatments reported

  • Surgery alone (unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy) for FIGO stage Ia; MAKEI protocol also no adjuvant therapy for JGCT stage Ic with intraoperative rupture (ovarian JGCT, localized)
    Outcome: 9 of 12 stage Ic (intraoperative rupture) patients received no adjuvant therapy and all survived without relapse (Schneider et al, cited)
  • Platinum-based chemotherapy: 4 or 6 cycles PEB (cisplatin/etoposide/bleomycin) or PEI (cisplatin/etoposide/ifosfamide) (recommended for JGCT stage Ic with preoperative rupture or malignant ascites, and stages II-IV)
    Outcome: No randomized data; stage Ic with preoperative rupture/malignant ascites EFS 0.44+/-0.17 in cited series (n=9)
  • Cisplatin-based chemotherapy plus regional deep hyperthermia (MAKEI strategy for FIGO IV or stage II/III with high mitotic index)
    Outcome: limited by availability; no prospective data
  • Surgery alone, testis-sparing surgery reasonable; no adjuvant therapy (testicular JGCT, stage I)
    Outcome: considered benign; surgical resection curative; little to no evidence of recurrence or metastasis
