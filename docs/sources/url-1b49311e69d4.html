Why it matters for JGCT

Authoritative clinical-genetics chapter placing JGCT within the DICER1 spectrum as a rare and mainly anaplastic or high-mitotic-index association, and giving DICER1 testing, pelvic ultrasound surveillance and adjuvant chemotherapy thresholds relevant to JGCT.

Key findings

  • GeneReviews chapter (initial posting 2014-04-24, last update 2026-03-25) on DICER1-related tumor predisposition; more than 1,500 individuals with a germline DICER1 pathogenic variant identified from literature and unpublished International PPB/DICER1 Registry and NIH Natural History Study data; population prevalence estimated at 1 in 6,099 to 8,416.
  • Juvenile granulosa cell tumor (juvGCT) is listed among ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors that should prompt DICER1 consideration, 'especially if anaplasia and/or high mitotic index'; Table 2 states 'Juvenile granulosa cell tumor is rarely assoc w/DICER1', whereas about 10% of DICER1 carriers develop ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors and up to 60% of persons with SLCT or gynandroblastoma have DICER1.
  • JuvGCT, especially anaplastic juvGCT, 'may be associated with DICER1' (Baillard 2021, Onder 2021, Boyraz 2026 cited). Presentation: abdominal pain, distention or mass; precocious puberty, secondary amenorrhea or virilization; markers variable (inhibin A, inhibin B, CA125, testosterone); inhibin B may be elevated at diagnosis and at recurrence.
  • Prognosis of juvGCT: generally favorable at stage 1A or 1C1; high mitotic index, anaplastic features or higher stage are associated with worse prognosis; WHO classifies juvGCT as borderline malignant potential, but malignant behavior is described in older children, adolescents and those with higher-stage or higher mitotic index tumors.
  • Treatment: surgical resection with staging per modified FIGO (NCCN); no formal lymph node dissection of normal-appearing nodes; fertility-sparing surgery recommended for most girls and young women; avoid tumor rupture (timing of rupture should be documented). Treatment regimens were mainly studied in granulosa cell tumors and are usually platinum-based; clinical trials are lacking.
  • EXPeRT/PARTNER consensus (Schneider 2021) as summarized: adjuvant chemotherapy for all children and adolescents with juvGCT higher than stage IC1; high mitotic index (MI >=20 per 10 high-power fields) strongly predicts worse prognosis and recurrence in low-stage high-MI cases has been reported, so adjuvant chemotherapy for high MI but low stage may be considered individually.
  • Preoperative work-up when an ovarian sex cord-stromal tumor is suspected: testosterone, AFP, inhibin A and B, estradiol to find a usable tumor marker; abdominopelvic CT or MRI; baseline chest CT reasonable. SLCT and rarely juvGCT may secrete AFP, leading to consideration of immature teratoma or yolk sac tumor; markers may or may not be elevated at recurrence.
  • Gynandroblastoma usually has both SLCT and juvGCT components; gynandroblastoma with SLCT and follicular differentiation mimicking juvGCT is considered better classified as SLCT and is associated with DICER1.
  • Surveillance for DICER1 carriers (female reproductive tract incl. juvGCT): symptom review every 6 months or at each visit and pelvic ultrasound every 6 months from diagnosis until at least age 40 years (95% of SLCT diagnosed before 40; shared decision after 40); pelvic ultrasound judged more sensitive than biochemical surveillance. Other schedules: chest radiograph, chest CT at 3 and 30 months, thyroid US every 3 years from age 8, abdominal US every 6 months to age 8 then annually to 12.
  • Testing: DICER1 diagnosis requires a heterozygous germline pathogenic/likely pathogenic variant (sequence analysis detects >90%, deletion/duplication analysis <10%); tumor tissue testing for somatic DICER1 variants may suggest a germline variant but biallelic somatic variants have been found without germline variants; about 10% of de novo carriers are mosaic. Cascade testing of first-degree relatives recommended; newborns of at-risk families tested before 4 months.
  • Penetrance is reduced and age dependent: in 102 non-proband carriers 5.3% had a neoplasm by age 10 years and 19.3% by age 50 (females 26.5%, males 10.2%). Ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors present most often in the reproductive years (95% before age 40). Contralateral metachronous SLCT reported in nearly 20% at ten years in DICER1-related SLCT.

Presentation

  • Juvenile granulosa cell tumor: abdominal pain, distention or mass
  • precocious puberty, secondary amenorrhea or virilization
  • inhibin A, inhibin B, CA125 and testosterone may be raised; AFP rarely raised (typically <500 ng/mL in sex cord-stromal tumors)

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: germline loss-of-function pathogenic variant plus somatic RNase IIIb hot-spot missense variant (codons 1705, 1709, 1713, 1809, 1810, 1813) (JGCT is stated to be rarely associated with DICER1 (Table 2); up to 60% of SLCT or gynandroblastoma have DICER1)
    JuvGCT, especially anaplastic juvGCT, may be associated with DICER1; sporadic juvenile granulosa cell tumor frequently contains biallelic somatic DICER1 variants not in the germline
  • FOXL2: not detected in well-differentiated SLCT (stated alongside DICER1)
    Mentioned only for well-differentiated SLCT, not JGCT

Treatments reported

  • Surgery with staging (usually unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, peritoneal washings, avoid rupture); fertility-sparing for most; adjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy by histology and stage (ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors incl. juvGCT)
    Outcome: Consensus (EXPeRT/PARTNER): adjuvant chemotherapy for juvGCT higher than stage IC1; for SLCT higher than stage IA; PEB/BEP and PEI most common in children; some adults received paclitaxel/carboplatin
