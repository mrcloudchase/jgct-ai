Juvenile granulosa cell tumor diagnosed in 6-month-old infant with precocious puberty.

Why it matters for JGCT

Infantile JGCT with marked estrogen excess; documents imaging features and an edematous-ovary hypothesis for the cystic component.

Key findings

  • 6-month-old girl referred for precocious puberty with breast buds, hyperpigmented areolas, axillary hair, Tanner III pubic hair, vaginal discharge, and acne.
  • Estradiol 668 pg/mL (normal <=25), estrone 36 pg/mL (normal <15), testosterone 90.51 ng/dL (normal 1.2-62.0), LDH 1107.0 U/L (normal 163-452).
  • Ultrasound: heterogeneous cystic and solid adnexal mass 9.0 x 7.5 x 5.5 cm with internal vascularity; post-pubertal uterus and thickened endometrium. MRI: mixed cystic and solid lesion 9.4 x 5.2 x 8.0 cm with enhancing solid component showing restricted diffusion.
  • Intraoperatively the mass arose from the left ovary (although imaging had placed it in the right lower quadrant and left ovary was not visualized); left oophorectomy with staging was done, and peritoneal washings were negative.
  • Histology of the solid component was JGCT; the cystic component was edematous ovarian tissue, which the authors hypothesize reflects estrogen stimulation akin to ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome or hyperreactio luteinalis.
  • Literature statements by the authors: JGCT are about 3%-7% of ovarian neoplasms, about 82% of prepubertal patients present with precocious puberty, more than 90% are FIGO stage I at diagnosis, and stage I disease responds well to unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy.

Presentation

  • precocious puberty since birth: breast buds Tanner II, pubic hair Tanner III, axillary hair, vaginal discharge, acne
  • accelerated growth (weight and length >99th percentile)

Treatments reported

  • Left oophorectomy with surgical staging and peritoneal washings (left fallopian tube spared) (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: Histology JGCT; peritoneal washings negative; no follow-up reported

Notes

Text says left oophorectomy with a large mass in the right lower quadrant on MRI, with uterus deviated to the right; no outcome or follow-up duration reported. No IHC or molecular data reported.
