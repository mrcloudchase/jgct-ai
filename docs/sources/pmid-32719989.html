Risk factors and treatment outcomes of 239 patients with testicular granulosa cell tumors: a systematic review of published case series data.

Why it matters for JGCT

Testicular JGCT: 166 pooled published cases, all benign; informs surgery (testis-sparing) and prognosis. Ovarian JGCT not covered.

Key findings

  • PRISMA systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42018110112; searches to 5 May 2018): 88 publications, 239 patients with testicular granulosa cell tumor (tGrCT): 166 (69%) juvenile and 73 (31%) adult.
  • Juvenile tGrCT mean age 1.5 years (SD 5); median size 20 mm (IQR 13-38) among 36 with size; adult tGrCT mean age 42 years (SD 19).
  • Juvenile tGrCT presentation (63 with data): testicular enlargement 24 (38%), mass 22 (35%), hormonal changes 9 (14%), abdominal mass 7 (11%), incidental 6 (10%); AFP elevated in 11/63 juvenile cases (17%) versus 1 adult; ipsi- or contralateral cryptorchidism in 18/166 (11%) juvenile.
  • All 166 juvenile tGrCT were benign: no metastatic disease was observed; outcomes among juveniles: 80 no evidence of disease, 1 local recurrence, 3 died (mean follow-up 54 months).
  • Juvenile treatment: 72 orchiectomy, 62 gonadectomy, 15 testis-sparing surgery, 7 laparotomy (intra-abdominal cryptorchid testis), 2 biopsy only. The largest source was Kao 2015 (70 cases: 62 gonadectomy, 6 TSS, 2 biopsy).
  • Adult tGrCT: metastatic disease in 7/73 (10%); risk factors were larger primary (median 70 vs 24 mm, ROC cut-off 46 mm, AUC 0.86), angiolymphatic invasion (57% vs 4%) and gynecomastia (29% vs 3%).
  • Five of 7 metastatic adult cases reached complete remission with resection and/or BEP; one died of disease at 134 months and one had stable disease.
  • Authors conclude juvenile tGrCT is a benign entity, adult tGrCT has metastatic potential, literature supports testis-sparing surgery, germ-cell tumor markers (AFP, HCG, LDH) are not supported for follow-up, and an aggressive curative approach is suggested for metastatic disease.

Treatments reported

  • Orchiectomy/gonadectomy, testis-sparing surgery (TSS), laparotomy or biopsy (primary treatment, all tGrCT; n = 231)
    Outcome: 202/231 (87%) orchiectomy; 20/231 (9%) TSS with local recurrence in 1/20 (5%) 3 months after TSS, disease free at 18 months after hemiscrotectomy
  • Resection of metastases or cisplatin-based chemotherapy (BEP) (metastatic adult tGrCT; n = 7)
    Outcome: 5/7 complete remission; adult type only

Notes

Pooled published case reports and series; patient overlap between included reports is possible although same-study duplicates were counted once. Individual patient-level data for only 65 of the 166 juvenile cases. Retrospective, with inconsistent reporting; the OATH registry was opened to collect more data.
