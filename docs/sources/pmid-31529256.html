MR imaging in discriminating between benign and malignant paediatric ovarian masses: a systematic review.

Why it matters for JGCT

Imaging review of paediatric ovarian masses; JGCT appears only as 3 single-patient case reports (basic MRI descriptions, no quantitative or performance data). Shows the thin evidence base for MRI features of JGCT.

Key findings

  • PRISMA systematic review (PubMed and EMBASE, 2008-2018) of MR imaging to separate benign from malignant ovarian masses: 3015 records, 34 studies included (16 paediatric, 18 adult).
  • Of the 16 paediatric studies, 3 were cohort studies (Emil 2017 n=18; Marro 2016 n=32; Lin 2017 n=74 germ cell tumours) and 13 were case reports; paediatric studies were mostly Oxford evidence level 4. Mean age was 10.8 years in paediatric and 46.9 years in adult studies.
  • Three included paediatric case reports were juvenile granulosa cell tumours: Ghanbari 2013 (age 3 years; multiple cystic components; tumour adhesion to anterior bowel loops), Bedir 2014 (age 10; 76 x 87 x 75 mm; multiple cystic and solid components) and Calcaterra 2013 (age 8; 13 x 13 x 7.6 cm). No DWI, DCE or diagnostic-performance data were reported for these cases.
  • Across studies, MRI sensitivity and specificity for malignancy ranged 84.8-100% and 20.0-98.4%; DWI plus conventional MRI reached sensitivity 93.3-100% and specificity 85-96.8% in adult series; the authors suggest an ADC cut-off of 1.1 x 10-3 mm2/s.
  • The authors state that MRI descriptions in paediatric studies were insufficient, no purely paediatric cohort with DWI/DCE data existed, and prospective age-specific studies are needed.
  • The authors note that benign masses such as thecomas or sclerosing stromal tumours can be hypervascular on DCE, so time-intensity curve type alone may not separate benign from malignant.

Presentation

  • large multicystic/solid ovarian mass on MRI (case reports)
  • tumour adhesion to anterior bowel loops (one case report)
