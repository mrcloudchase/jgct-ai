A Rare Case of Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor in a Premenarchal Female: Clinical Presentation, Diagnosis, and Management.

Why it matters for JGCT

Prepubertal stage I ovarian JGCT with gonadotropin-independent precocious puberty and elevated testosterone and 17-OHP, cured by fertility-sparing surgery.

Key findings

  • A 4.5-year-old girl had 2 weeks of pubic hair and breast development; estradiol was 294.70 pg/ml (reference <27), testosterone 1.330 ng/ml (<0.250), 17-OHP 5.23 ng/ml (<1.00), DHEA-S 0.467 ug/ml (normal), FSH <0.30 and LH <0.02 mIU/ml (gonadotropin-independent); AFP and beta-hCG normal.
  • Ultrasound showed a pubertal-type uterus (387 cm3) and an 11.45 x 5.13 x 8.38 cm mixed cystic-solid left ovarian mass with blood flow; MRI confirmed a large multilocular mass from L3 to S1 without evidence of invasion.
  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy en bloc was performed; peritoneal fluid and washings were cytologically negative; histology showed predominantly solid and follicular growth, hemorrhagic necrosis without capsular disruption, moderate nuclear atypia and a mitotic index of 0-4 per 40x field (6-15 per 10 HPF).
  • Tumor cells were positive for calretinin, CD99, CD56, inhibin, SF1, SMA and AE1/AE3 and negative for EMA and synaptophysin; stage was pT1a (TNM/UICC).
  • At 1 year there was no recurrence, precocious puberty features had resolved, estradiol and 17-OHP normalized, and LH/FSH remained prepubertal.
  • The discussion states approximately 90% of JGCTs are FIGO stage I with 5-year disease-free survival 90-100%, contralateral involvement is rare (3%), BEP is the commonest adjuvant regimen for stage IC-IV, ESMO advises BEP for stage IC while ESGO suggests surgery alone may suffice for IC1 and chemotherapy for IC2/IC3; these are literature statements by the authors.
  • The authors list recurrence risk factors from the literature as nuclear pleomorphism, mitoses >4/10 HPF, rupture, residual disease and FOXL2 mutation expression, and state no analogous genetic driver has been linked to JGCT (AGCT has FOXL2).

Presentation

  • 2 weeks of sudden pubic hair growth and breast enlargement (gonadotropin-independent precocious puberty)
  • pubertal-sized uterus 10.81 x 5.96 x 11.46 cm
  • large mixed solid-cystic left ovarian mass with Doppler flow

Treatments reported

  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy en bloc with the mass via Pfannenstiel incision; no adjuvant chemotherapy (pT1a (TNM/UICC); n = 1)
    Outcome: Symptoms resolved, estradiol and 17-OHP normalized; no recurrence at 1 year

Conflicts with

  • Šafanda A 2025: Complex immunohistochemical and molecular study on 5 cases of ovarian juvenile granulosa cell tumors reveals a consistent alteration in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway.

Notes

The statement that JGCT lacks a recognized genetic driver is the authors'; no molecular testing was done. Mitotic counts given in two units in the paper. Follow-up only 1 year. The discussion's claim of 'FOXL2 mutation expression' as recurrence risk is as printed.
