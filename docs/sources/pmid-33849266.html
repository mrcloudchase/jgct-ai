Precocious Pseudo-puberty in a Two-year-old Girl, Presenting with Bilateral Ovarian Enlargement and Progressing to Unilateral Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumour.

Why it matters for JGCT

Molecularly negative (GNAS, AKT1 exon 3, FOXL2) JGCT preceded by bilateral cystic ovarian enlargement; informs the uncertain relationship of JGCT with McCune-Albright signaling.

Key findings

  • Girl aged 2.17 years with 3 months of breast development and rapid growth: height 94 cm (+2.6 SD), Tanner B3P2A1, bone age 5.0 years, one 3 cm cafe-au-lait patch; LH/FSH suppressed (LH basal/peak 0.43/0.18, FSH <0.1/<0.1 mIU/mL) with estradiol 130 pg/mL (prepubertal <20).
  • Ultrasound showed bilaterally enlarged multicystic ovaries (left 76 mL, right 139 mL estimated), pubertal uterus; MAS work-up (calcium, phosphate, IGF-1, cortisol, thyroid, bone scintigraphy) was normal.
  • Tamoxifen 20 mg daily started at 2.3 years; after 7 weeks height increased 4 cm, breast stage B3-4, menorrhagia, bone age 6.5 years, estradiol 184 pg/mL with gonadotrophins still suppressed.
  • Repeat imaging showed a vascular solid-cystic left ovarian tumor (US 10x8x6 cm; MRI 12x10x5 cm) with a normal right ovary (1.83 mL); AFP, hCG and CEA normal, CA-125 slightly raised at 43 IU/mL; inhibin B assay not available.
  • A week later torsion required urgent laparotomy with left oophorectomy and annexectomy: tumor 850 g, capsule intact, negative peritoneal lavage; FIGO stage IA.
  • Histology: nodular tumor with fibrous septae, necrotic foci, abundant pale eosinophilic cytoplasm, rare nuclear grooves and mitoses, reticulin around groups of cells, no follicles, pseudopapillae or Call-Exner bodies; strongly positive for inhibin B; AFP and CD30 negative.
  • Tumor DNA was negative for GNAS hot spots, AKT1 exon 3 hot spot and FOXL2 C134W; after surgery signs regressed immediately, at 5.7 years she was prepubertal with normal ultrasound and at 8.1 years remained prepubertal with mildly elevated basal LH and prepubertal estradiol.
  • Authors call the progression from bilateral ovarian enlargement to unilateral JGCT a novel observation, cannot confirm or exclude McCune-Albright syndrome, and cite AKT1 duplications in 10/16 JGCT and GNAS mutations in 9/30 JGCT from other studies.

Presentation

  • breast development and rapid growth over 3 months
  • height +2.6 SD, bone age 5.0 years at chronological age 2.2
  • Tanner B3P2A1
  • single cafe-au-lait patch
  • bilateral enlarged multicystic ovaries then unilateral tumor
  • menorrhagia on tamoxifen

Genetic and molecular findings

  • GNAS: Hot spots R201H, R201C, Q227L not found (0/1)
    PCR and restriction digestion of tumor DNA from paraffin
  • AKT1: Exon 3 (hot spot) no somatic mutation (0/1)
    Semi-nested PCR and Sanger sequencing; DNA of poor quality; whole-gene sequencing not done
  • FOXL2: C134W not found (0/1)
    Used to exclude adult granulosa cell tumor

Treatments reported

  • Tamoxifen 20 mg daily for 7 weeks, then urgent laparotomy with left oophorectomy and annexectomy for torsion (FIGO stage IA, capsule intact, tumor 850 g; n = 1)
    Outcome: Immediate regression of pubertal signs and metrorrhagia; prepubertal and well at last review aged 8.1 years (about 6 years after surgery)

Notes

Estradiol in the cited paper is quoted in pg/mL; detection limit stated as <10 ng/mL (likely pg/mL, as printed). The figure legend for Fig 2 says 'right ovary' while text says left. Authors calculated ovarian volumes from ultrasound dimensions with assumptions; these are estimates. Possible MAS not excluded. The authors' gene-expression comparison (GEO dataset, B-Raf enrichment) is exploratory.
