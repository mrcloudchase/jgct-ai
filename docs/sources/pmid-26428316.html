A survey of DICER1 hotspot mutations in ovarian and testicular sex cord-stromal tumors.

Why it matters for JGCT

Contributes only context for JGCT: no JGCTs were tested, but the study shows DICER1 hotspot mutations cluster in Sertoli-lineage tumors and not in testicular sex cord-stromal tumors, and that FOXL2 C134W marks granulosa-like gynandroblastoma components.

Key findings

  • Sanger sequencing of DICER1 exons 24 and 25 in 32 ovarian Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors, 8 Sertoli cell tumors, 5 gynandroblastomas, 1 sex cord tumor with annular tubules, 1 ovarian sex cord-stromal tumor NOS, 15 testicular sex cord-stromal tumors and 10 female genital tract tumors with rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation. No juvenile granulosa cell tumors were included.
  • DICER1 hotspot mutations in 20/32 (63%) ovarian Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors, 16/20 (80%) of which were p.E1705K; frequencies were 6/10 (60%) in poorly differentiated, 14/20 (70%) in intermediately differentiated, and 0/2 in well-differentiated tumors; 9/14 (64%) with heterologous or retiform elements versus 11/18 (61%) without.
  • Gynandroblastomas 2/5 (40%) and ovarian Sertoli cell tumors 5/8 (63%) carried DICER1 hotspot mutations, all p.E1705K; the sex cord tumor with annular tubules (0/1) and sex cord-stromal tumor NOS (0/1) were wild-type.
  • Two DICER1 wild-type gynandroblastomas carried FOXL2 p.C134W in both granulosa-like and Sertoli-Leydig-like components, suggesting gynandroblastoma encompasses two genetic groups (FOXL2-mutant granulosa-like or DICER1-mutant Sertoli-Leydig-like); FOXL2 and DICER1 mutations appeared mutually exclusive.
  • No DICER1 hotspot mutations in 15 testicular sex cord-stromal tumors (8 classical Sertoli cell tumors, 3 large cell calcifying variants, 4 NOS); combined with the earlier report, 20 testicular tumors tested were all wild-type, so the authors suggest ovarian and testicular sex cord-stromal tumor pathogenesis differ.
  • DICER1 hotspot mutations were found in 2/10 female genital tract rhabdomyosarcomatous tumors (an adult cervical rhabdomyosarcoma metastasis p.D1709N and the rhabdomyosarcoma component of a uterine carcinosarcoma p.E1813Q); 0/2 pediatric cervical embryonal rhabdomyosarcomas.
  • Authors note in the introduction that adult granulosa cell tumors usually harbor somatic FOXL2 mutations and that germline DICER1 mutations cause the DICER1 syndrome; they re-analyzed TCGA endometrial data (6/248, 2.4% with DICER1 hotspot residue mutations).

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: RNase IIIb hotspot mutations (E1705K, D1709N, E1813Q, G1809R) (ovarian Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor 20/32 (63%); Sertoli cell tumor 5/8 (63%); gynandroblastoma 2/5 (40%); testicular sex cord-stromal tumors 0/15)
    No juvenile granulosa cell tumors were tested in this study.
  • FOXL2: p.C134W (c.402C>G) (2/2 DICER1 wild-type gynandroblastomas tested (both components))
    Reported in granulosa-like and Sertoli-Leydig-like areas of each.

Notes

IMPORTANT: this paper contains no juvenile granulosa cell tumor cases; n_jgct_patients is 0. JGCT is mentioned only in the introduction as a sex cord-stromal subtype. Earlier abstract-based records may have implied JGCT content. Tumor site 'both' because ovarian and testicular tumors were studied. The Figure 1 legend says 'FOXL1' where FOXL2 is meant. The paper compares its 63% SLCT rate with 26/43 (60%) from Heravi-Moussavi (pmid-22187960) and 6/19 (32%) from Goulvent et al.
