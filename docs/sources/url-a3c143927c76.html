Juvenile granulosa cell tumour or adult granulosa cell tumour - a diagnostic dilemma (ECP 2019 abstract E-PS-09-021)

Why it matters for JGCT

Pathology case showing overlap between juvenile and adult granulosa cell tumour morphology in an adolescent.

Key findings

  • 16-year-old girl with irregular cycles; ultrasound showed a 12 cm right ovarian cyst, extirpated with ovary sparing, then salpingo-oophorectomy.
  • First specimen: firm fibrous 0.5 cm wall with cords and trabeculae of small uniform cells and some larger clear-cytoplasm cells, up to 8 mitoses/10 HPF; IHC negative for epithelial, muscle, germ cell, lymphocytic and neuroendocrine markers, strongly positive for vimentin, CD99 and CD56; inhibin and calretinin positive only in larger cells; reticulin fibers around cell groups; read as luteinized granulosa cell tumour.
  • Salpingo-oophorectomy specimen: macrofollicular and solid pattern, same IHC; some solid areas had 40 mitoses/10 HPF with proliferative activity up to 50%; a small area showed classic adult granulosa cell tumour (AGCT) morphology.
  • Authors conclude JGCT and AGCT share morphologic features and diagnostic difficulty arises when JGCT contains AGCT elements, as in this patient. Stage, treatment beyond surgery and outcome are not reported.

Presentation

  • irregular cycles
  • 12 cm right ovarian cyst on ultrasound

Treatments reported

  • Ovary-sparing cystectomy, then salpingo-oophorectomy (16-year-old with ovarian JGCT with adult-type areas; n = 1)
    Outcome: not reported

Notes

Whole abstract read from the supplement text (PMC7103147). The abstract's own conclusion calls the first-biopsy diagnosis luteinized granulosa cell tumour; no molecular testing. No stage or follow-up.
