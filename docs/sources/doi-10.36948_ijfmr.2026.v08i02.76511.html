Adolescent Girl with a Left Adnexal Solid-Cystic Mass-Granulosa Cell Tumor: A Case Report

Why it matters for JGCT

Adolescent stage IA JGCT presenting without endocrine symptoms and with normal conventional tumor markers.

Key findings

  • A 15-year-old girl had a year of intermittent lower abdominal pain and a firm, non-tender left lower abdominal mass; no menstrual irregularity or other hormonal symptoms.
  • Ultrasound and CECT showed an 8 x 10 cm heterogeneous solid-cystic left adnexal mass without peritoneal deposits, lymphadenopathy or significant ascites.
  • AFP, beta-hCG, LDH and CA-125 were normal; inhibin B and AMH were not measured.
  • At laparotomy about 50 mL of straw-colored ascitic fluid and an 8 x 10 cm left ovarian mass were found; frozen section suggested juvenile granulosa cell tumor.
  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy with staging procedures was done; final histopathology confirmed JGCT confined to the ovary, FIGO stage IA (follicle-like structures).
  • Recovery was uneventful; regular clinical, imaging and hormonal follow-up was advised. Authors conclude that GCT should be considered in adolescent adnexal masses even without hormonal symptoms or raised markers, and that fertility-sparing surgery gives excellent outcomes in early stage with prolonged follow-up needed.

Presentation

  • one year of intermittent lower abdominal pain, worse over 2 months
  • firm non-tender left lower abdominal mass
  • no menstrual irregularity or hormonal symptoms
  • minimal ascites (about 50 mL)

Treatments reported

  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy with peritoneal fluid sampling, pelvic lymph node sampling, omental biopsy, endometrial sampling (primary, fertility-sparing; n = 1)
    Outcome: FIGO stage IA; uneventful postoperative course; no adjuvant therapy described; follow-up duration not reported

Notes

Title says granulosa cell tumor; text and abstract specify juvenile type. No IHC reported, no follow-up interval given. Background survival figures (90-97% stage I) are cited from literature.
