Why it matters for JGCT

Single report of ovarian JGCT in an infant with Ollier's disease and 47,XXX; adds to the Ollier's disease-JGCT association but this letter provides no molecular data.

Key findings

  • A girl born preterm at 32 weeks with a prenatally detected 47,XXX karyotype (triple X syndrome) was referred at 22 months for left leg deformity and limb-length discrepancy; radiographs showed multiple lower-limb and right upper-limb enchondromas, diagnosing Ollier's disease.
  • At 18 months she developed sudden progressive ascites; ultrasound showed a large solid cystic multilocular mass with heterogeneous echogenicity and internal vascularity, and abdominopelvic CT showed an enhancing heterogeneous mass in the right and central abdomen.
  • Laparotomy and histology showed irregular cells in a myxoid background with basophilic secretions and a high mitotic count; tumor cells expressed cytokeratin, calretinin, Ki-67, inhibin and WT1, and the diagnosis was ovarian JGCT.
  • The letter reports no surgical extent, stage, chemotherapy, hormone levels, molecular testing or follow-up outcome.
  • The authors state this is the first report of triple X syndrome associated with Ollier's disease and JGCT, and conclude that Ollier's disease can increase the risk of various neoplasms, which should be considered in managing these patients.

Presentation

  • sudden progressive ascites at 18 months
  • large solid-cystic multilocular pelvic/abdominal mass with internal vascularity on ultrasound
  • left leg deformity and limb-length discrepancy (Ollier's disease) at 22 months

Notes

Letter to the editor; very limited clinical detail (no stage, treatment or outcome). Right-sided/central mass laterality of the ovary not stated explicitly. Possible topical overlap with the Ollier's disease case in pmid-37324278 (different authors/institutions, so no patient overlap is assumed).
