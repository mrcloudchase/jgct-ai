Why it matters for JGCT

Only a count of JGCT (4 of 212 patients with painful adnexal masses) and a statement of adjuvant chemotherapy; no JGCT outcomes. Useful as hospital-series frequency data for a Chinese pediatric gynecology center.

Key findings

  • Retrospective Peking Union Medical College Hospital cohort (2012-2019) of 212 pediatric and adolescent patients with an adnexal mass and abdominal pain; torsion in 78 (36.8%).
  • Sex cord-stromal tumors were 7 of 212 (3.3%): 4 juvenile granulosa cell tumors, 1 malignant steroid cell tumor and 2 moderately differentiated Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors.
  • The text states juvenile granulosa tumors and malignant steroid cell tumors received adjuvant chemotherapy after surgery; regimens, stages and outcomes are not given per tumor type.
  • Among 14 complex lesions suspected malignant on ultrasound, 6 were malignant germ cell tumors and 3 were sex cord-stromal tumors.
  • Table 4 lists one granulosa cell tumor in the torsion group managed by adnexectomy with omentum resection and biopsy.
  • Malignant germ cell tumors (8/212) were treated with 3-6 cycles of PEB/PEV; tumor markers were elevated in 26 of 105 patients tested.
  • Factors associated with torsion on multivariate analysis were mass over 5 cm (OR 4.134), acute onset pain (OR 15.911) and persistent or recurrent pain (OR 24.150).

Presentation

  • Series presented with adnexal mass and abdominal pain; JGCT-specific presentation not given

Treatments reported

  • Surgery followed by adjuvant chemotherapy (regimen not specified for JGCT) (juvenile granulosa cell tumors and malignant steroid cell tumor; n = 4)
    Outcome: Outcomes not reported for JGCT individually

Notes

Abstract odds-ratio labels for acute pain and persistent pain are swapped relative to Table 5 in the Results text (Table 5 gives acute onset OR 15.911 and persistent/recurrent pain OR 24.150 in multivariate analysis). The Table 4 'granulosa cell tumor' entry is not specified as juvenile.
