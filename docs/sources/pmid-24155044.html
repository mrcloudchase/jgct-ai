Why it matters for JGCT

Documents two JGCT patients presenting with precocious puberty in a pediatric cancer center series and illustrates diagnostic delay or misclassification as idiopathic central PP; the rest of the series is non-JGCT.

Key findings

  • St. Jude Children's Research Hospital review (1975-Oct 2011): 24 of 13,615 children with neoplasms (0.18%; 16 male, 8 female) had precocious puberty (PP) at or within 60 days of presentation; brain tumors 12, adrenocortical carcinoma 5, hepatoblastoma 4, other 3 (including 2 ovarian JGCT and 1 Leydig cell tumor).
  • Two patients had ovarian JGCT (Table I): patient 19, a girl aged 3.5 years (PP noted at 3 years, 6 months before diagnosis) with pubarche and an ovarian mass, endocrine testing done before diagnosis, outcome complete response; patient 17, a girl aged 6.8 years (PP at 5.8 years, 12 months before diagnosis) with central-type PP picture, 'benign' enlarged ovarian follicles and no response to GnRH analog, outcome CR.
  • Patient 17 was treated with a GnRH agonist for presumed idiopathic central PP before the gonadal tumor was found; the lesion was initially interpreted as benign. The authors note secondary central PP induced by persistently high sex hormones can mask the underlying tumor in gonadal neoplasms.
  • Time from PP onset to tumor diagnosis was median 6 months (range 0-48) across all 24; signs of PP were misinterpreted or underappreciated in half of the cohort, and 16 of 24 had PP signs for 3 months or more before the tumor diagnosis.
  • In the authors' pathophysiology grouping, JGCT falls under proliferative process with excessive sex hormone production (estradiol; first decade of life; endocrine abnormalities more frequent in younger patients); 8 of 24 patients were in this group (3 gonadal, 5 adrenocortical carcinoma).
  • The authors recommend that a final diagnosis of idiopathic PP not be made before assessment of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis and appropriate tumor markers/imaging of gonads and adrenals, and give a diagnostic algorithm (Fig. 2) including malignancy.

Presentation

  • precocious puberty
  • pubarche and ovarian mass (age 3.5)
  • central-type PP picture with enlarged ovarian follicles, no response to GnRH analog (age 6.8)

Treatments reported

  • not specified in detail (outcome only); GnRH agonist given for presumed idiopathic central PP before tumor found in patient 17 (ovarian JGCT with precocious puberty; n = 2)
    Outcome: both complete response (CR)

Notes

Study is of neoplasm-associated PP in general; JGCT data limited to Table I rows 17 and 19 and short text mentions. Treatment details for the JGCT patients are not reported beyond cancer outcome (CR). Patient 17's age at diagnosis 6.8 y is above typical infantile JGCT range.
