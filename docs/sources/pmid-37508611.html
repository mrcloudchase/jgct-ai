Why it matters for JGCT

Broad review of paediatric ovarian masses; JGCT is covered in one subsection (3.3.1) and in the marker and predisposition tables. No new JGCT patient data.

Key findings

  • Review of paediatric ovarian masses; the JGCT section states that JGCT is a malignant pure sex cord tumour of primitive granulosa cells growing in solid and follicular patterns.
  • JGCT accounts for 5% of granulosa cell tumours and 70% of all sex-cord stromal tumours in patients under 20 years; sex-cord stromal tumours are 10-20% of paediatric ovarian neoplasms; mean age at diagnosis is 13 years.
  • Young et al. (cited) studied 125 JGCT patients: 44% occurred in the first and 34% in the second decade of life.
  • JGCT is associated with Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome (enchondromatosis); listed in the review's table of cancer predisposition syndromes.
  • JGCT typically secretes estrogens; granulosa cells produce inhibin, so serum inhibin is described as a useful tumour marker for diagnosis and follow-up (Table 2: inhibin - JGCT).
  • Imaging is heterogeneous and nonspecific: typically large, mostly solid and cystic, may be entirely solid or cystic; hemorrhage, necrosis, fibrosis common; sponge-like T2 MRI appearance; T1-hyperintense hemorrhagic foci in cysts; endometrial thickening and uterine enlargement from estrogen.
  • Review states prognosis is favorable, late recurrences are rare, most patients have stage-limited-to-ovary disease with survival over 90% with surgery alone; rare advanced cases have poorer prognosis and may require chemotherapy.
  • General recommendation: management of paediatric ovarian masses should be curative, function-preserving and minimally invasive, in specialized centres; the review notes 46-54% of benign lesions undergo oophorectomy.

Presentation

  • isosexual peripheral precocious puberty in premenarchal patients (breast enlargement, vaginal bleeding, axillary/pubic hair, growth)
  • menstrual disorders (menorrhagia, amenorrhea) in postmenarchal patients
  • pelvic mass; torsion and rupture more frequent in children than adults
  • rare virilization

Notes

Review; JGCT figures are secondary citations of other studies (e.g. Young et al.). Journal and first author not stated in the retrieved full text.
