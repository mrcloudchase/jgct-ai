Why it matters for JGCT

Infant JGCT presenting with peripheral precocious puberty, with serial inhibin B, AMH and estradiol follow-up after surgery alone.

Key findings

  • 10-month-old girl with 4 months of breast development and 2 months of pubic hair, apocrine odor and whitish vaginal discharge; Tanner III breasts, abundant pubic hair and a palpable abdominal mass (peripheral precocious puberty).
  • Estradiol 457 pg/mL (reference <12), LH <0.5, FSH 0.66 mIU/mL (suppressed gonadotropins), LDH 2,690 mIU/mL (reference 210-420), hemoglobin 8.3 g/dL; AFP 10.7, beta-hCG 2.3, CEA 1.0 normal.
  • Ultrasound showed a large solid-cystic abdominopelvic mass; CT showed a heterogeneous 15 x 13 x 12 cm mass with mass effect, uterus 7 cm with endometrial thickening, adnexa not identified.
  • Ten days after the first visit a left salpingo-oophorectomy removed an 800 g mass with necrosis and hemorrhage; histology showed large and medium cells with granular cytoplasm and follicles of different sizes in an edematous stroma, compatible with juvenile granulosa cell tumor.
  • Pubertal signs and ultrasound abnormalities regressed; CT 2 months postoperatively was normal; inhibin B was 50 pg/mL initially (reference <44) then 17 and 10.8; AMH 1.24, 1.23 and 1.26 ng/mL (reference 0.5-4.7); estradiol fell to 15, 7, 6 and <5 pg/mL.
  • At 2 years transient thelarche with normal GnRH test and bone age was judged non-specific transient thelarche (right ovary volume 2.6 cc); no relapse in almost 4 years of follow-up.
  • The authors recommend follow-up with inhibin B or AMH every 6 months for 2 years then annually, and state that stage I tumors are curative with surgery (5-year survival 90-100%, cited).

Presentation

  • thelarche (4 months) and pubic hair (2 months), apocrine odor, whitish vaginal discharge
  • palpable abdominal mass 10 cm below costal margin
  • Tanner III breasts, estrogenized genitalia

Treatments reported

  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy and resection of an 800 g adnexal mass; no adjuvant therapy (stage I, primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: Regression of pubertal signs; CT 2 months after surgery normal; no relapse in almost 4 years of follow-up (inhibin B, AMH, estradiol normal)

Notes

Paper is in Spanish with an English abstract/translation in the PDF. The text calls LDH 'low' although 2,690 is above the quoted reference range, and lists testosterone 25 ng/ml against a reference of 0.2-0.6 (possible unit error); recorded as printed. Table 2 follow-up ages are partly ambiguous. FIGO stage I is stated in the discussion; no staging biopsies are described. DOI as printed in the PDF header.
