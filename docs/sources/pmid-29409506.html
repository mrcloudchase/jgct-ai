Why it matters for JGCT

Molecular review dominated by AGCT; contains only a few JGCT-specific statements (FOXL2 downregulation in aggressive JGCT, SMAD1/5 knockout mouse phenocopy, active TGF-beta/SMAD2/3 pathway). No patient data.

Key findings

  • Narrative review of molecular mechanisms of ovarian granulosa cell tumor (GCT), which it describes as 2-5% of ovarian cancers; about 95% are adult (AGCT) and the rest juvenile (JGCT); the review says JGCT occurs only in people younger than 30 years (the review's wording attributes hypoestrogenism and abdominal mass to JGCT, although GCT is estrogen-secreting).
  • Most of the review concerns AGCT: FOXL2 402C>G (C134W) in more than 97% of AGCT; the review adds that FOXL2 expression was reported to be downregulated in aggressive JGCT.
  • JGCT-specific molecular statements: conditional deletion of SMAD1 and SMAD5 in mouse granulosa cells causes metastatic GCT that phenocopies human JGCT, and the TGF-beta/SMAD2/3 pathway is active in JGCT, where TGF-beta may promote tumor growth by inhibiting apoptosis (cited work).
  • Other pathways covered, mostly from cell lines (KGN) and mouse models rather than JGCT: PI3K/AKT-FOXO-PTEN, Notch, activin/inhibin, BMP, AMH, GATA4 and VEGF; the review notes the relationship between these pathways and human GCT is often unclear.
  • Review conclusion: 5-year survival is up to 90%, recurrence is the main cause of death, surgery remains the most important treatment, and hormonal, anti-VEGF and pathway-targeted strategies remain at an early, adjunctive stage.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • FOXL2: 402C>G (C134W) somatic missense mutation (reported by the review in more than 97% of adult GCT (AGCT))
    the review states FOXL2 expression is also downregulated in aggressive JGCT; cited reports, not original data
  • SMAD1/SMAD5: conditional deletion in mouse granulosa cells causes metastatic GCT that phenocopies human JGCT
    mouse model
  • TGF-beta/SMAD2/3 pathway: reported active in JGCT
    cited statement; TGF-beta may promote JGCT by inhibiting apoptosis

Notes

Review does not mention AKT1 or GNAS mutations in JGCT. Its FOXL2 frequency (>97% in AGCT) differs from other sources (86/89, about 97%). Statements about JGCT are secondhand citations.
