Why it matters for JGCT

Pathology-oriented review with a dedicated JGCT section covering clinical, morphological, immunohistochemical and molecular features and the differential diagnosis against other ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors; reports no original patients.

Key findings

  • Narrative review (literature searched July-October 2023, PubMed Central and Google Scholar) of ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors organized by predominant cell morphology into five groups, with diagnostic algorithms; sex cord-stromal tumors are about 8% of ovarian tumors.
  • JGCT is described as a very rare pure sex cord neoplasm, 5% of all ovarian granulosa cell tumors (adult type 95%); mean age 13 years, under 30 years in 97%, range newborn to 67 years.
  • Clinical features: pelvic mass symptoms plus estrogenic manifestations (isosexual pseudoprecocity in 82% of prepubertal patients), rarely androgenic; rare associations with Ollier disease, Maffucci syndrome, DICER1 syndrome, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome and tuberous sclerosis. Over 97% unilateral, mean size 12.5 cm (3-32 cm), solid and cystic or predominantly solid, necrosis/hemorrhage in 13%.
  • Histology: diffuse, lobulated, follicle-like architecture in a myxoid/edematous/hyalinized background; abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm; nuclei usually lack grooves (grooves focally in about 50%); mitoses 1 to 32 per 10 HPF (mean 7), higher than adult GCT; necrosis in 40%; sclerosis in 95% of JGCT. Reticulin surrounds nests/cords, with monocellular network in thecomatous areas.
  • Immunohistochemistry: SF1, inhibin, calretinin, WT1 and FOXL2 positive as in adult GCT; AE1/AE3 may be expressed; EMA typically negative. IHC cannot separate juvenile from adult GCT.
  • Molecular: no FOXL2 variant; DICER1 variants in 6-23%, AKT1 activating variants in 29-60%, GNAS in 30%; recurrent KMT2C 52%, ARID1A 39%, KMT2D 35%, BRCA2 35%, ATM 26%, BRCA1 23%, POLE 23%, MLH1 19%, MSH6 10%, TERT variant 19% and rearrangement 13%, TP53 up to 16%; IDH1/2 in Ollier/Maffucci-associated cases. DICER1 and GNAS variants could worsen prognosis, but the authors state more data are needed.
  • Differentials: luteinized JGCT versus steroid cell tumor, thecoma, sclerosing stromal tumor; FOXL2 variant favors luteinized adult GCT, DICER1 variant favors luteinized JGCT or Sertoli-Leydig tumor; small cell carcinoma of the ovary hypercalcemic type (young patients, brisk mitoses, pseudo-follicles) should be excluded with EMA and BRG1 (SMARCA4 surrogate).
  • The authors suggest JGCT may be a distinct entity from adult GCT given different molecular alterations; DICER1 variant found in 6% of JGCT, 63-80% of Sertoli-Leydig tumors, 40% of gynandroblastomas (cited figures); DICER1 testing is not recommended to distinguish Sertoli-Leydig tumor from JGCT or gynandroblastoma. Gynandroblastoma shows no AKT1 variant in juvenile-type components.

Presentation

  • pelvic/abdominal pain and swelling
  • isosexual pseudoprecocity in 82% of prepubertal patients
  • menstrual irregularities, amenorrhea or uterine bleeding in older patients
  • rarely virilization

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: variants (6-23% of JGCT)
    sometimes with DICER1 syndrome; may worsen prognosis vs wild type (more data needed)
  • AKT1: activating variants (29-60% of JGCT)
    may play central pathogenic role
  • GNAS: variants (30% of JGCT)
    may worsen prognosis
  • KMT2C/ARID1A/KMT2D: variants (52% / 39% / 35%)
    genomic landscape study cited
  • BRCA2/ATM/BRCA1: variants (35% / 26% / 23%)
  • POLE/MLH1/MSH6: variants (23% / 19% / 10%)
  • TERT: variant 19%; rearrangement 13% (19% / 13%)
  • TP53: somatic variants (up to 16% of JGCT)
  • IDH1/IDH2: somatic variants (in JGCT with Ollier disease or Maffucci syndrome)
  • FOXL2: C402G (p.C134W) variant (absent in JGCT (present >95% in adult GCT))
    FOXL2 variants rarely reported in 'JGCT' but unclear whether these were true JGCT
