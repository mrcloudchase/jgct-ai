Why it matters for JGCT

Radiology-oriented overview of all pediatric ovarian masses; useful for JGCT mainly for the summary of its epidemiology, hormone and marker profile, and imaging appearance relative to other pediatric ovarian tumors. No original JGCT data.

Key findings

  • Narrative imaging review of pediatric (0-19 y) ovarian masses (PubMed search 1977 to April 2025, English only, non-systematic); JGCT is covered in one section plus a table; no JGCT patient series is reported.
  • Pediatric ovarian tumor incidence is given as 2.6 per 100,000 girls; malignancy is found in 3-8% of children and adolescents with an adnexal mass; sex cord-stromal tumors (SCSTs) are about 10-20% of pediatric ovarian neoplasms.
  • JGCT and Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors are the commonest pediatric SCSTs, roughly 75% and 15% of SCSTs; JGCT is only 5% of all granulosa cell tumors; mean age at diagnosis 13 years; bilateral in 4-5%.
  • JGCTs are often hormonally active, typically producing estradiol (usable as a diagnostic and follow-up marker); they can also secrete inhibin (particularly inhibin B) and Mullerian inhibiting substance, usable as tumor markers.
  • Reports of JGCT in patients with Maffucci syndrome and Ollier disease exist, but a definitive link with enchondromatosis syndromes is not confirmed.
  • Most JGCTs are diagnosed early with an excellent prognosis (survival over 90% after surgical resection); mitotic index correlates significantly with prognosis; long-term follow-up is needed because of rare late recurrence.
  • Pathology: large solid masses with cystic spaces, average diameter 12.5 cm, primitive granulosa cells in solid and follicular patterns, with hemorrhage, necrosis and fibrosis.
  • Imaging: nonspecific; US and CT show a large multicystic mass with solid, heterogeneous, highly vascular components and septations; MRI may show a sponge-like pattern of many small cysts among solid areas, with hemorrhage (high T1, fluid-fluid levels), plus endometrial thickening and uterine enlargement from estrogen.
  • Pediatric ovarian SCSTs and epithelial tumors are staged by FIGO (germ cell tumors by COG); the authors state that ovarian-sparing, minimally invasive surgery with fertility preservation and referral to specialized centers is important, and that cancer predisposition (genetic testing, family history) should be considered in children with ovarian tumors.
  • Authors note imaging risk scores (RMI, ROMA, IOTA, O-RADS) are poorly validated in children.

Presentation

  • isosexual peripheral precocious puberty in premenarchal girls (breast development, vaginal bleeding, accelerated growth, pubic/axillary hair)
  • menstrual irregularities (menorrhagia or amenorrhea) in postmenarchal patients
  • rarely virilization from androgen production
  • example figure: 17-year-old with abdominal distention, history of precocious puberty, ascites
