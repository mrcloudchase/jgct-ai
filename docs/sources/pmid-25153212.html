Continuous subcutaneous IGF-1 therapy via insulin pump in a patient with Donohue syndrome.

Why it matters for JGCT

Rare association: bilateral granulosa cell tumors (juvenile right, adult-type left) with borderline serous implants in a toddler with Donohue syndrome (INSR loss) on rhIGF-1 therapy. Causality unresolved. No marker, IHC or molecular tumor testing described.

Key findings

  • Girl with molecularly confirmed Donohue syndrome (compound heterozygous INSR frameshift mutations) treated from 19 months with subcutaneous recombinant human IGF-1 and from 30 months with continuous rhIGF-1 via insulin pump; HbA1c fell from 9.8% to 8.8% on pump therapy and weight gain rose from 175 to 202 g/month.
  • Polycystic ovaries were present since infancy and enlarged with age; cyst marsupialization was done at 21 months.
  • At 35 months she presented with respiratory distress and an enlarged abdomen; ultrasound showed a large cystic abdominal structure with possible ovarian torsion.
  • Bilateral oophorectomy: right ovarian mass 1.2 kg, 16.5 x 12 x 9 cm, consistent with juvenile granulosa cell tumor with desmoplastic non-invasive implants of borderline serous tumor; an adult-type granulosa cell tumor was also seen in the left ovary.
  • Postoperative course: prolonged intubation for acute respiratory distress syndrome; parents withdrew care and she died at 36 months.
  • Authors state it was unclear whether the ovarian tumor was related to rhIGF-1 treatment or to the natural course of Donohue syndrome; JGCT had been reported in another infant with Donohue syndrome who never received rhIGF-1 (cited).
  • Authors discuss theoretical concerns that IGF-1 signalling (Akt pathway, proliferation) and high IGF-1 levels are linked to malignancy.

Presentation

Genetic and molecular findings

  • INSR: compound heterozygous frameshift: c.260_261insG (p.Tyr87X) maternal and c.15_24dup10 (p.Ala9fs) paternal (Donohue syndrome) (1 patient)
    germline; predicted complete INSR loss of function

Treatments reported

    Outcome: prolonged intubation for ARDS postoperatively; care withdrawn, death at 36 months
