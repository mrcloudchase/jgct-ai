Constitutive Activation of PI3K in Oocyte Induces Ovarian Granulosa Cell Tumors.

Why it matters for JGCT

Preclinical mouse model of granulosa cell tumor with JGCT-like features driven by PI3K-AKT pathway activation; provides mechanistic context for AKT1 and GNAS findings in human JGCT. No JGCT patients.

Key findings

  • Mouse study (not human patients): constitutively active PI3K (PIK3CA*) in primordial oocytes (Gdf9-iCre) produced bilateral ovarian granulosa cell tumors with 100% penetrance at postnatal day 65 (n>=35); mice became cachectic and were euthanized by day 85.
  • The authors judged the mouse tumors to resemble JGCT rather than adult GCT: cord-like architecture was similar to human AGCT, but few nuclear grooves or Call-Exner bodies and follicle-like cysts resembled JGCT, and FOXL2 was not mutated.
  • Tumor cells expressed FOXL2 and inhibin alpha, overexpressed inhibin betaA, SMAD3 and GATA4, had high p-AKT, and the mice had high serum AMH, activin A, estradiol, testosterone and inhibin A with low FSH.
  • Tumor cells did not express the mutant PI3K transgene (PCR) and tumor grafts grew independently of oocytes, but interaction with mutant oocytes at primordial/primary follicle stage was essential (co-grafted normal ovaries stayed tumor-free; Zp3-Cre activation at secondary follicle stage caused no tumors).
  • Tumor growth correlated with the activin A to inhibin A ratio (R2 = 0.784 with Ki67 index); cultured tumor cells secreted 40 times more activin A than normal granulosa cells and proliferated in response to activin A (25 ng/ml), suggesting an activin A-SMAD3 autocrine growth circuit.
  • FOXO1 and PTEN were retained in the tumors, so loss of these suppressors was not the cause in this model.
  • The authors suggest, as speculation, that AKT1 mutations in human JGCT may contribute to such an activin A autocrine circuit and that GNAS R201 mutations may sensitize granulosa cells to activin A.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • PIK3CA (constitutively active transgene in oocytes, mouse): oocyte-specific activation (Gdf9-iCre) causes bilateral granulosa cell tumors in mice (100% penetrance at postnatal day 65 (n>=35))
    mouse model; tumor cells did not carry the transgene; authors say the model reflects JGCT biology
  • AKT1: recurrent in-frame duplications in exon 3 reported in human JGCT (cited from other studies)
    background citation
  • FOXL2: C402G missense mutation in over 90% of AGCT and less than 10% of JGCT (cited)
    background citation
  • GNAS: R201 mutations in JGCT discussed as sensitizing granulosa cells to activin A via constitutive FSH-receptor signaling (cited)
    discussion

Notes

Mouse data; findings should not be read as human JGCT evidence. Human JGCT mutation statements (AKT1 duplications, FOXL2 <10%, GNAS R201) are cited from other papers. Authors' classification of the model as JGCT-like is their interpretation.
