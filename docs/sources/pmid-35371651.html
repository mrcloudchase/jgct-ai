Transformation of Peripheral Sexual Precocity to Central Sexual Precocity Following Treatment of Granulosa Cell Tumor of the Ovary.

Why it matters for JGCT

Illustrates a late endocrine sequela of JGCT-related precocious puberty (secondary central puberty after tumor removal and chemotherapy), relevant to long-term follow-up of young JGCT patients.

Key findings

  • Case report: 5 year 5 month old girl with 2.5 years of progressive bilateral breast enlargement, intermittent vaginal spotting and growth acceleration; no pubic or axillary hair; hormones initially indicated peripheral precocious puberty.
  • Preoperative labs: LH 0.28 IU/L, FSH 0.81 IU/mL, estradiol 21.10 pg/mL, CA 125 48.31 U/mL (0-35), LDH 310 U/L (140-280); triptorelin stimulation preoperatively gave basal LH 0.01 and 60/180-minute LH 2.2 and 1.7 (not pubertal).
  • Ultrasound: uterine volume 6 mL, uterus 3.6 x 1.2 x 2.6 cm, right adnexal mass 9.46 x 9.2 x 6.4 cm.
  • Laparotomy with tumor resection; histology: large atypical cells in tubulocystic and solid sheet patterns, nuclear atypia, eosinophilic cytoplasm, basophilic mucin secretion and mitoses; IHC positive for inhibin, vimentin, calretinin, CD56 and CD99; diagnosis JGCT; she then received cisplatin, etoposide, bleomycin.
  • At 1.5 years after surgery breast development had progressed (Tanner 3), spotting persisted (every 15-20 days), height 121 cm (+1.81 SD), weight 24 kg, bone age 11 years; pelvic ultrasound showed no mass and CA 125 (15.2 U/mL) and LDH (207 U/L) were normal, excluding recurrence.
  • Postoperative hormones: LH 0.70 IU/L, FSH 3.6 IU/mL, estradiol 11 pg/mL; post-triptorelin LH 5.2 at 60 and 180 minutes with basal 0.01, confirming central precocious puberty; leuprolide depot 11.25 mg every 3 months stopped spotting and regressed breasts.
  • Authors' conclusion: JGCT causing peripheral precocious puberty is known; central precocious puberty after resection is rarer and justifies close follow-up of such patients.

Presentation

  • progressive bilateral breast enlargement
  • intermittent vaginal spotting every 4-7 days for 2.5 years
  • growth acceleration, no pubic or axillary hair
  • right adnexal mass 9.46 x 9.2 x 6.4 cm on ultrasound

Treatments reported

  • Laparotomy with tumor resection, then cisplatin, etoposide, bleomycin (cycles not stated) (juvenile granulosa cell tumor, stage not stated; n = 1)
    Outcome: Tumor markers normalized, no mass on ultrasound at 1.5 years; central precocious puberty developed and was treated with leuprolide depot 11.25 mg every 3 months with stoppage of spotting and gradual breast regression

Notes

Stage, chemotherapy cycle number and mitotic count are not stated. Discussion states 30% of granulosa cell tumors do not produce estradiol and that estradiol is not a reliable activity marker (cited). Journal and first author not in cached text.
