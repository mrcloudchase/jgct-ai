Why it matters for JGCT

Small pediatric ovarian-mass surgical series with a single JGCT case (surgery alone, disease free); little else JGCT-specific.

Key findings

  • Single-center retrospective series of 30 children under 18 who had surgery for ovarian masses (2009-2023): 5 non-neoplastic, 10 benign, 3 borderline and 12 malignant neoplasms.
  • Only 1 of 30 patients (3.3%) had juvenile granulosa cell tumor; Table 1 classifies it under borderline malignant tumors.
  • The JGCT patient had normal preoperative tumor markers and received no postoperative chemotherapy; the authors note surgical resection alone with free margins was considered curative for such patients.
  • At mean follow-up of 53.5 months (range 4-117) all 30 patients were alive and disease free, including the JGCT patient.
  • Authors cite a meta-analysis in which sex cord-stromal tumors such as JGCT made up 14% of malignant pediatric ovarian masses and mixed germ cell tumors 30%.
  • Cohort-level conclusions: tumor size, solid component and raised AFP/beta-hCG/LDH help distinguish malignant from benign masses; overall prognosis favorable.

Treatments reported

  • Surgery only (no adjuvant chemotherapy); normal preoperative tumor markers (primary; n = 1)
    Outcome: Disease free at follow-up (cohort mean 53.5 months, range 4-117)

Notes

Only one JGCT; no age, stage, or presentation detail for it. Internal inconsistencies: abstract counts 'benign 10, borderline 3, malignant 12' but the abstract says 25 benign neoplasms; mean follow-up range 4-117 vs 4-114 in discussion; 5 functional masses given as 16.7% and 18.5%.
