Why it matters for JGCT

Mixed paediatric ovarian-mass series; JGCT-specific content is limited to the count of 10 JGCTs being the most frequent malignant ovarian tumour in this Polish national cohort. Also includes a scoping review of ovarian mass studies of at least 200 children.

Key findings

  • Retrospective review of 595 girls aged 0-18 operated on for ovarian lesions at 17 Polish paediatric surgery departments (2012-2017); 44 (7.39%) had malignant tumours.
  • Juvenile granulosa cell tumour was the most common malignant lesion: 10 of the 44 malignant tumours, within 12 stromal tumours (JGCT 10, Sertoli cell tumour 1, serous papillary carcinoma 1 as listed in Table 2).
  • The other malignant tumours were germ cell tumours (22: dysgerminoma 6, immature teratoma 5, yolk sac 4, mixed 7), epithelial (4) and other (6).
  • No malignant tumour occurred in girls younger than 1 year; malignant tumours were not different in age from non-malignant ones (p=0.570); median age of the malignant group 13.1 years.
  • Across all malignant tumours, positive tumour markers (OR 10.3), lesion larger than 6 cm (OR 4.17) and solid mass on ultrasound (OR 5.34) independently distinguished malignant from non-malignant lesions; marker panel included AFP, beta-hCG, CA125 and LDH.
  • Bilateral masses were more frequent in the malignant group (6/44 vs 30/551); ovarian torsion was mostly in non-malignant lesions (3 malignant vs 133 non-malignant).
  • JGCT-specific clinical, treatment and outcome data are not reported; the paper says malignant tumours were treated per oncological guidelines.

Presentation

  • abdominal distension and other symptoms (precocious maturation, vaginal bleeding, nausea/vomiting, fever, weight loss) predominated in malignant tumours (whole malignant group, not JGCT alone)

Notes
