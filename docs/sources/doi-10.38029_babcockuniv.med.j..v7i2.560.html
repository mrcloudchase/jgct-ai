Pseudo-precocious puberty secondary to Juvenile Granulosa cell tumour in a 30-month-old female toddler: A case report

Why it matters for JGCT

Toddler ovarian JGCT with peripheral precocious puberty in Nigeria, stage IA, treated by oophorectomy alone.

Key findings

  • A 30-month-old Nigerian girl presented with 3 months of vaginal bleeding, breast enlargement, sparse pubic/axillary hair, accelerated height (94 cm, 97th centile) and temper tantrums; Tanner stage 2 breast and pubic hair; a 4 x 6 cm firm lower abdominal mass; bone age was normal; no cafe-au-lait spots.
  • A 2-year history of maternal use of a steroid-containing skin cream (stopped 3 months before symptom onset) initially clouded the work-up.
  • Estradiol was <9.0 pg/ml at presentation, 64.6 pg/ml 6 months later (reference <15), and 26.9 pg/ml 1 month after surgery; FSH and LH stayed low-normal; AFP, CA-125 and beta-hCG were normal.
  • Ultrasound showed a 62.1 x 33.6 x 53.8 mm heterogeneous left lower abdominal mass; pelvic MRI showed a 47.8 x 38.6 x 50.4 mm round heterogeneous mass with multiple cystic foci inseparable from the left ovary.
  • At laparotomy 7 months after presentation: encapsulated 6 x 4 cm left ovary with adherent tube, about 60 ml ascites, normal pelvic and para-aortic nodes; left oophorectomy was done. Peritoneal fluid cytology showed no malignant cells.
  • Histology: solid sheets of tumor cells with small immature follicles containing secretions, round hyperchromatic ungrooved nuclei, occasional Call-Exner bodies, consistent with JGCT; no IHC is reported. Staged FIGO IA.
  • No recurrence of symptoms 17 months after surgery; authors note that follow-up was hampered by financial difficulties, and conclude that rare causes such as JGCT should be considered in prepubertal girls with pseudo-precocious puberty.

Presentation

  • vaginal bleeding for 3 months (up to 6 changes of underwear a day, interval shortening to 14 days)
  • breast enlargement and sparse pubic and axillary hair (Tanner 2)
  • height 97th centile, temper tantrums
  • palpable 4 x 6 cm lower abdominal mass
  • 2-year history of steroid-containing skin cream use

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy with left oophorectomy, 7 months after presentation; no adjuvant therapy (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: no recurrence of symptoms 17 months after surgery; follow-up limited by finances

Notes

The abstract says 'cytological analysis of excised tissues revealed Call-Exner bodies', whereas the text shows these were histological findings. No immunohistochemistry reported. The 7-month interval between presentation and surgery is stated. Authors state the 'staging according to FIGO 2014'.
