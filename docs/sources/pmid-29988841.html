Why it matters for JGCT

Pregnancy-associated ovarian cancer review; JGCT contributes 15 pooled case-report patients and a brief statement on adnexectomy; no separate maternal or tumor outcomes.

Key findings

  • Literature review (PubMed, 1986-2016, English) of 262 cases of gestational ovarian cancer from 45 reports; 193 were non-epithelial ovarian cancer, of which 145 germ cell tumors and 48 sex cord-stromal tumors.
  • Of the 48 sex cord-stromal tumors, juvenile granulosa cell tumor was the most common subtype with 15 patients (Table 3: 7.7% of 193), followed by Sertoli-Leydig tumor in 5 and other types in 28.
  • Authors state that juvenile granulosa cell tumors could be adequately treated with adnexectomy, with similar surgical management as in non-pregnant women (citing one reference).
  • For sex cord-stromal tumors as a group: obstetric outcome normal in 37/48, IUGR 4/48, fetal death 3/48, abortion 1/48, ventriculomegaly 1/48; neonatal outcome healthy 43/44, one neonatal death; chemotherapy needed in 8 patients (16.6%).
  • A cited series of 46 pregnant patients with sex cord-stromal tumors (mostly USO or node removal in 2nd/3rd trimesters) reported 69.4% fetal preservation, serious adverse events in 40% concentrated in higher stage and older patients.
  • General review conclusions: surgery best in the second trimester; carboplatin-paclitaxel standard for epithelial ovarian cancer in pregnancy; BEP considered reasonable for non-epithelial cancer with alternatives (PVB, cisplatin plus paclitaxel) discussed.

Treatments reported

  • Adnexectomy (unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy); chemotherapy in a minority (cisplatin/cyclophosphamide with or without epirubicin most common) (during pregnancy; n = 15)
    Outcome: Pooled with other sex cord-stromal tumors: 8 of 48 (16.6%) needed chemotherapy; mostly live births (77%); JGCT-specific outcomes not separated

Notes

Pooled literature cases (case reports and series), likely overlapping with other pregnancy-associated JGCT reports in the knowledge base. Table 3 shows percentages relative to all 193 non-epithelial cases. Journal and first author not in fetched text.
