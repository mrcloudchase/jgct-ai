Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor as the Presenting Feature of McCune-Albright Syndrome.

Why it matters for JGCT

Documents co-occurrence of JGCT and McCune-Albright syndrome driven by the same mosaic GNAS R201C mutation, linking JGCT biology to GNAS and raising surveillance questions.

Key findings

  • 15-year-old girl with secondary oligomenorrhea (irregular periods from age 14 y 4 mo; menarche at 12 y 8 mo), Hashimoto hypothyroidism on levothyroxine; no cafe-au-lait spots.
  • Labs: LH and FSH suppressed (both <0.10 IU/L), estradiol 1074 pg/mL (immunoassay) and 1030 pg/mL by tandem mass spectrometry, inhibin B >4325 pg/mL (ref 50-475), prolactin 56.67 ng/mL, SHBG 175.7 nmol/L, low IGF-I 182.0 ng/mL; hCG, AFP (1.0 ng/mL) and CA125 (14 unit/mL) normal; brain MRI normal.
  • Imaging: pelvic ultrasound 11 x 12 x 8 cm heterogeneous predominantly solid right adnexal mass; MRI and PET/CT showed minimal FDG uptake in the mass but intense multifocal skeletal uptake with expansile lytic lesions (including bilateral proximal femurs, a right femoral neck linear abnormality), characteristic of polyostotic fibrous dysplasia, initially concerning for metastases; tibial biopsy equivocal/benign, suggestive of fibrous dysplasia.
  • Surgery: laparotomy with peritoneal fluid suspicious for rupture, right salpingo-oophorectomy; pelvic washings negative, no lymphadenopathy; tumor 14.5 cm encapsulated, solid and cystic with necrosis; coffee-bean nuclei; IHC positive for inhibin, calretinin and SF1, diagnosis JGCT.
  • Tumor and tibial bone both carried GNAS p.R201C while peripheral blood was wild-type at codon 201, consistent with mosaicism; first report of the same GNAS mutation in JGCT and McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS) in one patient.
  • Classified FIGO stage IC2 because of rupture and treated with 4 cycles of bleomycin, cisplatin and etoposide; hormones normalized and she remained in remission 16 months after therapy; prolactin also normalized, attributed to estradiol effect.
  • Authors cite that >60% of JGCTs carry AKT1 duplications and that Kalfa et al. found codon 201 GNAS mutations in 30% of 30 JGCT patients without MAS signs; in a large MAS series (40 of 113 with ovarian sampling) none had JGCT.
  • Authors raise the question of tumor-marker surveillance in MAS with ovarian abnormalities and suggest investigating for the other disorder in patients diagnosed with either JGCT or MAS.

Presentation

  • secondary oligomenorrhea (up to 2 months without bleeding then heavy bleeding up to 2 weeks)
  • suppressed LH/FSH
  • hyperprolactinemia 56.67 ng/mL
  • 12 cm right adnexal mass
  • no cafe-au-lait spots

Genetic and molecular findings

  • GNAS: c.601C>T p.R201C (42% of 478 reads in tumor; same variant in tibial bone biopsy; wild-type in peripheral blood)
    mosaic distribution; no other Tier 1-3 mutations or copy number variants on 447-gene panel

Treatments reported

  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy via laparotomy, then bleomycin, cisplatin, etoposide x4 cycles (FIGO stage IC2 (intra-operative rupture); n = 1)
    Outcome: inhibin B, FSH, LH and estradiol normalized; in remission 16 months after therapy

Notes

Single case; literature figures quoted from cited papers. Older record may have described same facts from abstract; tumor size is 12 cm on imaging vs 14.5 cm at gross pathology.
