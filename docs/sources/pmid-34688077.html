Adult granulosa cell tumor with minor foci of juvenile granulosa cell tumor in postmenopausal woman: A rare case report.

Why it matters for JGCT

Postmenopausal tumor that is not a JGCT in the usual sense; shows that juvenile-pattern foci can occur within an adult GCT, relevant to pathologic classification only.

Key findings

  • A 63-year-old postmenopausal woman with 6 months of abdominal distension and postmenopausal bleeding had an 18 x 15 x 14 cm solid right ovarian mass at laparotomy (CT 13 x 18 x 19 cm with central necrosis, no ascites or seeding); beta-hCG <1.2 U/mL, AFP 6.51 ng/mL, LDH 185 U/L, CA125 186.3 U/mL.
  • The tumor was predominantly adult-type granulosa cell tumor (diffuse growth, Call-Exner bodies, nuclear grooves, 1-2 mitoses/10 HPF) with minor foci resembling JGCT: well-circumscribed solid areas with many follicle-like cavities of varying size containing thin eosinophilic secretion, small round deeply stained nuclei, no nuclear grooves and no Call-Exner bodies, with luteinization in the minor foci.
  • Two expert gynecopathologists were consulted and agreed with the finding; no immunohistochemistry or molecular testing is reported.
  • The authors state this is the first report of adult GCT with minor foci of JGCT in a postmenopausal woman, and cite prior reports of JGCT with minor adult-type foci (Zhang et al., Young et al.).
  • Authors' histologic criteria for JGCT: diffuse growth with follicle-like structures, absence of Call-Exner bodies, small round nuclei with deep chromatin and no grooves; they note follicle-like structures occur in a small part of adult GCT and 60% of adult GCT may lack Call-Exner bodies.
  • The authors state that age and clinical symptoms cannot discriminate adult from juvenile GCT, imaging cannot either, and an experienced pathologist is needed; they state 3% of JGCTs occur in women over 30 years and that prognosis follow-up of mixed tumors needs more study (only 3 months here).

Presentation

  • abdominal distension and postmenopausal bleeding for 6 months
  • palpable mobile solid abdominal mass
  • CA125 186.3 U/mL

Treatments reported

  • Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy with total hysterectomy (FIGO stage IC (no spill, rupture or malignant ascitic cells), adult GCT with minor JGCT foci; n = 1)
    Outcome: No complications; no recurrence or metastasis at 3 months

Notes

Not a juvenile-age or clinically juvenile GCT; JGCT component is a minor focus within an adult GCT, diagnosed by H&E morphology only. Follow-up 3 months. Clinically staged FIGO 1C in text, table lists 1C. Use cautiously in any JGCT outcome statistics.
