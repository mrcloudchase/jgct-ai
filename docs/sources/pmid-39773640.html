Complex immunohistochemical and molecular study on 5 cases of ovarian juvenile granulosa cell tumors reveals a consistent alteration in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway.

Šafanda A · Diagnostic pathology · 2025

Why it matters for JGCT

Small but deeply characterized ovarian JGCT cohort showing AKT1 ITD in 2/5 and PTEN loss in the AKT1-wild-type tumors, consistent with PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway activation; also gives a broad negative and positive IHC panel useful for pathology.

Key findings

  • Of 10 cases submitted as JGCT, central expert review reclassified 4 (2 Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors, 2 sex cord-stromal tumors NOS) and 1 was a duplicate (primary of a recurrent case), leaving 5 primary JGCTs and 1 recurrence; ages 6-43 years (mean 19, median 16), FIGO IC in 4 and IIIB in 1.
  • Recurrence occurred in 3 patients (cases 1, 3, 4), all within the first year of diagnosis per the text; chemotherapy for recurrence was mostly platinum-based, with some paclitaxel; case 1 also received bevacizumab with limited response and died of disease at 21 months; case 3 was alive with disease at 79 months; case 4 had no evidence of disease at 44 months.
  • Immunohistochemistry on tissue microarrays: SF1, inhibin A, calretinin, FOXL2, ER, PR, AR, CKAE1/3 and CD99 were positive in 5/5; WT1 in 1/5; GATA3, napsin A, SATB2, MUC4, CAIX, OCT3/4, SALL4, TTF1 and p16 negative; p53 wild type in all; BRG1, INI1, ARID1A, DPC4 and mismatch repair proteins retained; HER2 negative and PD-L1 negative (TPS <1%); Ki-67 median 30 (range 4-47).
  • Targeted NGS (788-gene panel) found pathogenic or likely pathogenic mutations in 80% (4/5): AKT1 internal tandem duplication in 2/5 (40%), TERT promoter mutation in 1, EP400 in 1; none had the FOXL2 c.402C>G mutation or DICER1 mutations; tumor mutation burden was low (average 5 mutations/Mb); the case 3 recurrence had a mutational profile identical to its primary.
  • PTEN protein expression was lost in 3/5 (60%) of tumors, all AKT1 wild type, without PTEN mutation, CNV deletion or promoter methylation, suggesting other mechanisms such as miRNA interference or post-translational modification.
  • The two tumors with TERT and EP400 mutations both recurred; the AKT1-mutated tumors had lower Ki-67.
  • The authors conclude that JGCT pathogenesis may be driven by PI3K/AKT/mTOR activation (AKT1 ITD or PTEN loss), that this needs a larger cohort, and note that PTEN/mTOR-targeted strategies are under investigation; they note Bessiere et al. found AKT1 ITD in 60% of 16 JGCTs restricted to patients under 15, and Onder et al. found DICER1 mutations in 2/8 (cited, not independently read).

Presentation

  • abdominal pain in all four patients with available data
  • acute abdomen due to torsion (case 4)
  • no hormonal irregularities in any case with data
  • multicystic tumors 8-18 cm (median 12 cm)

Genetic and molecular findings

  • AKT1: internal tandem duplication (2/5 (40%))
    One of the two also had a likely pathogenic CHEK2 mutation (class 4); both AKT1-mutated tumors had lower Ki-67 than AKT1 wild-type tumors
  • TERT: promoter mutation c.-124C>T (1/5)
    Present in the primary tumor and its recurrence (case 3); AKT1 wild type
  • EP400: mutation (1/5)
    AKT1 wild type; chromatin remodeling gene
  • PTEN: loss of protein expression by IHC without mutation, deletion (CNV) or promoter hypermethylation (3/5 (60%) lost expression)
    All three were AKT1 wild type
  • FOXL2: c.402C>G p.Cys134Trp not detected (0/5)
    FOXL2 protein expressed in 5/5
  • DICER1: no somatic mutations (0/5)

Treatments reported

  • Hysterectomy plus bilateral adnexectomy, then chemotherapy and bevacizumab for recurrence (Case 1, age 43, FIGO IC, recurrence; n = 1)
    Outcome: Died of disease, follow-up 21 months
  • Unilateral adnexectomy (Case 2, age 6, FIGO IC; n = 1)
    Outcome: Follow-up not available
  • Unilateral adnexectomy with chemotherapy; recurrence treated by resection and chemotherapy (Case 3, age 16, FIGO IIIB, positive ascites; n = 1)
    Outcome: Alive with disease at 79 months
  • Unilateral adnexectomy; recurrence treated by resection and chemotherapy (Case 4, age 10, FIGO IC, acute abdomen from torsion; n = 1)
    Outcome: No evidence of disease at 44 months
  • Unilateral adnexectomy (Case 5, age 21, FIGO IC, no recurrence; n = 1)
    Outcome: No evidence of disease at 20 months

Conflicts with

  • Kanna E 2024: A Rare Case of Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor in a Premenarchal Female: Clinical Presentation, Diagnosis, and Management.

Notes

Only 5 cases; includes patients aged 21 and 43 years, so not purely juvenile age. The text says all recurrences occurred within the first year and that none had hormonal features, and that 'all cases with available data presented with abdominal pain' while case 4 is listed with acute abdomen; table lists case 2 clinical data as N/A. The abstract states TERT and EP400 mutated cases both recurred, but the full text assigns TERT to case 3 with recurrence while the location of the EP400 case is not given in the text (it is in Figure 1). The patient-level genotype-to-case mapping is in Fig. 1, which is not available as text.
