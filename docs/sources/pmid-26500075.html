Cytoreductive surgery (CRS) and hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) in pediatric ovarian tumors: a novel treatment approach.

Why it matters for JGCT

Describes one heavily pretreated recurrent JGCT patient treated with CRS and HIPEC (died at 12 months); evidence for HIPEC in JGCT is therefore a single unsuccessful case within a mixed series.

Key findings

  • Single-institution trial series of CRS plus HIPEC in 8 girls with ovarian primary tumors and multifocal peritoneal disease (from 101 pediatric CRS/HIPEC operations): 3 yolk sac/germ cell tumors, 1 Sertoli-Leydig, 1 PNET, 1 choriocarcinoma, 1 JGCT and 1 mucinous adenocarcinoma.
  • HIPEC used closed technique with cisplatin 100 mg/m2 for 90 minutes at 41 C after complete (CCR 0) or near-complete (CCR 1, residual <2.5 cm) cytoreduction; all patients had prior surgery and chemotherapy, one had abdominal radiation; patients with extra-abdominal disease were excluded.
  • The JGCT patient (patient 3, age 18) had a tumor that had recurred repeatedly over 8 years after BEP, unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, cisplatin/taxane, surgery, high-dose abdominal radiation and stem cell transplant; PCI 15, CCR 1 resection (peritonectomy, jejunal and right colon resection); she developed an enterocutaneous fistula and died of disease at 12 months.
  • Whole cohort: overall survival 64% and relapse-free survival 62%; 3 of 8 (37%) recurred and died (JGCT, Sertoli-Leydig/sarcomatoid, yolk sac), the other 5 are disease free 13 to 74 months after HIPEC (text states 2 to 6 years).
  • No perioperative deaths; complications were 2 wound infections, 1 urinary tract infection and 1 enterocutaneous fistula (in the JGCT patient); 6 of 8 (75%) had no complications.
  • The authors note that heavily pretreated patients offered HIPEC at the 3rd or 4th relapse did worse and all died, and conclude CRS plus HIPEC is safe and may be effective in pediatric-type ovarian tumors, but that the sample is too small for definitive conclusions.

Presentation

  • JGCT repeatedly recurred over 8 years
  • diffuse peritoneal disease, 'frozen abdomen'

Treatments reported

  • Cytoreductive surgery (peritonectomy, jejunal and right colon resection; CCR 1) plus HIPEC cisplatin 100 mg/m2 for 90 min at 41 C; previous BEP, USO, cisplatin/taxane, surgery, abdominal radiation, stem cell transplant (recurrent JGCT, multifocal peritoneal disease, PCI 15; n = 1)
    Outcome: Died of disease at 12 months; enterocutaneous fistula as complication

Notes

Abstract says age 4 to 18 years while the results say 3 to 18 (table lists a 3-year-old). Abstract says disease-free 2 to 6 years while the table shows 13-74 months. The introduction describes juvenile granulosa cell tumors as 'most commonly benign' (cited). The JGCT patient's table entry: age 18, previous BEP/USO/CDDP-taxane/surgery/radiation/SCT, PCI 15, CCR 1, DOD 12 mo. Journal, first author and DOI not stated in the fetched text.
