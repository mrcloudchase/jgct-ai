Why it matters for JGCT

Source for the testicular JGCT: its congenital presentation, imaging features and surgical approach in the context of all pediatric testicular tumours. No outcome data and no patient series.

Key findings

  • Narrative imaging/management review of pediatric testicular tumours drawing on histologically confirmed cases from the authors' hospital; JGCT is covered in short passages and one illustrative case (Fig. 15), not a series.
  • JGCT is described as the most frequent congenital testicular tumour and the most frequent testicular tumour detectable on prenatal ultrasound; the left testis is most often affected and up to 20% have ambiguous external genitalia.
  • Sex cord-stromal tumours (Sertoli, Leydig, juvenile granulosa cell) account for about 10% of pediatric testicular neoplasms; the 2016 WHO classification lists granulosa cell tumour among the sex cord-stromal tumours.
  • Pathology: reticular appearance with follicle-like structures filled with mucoid material; the illustrated case showed many layers of granulosa cells with oval nuclei and abundant cytoplasm in a multilocular mass.
  • Ultrasound: well-circumscribed, non-invasive multilocular cystic mass with thick septations, or less often a solid mass with intralesional cysts; color Doppler shows hypervascularity of solid components and septations. MRI: hypointense on T1, hyperintense on T2, with wall and septal enhancement.
  • Illustrative case: 16-day-old boy with a left scrotal mass since birth, multiseptated cystic intratesticular mass with hydrocele on US, treated by radical inguinal orchiectomy.
  • Management statement: JGCT is considered a borderline tumour; radical inguinal orchiectomy is normally performed, though tumour enucleation could be sufficient when healthy testicular tissue is present.
  • Testicular tumours overall are 2-4% of childhood cancers; AFP physiologically high under 12 months must be considered when interpreting markers; percutaneous biopsy is generally avoided because of lymphatic seeding risk.

Presentation

  • swollen scrotal mass present since birth (illustrative case)
  • can be detected on prenatal ultrasound
  • up to 20% have ambiguous external genitalia
