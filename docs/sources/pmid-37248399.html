Reclassification of two germline DICER1 splicing variants leads to DICER1 syndrome diagnosis.

Why it matters for JGCT

Illustrates that a childhood ovarian tumor labelled JGCT (age 5, torsion) was reclassified as retiform SLCT in the context of DICER1 syndrome; relevant to differential diagnosis and to considering DICER1 testing. No confirmed JGCT.

Key findings

  • Report of two unrelated teenage girls (13 and 15 years) with thyroid follicular nodular disease and ovarian tumors; both had DICER1 somatic RNase IIIb hotspot mutations and no coding germline DICER1 pathogenic variant, but germline intronic splice variants (c.5365-4A>G, c.5527+3A>G) that skip exon 25 on RNA assays and were classified likely pathogenic under ClinGen DICER1 rules.
  • Case 2 had an emergency operation for ovarian torsion at age 5; the right ovarian mass was diagnosed as juvenile granulosa cell tumor (inhibin positive, calretinin negative) and treated with oophorectomy and two cycles of MAKEI-protocol chemotherapy.
  • At age 15, expert gynecological pathology review of that tumor found lobulated architecture with slit-like spaces, bland epithelioid cells, focal inhibin positivity and no heterologous elements, and reclassified it as retiform Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor.
  • The retiform SLCT carried the somatic DICER1 hotspot p.E1813D; the girl later developed intracerebral hemorrhages and a DICER1-mutant primary intracranial sarcoma (somatic NF1 and TP53 variants) and died at 16.
  • Case 1 had a poorly differentiated SLCT (7.5 cm, hyperandrogenism, inhibin focally positive) treated with surgery and 4 cycles of bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin and remained in remission.
  • Authors conclude that expert histopathological review is essential in atypical presentations and that DICER1 testing should follow rare DICER1-associated tumors, noting that overlapping sex cord-stromal morphology complicates diagnosis, as in case 2's initial JGCT diagnosis.

Presentation

  • case 2: ovarian torsion at age 5 requiring emergency surgery

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: germline c.5527+3A>G (intron 25) causing exon 25 skipping; likely pathogenic (case 2 (and her mother))
    somatic RNase IIIb hotspot p.E1813D in the retiform SLCT (originally diagnosed JGCT)
  • DICER1: germline c.5365-4A>G (intron 24); likely pathogenic (case 1 (poorly differentiated SLCT))
    somatic hotspots p.D1709N (thyroid) and p.E1705K (SLCT)

Treatments reported

  • Oophorectomy plus 2 cycles MAKEI-protocol chemotherapy (case 2 ovarian tumor at age 5 (initially labelled JGCT); n = 1)
    Outcome: later primary intracranial sarcoma (DICER1-mutant) died age 16; ovarian tumor course after surgery not otherwise described
  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy, lymph node dissection, omentectomy, 4 cycles PEB (case 1 poorly differentiated SLCT; n = 1)
    Outcome: remission, no recurrence on pelvic MRI

Notes

JGCT patient count set to 0 because the only JGCT diagnosis was revised by the authors' pathology review. No tumor from this paper was confirmed as JGCT.
