Biallelic MAD2L1BP (p31comet) mutation is associated with mosaic aneuploidy and juvenile granulosa cell tumors.

Abdel-Salam GMH · JCI insight · 2023

Why it matters for JGCT

Describes the first germline genetic cause (biallelic MAD2L1BP loss of function with mosaic variegated aneuploidy) of familial JGCT affecting both ovary and testis; sporadic JGCT lacked MAD2L1BP mutations.

Key findings

  • Two siblings of consanguineous Egyptian parents carried a homozygous MAD2L1BP nonsense variant R253* (c.757C>T); the sister (patient 1a) had a left ovarian JGCT treated by surgery at 14 months and the brother (patient 1b) had a left testicular JGCT treated by radical orchiectomy at 11 months.
  • Patient 1a received tamoxifen for 12 months after surgery and has been in complete remission since; she also had microcephaly, brain cysts, dysgyria/polymicrogyria, IQ 56 and generalized tonic-clonic seizures from age 5.5 years. Patient 1b had microcephaly with less pronounced brain abnormalities and normal psychomotor development.
  • Testicular JGCT of patient 1b: macrofollicular pattern with cysts, progesterone receptor positive, diffuse S100, faint estrogen receptor, patchy inhibin. The ovarian JGCT of patient 1a showed macrofollicular architecture on H&E with reticulin and PAS staining typical for JGCT.
  • Cultured lymphocytes and fibroblasts of both siblings showed high-grade mosaic variegated aneuploidy; parents and healthy sisters did not. A third patient (family 2, Saudi Arabia) with homozygous R227* had 10% aneuploid cells (200 metaphases reanalyzed), severe epileptic encephalopathy, elevated blood glucose and died at 23 months; he had no tumor.
  • Tumor WES of both JGCTs (FFPE, variants filtered to allele frequency >=5%) showed low tumor mutational burden, microsatellite stability, no mutational signature indicating a DNA repair defect, and no oncogenic alteration in patient 1b; patient 1a had only a very-low-frequency NF1 splice-region variant.
  • No MAD2L1BP mutations were found in 23 unrelated patients with sporadic JGCT (22 ovarian, 1 testicular; age at diagnosis 1 month to 22 years; no familial JGCT).
  • Functionally, R253* leads to partial nonsense-mediated decay and a truncated p31comet with half-life of about 15 minutes (reduced by more than 80% in fibroblasts); truncated p31comet could not bind Trip13, was defective in Mad2 and Rev7 binding, could not support spindle assembly checkpoint silencing or homology-directed repair, and lost its interaction with tp53. Patient fibroblasts proliferated slower and were more resistant to paclitaxel and slightly to etoposide.
  • In p31comet immunostaining, patient 1b's testicular JGCT showed faint nuclear expression in <5% of tumor cells versus about 50% in a reference testicular JGCT.
  • The authors conclude that biallelic germline MAD2L1BP mutations predispose to the (usually sporadic) infantile JGCT in both sexes, probably through high-grade aneuploidy, possibly with the impaired p31comet-tp53 interaction; they note no tumor history in heterozygous carriers' families and no increased mutational rate.

Presentation

  • JGCT of ovary (sister) and testis (brother) in infancy
  • microcephaly
  • brain malformations (cysts, polymicrogyria, thin corpus callosum)
  • seizures
  • developmental delay
  • nystagmus and retinal pigmentation abnormalities (family 1)

Genetic and molecular findings

  • MAD2L1BP: homozygous nonsense NM_014628.3:c.757C>T p.(Arg253*) (R253*) (2/2 siblings with JGCT (family 1))
    Germline; consanguineous Egyptian parents (first cousins); gnomAD allele frequency 3/248,598, no homozygotes; hypomorphic with partial NMD and residual unstable truncated protein
  • MAD2L1BP: homozygous nonsense c.679C>T p.(Arg227*) (R227*) (1 patient (family 2), no tumor)
    Boy with microcephaly, epileptic encephalopathy, low-level aneuploidy (10% of 200 metaphases), died at 23 months
  • MAD2L1BP: no mutation found in sporadic JGCT (0/23 unrelated sporadic JGCT patients)
    WES also screened for FOXL2 c.402C>G and DICER1 variants in these 23
  • NF1: oncogenic splice-region variant at very low allele frequency in ovarian JGCT of patient 1a (1/2 tumors)
    Considered possibly artifact-prone; a suggested low-level NF1 deletion was not confirmed by b allele frequencies and was considered a likely artifact; no oncogenic alterations in patient 1b tumor

Treatments reported

  • Surgery for left ovarian JGCT then tamoxifen for 12 months (patient 1a, age 14 months; n = 1)
    Outcome: complete remission since then
  • Radical left orchiectomy for testicular JGCT (patient 1b, age 11 months; n = 1)
    Outcome: no recurrence information beyond diagnosis stated in the text read
