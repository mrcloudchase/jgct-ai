Successful pregnancy outcome with a large ruptured juvenile granulosa cell tumor: A case report

Why it matters for JGCT

Adult-age JGCT in late pregnancy presenting with torsion and rupture (stage IC2) treated with surgery and platinum-taxane chemotherapy; short follow-up.

Key findings

  • 19-year-old primigravida at 36 weeks with abdominal pain, headaches and reduced fetal movements; ultrasound showed a 15.7 x 15 cm cyst with a solid hypervascular mass and maternal ascites; fetal biophysical profile 4/8.
  • Emergency laparotomy found a massive torsed right ovary with a ruptured mass and edematous, necrosed fallopian tubes (abstract also refers to a left-sided mass on ultrasound).
  • After cesarean delivery of the fetus, right salpingo-oophorectomy with cystectomy was performed; histology confirmed JGCT, FIGO stage IC2.
  • Postoperative chemotherapy: six sessions of cisplatin and paclitaxel; post-chemotherapy MRI showed normal abdominal viscera with minimal free fluid in the posterior cul de sac and a normal left ovary; inhibin B dropped to 3.17 (units not stated).
  • Planned follow-up for 5 years with tumor markers and CT; authors conclude aggressive treatment is necessary for advanced-stage tumors like this one to prevent recurrence or death.

Presentation

  • 2 days of generalized abdominal pain, headaches, reduced fetal movements at 36 weeks
  • left-sided 15.7 x 15 cm cyst and solid vascular mass with maternal ascites
  • fetal biophysical profile 4/8
  • torsed ruptured right ovarian mass at laparotomy

Treatments reported

  • Emergency laparotomy and cesarean delivery, right salpingo-oophorectomy with cystectomy, then 6 cycles cisplatin and paclitaxel (FIGO stage IC2, 36 weeks of pregnancy; n = 1)
    Outcome: post-chemotherapy MRI normal viscera, minimal free fluid in cul de sac, normal left ovary; inhibin B fell to 3.17; follow-up planned for 5 years
