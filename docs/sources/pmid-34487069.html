Why it matters for JGCT

Well-documented infant JGCT with isosexual pseudo-puberty and a high inhibin/AMH profile that normalized after surgery, plus a short tabulated review of infant JGCT cases.

Key findings

  • A 9-month-old girl presented with vaginal bleeding; breast development and fine genital hair had been noticed 2 months earlier; Tanner stage 2 breasts, no palpable abdominal mass.
  • Estradiol 1,091 pmol/L (reference 0-132), AMH >1714 pmol/L (0.1-38.6), inhibin A 385 pg/mL (<4.7), inhibin B 3805 pg/mL (<111), androstenedione 6.8 nmol/L (high); FSH and LH <0.3 IU/L (suppressed); AFP 6 kIU/L, beta-hCG, DHEAS, testosterone normal.
  • Ultrasound showed a 5.4 x 4.0 x 5.4 cm solid heterogeneous left adnexal mass with enlarged uterus (5.2 x 2.4 x 2.3 cm); MRI confirmed a 5.4 x 4.12 x 5.39 cm enhancing solid lesion with minimal central necrosis; chest CT normal; bone age advanced by 1.8 years.
  • Surgery: left total salpingo-oophorectomy with biopsies of peritoneum, round ligament and omentum; encapsulated yellow solid tumor 6.5 x 4 x 6.5 cm, 75 g, no hemorrhage or necrosis; histology JGCT FIGO IA, calretinin and inhibin A positive.
  • At 1 week and 1 month postoperatively estradiol (62, <55 pmol/L) and AMH (9 pmol/L) were normal; no evidence of recurrence at 12 months; no chemotherapy or radiotherapy needed.
  • Literature update in infants under 12 months (table of 10 cases, mostly case reports): presentations were premature thelarche/adrenarche or signs of puberty, abdominal mass or distension, acute abdomen from rupture, ascites; hormonal data often not reported. The authors cite a 1997 review by Bouffet et al. of 36 JGCT cases under 2 years, 21 under 1 year.
  • The authors, citing other sources, state that stage is the most important prognostic factor (stage IA survival 83-98%, overall 78-92%), recurrences can occur up to 3 years after surgery, inhibin B and AMH are useful monitoring markers, and unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy appears curative.

Presentation

  • vaginal bleeding at 9 months
  • bilateral breast development (Tanner 2) from about 7 months
  • fine pubic hair
  • advanced bone age
  • no palpable mass

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy, left total salpingo-oophorectomy; no chemotherapy or radiotherapy (FIGO IA; n = 1)
    Outcome: estradiol, AMH normalized within 1 week; breast development regressed within 6 months; no recurrence at 12 months

Notes

Bone age is reported as +1.8 years (+4 SD) in the text but +3.4 SD in the Figure 2 legend and in the Discussion. Table 3 is titled 'from 1998 to 2021' but lists articles from 1992 and 1997. Several statements in the Discussion (incidence 0.4-1.7/100,000/year, survival ranges) are citations of other works. The infant cases in Table 3 may overlap with other single case reports in the knowledge base.
