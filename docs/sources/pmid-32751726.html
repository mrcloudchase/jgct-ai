Ovarian Gynandroblastoma with a Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor Component in a Postmenopausal Woman.

Why it matters for JGCT

Shows a JGCT component within a gynandroblastoma in an elderly woman, extending the age range and highlighting the JGCT/Sertoli-Leydig morphologic overlap.

Key findings

  • 65-year-old woman (15 years postmenopausal) with an abdominal mass; MRI showed a 20 cm multiseptated cystic left ovarian mass without peritoneal seeding, lymphadenopathy or hematogenous metastasis; CA 125 37.1 U/mL, CA 19-9 32.1 U/mL (normal).
  • Treated as presumed ovarian cancer with total hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy; gross: smooth glistening surface, yellow-tan solid areas with necrosis, myxoid change and hemorrhage plus multilocular cysts with serosanguinous fluid.
  • Histology: ovarian gynandroblastoma with a juvenile granulosa cell tumor component (nodular growth, follicle-like spaces with eosinophilic/basophilic secretions, no nuclear grooves, up to 8 mitoses per 10 HPF, foci of coagulative necrosis) and a moderately differentiated Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor component (cords, solid tubules, nests, Sertoli tubules).
  • IHC: inhibin-alpha positive in both components, Ki-67 about 70% in the JGCT component; negative for EMA, pan-cytokeratin and PAX8.
  • No postoperative therapy; no recurrence at 2 months. Authors state this is the second reported gynandroblastoma with JGCT in a postmenopausal woman and that most gynandroblastomas have adult-type granulosa component and occur in children, adolescents or reproductive-age women.

Presentation

  • abdominal mass
  • 20 cm multiseptated cystic left ovarian mass
  • CA 125 37.1 U/mL (slightly elevated)
  • postmenopausal for 15 years

Treatments reported

  • Total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy; no adjuvant chemotherapy or radiation (gynandroblastoma with JGCT component; n = 1)
    Outcome: well without recurrence or metastasis at 2 months after surgery

Notes

Stage not stated in the text read. Only 2 months of follow-up. No molecular (e.g., DICER1) testing reported. Full text consists of abstract, figure legends and back matter (case narrative is in the abstract and legends).
