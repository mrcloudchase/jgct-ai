Why it matters for JGCT

Classic JGCT presentation in a toddler: estrogen excess with precocious pseudopuberty, large ovarian mass, stage I-type surgery with hormonal normalization.

Key findings

  • Indonesian-language case report of a 16-month-old girl with isosexual peripheral precocious puberty: vaginal bleeding from 1 year, breast enlargement from 7 months, sparse pubic hair from 15 months (Tanner M3P2), a palpable immobile pelvic mass.
  • Estradiol 2230 pg/mL with LH under 0.1 and FSH 0.14; AFP 3.63 ng/mL, CEA under 5.3, beta-hCG 0.10 mIU/mL, CA-125 87.8 U/mL, LDH 620 U/L; karyotype 46,XX; chest X-ray showed thymic hyperplasia.
  • Ultrasound showed a solid pelvic mass and thickened endometrium; CT showed a hypodense heterogeneous intraperitoneal mass 11 x 7.4 x 11.3 cm and enlarged uterus; the left ovarian tumor measured 13 x 10 x 6 cm at surgery.
  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy by laparotomy (5 October); H&E suggested sex cord-stromal tumor (granulosa cell versus Sertoli-Leydig), and IHC with CK7, EMA, CD99 and calretinin led to a diagnosis of juvenile granulosa cell tumor (individual stain results are not given).
  • Bleeding stopped 6 days after surgery; estradiol after surgery was 15.26, 24.47 and 5.30 pg/mL on three later dates; no residual tumor on ultrasound at 14 October; uterus still consistent with precocious puberty; secondary sexual characteristics were still monitored monthly; no chemotherapy is reported.
  • The authors state, citing others, that JGCT is mostly confined to the ovary and clinically benign despite malignant-appearing histology, that recurrence is rare and usually within the first year, and that inhibin positivity is definitive.

Treatments reported

    Outcome: Menstrual-type bleeding stopped 6 days after surgery; estradiol fell from 2230 to 15.26 pg/mL; no residual intra-abdominal tumor on ultrasound

Notes
