Why it matters for JGCT

Radiology teaching case of a prepubertal JGCT with imaging features and background statements; no outcome data.

Key findings

  • A 3-year-old girl presented to the emergency department with an abdominal mass, symptoms of bowel obstruction, and precocious puberty.
  • Abdominal radiograph raised suspicion of intussusception; ultrasound showed a large complex low anterior abdominal mass (10 x 6.6 x 9.3 cm) abutting the uterine fundus, with an endometrial stripe of about 7 mm.
  • Serum estradiol was 'dramatically elevated' (no value given).
  • CT showed a well-circumscribed, heterogeneous, enhancing mass with cystic and solid components, no calcifications, free abdominal/pelvic fluid, no lymphadenopathy, and prominent subareolar breast tissue.
  • Treatment was laparotomy with tumor resection and unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy; diagnosis was juvenile granulosa cell tumor of the ovary. No histology details, stage or follow-up are given.
  • Discussion (citing Young et al. 1984): of 125 JGCT patients, 78% presented within the first 2 decades; hyperestrogenism (precocious puberty) is typical; most patients are stage I with favorable prognosis, stage II-IV have higher recurrence; the role of chemotherapy/radiation is uncertain; long-term follow-up recommended.
