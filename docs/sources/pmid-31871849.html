Why it matters for JGCT

Documents prenatal ultrasound detection of testicular JGCT with benign long-term outcome after orchiectomy.

Key findings

  • Left testicular multilocular cystic mass (2 x 2 cm) seen on fetal ultrasound at 38 weeks (not seen on earlier routine scans), suspected JGCT; painless enlarged left scrotal mass at birth in a healthy newborn.
  • Postnatal ultrasound excluded torsion and showed multicystic mass without normal parenchyma; AFP and beta-hCG normal for age, karyotype normal, abdominal ultrasound normal.
  • Inguinal approach with left funiculo-orchiectomy because organ-sparing excision was not possible; specimen 2 x 1.5 cm well-circumscribed white mass with thin-walled cysts; tumor did not extend into spermatic cord, epididymis or tunica vaginalis; small rim of residual testis only.
  • Histology: follicle-like structures with basophilic mucicarmine-positive fluid, scant pale cytoplasm, no nuclear grooves, low mitotic activity; IHC positive for alpha-inhibin, CD99, calretinin, focal cytokeratin; negative for FLAP, AFP, CD30, vimentin, beta-hCG; Ki67 1%.
  • Seven-year follow-up uneventful; authors cite this as only the third case suspected prenatally (earlier cases at 36 and 38 weeks) and that testicular JGCT has had a benign outcome in all reported cases; they conclude inguinal orchiectomy is the safest oncological approach when the tumor replaces the testis, although testis-sparing and trans-scrotal surgery have been reported.
  • From literature in the paper: JGCT is the most common testicular neoplasm in first 6 months of life and accounts for 1.2% of prepubertal testis tumors in the Prepubertal Testis Tumor Registry.

Presentation

  • left multilocular cystic testicular mass on fetal ultrasound at 38 weeks
  • painless left scrotal mass at birth

Treatments reported

  • Inguinal funiculo-orchiectomy (left) (neonate, tumor replacing all testicular parenchyma; n = 1)
    Outcome: discharged 2 days after surgery; uneventful 7-year follow-up

Notes

Single case; registry statistics are from cited sources.
