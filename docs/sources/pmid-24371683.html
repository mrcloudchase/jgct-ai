Hypercalcemia in a child with juvenile granulosa cell tumor of ovary: Report of an unusual paraneoplastic syndrome and review of the literature.

Why it matters for JGCT

Documents paraneoplastic hypercalcemia as a rare presentation of JGCT, with a differential diagnosis against small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcemic type, and a note that the patient had stage IA disease.

Key findings

  • A 15-year-old girl (menarche at 14) presented with 2 days of abdominal pain, vomiting, fever and a painful right lower quadrant mass; appendicitis was initially suspected.
  • Corrected serum calcium was 14.9 mg/dL (3.70 mmol/L), phosphorus 0.50 mmol/L, intact PTH 10 pg/mL (normal 10-65), CA-125 204 U/mL, CRP 259 mg/L, LDH 348 U/L; AFP and beta-hCG normal; serum inhibin was never checked; PTHrP was not assayed.
  • Ultrasound and CT showed a semisolid right ovarian mass (125 x 105 x 65 mm) with pelvic ascites; bone scan showed no bone metastasis.
  • Hypercalcemia was treated with saline hydration, pamidronate 1 mg/kg and furosemide; right salpingo-oophorectomy, omentectomy and ascites drainage followed on day 6 and calcium remained normal.
  • Tumor 120 x 90 x 65 mm, intact capsule; histology solid nodular proliferation with pseudofollicles, necrosis, luteinization, 7 mitoses per 10 HPF; positive for alpha-inhibin, calretinin, estrogen and progesterone receptors, CD56, SMA, focal KL1; negative for TTF1, synaptophysin, chromogranin, S100, EMA and CD99; diagnosis JGCT, FIGO IA, excluding small cell and clear cell carcinoma.
  • No chemotherapy was proposed given stage IA; calcium checked every 3-4 months was normal and ultrasound at 24 months showed no recurrence. The authors attribute the hypercalcemia to tumor PTHrP secretion because it did not recur after resection (not measured).
  • The authors report this as the third published JGCT with hypercalcemia (Daubenton, age 11, stage III, died of disease at 4 months after surgery and chemotherapy; Piura, age 25, stage IC, remission at 10 months after chemotherapy) and note FOXL2 expression prognostic data in JGCT are controversial (cited).

Presentation

  • abdominal pain and vomiting
  • fever 38 C
  • right lower quadrant mass
  • hypercalcemia (corrected calcium 14.9 mg/dL)
  • pelvic ascites
  • tachycardia and hypertension

Genetic and molecular findings

  • FOXL2: c.402C>G (p.C134W) not found (0/1)
    Authors state no FOXL2 mutation was found, confirming it was not adult GCT; testing method not described in the text

Treatments reported

  • Hypercalcemia treatment (saline, pamidronate 1 mg/kg, furosemide), then right salpingo-oophorectomy, omentectomy, ascites drainage; no chemotherapy (FIGO IA; n = 1)
    Outcome: calcium stayed normal; no recurrence and normal ultrasound at 24 months

Notes

PTHrP not measured, so mechanism is inferred. FOXL2 method not given. Table 1 abbreviation 'CT' means chemotherapy, not computed tomography. The review is limited to two previously published cases.
