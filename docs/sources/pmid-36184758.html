Why it matters for JGCT

Imaging consensus covering all pediatric ovarian tumors; JGCT-specific content is limited to presentation, marker (inhibin), MRI 'sponge-like' appearance, staging system and surveillance remarks.

Key findings

  • Consensus imaging recommendations from the COG Diagnostic Imaging Committee and SPR Oncology Committee for pediatric patients with known or suspected primary ovarian malignancy, at diagnosis and during follow-up.
  • Girls with sex cord stromal tumors tend to present with smaller tumors because of hormone production; isosexual precocious puberty is the example given for juvenile granulosa cell tumor.
  • Sex cord stromal and epithelial ovarian tumors are staged with FIGO, whereas ovarian germ cell tumors use the COG surgical staging (2004), so distinguishing tumor types before resection is important.
  • Table 1 lists JGCT as a sex cord stromal tumor with a 'sponge-like' imaging appearance and inhibin as the tumor marker; Sertoli-Leydig tumors are linked to DICER1 mutation.
  • Recommendations: transabdominal US first; MRI abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast for further evaluation (and for cystic lesions >7.5 cm, lesions not fully seen, or with solid components); CT acceptable if MRI not possible; chest CT if malignancy present; pelvic US or MRI surveillance in all girls with a resected ovarian tumor (GRADE C, SOR 1.40).
  • Surveillance statement: adult granulosa cell tumor can recur 10 years after treatment and needs long-term follow-up, though JGCTs 'may be less aggressive'; serum inhibin may be followed for recurrence; adult sex cord stromal data suggest recurrence is unlikely after three years.
  • No formal guidelines exist for post-treatment imaging surveillance in children and none for tumor response assessment specific to pediatric ovarian tumors.

Notes

Multi-tumor white paper dominated by germ cell tumors; JGCT is a minor topic. Statements on JGCT behavior are the authors' citations to other work.
