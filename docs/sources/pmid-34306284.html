Coincidence of juvenile granulosa cell tumor and serous cystadenoma in a pediatric patient: Case report and literature review.

Why it matters for JGCT

Cautions that a new contralateral cystic ovarian mass after JGCT resection may be a benign tumor rather than recurrence; documents imaging (sponge-like, hemorrhagic) of a childhood JGCT.

Key findings

  • 4-year-old girl with 6 weeks of breast enlargement, weight 22 kg and height 116 cm (both 85th-97th centile), a palpable left iliac fossa mass and serum estradiol 133.2 pg/mL.
  • Ultrasound showed a large multilobulated cystic left ovarian mass with septa of varying thickness in a sponge-like pattern, hypovascular walls and fluid-fluid levels; MRI without contrast showed a honeycomb-like multiseptated cystic mass up to about 101 mm with blood-fluid levels (T1 hyperintense hemorrhage), bladder compression and uterine deviation, and about 5 mm of Douglas pouch fluid.
  • Laparotomy: 10 x 5 x 4 cm left ovarian cystic mass with several ruptured cysts; pelvic lymph nodes and ascites negative for malignancy; left salpingo-oophorectomy; staged FIGO IC.
  • Histology: solid nests of cells with narrow cytoplasm and grooved round hyperchromatic nuclei, microcysts with eosinophilic secretions; IHC positive for calretinin, inhibin and Ki-67, consistent with JGCT.
  • Estradiol fell to normal after resection and she received adjuvant chemotherapy (agents not specified) with routine monitoring.
  • One year later a new multilobulated cystic right ovarian mass appeared with normal estradiol (<0.5 pg/mL); after 5 months of follow-up it was resected and was histologically serous cystadenoma (single layer of tall ciliated tubal-type cells), not JGCT recurrence.
  • Authors state that two different tumors in each ovary in a child is extremely rare, that benign thin-walled cystic features on US/MRI helped separate cystadenoma from recurrent GCT, and that chemotherapy is currently recommended only for advanced, recurrent or metastatic disease.

Presentation

  • breast enlargement for 6 weeks
  • palpable left iliac fossa mass
  • growth above age-expected height and weight (precocious puberty)
  • estradiol 133.2 pg/mL

Treatments reported

  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy; adjuvant chemotherapy (agents and cycles not stated) (FIGO stage IC (cyst rupture); n = 1)
    Outcome: Estradiol normalized after resection; new right ovarian cystic mass at 1 year proved benign serous cystadenoma after resection; no JGCT recurrence reported

Notes

Adjuvant chemotherapy was given for stage IC disease but regimen is unspecified. The Discussion states juvenile GCT has 'an increased risk of recurrence compared with adult GCT', which differs from other sources in this repository that describe late recurrence as characteristic of adult GCT; this is the authors' statement. Follow-up duration beyond about 1.5 years is not given.
