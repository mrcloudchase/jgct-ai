Why it matters for JGCT

Case of a very large non-secreting JGCT in a school-age child managed by unilateral adnexectomy alone.

Key findings

  • An 8-year-old girl without history had about 15 days of moderate paroxysmal pelvic pain, increasing abdominal volume and weight loss; examination found a hard, ill-defined, painless abdominopelvic mass of about 25 x 20 cm with collateral venous circulation and no signs of hypersecretion.
  • Ultrasound showed a very large cystic multiseptated abdominopelvic mass occupying almost the whole abdomen with small intraperitoneal effusion, no deep lymph nodes and no liver involvement; CT showed a multiseptated cystic left supravesical mass.
  • Alpha-fetoprotein and beta-hCG were normal; inhibin and estradiol are not reported for the patient.
  • Surgery found a 25 x 20 cm tumor of the left ovary with serohemorrhagic effusion; pathology with immunostaining supported juvenile granulosa cell tumor (strong vimentin positivity); FIGO stage is not stated.
  • Left adnexectomy was performed; a multidisciplinary meeting decided that neither chemotherapy nor radiotherapy was necessary; periodic clinical and radiological follow-up was unremarkable.
  • The authors state in the discussion (citing literature) that JGCT is about 5% of ovarian tumors in children and adolescents, 80% occur under 20 years and 50% under 10 years, unilateral in 97% with average size 12.5 cm, 5-year survival is 92%, adjuvant chemotherapy is reserved for recurrence, and poor prognostic factors are large size, ascites and capsular rupture.

Presentation

  • 15 days of paroxysmal pelvic pain
  • increasing abdominal volume
  • unquantified weight loss
  • painless hard abdominopelvic mass about 25 x 20 cm
  • collateral venous circulation
  • no signs of hormone hypersecretion
  • small intraperitoneal effusion

Treatments reported

  • Left adnexectomy (salpingo-oophorectomy); no chemotherapy or radiotherapy per multidisciplinary meeting (8-year-old girl with 25 x 20 cm tumor and serohemorrhagic effusion; n = 1)
    Outcome: uneventful postoperative course; clinical and radiological follow-up (chest X-ray, abdominal ultrasound) without particularities; duration not stated

Notes

Original text is in French and was read in French. Immunohistochemistry other than vimentin (e.g. inhibin) is not reported; FIGO stage, follow-up duration and histologic details (mitoses) are not given. The introduction calls JGCT 'malignant tumors' while also 'low malignant potential'. Review statements in the Discussion are citations from other sources.
