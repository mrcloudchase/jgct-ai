Why it matters for JGCT

Minimal: general paediatric urogenital tumour imaging review with a brief JGCT statement (most common paediatric sex cord stromal tumour; oestrogen-driven precocious puberty; Kiel registry mean age 7 years). No JGCT patient data of its own.

Key findings

  • Imaging-focused review of paediatric urogenital tumours (renal tumours, rhabdomyosarcoma, ovarian and testicular tumours); JGCT receives two sentences in the ovarian non-germ-cell section.
  • Sex cord stromal tumours are described as approximately 10% of ovarian malignancies in adolescent girls; the two main categories are granulosa theca cell and Sertoli-Leydig cell tumours.
  • In the first 2 decades granulosa theca cell tumours tend to be juvenile granulosa cell tumour (JGCT), the most common paediatric sex cord stromal tumour, which often presents early with oestrogen-led precocious puberty.
  • Of 70 patients in the Kiel Pediatric Tumour Registry (sex cord stromal tumours), mean age at diagnosis was 7 years for JGCT and 14 years for Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour (secondary citation).
  • The review's ovarian imaging and staging sections concern germ cell tumours (COG staging for ovarian GCT); it states non-germ-cell ovarian tumours cannot be reliably distinguished from GCTs on imaging.
  • The testicular section covers germ cell, Leydig cell and secondary tumours only; JGCT of the testis is not mentioned.

Presentation

  • oestrogen-led precocious puberty

Notes
