Why it matters for JGCT

Hospital pathology frequency study; JGCT provides only a count (2 cases, both under 20 years, classified malignant) within ovarian neoplasms of all ages. No clinical, molecular or outcome data.

Key findings

  • Retrospective single-centre study (King Abdulaziz University Hospital, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, 2016-2020) of 565 ovarian specimens: 357 (63.2%) neoplasms and 208 (36.8%) non-neoplastic; of neoplasms, 64.4% benign, 6.2% borderline, 29.4% malignant.
  • Sex cord/stromal tumors were 32 of 357 neoplasms (9.0%): fibroma 17, fibrothecoma 6, Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor 2, juvenile granulosa cell tumor 2 (6.3% of sex cord tumors), adult granulosa cell tumor 5.
  • Both juvenile granulosa cell tumors were classed as malignant and both occurred in the under-20 age group (Table 4: 2 of 105 malignant tumors, 1.9%).
  • Only four malignant tumors occurred under 20 years: two JGCT, one adult granulosa cell tumor and one immature cystic teratoma; no surface epithelial malignancies were seen under 20.
  • The authors note this agrees with previous studies supporting the hypothesis that malignancy is related to older age; the study does not report JGCT stage, treatment or outcome.

Presentation

  • both JGCT cases were in the under-20 age group (individual presentations not reported)

Notes

Internal data inconsistencies: the age-group counts and percentages in Results do not add up (e.g. '<20 (n=35, 46.8%)') and Table 1 gives 21 neoplasms under 20, while the text gives 565 specimens overall; Table 1 shows 4 sex cord tumors under 20. Specimens included referrals from other hospitals, so counts are not population incidence.
