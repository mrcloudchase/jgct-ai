Why it matters for JGCT

JGCT presenting with massive ascites and raised CA125 without endocrine symptoms in a 19-year-old; treated with platinum-taxane adjuvant therapy; only 6 months of follow-up.

Key findings

  • A 19-year-old woman presented with a week of painless progressive abdominal distention and shifting dullness; ascites was exudative with cytology negative for malignancy; serum and ascitic CA125 were markedly raised while AFP, CEA, CA19-9, CA15-3 and beta-hCG were normal and sex hormones were unremarkable.
  • Ultrasound and CT showed massive ascites, a 50 x 40 mm well-marginated left adnexal lesion read as teratoma, and an enlarged right ovary with a 50 x 50 mm solid-cystic lesion; no lymphadenopathy or infiltration.
  • At laparoscopic exploration about 1200 mL clear ascites was drained, the right tumor had a ruptured capsule with irregular cauliflower surface, and the omentum and peritoneum had several corn-like nodules; a left oophorectomy and right salpingo-oophorectomy with omental and peritoneal biopsies were performed.
  • Frozen section suggested sex cord-stromal tumor; histology showed nodules of ovoid and polygonal pale eosinophilic or clear cells and irregular follicles with pseudopapillary projections; immunohistochemistry positive for inhibin-alpha, CD99, vimentin and calretinin, negative for AE1/AE3, EMA, SMA, Melan-A and CD10; Ki-67 >5%.
  • The authors classified the case as FIGO stage IC on the basis of malignant ascites only; she received 6 courses of nedaplatin and liposomal paclitaxel and at 6 months CA-125 was normal, ascites resolved and no recurrence was seen.
  • The authors conclude that advanced-stage JGCT with massive ascites should be considered in young girls, CA125 and estradiol with imaging may alert to JGCT, but diagnosis depends on histology and immunohistochemistry; the discussion cites platinum-based chemotherapy response rates of 63% to 80% and states no evidence supports radiation or hormonal therapy.

Presentation

  • 1 week of progressive painless abdominal distention
  • massive ascites (about 1200 mL straw-colored)
  • markedly raised serum and ascitic CA125
  • no hormonal symptoms; regular menses since menarche at 12

Treatments reported

  • Left oophorectomy plus right salpingo-oophorectomy with omental and peritoneal biopsies (no lymph node dissection), then 6 courses nedaplatin + paclitaxel liposome (Stated FIGO stage IC with malignant ascites; ruptured right-sided tumor; n = 1)
    Outcome: CA-125 normalized, ascites resolved; no recurrence or metastasis at 6 months

Notes

Internal inconsistencies: the staging as IC is based on 'malignant ascites' although ascites cytology was negative and the omental/peritoneal biopsy results are not reported; the caption says nuclei lack grooves while the discussion mentions Call-Exner bodies and atypia; a left-sided lesion read as 'teratoma' on CT was removed but its histology is not described; the abstract's 'estradiol raised' conclusion conflicts with the text stating sex hormones were unremarkable. Follow-up only 6 months. The age of 19 is above the pediatric range.
