Why it matters for JGCT

Authoritative NCI summary of JGCT epidemiology, presentation, stage distribution and the stage and mitotic-rate-based indication for cisplatin-based chemotherapy.

Key findings

  • NCI PDQ summary (updated 03/03/2025, editorial changes only) on childhood ovarian cancer; most ovarian masses in children are not malignant; malignant types in order are germ cell, epithelial, stromal, other; most malignant ovarian tumors occur in girls aged 15 to 19 years.
  • Juvenile granulosa cell tumor (JGCT) is the most common sex cord-stromal tumor subtype in girls younger than 18 years (median age 7.6 years; range birth to 17.5 years); about 5% of ovarian tumors in children and adolescents; distinct from adult granulosa cell tumors.
  • Risk factors: JGCT has been reported in children with Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome (cited case reports).
  • Presentation: precocious puberty (most common, from estrogen secretion), abdominal pain, abdominal mass, ascites. Metastatic spread of sex cord-stromal tumors is rare, usually confined to the peritoneal cavity, and distant metastases mostly occur at relapse.
  • As many as 90% of children with JGCT have FIGO stage I disease and are usually curable with unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy alone; in one series 15 of 17 had fertility-sparing surgery and only 2 got adjuvant chemotherapy, with no recurrences.
  • Patients with spontaneous rupture or malignant ascites (FIGO IC2, IC3), advanced disease (stages II-IV) or high mitotic activity have poorer prognosis and require chemotherapy; cisplatin-based regimens have been used with some success in adjuvant and recurrent settings.
  • Prognostic factors in pediatric sex cord-stromal tumors (German MAKEI, 54 patients, 48 stage I, 6 peritoneal metastases): stage IC, preoperative rupture, stages II-III and high mitotic count identify patients at risk. French TGM-95 registry (38 girls): of 15 with rupture and/or ascites, 11 received chemotherapy without recurrence while all 4 who did not receive chemotherapy recurred and 2 died.
  • DICER1: the PDQ links Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors, not JGCT, to germline DICER1 variants (191 SLCT patients in registries; 58% of 156 tested had a germline P/LP variant; RNase IIIb hotspot variants in 97% (88/91) of intermediate/poorly differentiated tumors; 3-year RFS 93.6% stage IA vs 67.1% IC vs 60.6% stage II-IV).
  • Registries and consensus: US International Ovarian and Testicular Stromal Tumor Registry; European national rare tumor groups; EXPeRT/PARTNER consensus recommendations on diagnosis and therapy; work-up, staging and therapy harmonized between registries. Table of FIGO staging for ovarian carcinoma provided (IA to IVB).

Presentation

  • precocious puberty (most common; estrogen secretion)
  • abdominal pain
  • abdominal mass
  • ascites

Treatments reported

  • Unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy alone (fertility-sparing) (FIGO stage I JGCT (as many as 90% of children); n = 17)
    Outcome: One series: 15 of 17 had fertility-sparing surgery and only 2 received adjuvant chemotherapy; no recurrences reported
  • Cisplatin-based chemotherapy (stage IC2/IC3, II-IV, or high mitotic activity; adjuvant and recurrent disease)
    Outcome: Used 'with some success'; these patients are described as having poorer prognosis and requiring chemotherapy
