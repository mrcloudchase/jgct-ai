Surgical Management of Ovarian Masses in Children: A Comparative Analysis by Pediatric Surgeons and Gynecologists at Two Academic Hospitals in Johannesburg.

Why it matters for JGCT

Mixed paediatric ovarian-mass series from South Africa; JGCT-relevant content is limited to 10 granulosa cell tumours (adult and juvenile combined), all precocious puberty cases in the series, marker results and absence of deaths in this group.

Key findings

  • Retrospective 15-year review (2007-2022) of 288 children and adolescents (294 ovarian masses) surgically treated at two Johannesburg academic hospitals, 117 by paediatric surgery and 171 by gynecology; 51 (17.70%) were malignant.
  • Granulosa cell tumours (adult and juvenile not separated) numbered 10 of the 51 malignant tumours and 10 of 18 malignant sex cord-stromal tumours; 4 were managed by paediatric surgery and 6 by gynecology.
  • All 10 patients with precocious puberty in the series had sex cord-stromal tumours, specifically granulosa cell tumours.
  • Tumour markers in granulosa cell tumours: AFP positive 0/6, beta-hCG 0/3 and CA125 3/5 (60%).
  • Two gynecology patients (one juvenile, one adult granulosa cell tumour, FIGO IA) had minimally invasive surgery because they needed emergency exploration; all malignant tumours otherwise underwent oophorectomy, mostly open.
  • Overall 5-year survival in the malignant cohort was 44/51 (86.28%); the 7 deaths were four yolk sac tumours, advanced dysgerminoma, stage 4 Sertoli-Leydig tumour and mesothelioma, none a granulosa cell tumour.
  • Staging used COG in paediatric surgery (18 of 38 documented) and FIGO in gynecology (13/13); chemotherapy given to 21 of 38 paediatric surgery malignant patients was JEB or BEP, with no JGCT-specific regimen reported.
  • The authors conclude that laparoscopic, ovary-sparing surgery should be preferred for benign lesions and call for standardised guidelines between paediatric surgeons and gynecologists.

Presentation

  • precocious puberty in 10 patients, all with granulosa cell tumours (adult and juvenile combined)
  • granulosa cell tumour markers: AFP 0/6, beta-hCG 0/3, CA125 3/5 positive

Treatments reported

  • Oophorectomy (all malignant tumours); two granulosa cell tumour patients (one juvenile, one adult, FIGO IA) had minimally invasive surgery as emergency explorations (surgery for malignant ovarian tumours, whole cohort; n = 10)
    Outcome: Survival by granulosa cell tumour subgroup is not given; overall 5-year survival of the 51 malignant cases was 44/51 (86.28%) and none of the 7 deaths was attributed to a granulosa cell tumour

Notes

Journal not stated in the extracted text. Table 1 counts 10 precocious puberty cases all in the malignant group. Possible internal inconsistency: the abstract says laparoscopy in 57 patients with 24 in paediatric surgery and 19 in gynecology while the discussion says 33 in gynecology (Table 4 supports 33).
