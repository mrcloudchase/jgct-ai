Why it matters for JGCT

Imaging and clinical features of pediatric sex cord-stromal tumors (mainly JGCT per the authors) at one center: solid, non-calcified, AFP-normal, estrogenic presentation in a minority; no JGCT-specific outcomes.

Key findings

  • Single-center retrospective study (Children's Hospital of Zhejiang University, 2020-2024) of 49 girls with malignant ovarian tumors: germ cell tumors 37 (75.5%), sex cord-stromal tumors 8 (16.3%), lymphoma 4 (8.2%).
  • The authors state the sex cord-stromal tumors were mainly juvenile granulosa cell tumors; the exact JGCT count is not given.
  • Sex cord-stromal tumors (n=8): mean maximum diameter 9.60 cm; 5/8 were 10 cm or smaller; solid or predominantly solid in 4/8; no calcification; O-RADS 4 in 4 and O-RADS 5 in 4; AFP median 1.11 ng/mL (IQR 0.71-1.62), far below germ cell tumors.
  • A representative case: a 12-year-old girl with a 9.4 x 7.4 x 6.4 cm solid right adnexal mass, minimal flow, O-RADS 5, twisted 540 degrees at surgery, with juvenile granulosa cell tumor on pathology.
  • Torsion occurred in 1/8 sex cord-stromal tumors; rupture in none.
  • Authors attribute precocious puberty and menstrual irregularity to estrogen secretion; no treatment or outcome data were collected (authors note lack of long-term prognostic data).

Presentation

  • Sex cord-stromal tumors: precocious puberty 3/8 (37.5%), menstrual irregularity 1/8 (12.5%), abdominal pain/distension 2/8

Notes

JGCT count not separated from the 8 sex cord-stromal tumors. The pathologic subtype of all 8 is not tabulated.
