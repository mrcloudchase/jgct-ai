Tracing metastatic spread in pediatric solid tumors using copy number and targeted deep sequencing.

Why it matters for JGCT

Single metastatic JGCT case with multiregional genomics (AKT1 gain, MYC amplification, intermetastatic spread); other tumors in the cohort are not JGCT.

Key findings

  • Swedish single-center study (diagnosed 2000-2020) of whole-genome copy number profiling (OncoScan SNP array) on 171 samples from 17 children with neuroblastoma (8), Wilms tumor (7) or gonadal tumors (2), with targeted deep sequencing (300-gene panel) of 64 samples from 8 patients; only one patient (GT1) had juvenile granulosa cell tumor.
  • GT1: a JGCT with relapse and widespread metastasis despite the usually favorable prognosis; 19 samples from primary and metastatic sites (including liver and several peritoneal sites) were analysed.
  • Targeted sequencing of GT1 found no truncal somatic point mutations; only RBM10 and AR mutations in two different metastases; copy number analysis showed a complex landscape with 135 unique CNAs.
  • GT1 showed a segmental 14q gain including AKT1 in the stem, which the authors say confirms the reported AKT1 duplication/mutation in JGCT, plus a subclonal MYC amplification (8q24) and an amplified 2q31 region with no known oncogene.
  • GT1 showed later, polyphyletic and polyclonal metastatic spread at several time points after local relapse and intermetastatic spread from liver metastases to two peritoneal sites; the authors say this is the first demonstration of intermetastatic spread in these tumors.
  • Many small gains (<5 Mbp) were present in all GT1 samples and were enriched for serine-type endopeptidase inhibitor genes (18 genes) with 233 genes in lost segments linked to serine metabolism, which the authors suggest as a possible driver of aggressiveness.
  • Across the cohort, 11 of 17 patients showed earlier metastatic spread, and intermetastatic spread occurred in 8 of 9 patients with metastases at two or more sites, including GT1; distant metastases had lower genomic diversity than primary tumors.

Presentation

  • relapsed and widely metastatic JGCT (atypical for the tumor type)

Genetic and molecular findings

  • AKT1: segmental gain of 14q including AKT1 (stem/truncal) (1/1)
    authors cite prior work that all JGCT have AKT1 duplication or activating mutation
  • MYC: amplification at 8q24 (subclonal, >8 copies) (1/1)
    plus an amplified 2q31 region without a known oncogene
  • RBM10: exonic nonsynonymous point mutation in one metastasis (1/1)
    no truncal somatic mutations found by targeted deep sequencing
  • AR: exonic nonsynonymous point mutation in one metastasis (a different peritoneal metastasis) (1/1)
    two point mutations in total, in two different metastases

Notes

Only one JGCT (GT1); the second gonadal tumor (GT3) is an intracranial germ cell tumor. Age, sex, primary site and treatment of GT1 are given only in a figure and supplementary files, not in the main text, so they are not recorded. Genomic findings are from one patient and cannot be generalised.
