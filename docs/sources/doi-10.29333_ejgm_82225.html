Why it matters for JGCT

Toddler JGCT presenting with pseudo-Meigs syndrome, high CA-125 and estradiol that normalized after surgery.

Key findings

  • A 2-year-old girl presented with one month of breast enlargement (Tanner III), abdominal distension and a 15 cm mobile mass, with ascites and right pleural effusion (pseudo-Meigs syndrome).
  • CA-125 was 366 U/mL (normal 0-35) and estradiol 602.5 pg/mL (normal 6.0-27).
  • Ultrasound/MRI showed a 12 x 7.5 cm (US) to 13 x 10 x 8 cm (MRI) cystic-solid mass with peripheral enhancement and necrosis, thought to arise from the left ovary.
  • At surgery a 15 cm capsulated left ovarian tumor adherent to the left uterine cornu and omentum was removed with the cornu and tube; pathology size 17 x 16 x 15 cm; solid islets and follicle-like structures, no nuclear grooves, notable mitoses, occasional theca cells.
  • At 1 month CA-125 fell to 12 U/mL, estradiol to 30 pg/mL, and the hydrothorax had disappeared; follow-up showed no problems (duration not specified).
  • Authors state this was the youngest reported JGCT with pseudo-Meigs syndrome and that thoracic intervention is unnecessary unless respiratory distress is present.

Presentation

  • breast enlargement for one month (Tanner III thelarche)
  • abdominal distension with 15 cm mobile mass
  • ascites
  • right pleural effusion (pseudo-Meigs syndrome)
  • right inguinal hernia
  • no respiratory problems

Treatments reported

  • Excision of 15 cm capsulated left ovarian mass with left uterine cornu and fallopian tube; ascites aspirated; hernia repair (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: Hydrothorax resolved at 1 month; CA-125 12 U/mL, estradiol 30 pg/mL at 1 month; follow-up without problems

Notes

Originally in English with Turkish abstract. No IHC or formal stage reported. Claim of youngest case is the authors' own.
