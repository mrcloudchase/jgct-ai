Ovarian Gynandroblastoma with a Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor Component in a Postmenopausal Woman: A Case Report and Literature Review.

Why it matters for JGCT

Rare gynandroblastoma with JGCT component in a 71-year-old, illustrating JGCT-like histology well outside the usual age range; not a pure JGCT.

Key findings

  • 71-year-old postmenopausal woman (G6P4, menopause at 53) with acute right lower quadrant abdominal pain; CT and ultrasound showed a large multiloculated cystic mass of about 13 cm in the right ovary; no endocrine symptoms; CA 19-9 19.48 U/mL and CA125 7.45 U/mL were normal.
  • Preoperative diagnosis was torsion of the right ovarian tumor; total abdominal hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy were performed.
  • Gross: 13.0 x 7.5 x 6.5 cm multilocular cyst with serosanguinous fluid and a 3.7 x 1.8 cm solid yellowish mass; uterus, tubes and left ovary unremarkable.
  • Histology: tumor was 40% juvenile granulosa cell tumor (nodular growth, follicles with mucin-like basophilic secretions, round to oval nuclei with open chromatin, no striking atypia, no Call-Exner bodies) and 60% well-differentiated Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor, diagnosed as gynandroblastoma.
  • Both components were positive for calretinin and inhibin and negative for EMA. The tumor was stage I without extra-ovarian involvement; no recurrence during 2 years of follow-up.
  • Authors report this as the first gynandroblastoma with a JGCT component in an elderly patient; they cite 6 of 28 reported gynandroblastomas in English literature having a JGCT component, all in premenopausal women aged 15 to 39 years.
  • Literature statements by the authors: about 97% of JGCTs occur in the first three decades; about 30% of JGCTs reported to harbor the gsp oncogene and 60% an AKT1 mutation; somatic DICER1 hot-spot mutations in about 60% of Sertoli-Leydig tumors; FOXL2 C134W is typical of adult GCT. No molecular testing was done on this case.

Presentation

  • acute right lower quadrant / right upper quadrant abdominal pain (abstract says right upper quadrant)
  • 13 cm multilocular cystic right ovarian mass
  • no estrogenic or androgenic symptoms

Treatments reported

  • Total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy; no adjuvant therapy reported (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: No recurrence or metastasis at 2 years of follow-up (text also says 26 months)

Notes

Not a pure JGCT: mixed gynandroblastoma (40% JGCT, 60% Sertoli-Leydig). Internal inconsistencies: abstract says right upper quadrant pain while case text says right lower quadrant; follow-up is stated as two years and as 26 months. Figure legend for Fig 3 describes nuclei as lacking grooves 'characteristic of adult GCT' (wording appears reversed). The molecular statements (gsp 30%, AKT1 60%) are the authors' citations, not their own data.
