Why it matters for JGCT

Current European consensus standard for staging, surgery, risk-based adjuvant chemotherapy (cisplatin-based, from IC2), genetic testing and follow-up of pediatric ovarian JGCT.

Key findings

  • European Standard Clinical Practice guideline from EXPeRT/ERN PaedCan for ovarian sex cord stromal tumors (OSCST) in children and adolescents, based on national and one European cohort analyses, a systematic literature review and expert consensus; evidence mostly level III (prospective cohorts) with grades A-E; no high-level (I-II) evidence exists.
  • Diagnostic work-up: history (puberty, Ollier or Peutz-Jeghers, DICER1-spectrum family history), AFP, beta-hCG/total HCG to exclude germ cell tumor (note approximately 10% of SLCT show AFP up to 1000 ug/L), inhibin (Level III, Grade B), estrogen, DHEAS, LH, FSH, calcium (elevated calcium suggests small cell carcinoma, hypercalcemic type, the most important differential of JGCT), CA125; abdominal ultrasound and pelvic/abdominal MRI; chest imaging only if symptoms, abdominal metastases or DICER1 surveillance.
  • JGCT characteristics per the guideline (Table 1): bimodal age (children versus adolescents/young adults), estrogen producing, signs of precocious puberty, abdominal distension or pain, favorable prognosis in stage IA-IC1; adult GCT is distinguished by FOXL2 mutation, occurs in adults and elderly and may recur very late (>10 years).
  • Pathology: stage with the updated FIGO system for epithelial ovarian cancer, WHO classification, reference pathology required (Level III, Grade A); in JGCT the proliferation rate and mitotic index are prognostic (Level III, Grade B); SLCT should be checked for JGCT components and vice versa.
  • Genetics: DICER1 testing advised in anaplastic JGCT (Level IV, Grade B) and in all SLCT; in multiple enchondromatosis (Ollier, Maffucci) with JGCT, germline testing is recommended and tumor tissue should be analysed for IDH1/IDH2; McCune-Albright (somatic GNAS) has also been described with JGCT.
  • Surgery: open approach to avoid rupture (rupture is a major recurrence risk); laparoscopy only if rupture can be safely avoided; ascites or peritoneal lavage cytology, peritoneal biopsies of suspicious areas, lymph node biopsy only if suspicious; unilateral oophorectomy usually sufficient for encapsulated tumors; no contralateral biopsy, hysterectomy avoided, omentectomy not routine; after ovary-sparing excision with high mitotic count in JGCT, re-excision with oophorectomy is recommended (Level III, Grade B).
  • Chemotherapy (Table 3): stage IA/IB surveillance; IC1 JGCT surveillance (consider chemotherapy if high mitotic count); IC2/IC3 JGCT 3(-4) or 4 cycles PEI/VIP or BEP; II-III at least 4(-6) cycles; extension to 5-6 cycles may be considered for JGCT with high mitotic index; carboplatin/paclitaxel proved as effective as cisplatin regimens in adults with OSCST in a study that included no JGCT.
  • Radiotherapy not routinely recommended; GnRH blockade may be an add-on for relapsed tumors (Level V, Grade C), citing an infant with bilateral testicular JGCT who achieved complete remission with GnRH inhibitors as sole therapy.
  • Follow-up: tumor markers (inhibin, estrogen/testosterone, CA125) if previously elevated; examination with abdominal and pelvic ultrasound every 3 months for the first 2 years after therapy, then longer intervals; MRI only if ultrasound unclear; no routine imaging above the diaphragm.
  • Relapse: usually within 2 years (except adult GCT), most often peritoneal, prognosis poor with long-term survival approximately 20% or lower even in chemotherapy-naive patients; metachronous contralateral tumors (often with DICER1) usually appear after 2 years or more and have prognosis comparable to a first tumor.

Presentation

  • abdominal distension, pain, ovarian torsion at any age
  • infants and children: breast swelling, vaginal bleeding or discharge, pubic hair
  • adolescents: amenorrhea, acne, hirsutism, voice change (androgenic tumors, mostly SLCT)
  • bimodal age distribution of JGCT (children and adolescents/young adults)

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: germline/somatic pathogenic variants (germline in about 60% of Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors (per guideline); testing advised in anaplastic JGCT)
    Metachronous contralateral tumors often arise with DICER1 predisposition
  • IDH1/IDH2: somatic mosaic variants in Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome associated with JGCT
    Guideline recommends analysing tumor tissue for IDH1/IDH2 variants; germline testing recommended in multiple enchondromatosis with JGCT (Level III, Grade B)
  • GNAS: somatic mosaic mutations in McCune-Albright syndrome described with JGCT
    Mosaic tumor-prone condition rather than inherited predisposition
  • FOXL2: mutation characteristic of adult granulosa cell tumor
    Used to distinguish adult GCT from JGCT

Treatments reported

  • Oophorectomy (open approach, avoid rupture) then surveillance (FIGO IA/IB, all histologies; IC1 JGCT and other histologies (JGCT with high mitotic count: consider adjuvant chemotherapy))
    Outcome: Prognosis favorable in stage IA-IC1 JGCT per Table 1
  • Oophorectomy then 3(-4) or 4 cycles cisplatin-based chemotherapy (PEI/VIP: cisplatin, etoposide, ifosfamide; or BEP) (FIGO IC2/IC3 JGCT; three cycles may be considered for JGCT with low mitotic index)
    Outcome: Level III, Grade A
  • Salpingectomy (adnexa) with resection of metastases then at least 4(-6) cycles PEI/VIP or BEP; neoadjuvant 2 cycles with delayed resection for infiltrating or metastatic disease (FIGO II-III)
    Outcome: Level III-IV, Grades A-B
  • Platinum-based chemotherapy, dose-intensified chemotherapy with autologous stem cell transplantation, resection with peritonectomy/HIPEC, regional deep hyperthermia (one German center), radiotherapy, GnRH antagonists (Relapse (no harmonized strategy))
    Outcome: Long-term survival after relapse approximately 20% or lower
