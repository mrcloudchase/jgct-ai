Why it matters for JGCT

Case of adolescent JGCT presenting as acute abdomen with ruptured capsule and ascites, stage IC, treated by fertility-sparing surgery.

Key findings

  • 14-year-old previously healthy girl with regular menses presented with 5 days of acute abdominal pain and gross distension; clinically suspected gut perforation. No palpable lump, mildly anemic.
  • Ultrasound: multilobulated cystic mass about 11.5 x 7 cm in the right ovarian region with moderate peritoneal fluid; plain abdominal X-ray normal.
  • At laparotomy about 3 L of yellowish watery mucous fluid escaped; 12 x 7 cm multilobulated cystic right ovarian mass with a ruptured capsule, no peritoneal seedlings or enlarged nodes; peritoneal fluid cytology negative; staging done and right salpingo-oophorectomy performed (no frozen-section facility).
  • Histopathology confirmed JGCT (immature nuclei, atypia, high mitotic rate); final FIGO stage IC; referred to an oncologist and healthy without recurrence at time of reporting (duration not stated). No tumor markers or immunohistochemistry reported.
  • Discussion cites literature: JGCT about 5% of granulosa cell tumors; 97% of patients under 30; about 90% FIGO stage I at presentation with 5-year survival 90-100%; stage II 55-75% and stage III-IV 22-50%; ascites in about 10% and rupture at presentation in 10%; cisplatin-based multidrug regimens used in advanced or recurrent disease; inhibin for monitoring.

Presentation

  • 5 days of acute abdominal pain and abdominal distension
  • initially suspected gut perforation
  • multilobulated cystic right ovarian mass 11.5 x 7 cm with moderate ascites on ultrasound

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy, staging, right salpingo-oophorectomy; referred to oncologist, no chemotherapy described (FIGO stage IC (capsule rupture), fertility-sparing; n = 1)
    Outcome: healthy with no recurrence at time of reporting (duration not stated)

Notes

Text inconsistency: abstract mentions an 'ipsilateral' salpingo-oophorectomy and the discussion text cites stage survival figures from other sources (not this case). Follow-up duration not given; no inhibin or IHC. Year taken from the article citation (Mediscope 2014;1(1)), ledger lists 2015. Cited survival figures are second-hand.
