A rare tumor during pregnancy; juvenile granulosa cell tumor (Gebelik sirasinda nadir gorulen bir tumor; juvenil granuloza hucreli tumor)

Why it matters for JGCT

Ovarian JGCT diagnosed during pregnancy at age 17, with 3-year inhibin-based follow-up and a PET false-positive scenario.

Key findings

  • Case of a patient with ovarian JGCT found during late pregnancy: she presented with pain at 38 weeks and ultrasound showed a 17 cm right adnexal mass; cesarean delivery with right salpingo-oophorectomy was done (no frozen section in an emergency).
  • Gross: 17 x 14 x 9 cm smooth solid-cystic mass with yellow soft contents; microscopy: necrosis within fibrovascular stroma, diffuse spindle cells with vacuolated cytoplasm and mild pleomorphic nuclei, atypical mitoses.
  • IHC: EMA, cytokeratin 7, CA125 and calretinin negative; inhibin focally positive; diagnosed as poorly differentiated juvenile-type granulosa cell tumor; staged IA.
  • Because of early stage and incomplete fertility (patient was 17 and parous), surgery was limited to unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy; no adjuvant treatment was given; follow-up used serum inhibin, which did not rise.
  • Three years later (age 20), surveillance ultrasound showed a left ovarian cystic mass and PET/CT showed a hypermetabolic left adnexal focus; she was found to be 5 weeks pregnant with a 5x4 cm simple cyst thought to be a corpus luteum cyst, and after counselling about PET/CT she chose to terminate; one month later transvaginal ultrasound showed a normal left ovary, so this was considered a pregnancy luteoma/false positive, not recurrence.
  • The authors note PET/CT should not be done without excluding pregnancy, and cite from literature that stage I 5-year survival is 75-90%, adjuvant chemotherapy is recommended for stage IC-IV, and recurrence may occur after a mean 4-6 years.
  • The authors claim this is the youngest patient with JGCT diagnosed in pregnancy in the literature.

Presentation

  • pain at 38 weeks of gestation
  • 17 cm right adnexal mass found on ultrasound
  • no precocious puberty or menstrual irregularity reported

Treatments reported

  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy at cesarean delivery (no frozen section); no adjuvant therapy because early stage; surveillance with serum inhibin (primary surgery in pregnancy at 38 weeks; n = 1)
    Outcome: no recurrence at 3 years of follow-up; inhibin did not rise; spontaneous pregnancy later

Notes

Original in Turkish; read in Turkish. Internal discrepancy: the abstract says the patient was 20 years old with a 17 cm mass during pregnancy, but the case text says the tumor was found 3 years earlier at age 17 (38 weeks) and she was 20 at the follow-up pregnancy. Statement that JGCT incidence among ovarian tumors in pregnancy is 10% is the authors' citation and appears implausible. Title translation is the English title given on the article.
