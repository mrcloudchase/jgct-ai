Why it matters for JGCT

Provides only a JGCT frequency (8/158, 5.1%) among ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors in one Indian institution.

Key findings

  • Retrospective histopathology series at SMS Medical College, Jaipur (July 2009 to June 2017): 158 ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors, ages 8 to 82 years, most commonly 30-40 years (22.8%) and 40-50 years (17.7%, text says maximum in 40-50 group).
  • Histologic types: adult granulosa cell tumor 67 (42.4%), juvenile granulosa cell tumor 8 (5.1%), fibroma 31 (19.6%), Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor 24 (15.2%), fibrothecoma 14 (8.9%), sclerosing stromal tumor 7 (4.4%), thecoma 6 (3.8%), sex cord tumor with annular tubules 1.
  • Whole series presentation: abdominal pain and lump 81, menstrual irregularity 49, anorexia and weight loss 14, virilization 12, precocious puberty 2; 152 tumors were unilateral and 6 bilateral (3 granulosa cell tumors, 2 fibromas, 1 SCTAT); 87 solid and 71 solid-cystic; 142 of 158 measured 5-15 cm.
  • No JGCT-specific age, stage, treatment or outcome data are given. The authors note their JGCT percentage exceeds that of Cartault and Schneider and resembles Haroon et al.
  • Authors conclude sex cord-stromal tumors are rare in Indian women and need a high index of suspicion and clinicopathological correlation.

Notes

Histology by H&E only; no immunohistochemistry or molecular testing, so JGCT classification is morphologic. Age table totals 158; the abstract says 30-40 years most common while the Results text says 40-50 years. No outcomes reported.
