Why it matters for JGCT

Narrative review; JGCT content is limited to epidemiologic context (about 5% of GCTs), Ollier disease association, FOXL2 mutation absent in 9/10 JGCTs and the idea that JGCT and adult GCT are molecularly distinct. No original patient data.

Key findings

  • Review of pediatric ovarian cancers with emphasis on microRNA biology; states that about 40% of ovarian tumors in the young are germ cell tumors and 25% sex cord-stromal tumors, and that 2-8% of pediatric adnexal masses are ultimately cancer.
  • Granulosa cell tumors are the most common sex cord-stromal tumor; juvenile GCT occurs mainly in premenarchal girls and is roughly 5% of GCTs. Coffee-bean nuclei and Call-Exner bodies are typical of adult GCT and only rarely seen in JGCT.
  • Estrogen secretion by GCT can present as premature menarche or isosexual precocious puberty (elevated serum estrogen, no gonadotropin response to GnRH stimulation); contrasexual precocity with virilization is attributed mainly to Sertoli-Leydig tumors.
  • Ollier disease: nine cases of ovarian JGCT reported in individuals with Ollier disease (cited as a number greater than expected by chance); the ovarian tumors are usually unilateral and often on the same side as the most recently resected enchondroma. Estimated Ollier prevalence about 1 per 100,000.
  • The FOXL2 somatic mutation was found in 86/89 adult GCTs but only 1/10 JGCTs and 3/14 thecomas, and none of 49 other sex cord-stromal tumors or 329 unrelated tumors; the authors state that juvenile and adult GCTs may be molecularly distinct despite shared clinical and histologic features.
  • DICER1 germline mutations occur in familial pleuropulmonary blastoma and multinodular goiter; somatic DICER1 mutations are reported in up to 60% of Sertoli-Leydig tumors, a finding not described for JGCT in this review.
  • Mouse models: Smad1/Smad5 conditional knockout in granulosa cells and inhibin-alpha knockout give granulosa cell tumors; PI3K/mTORC1 hyperactivation is common to human and mouse GCT, but everolimus only slowed tumor growth in one mouse model.

Presentation

  • premature menarche / isosexual precocious puberty from estrogen secretion

Genetic and molecular findings

  • FOXL2: somatic c.C>G missense mutation at position 412 (as written in the paper) (86/89 adult GCT, 1/10 JGCT, 3/14 thecomas; 0/49 other sex cord-stromal tumors)
    cited from Shah et al. 2009
  • DICER1: somatic hot-spot mutations (Asp1709, Glu1813, RNase IIIb domain) (up to 60% of Sertoli-Leydig tumors (not JGCT))
    germline DICER1 in familial PPB and multinodular goiter; ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors in 9/325 PPB-syndrome individuals
  • chromosomes: monosomy 22, gains of 12 and 14, loss of X (recurrent in adult and juvenile GCT (cited))

Conflicts with

  • Trecourt A 2023: Relevance of Molecular Pathology for the Diagnosis of Sex Cord-Stromal Tumors of the Ovary: A Narrative Review.

Notes

Secondary review. FOXL2 mutation position given as 'position 412 (C to G)' in the paper (commonly written c.402C>G); recorded as written. Journal, first author and DOI are not stated in the fetched text.
