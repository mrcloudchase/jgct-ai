Improving diagnosis and management of pediatric ovarian masses: development of a risk stratification model incorporating sonographic and clinical features.

Why it matters for JGCT

Very limited: a general paediatric ovarian mass surgical series reporting 4 JGCT (of 348 masses) in an age table; no JGCT-specific outcomes.

Key findings

  • Single-center retrospective study (Children's Hospital of Soochow University, China; 2018-2024) of 344 patients aged <=18 years (348 masses) who had ovarian surgery; aim was a sonographic/clinical malignancy prediction model.
  • Histology: germ cell tumors 72.7% (253/348), epithelial 15.2% (53), sex-cord stromal 2.0% (7), follicular cysts 10.1% (35); malignant lesions 11.5% (40/348).
  • Sex-cord stromal tumors (7) comprised juvenile granulosa cell tumor 4/7 (57.1%), Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor 2/7 and fibroma 1/7; by age, JGCT occurred in 3 children aged 1-8 years and 1 aged 9-16 years (none under 1).
  • No JGCT-specific clinical, marker, imaging, stage, treatment or outcome data are reported; JGCT patients are not analyzed separately.
  • Authors attribute the greater frequency of precocious puberty in malignant than benign masses (20.0% vs 8.6%, p=0.025) to hormone-secreting tumors such as juvenile granulosa cell tumors (an interpretation, not a JGCT-specific measurement).
  • Model result: solid component proportion >80% strongest malignancy predictor (OR 576.5, 95% CI 74.0-4492.6); combined size plus solid proportion AUC 0.93, sensitivity 87.5%, specificity 83.2%.
