Why it matters for JGCT

Largest registry cohort of ovarian JGCT with central review; quantifies stage distribution, treatment and stage- and mitotic-index-specific survival, identifying high mitotic index as the main prognostic factor.

Key findings

  • 70 ovarian juvGCT patients enrolled in the International OTST and/or IPPB/DICER1 Registries, diagnosed 2001-2024; central pathology review for 80% (56/70); median age 10 years (range 1 day-38 years), 93% (65/70) younger than 20; median follow-up 31 months (range 0-271).
  • Presentation: abdominal pain 35/65, distention 29/65, precocious puberty 17/65, secondary amenorrhea 7/34, virilization 8/65; median tumor size 12.0 cm (4.6-30.0); 36/69 right, 31/69 left, 2/69 bilateral. Inhibin B elevated in 38/42 (90%; median 3,759 pg/ml), inhibin A 22/34 (65%), CA125 20/42 (48%), total testosterone 9/21 (43%), AFP 2/46 (4%).
  • Surgery: laparotomy 55/67 (82%), laparoscopy 12/67 (18%); salpingo-oophorectomy 48/69 (70%), oophorectomy 18/69, cystectomy 2/69; preoperative rupture (IC2) 15/66 (23%), intraoperative rupture (IC1) 25/66 (38%); second-look staging in 16/69. Peritoneal fluid collected in 58/70, malignant cells in 6/58, all with rupture and/or extra-ovarian tumor; no sampled lymph node involved. Only 14/70 (20%) had complete FIGO staging.
  • 2014 FIGO stage: IA 25/70 (36%, three were IX), IC1 20/70 (29%), IC2 n=7 / IC3 n=2 / ICX n=3 (12/70, 17%), II-IV 10/70 (14%), not documented 3/70; 81% (57/70) were stage I.
  • Mitotic index (MI) available in 60/70 (86%; median 26/10 HPF, range 1-104): low (<10) 9/60, intermediate (10-19) 14/60, high (>19) 37/60 (62%). High MI by stage: IA 11/21, IC1 11/17, IC2-IC3 7/12, II-IV 7/9.
  • Adjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy in 21/70 (30%), 17/21 with bleomycin-etoposide-platinum; in low-risk stages 1 stage IA and 3/20 stage IC1 received chemotherapy; no upfront radiation. Molecular (small subset): AKT1 in-frame duplications in 3 patients, GNAS p.R201C and TERT promoter in 2 each, TP53 in 4, one anaplastic tumor with somatic DICER1 RNase IIIb hotspot variant (patient died).
  • Outcome: 14 recurrences (median 8.5 months, range 3-32; 13/14 within 2 years); 8 deaths, all from recurrent juvGCT (median 35 months from diagnosis, range 9-69). Of 6 surviving recurrent patients, 4 on treatment and 2 NED. Inhibin B was elevated at recurrence in 3/5 of those with elevated inhibin B at diagnosis who recurred.
  • 3-year EFS / OS overall 73.2% (95% CI 61.9-86.5) / 91.7% (84.2-99.9). By stage 3-year EFS: IA 80.2% (62.4-100.0), IC1 87.4% (72.4-100.0), IC2-IC3 63.6% (40.7-99.5), II-IV 48.0% (24.6-93.8) (p=.05). Low-risk (IA/IX, IC1) vs high-risk (>=IC2 incl ICX) 3-year EFS 82.2% (68.8-98.2) vs 55.5% (37.4-82.4) (p=.02); 3-year OS 96.2% (89.0-100.0) vs 84.0% (68.8-100.0) (p=.13).
  • 3-year OS by stage: IA/IX 100% (but two IA/IX patients died of recurrence at 64 and 69 months, both high MI and no chemotherapy), IC1 90.9% (75.4-100.0; 1 death, high MI, no chemotherapy), IC2/IC3/ICX 87.5% (67.3-100.0; 1 death, IC2, high MI, received chemotherapy), II-IV 77.8% (54.9-100.0; 4 deaths: 3 high MI with chemotherapy, 1 stage IIIC with unknown MI and no chemotherapy) (p=.01).
  • High MI predicted worse outcome: 3-year EFS 55.5% (39.6-77.9) with high MI vs 100% with low/intermediate MI (p<.01); all 13 recurrences with known MI had high MI; relapse 13/37 high MI vs 0/23 low/intermediate; juvGCT death 7/37 vs 0/23. 3- and 5-year OS 85.5% / 79.3% (high MI) vs 100% (p=.03).
  • Within low-risk stages with known MI, surgery-only: 16 patients with low MI none recurred; 18 with high MI had 5 recurrences, 3 deaths (3-year EFS 64.1%, 42.7-96.2, p=.12; 3-year OS 92.9%). Within high-risk stages: 14 high MI (12 chemo, 8 recurred) vs 7 low/intermediate (5 chemo, none recurred); 3-year EFS 37.1% vs no events (p=.02); 5-year OS 55.5% vs 100% (p=.08).
  • Among low-risk surgery-only patients (n=41) larger tumor dimension (HR 1.13, 1.01-1.27) and MI as continuous variable (HR 1.15, 1.02-1.28) were associated with recurrence; >12 cm HR 4.48 (0.50-40.26), age and intraoperative rupture not significant. Authors strongly recommend MI assessment for all juvGCT, support chemotherapy beyond stage IC1, suggest considering chemotherapy individually for low-stage high-MI tumors, surveillance with imaging and markers up to 5 years, and tumor testing for targetable alterations (e.g. AKT-directed therapy).

Genetic and molecular findings

  • AKT1: in-frame duplications (3 patients (1 at diagnosis, 2 at recurrence); 10 tumors sequenced at diagnosis, 5 at recurrence)
  • GNAS: p.R201C (2 tumors)
  • TERT: promoter variants (2 tumors)
  • TP53: variants (4 patients)
  • DICER1: somatic RNase IIIb hotspot variant (1 tumor (anaplastic juvGCT, MI 73/10 HPF); germline status unknown)
    that patient died of juvGCT

Treatments reported

  • Surgery only (no adjuvant therapy) (all stages; n = 49)
    Outcome: Table 2: 49/70 surgery only; 18/23 low/intermediate MI, 21/37 high MI, 10/10 unknown MI
  • Adjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy (most commonly bleomycin, etoposide, platinum, 17/21) (adjuvant, 30% of cohort; n = 21)
    Outcome: 1 stage IA and 3/20 stage IC1 patients received it; 12/14 high-MI high-risk stage patients received it and 8 recurred (7/8 had chemotherapy); 3-year EFS for high-risk stage with high MI 37.1% (95% CI 17.9-77.1); no analysis of chemotherapy effect possible (confounding)
  • Radiation therapy (upfront; n = 0)
    Outcome: none received

Notes

Abstract and full text agree on key numbers. Staging rules: missing staging components assumed negative; IX grouped with IA; ICX when rupture status unknown. Local and central MI pooled. Chemotherapy was not randomized so its effect could not be assessed. Possible patient overlap with other IOTSTR/DICER1 registry publications. Small sample, few events, enrollment/ascertainment bias acknowledged. Table 1 lists elevated inhibin B 'median [range]' n=37.
