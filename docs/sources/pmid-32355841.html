Why it matters for JGCT

General AYA gynecologic oncology review; JGCT is covered in two paragraphs (epidemiology, presentation, marker, staging-based treatment) plus generic oncofertility guidance relevant to JGCT survivors.

Key findings

  • Narrative review of gynecological tumors in adolescents and young adults (AYA, age 15-39 per NCI/ENCCA) with a proposed multidisciplinary model of care covering fertility, ovarian failure, contraception and psychosocial effects; it reports no new patient data.
  • The review states JGCT accounts for about 70% of ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors in patients younger than 20 years.
  • Approximately 80% of prepubertal girls with JGCT present with isosexual precocious puberty related to estrogen secretion; rarely the tumor produces androgen with virilizing features; in children JGCT is more frequently associated with acute complications such as torsion and rupture.
  • Inhibin is described as a useful serum tumor marker for diagnosis and follow-up.
  • Up to 90% of JGCT are low-stage disease confined to the ovary and are usually curable with conservative surgery; patients with advanced disease or high mitotic activity have a poorer prognosis and should be treated with chemotherapy.
  • Surgery is needed for histological diagnosis, staging and debulking; chemotherapy is indicated for FIGO stage IC or higher, based on cisplatin with ifosfamide and etoposide (PEI) or etoposide and bleomycin (PEB).
  • For fertility, conservative surgery preserving uterus and contralateral ovary is advised for malignant germ cell tumors and selected stage 1 lesions; AMH is suggested as a marker of ovarian reserve; alkylating agents (ifosfamide, cyclophosphamide) carry high gonadotoxicity, platinum agents moderate risk.

Presentation

  • isosexual precocious puberty in about 80% of prepubertal girls with JGCT
  • torsion and rupture as acute complications
  • rarely androgen production with virilization

Treatments reported

  • Platinum-based chemotherapy (PEI: cisplatin/ifosfamide/etoposide; or PEB: cisplatin/etoposide/bleomycin) (stromal ovarian tumors (juvenile granulosa/theca) at FIGO stage IC or higher)
    Outcome: review statement; no outcome data given

Notes

Secondary review; JGCT statements rely on cited sources not read in this run (e.g. 70% of pediatric SCST, ~80% precocious puberty). The review does not separate ovarian from other sites for fertility advice.
