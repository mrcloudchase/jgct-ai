Ovarian cancer: diagnostic accuracy and tumor types distribution in East Africa compared to North America.

Why it matters for JGCT

Shows JGCT as 4 of 210 East African ovarian cancers and gives IHC marker frequencies for 4 JGCT; no clinical outcomes.

Key findings

  • Retrospective study of 210 ovarian cancer cases (2002-2017) from three Tanzanian centers and Makerere University, Uganda, re-reviewed with H&E and a 20-antibody IHC panel on tissue microarrays and compared with the Alberta Cancer Registry.
  • Sex cord stromal tumors were 30/210 (14.3%) in East Africa versus 17 (0.92%) in Alberta; among East African SCST, 24 (80.0%) were adult GCT, 4 (13.3%) juvenile GCT, 2 (6.7%) Sertoli-Leydig; no JGCT among the Alberta SCST (adult GCT 12, Sertoli-Leydig 4, other 1).
  • JGCT IHC (Table 2, 4 cases): FOXL2 positive 50%, vimentin diffuse 75%, p16 abnormal 75%, PAX8 positive 25%, ER positive 50%; WT-1, PR, p53 mutant, Napsin A and MSH6/PMS2/ARID1A loss all 0%.
  • Comparator adult GCT (24 cases): FOXL2 66.7%, vimentin 95.8%, WT-1 47.8%, PR 33.3%.
  • Concordance of original vs IHC-integrated diagnosis was 83.3% for SCST (kappa 0.70 for major categories); one case of carcinoma NOS was re-diagnosed as adult GCT after a new H&E stain; one 'neuroblastoma' was reclassified as SCCOHT (BRG1 loss).
  • Stage was available for only four patients and no recurrence or survival data were collected; JGCT age, stage and outcomes are not reported individually.
  • Authors caution that selection bias (younger women with GCT/SCST more likely to reach tertiary care) limits comparison of incidence and tumor-type proportions.
