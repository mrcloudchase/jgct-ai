Juvenile granulosa cell tumor in a transgender male with Ollier disease: A case report.

Why it matters for JGCT

Rare report of JGCT in an adult on long-term testosterone with Ollier disease, relevant to tumor hormone-receptor expression and to the link between enchondromatosis and granulosa cell tumors.

Key findings

  • 34-year-old transgender man (G0) with Ollier disease (history of low-grade chondrosarcoma 2011, right below-knee amputation) on testosterone since February 2016 (200 mg IM every 2 weeks for ~5 years, then 160 mg subcutaneously) presented with 1 month of left lower quadrant pain, early satiety and bloating.
  • Labs: CA-125 198 U/mL, LDH 426 U/L, inhibin A 473 pg/mL, inhibin B 23,419 pg/mL; hormone levels at presentation not obtained (last testosterone 567 ng/dL in 5/2022).
  • CT: 15 cm cystic and solid right ovarian mass, 4.9 cm uterine fibroid, endometrial thickness 1.4 cm, omental stranding, trace right pleural effusion and 1.5 cm pleural nodule; ultrasound 20 cm right adnexal mass; Pap smear HPV 16 positive with atypical endometrial cells; endometrial biopsy non-atypical hyperplasia.
  • Surgery: laparotomy, total abdominal hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, omentectomy with optimal cytoreduction; right ovarian mass 3340 g; pathology stage IA JGCT confined to right ovary, omentum negative; focal atypical endometrial hyperplasia in uterus.
  • IHC on tumor: estrogen receptor 40% weak-moderate, progesterone receptor 90% moderate-strong, androgen receptor >90% strong.
  • The pleural nodule regressed spontaneously before planned biopsy; no adjuvant therapy; surveillance by inhibin A and B every 3 months in year 1, every 4 months year 2, every 6 months year 3, then annually.
  • Authors discuss association of Ollier disease with granulosa cell tumors (proposed generalized mesodermal dysplasia; IDH1 mutation reported in both enchondroma and JGCT in cited studies; not tested here) and ask whether testosterone therapy contributed to tumor growth; they recommended stopping testosterone for now and suggest pre-screening for hormonally responsive conditions in patients on hormone therapy.

Presentation

  • one month of left lower quadrant pain
  • early satiety and bloating
  • abdominal distension and tenderness
  • amenorrhea on testosterone

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy, TAH, BSO, omentectomy, optimal cytoreduction to no gross residual disease; testosterone therapy discontinued on recommendation (FIGO stage IA JGCT; n = 1)
    Outcome: no evidence of metastatic disease at 4 months; inhibin A/B surveillance planned; testosterone 31 ng/dL and estradiol 8 pg/mL at 4 months

Notes

Internal date inconsistency in paper: case section says presentation April 2024, later text says April 2023 with surgery in May 2023 (published 2023, so 2023 is likely). Tumor was not molecularly tested; IDH1 statements are literature citations. Another 2020 gender-dysphoria JGCT case is cited.
