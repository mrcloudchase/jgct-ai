Endogenous c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) activity marks the boundary between normal and malignant granulosa cells.

Why it matters for JGCT

Preclinical evidence of elevated JNK activity and JNK-inhibitor sensitivity that includes two juvenile-type tumor samples; most experiments use adult-type-derived cell lines, so JGCT-specific inference rests on n=2.

Key findings

  • Translational laboratory study of granulosa cell tumor (GCT) of the ovary; patient material was 4 fresh tumors (2 adult-type, 2 juvenile-type, confirmed by two gynecologic pathologists) plus the GCT lines COV434 and KGN; non-neoplastic comparators were HGrC1, SIGC and luteinized granulosa cells from 10 IVF patients.
  • Endogenous JNK kinase activity was significantly higher in both adult- and juvenile-type GCT samples and in COV434 and KGN than in non-neoplastic granulosa cells (HGrC1, HLGC, SIGC), including mitotically active non-malignant lines.
  • Ex vivo treatment of fresh adult and juvenile GCT samples with JNK inhibitors for 24 h significantly decreased in vitro growth and estradiol and AMH production and increased cell death.
  • In COV434, JNK inhibition (SP600125, AS601245, or siRNA) gave dose-dependent growth reduction, apoptosis, lower estradiol and AMH, fewer phospho-histone H3-positive cells (2% and 3% vs 17% in controls, p<0.01), reduced EdU uptake (6% and 9% vs 47%), and G2/M accumulation.
  • In a COV434 subcutaneous xenograft in SCID mice (n=4 per group), single-dose JNK inhibitors (10 mg/kg i.p.) or intratumoral JNK siRNA halted growth over the following week and lowered serum AMH.
  • Background statements by the authors: JGCT is about 5% of GCT, lacks the FOXL2 C134W mutation found in 97% of adult-type GCT, affects pre-pubertal girls and young women (mean onset around 8 years), has reduced FOXL2 expression; AKT1 tandem duplications were reported in 16 JGCTs and activating Gas codon 201 mutations in 30% of a JGCT cohort (cited from other studies).
  • Authors' conclusion: targeting the JNK pathway may offer therapeutic benefit in GCT, for which no curative therapy exists beyond surgery; further investigation is warranted.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • JNK pathway (c-Jun phosphorylation): markedly higher endogenous JNK kinase activity in tumor samples (2 adult, 2 juvenile) and COV434/KGN lines than in non-neoplastic granulosa cells (4 patient tumors)
    measured by kinase assay; not a mutation

Treatments reported

  • JNK inhibitors SP600125 and AS601245, or JNK siRNA (preclinical: GCT cell lines, ex vivo patient tumor cultures (adult and juvenile), COV434 xenograft in SCID mice (n=4 per group); n = 2)
    Outcome: Reduced growth, hormone output and viability ex vivo in both adult and juvenile samples; reduced xenograft growth and serum AMH

Notes

Only 2 JGCT samples; the cell lines COV434 (reported as GCT-derived) and KGN (derived from invasive granulosa cell carcinoma) are not JGCT-specific. First author not in cached text. Epidemiology and molecular statements about JGCT are cited background, not original findings.
