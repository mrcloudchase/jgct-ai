Why it matters for JGCT

Case of infantile JGCT with precocious puberty features and a very large (2900 g) stage I tumor; the report concerns anaesthetic management, with limited oncologic detail.

Key findings

  • Case report with an anaesthesia focus: 1-year-old girl, 8.5 kg, with 2 months of weight loss, abdominal swelling and pain, bilateral breast enlargement and premature pubic hair; abdominal lump about 8 x 10 cm from epigastrium to pelvis.
  • Contrast CT: heterogeneously enhancing, well-defined abdominopelvic mass from the right adnexa up to the subhepatic region, suggestive of ovarian origin.
  • Complete excision removed a 2900 g mass; histopathology confirmed JGCT (no immunohistochemistry or marker results given); the tumor was assigned FIGO stage I.
  • Intraoperative course was hemodynamically stable with uneventful extubation; the authors emphasize airway, hemodynamic, respiratory and fluid risks of giant ovarian masses (vena cava compression, post-excision hypotension, pulmonary edema).
  • Authors conclude complete surgical resection is the key prognostic factor for localized disease, with patients responding well to surgery alone. No postoperative follow-up or hormone data are reported.

Presentation

  • weight loss, abdominal swelling and pain for 2 months
  • bilateral breast enlargement
  • pubic hair (described as Tanner stage 1)
  • abdominal lump about 8 x 10 cm from epigastrium to pelvis

Treatments reported

  • Complete excision under general anaesthesia (fentanyl, propofol, rocuronium; 10 ml/kg Ringer lactate preloading) (FIGO stage I; n = 1)
    Outcome: 2900 g mass removed; hemodynamically stable, uneventful extubation; no follow-up reported

Notes

Title says 'Malignant' GCT whereas text describes JGCT. 'Tanner stage 1' alongside pubic hair is as stated in the paper. No outcome follow-up. Abstract claims JGCT occurs mostly under five years of age (literature).
