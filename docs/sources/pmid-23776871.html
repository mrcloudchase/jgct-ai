Juvenile granulosa cell tumor presenting as isosexual precocious puberty: A case report and review of literature.

Why it matters for JGCT

Typical estrogen-secreting stage IA ovarian JGCT with isosexual precocious puberty cured by unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy alone.

Key findings

  • A 7-year-old girl presented with 18 months of progressive breast development (Tanner IV breast, Tanner III pubic hair), menarche a year earlier, and height 125.2 cm and weight 26 kg above the 75th centile; bone age was 8.5 years at chronological age 7 years 2 months.
  • Serum estradiol was 414 pg/ml (prepubertal <20 pg/ml) with suppressed FSH (<1 IU/L); beta-hCG, CEA and AFP were negative; inhibin was not measured because testing was unavailable at the center.
  • Pelvic ultrasound showed a 6 x 7 x 9 cm heterogeneous solid right adnexal mass with hypoechoic necrotic areas and increased vascularity; elective excision was planned but she presented a week later with ovarian torsion and underwent emergency laparotomy with right salpingo-oophorectomy.
  • The resected tumor was encapsulated, 450 g, 10 x 8 x 5 cm, without capsular breach; histology showed microfollicular and nodular patterns, nuclear grooving, inconspicuous nucleoli and extensive luteinisation; ascitic fluid was negative for malignant cells; final diagnosis was benign feminizing JGCT, FIGO stage IA.
  • After surgery the breast size regressed, bleeding stopped and estradiol normalized; no recurrence at 3.5 years. No chemotherapy was given.
  • In its literature discussion the paper states JGCT is under 5% of childhood ovarian tumors, 5-year survival is 90-95% for FIGO stage I and 25-50% for advanced stages, recurrences occur mostly in the first 3 years, and cisplatin-based adjuvant chemotherapy is needed for stage Ic, IIIc or high mitotic rate (these are the authors' review statements, not data from this patient).
  • The paper cites activating G-alpha-s mutations in 30%, trisomy 12, 14 and monosomy 22 as genetic aberrations, and states FOXL2 expression, age <10 years, precocious pseudopuberty and stage I are associated with good prognosis; no molecular testing was performed in this patient.

Presentation

  • isosexual precocious puberty with 18 months of progressive breast development
  • menarche one year earlier with 3-4 bleeding episodes
  • pubic hair growth, accelerated growth, advanced bone age
  • ovarian torsion presenting as acute abdomen

Treatments reported

  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy (emergency laparotomy for torsion); no adjuvant chemotherapy (FIGO stage IA; n = 1)
    Outcome: Bleeding stopped, breast size regressed, estradiol normalized; no recurrence at 3.5 years

Notes

Narrative review statements are the authors' citations of other work and are not independently verified here. Abstract wording matches full text; the full text gives the 3.5-year follow-up.
