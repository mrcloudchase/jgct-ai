Ovarian Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor in 9-Month-Old female Infant presented with Isosexual Pseudo Puberty: Case Report

Why it matters for JGCT

Infant JGCT with very high AMH and inhibin A/B at diagnosis and normalisation of estradiol and AMH after surgery alone.

Key findings

  • 9-month-old girl with a day of bloody vaginal secretions, bilateral breast enlargement for 2 months (Tanner 2) and fine pubic hair; bone age 1.8 years (+4.3 SDS).
  • Labs: estradiol 1091 pmol/l (ref 0-132), FSH and LH <0.3 IU/l, AMH >1714.0 pmol/l (ref 0.1-38.6), inhibin A 385 pg/ml (<4.7), inhibin B 3805 pg/ml (<111), androstenedione 6.8 nmol/l, testosterone 2.2 nmol/l, beta-hCG 0.9 IU/l, AFP 6 kIU/l (normal).
  • Ultrasound: round heterogeneous hypoechoic lesion 5.4x4.12x5.39 cm (62.8 ml), no calcifications; MRI: large solid pelvic lesion related to the left ovary with enhancement and minimal central necrosis; chest CT normal.
  • Open total left salpingo-oophorectomy: encapsulated ovoid 75 g mass, 100x50x40 mm, solid yellow nodular cut surface, capsule intact; histopathology juvenile-type granulosa cell tumor pT1a, inhibin A positive; no chemotherapy or radiation.
  • At 1 month estradiol was 62 pmol/l and AMH 9 pmol/l; authors conclude surgical removal was curative with all hormones regressing to prepubertal levels.
