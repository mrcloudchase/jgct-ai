Why it matters for JGCT

Marginal: gives a single JGCT among 15 surgically treated adolescent ovarian cysts and tumors (6.7%) with no JGCT-specific clinical data. Useful only as an illustration that JGCT can present as an ovarian cyst/mass in a general adolescent surgical series.

Key findings

  • Retrospective single-center series (Selcuk University, Konya, Turkiye; March 2023 to March 2024) of 15 adolescent girls (median age 14.8 +/- 1.15 years) who underwent ovarian cystectomy.
  • Pathology (Table 2): simple cyst 7 (46.7%), paratubal serous cyst 4 (26.7%), endometrioma 2 (13.3%), serous cystadenoma 1 (6.7%), juvenile granulosa cell tumor 1 (6.7%).
  • The paper gives no individual details for the JGCT patient (age, presentation, markers, imaging, stage or treatment are not separately reported); the discussion refers to the tumor and the cystadenoma as the two patients with a solid component on ultrasound.
  • Across the series no hormonal disorders were found: LH, FSH, estradiol, testosterone and tumor markers (AFP, beta-hCG, CEA, CA-125, LDH) were summarized as means; no abnormalities were reported; hirsutism was mild/moderate in 3 with normal ACTH, 17-OHP and DHEA-S.
  • Ultrasound: mean long axis of cyst 75 +/- 55 mm in 13 patients; septate in 4, solid component in 2, otherwise pure cystic.
  • No recurrent cysts were seen in approximately 9 months of postoperative follow-up; the authors emphasize ovary-preserving surgery given the low malignancy rates in this age group, and note that the small sample limits conclusions.

Presentation

  • series: abdominal pain in 11/15 (73.3%), incidental in 2/15, acute abdomen from ovarian torsion in 2/15

Notes

Internal inconsistency: the discussion says the solid component was seen in the tumor and adenoma patients, but also reports a solid component in the patient with endometrioma. The abstract says the study evaluated outcomes, but only a roughly 9-month cyst recurrence statement is given and nothing JGCT-specific.
