Why it matters for JGCT

Largely an adult-type GCT series; contributes 2 individually described relapsed JGCT cases (one fatal at 7 months) and literature statements. JGCT numbers in the whole series are not reported.

Key findings

  • Retrospective multi-institution review from Chennai, India (records 1993/1995-2015) of 126 patients with ovarian granulosa cell tumour, mostly adult type; 34 relapsed patients are tabulated individually.
  • Only two JGCT patients appear in the relapse table: a 14-year-old with stage Ia, 10 cm JGCT treated with fertility-sparing surgery who progressed at 7 months (pelvis), received EP and died at 7 months; and a 21-year-old with stage IIIc JGCT, fertility-sparing surgery, relapse in the pelvis, 2 cycles of chemotherapy, then lost to follow-up.
  • Of the 126 patients, 3% were aged under 10 and 3% aged 10-19 years; precocious puberty was reported in 2% of the series.
  • Whole-series findings (mainly adult GCT): 84-85 had complete staging, 41 incomplete surgery, 7-10 fertility-sparing surgery; patients with complete surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy did not relapse; of 34 relapsed patients, 19 survived; relapse was more frequent with tumour >9-10 cm, spill and advanced stage.
  • Authors' discussion states juvenile type occurs in younger patients with increased recurrence risk and poorer prognosis, cites FOXL2 mutation in 97% of adult and 10% of juvenile type, and says adjuvant chemotherapy yields complete, long-lasting response in children with stage Ic JGCT with a high mitotic index (>20/HPF); these are literature statements, not results of this series.
  • Authors conclude chemotherapy has a compelling role in advanced, inoperable and metastatic cases, with tumour spill or rupture, certain variants such as JGCT, and tumours >10 cm (abstract says >9 cm).

Presentation

  • whole series: abdominal pain 40%, menorrhagia 33%, postmenopausal bleeding 24%, palpable mass 20%, precocious puberty 2%

Treatments reported

  • Fertility-sparing surgery, then EP (etoposide-cisplatin) (relapsed JGCT, stage Ia, 10 cm, age 14; n = 1)
    Outcome: progressed at 7 months in pelvis; died at 7 months
  • Fertility-sparing surgery, 2 cycles chemotherapy (relapsed JGCT, stage IIIc, age 21; n = 1)
    Outcome: defaulted; lost to follow-up

Notes

Internally inconsistent: premenopausal 53 (42%) vs 58 in the table; study period 1993 vs 1995-2015; complete staging 84 vs 85; fertility-sparing 7 vs 10; cut-off '>9 cm' in abstract vs '>10 cm' in conclusion; histology column mixes AGCT, SCST, JGCT and 'androgen secreting'. The text states chemotherapy benefit without a formal comparison reported for JGCT.
