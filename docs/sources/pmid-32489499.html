Evaluation of the usefulness of laparoscopy in the diagnosis and treatment of adnexal pathologies in the pediatric population.

Why it matters for JGCT

Minor. Surgical series of pediatric adnexal pathology in which 2 of 89 patients had a JGCT (one in a mixed SCTAT tumor); only presentation and imaging details are given for them.

Key findings

  • Retrospective single-center series of 89 patients aged 0-18 years (mean 12.62) who had laparoscopy for suspected adnexal pathology, 2010-2017 (37 emergency, 52 elective).
  • Adnexal cysts were 70.79% (n=63) of postoperative diagnoses and ovarian tumors 28.01% (n=25), of which 23 were benign (20 mature teratomas) and 2 malignant; malignant to benign ratio 2:23 (0.087).
  • The two malignant tumors were a juvenile granulosa cell tumor (13-year-old) and a mixed tumor, mainly sex cord tumor with annular tubules (SCTAT) with separate foci of juvenile granulosa cell tumor (10-year-old).
  • 13-year-old with JGCT: CT showed a cystic structure with small solid component, probably from the right ovary, 85 x 175 x 260 mm reaching the epigastrium; only LDH was raised (200 IU/ml), other tumor markers not elevated.
  • 10-year-old with SCTAT plus JGCT foci: growing abdominal girth, polycyclic cystic lesion filling pelvis and abdomen, CA-125 101.4 IU/ml; the tumor was resected according to an oncological protocol.
  • Series-level outcomes: mean operation time 63.7 min, mean hospital stay 4.34 days, 3 conversions, no intraoperative or perioperative complications; ultrasound sensitivity/specificity was 67.21%/50% for cysts and 26.32%/92.75% for torsion.
  • The authors conclude that pediatric laparoscopy for adnexal pathology appears safe and that ovarian parenchyma-sparing surgery should be used where preoperative diagnosis is not malignant; the 29 cm largest lesion was benign (seromucinous cystadenoma), so size did not indicate malignancy.

Presentation

  • 10-year-old: growing abdominal circumference with mass effect, polycyclic cystic lesion filling pelvis and abdomen, CA-125 101.4 IU/ml
  • 13-year-old: cystic tumor with small solid component, probably right ovary, 85 x 175 x 260 mm on CT, only LDH elevated (200 IU/ml)

Treatments reported

  • Laparoscopic procedures (cystectomy, tumorectomy, tumoro-ovariectomy etc.); the two malignant tumors were resected laparoscopically (one 'according to an oncological protocol') (surgery, retrospective series 2010-2017; n = 2)
    Outcome: no operative complications reported in the series; no follow-up outcome given for the malignant cases

Notes

Stage, histology details, adjuvant therapy and outcome of the JGCT patients are not reported. Journal, first author and DOI not stated in the fetched text. Two JGCT-containing tumors counted, one of them mixed with SCTAT.
