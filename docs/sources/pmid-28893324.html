Why it matters for JGCT

Provides a frequency count of JGCT (4 of 22 malignant ovarian tumors) in a large pediatric surgical series; no JGCT-level clinical or outcome data.

Key findings

  • Wroclaw (Poland) pediatric surgery series of 214 girls operated 1991-2014 for ovarian lesions: 127 non-neoplastic, 65 benign tumors, 22 malignant (10.28%).
  • Malignant lesions: dysgerminoma 7, juvenile granulosa cell tumor 4, immature teratoma 4, Burkitt lymphoma 2, and single cases of malignant mesenchymoma, embryonal carcinoma, serous papillary carcinoma, androblastoma and Sertoli cell tumor.
  • JGCT-specific age, stage, treatment, marker values and outcome are not reported; the 4 JGCT are included in malignant-group figures only.
  • In the malignant group, 17/22 (77.3%) lesions were large (10 cm or more) versus 32.8% of non-malignant; solid or mixed structure in all malignant tumors; markers positive in 14/20 (70%).
  • Positive tumor markers, large size and age under 14 years independently distinguished malignant from non-malignant lesions; no malignancy occurred in 54 patients with purely cystic structure and negative markers.
  • Authors report nearly 80% of malignant cases presented with palpable mass, suggesting delayed diagnosis, and propose a gonad-sparing management algorithm.

Presentation

  • Malignant ovarian group: palpable mass 81.8%, abdominal distension, abdominal pain 50%

Notes

Text percentages for ultrasound subgroups contain small internal inconsistencies (e.g. 5.29% versus 5.55%). The paper does not state whether the 4 JGCT were among those with positive markers.
