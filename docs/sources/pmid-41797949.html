Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor of the Ovary in Pregnancy: A Case Report.

Why it matters for JGCT

Adds a pregnancy-associated adult-onset JGCT case with surveillance by inhibin B and a tabulated review of published cases and management.

Key findings

  • 28-year-old primigravida (conceived after ovulation induction) at 22+5 weeks presented with 1 month of dull abdominal pain; ultrasound showed a 15 x 11 cm predominantly solid right adnexal mass with mild ascites; MRI 14 x 11 x 13 cm solid-cystic right ovarian mass with omental thickening (O-RADS 5).
  • Markers: CA-125 785 U/ml, AFP 51 ng/ml, beta-hCG 33547 mIU/ml (in pregnancy); inhibin B within normal limits (86.5 ng/ml) at 32 weeks and raised at 36 weeks (118 ng/ml).
  • Surgery at 23+4 weeks: about 500 ml ascites, 15 x 10 cm solid right ovarian mass with intact capsule, omental nodularity; right salpingo-oophorectomy, infracolic omentectomy, left ovarian biopsy and bilateral paracolic gutter biopsies; ascites cytology, left ovary, omentum and peritoneal biopsies negative.
  • Histology: sheets and nests of small cuboidal-polygonal cells with scant cytoplasm and grooved nuclei, necrosis and hemorrhage; IHC inhibin and calretinin positive; final diagnosis JGCT FIGO stage IA.
  • Because of rising inhibin B, repeat MRI showed no recurrence; elective cesarean at 37 weeks with re-exploration and pelvic and para-aortic lymphadenectomy (frozen and final histopathology negative); uterus and left ovary preserved; no adjuvant chemotherapy; disease-free at 24 months.
  • Literature table of 12 published JGCT-in-pregnancy cases (since 1993): stages from IA to IIIB; stage IIIB and IC cases received chemotherapy (BEP, paclitaxel-carboplatin, or methotrexate/actinomycin D/chlorambucil) with disease-free follow-up of 30, 84 months in some; one IC3 case recurred at 3 months.
  • Authors state that adjuvant chemotherapy is advised for mitotic index >20 per 10 HPF or stage IC or above; inhibin B (unlike inhibin A) is not affected by pregnancy and is a sensitive marker; conclusion that stage IA has excellent prognosis and fertility-preserving surgery has a role.

Presentation

  • dull abdominal pain for 1 month and weakness at 22+5 weeks gestation
  • abdominal distension with 10 x 15 cm mass
  • mild ascites and left pleural effusion

Treatments reported

  • Fertility-sparing staging laparotomy at 23+4 weeks (right salpingo-oophorectomy, infracolic omentectomy, left ovarian biopsy, paracolic gutter biopsies); at 37 weeks elective cesarean with re-exploration and pelvic/para-aortic lymphadenectomy (FIGO stage IA JGCT in pregnancy; n = 1)
    Outcome: no adjuvant chemotherapy; disease-free at 24 months; healthy 2072 g female baby delivered

Notes

Case report; literature table figures are from other papers. The paper's reference citations in the introduction are garbled in the source text. Tumor size in text: 10 x 15 cm clinical, 15 x 11 cm USG, 14 x 11 x 13 cm MRI, 15 x 10 cm at surgery.
