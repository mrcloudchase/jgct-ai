Why it matters for JGCT

Low-value single report of a tumor labeled JGCT in a 33-year-old with no IHC or mitotic data, so the juvenile-type classification is unsupported in the text; shows radical completion surgery chosen over fertility sparing for a ruptured stage Ia tumor.

Key findings

  • Case report from Oran, Algeria: 33-year-old mother of two, labeled juvenile granulosa cell tumor, presenting with paroxysmal left pelvic pain.
  • Ultrasound: heterogeneous left lateral uterine mass 10 cm with solid and fluid components and moderate ascites. CT: well-circumscribed pelvic mass about 80x110 mm enhancing after contrast with fluid areas and calcification, free ascites, no lymphadenopathy. CA 125 62.84 U/ml; biology otherwise unremarkable.
  • Laparotomy: solid-cystic grayish-white tumor of about 8x12 cm with necrotic and hemorrhagic changes and citrine ascites; enucleation cystectomy done first; ascitic fluid cytology negative for malignant cells.
  • Pathology of the specimen suggested granulosa cell tumor (no immunohistochemistry or mitotic counts are given for this patient).
  • A second laparotomy completed surgery with total hysterectomy, bilateral adnexectomy, pelvic lymph node dissection and omentectomy: no residual tumor; three right and four left lymph nodes reactive; staged pT1a pN0 pMx.
  • Authors' discussion (cited statements): JGCT is 5% of granulosa tumors; classically in women under 30 or prepubertal; 92% 5-year survival; poor prognostic factors are large size, ascites and capsular rupture; pseudo-precocious puberty reveals the tumor in 70% of cases; surgery is the treatment, conservative in young women wanting pregnancy with stage Ia.
  • The authors chose radical surgery for this stage Ia patient because of the ascites and capsular rupture.

Presentation

  • paroxysmal left pelvic pain
  • 10 cm heterogeneous left adnexal mass with moderate ascites
  • CA 125 62.84 U/ml

Treatments reported

  • Enucleation cystectomy with ascitic fluid sampling, then completion total hysterectomy, bilateral adnexectomy, pelvic lymph node dissection and omentectomy (staged pT1a pN0 pMx; capsular rupture and ascites cited as reasons for radical surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: No residual tumor, 7 reactive lymph nodes; no follow-up reported

Notes

Classification concerns: patient is 33 years old and the text says mitotic activity is evident in juvenile form but gives no counts, IHC or molecular data for this patient; Figure 4 is described as juvenile-type morphology. The authors call the tumor stage Ia while also describing capsular rupture (which would normally upstage to IC). No follow-up duration or outcome is reported. Text contains translation artifacts from French.
