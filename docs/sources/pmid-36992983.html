Juvenile granulosa cell tumor in an adult woman during pregnancy: A case report and review of the literature.

Why it matters for JGCT

Advanced-stage (IIIB) JGCT in an adult during pregnancy with long survival after surgery and delayed BEP; supports inhibin A/B as markers.

Key findings

  • 33-year-old G3P1 had an 8 cm left adnexal mass found on 22-week anatomy ultrasound (not seen at 14 weeks); an 11 cm solid mass at 4 days later was presumed a degenerating fibroid; MRI showed a 14.8 x 9.8 x 14.0 cm left ovarian mass with ascites and omental, cul-de-sac and probable paracolic implants.
  • At 24 weeks she presented with an acute abdomen; inhibin A 426 pg/mL and inhibin B 2,593.8 pg/mL were markedly elevated, AFP 74.5 ng/mL and CA-125 269.6 U/mL elevated, with normal LDH, CEA and CA 19-9.
  • Laparotomy: ruptured 15 cm left ovarian mass adherent to small bowel and transverse colon with metastases; left salpingo-oophorectomy, omental biopsy and small bowel resection achieved R0; omental biopsy negative and ascites nonmalignant.
  • Pathology: JGCT with capsular rupture and surface involvement, lacking Call-Exner bodies, hyperchromatic nuclei without grooves, numerous mitoses, focal inhibin positivity; outside review confirmed FIGO stage IIIB.
  • Adjuvant BEP was delayed to after delivery because of fetal toxicity concerns, with monthly MRI surveillance; no progression during pregnancy; induction at 37 weeks and uncomplicated vaginal delivery; 3 cycles of BEP started 6 weeks postpartum.
  • Inhibin A and B remained normal, menses resumed, and there was no recurrence at last contact over 5 years after initial presentation.
  • Authors' literature statements: JGCT average age 13 years and 3% over 30; about 90% stage I; in Young et al. (125 cases) 94 stage IAI, 15 IAII, 2 IB, 10 IC, 2 IIB, 1 IIC with 6 of 7 deaths in stage IAII or higher and 0% one-year survival for IIB and IIC; two other pregnancy cases cited had no evidence of disease at 9 months to 7 years.
  • Authors conclude shared decision making should guide chemotherapy timing in pregnancy and that large adnexal masses be removed in the second or third trimester.

Presentation

  • incidental 8 cm left adnexal mass at 22 weeks gestation
  • abdominal pain, acute abdomen at 24 weeks with ruptured tumor and 600 mL bloody ascites

Treatments reported

  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy, omental biopsy, small bowel resection (R0 cytoreduction) at 24 weeks; then 3 cycles of BEP (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin) starting 6 weeks postpartum (stage IIIB, chemotherapy deliberately delayed until after delivery (about 18 weeks from surgery); n = 1)
    Outcome: No evidence of disease; inhibin A and B normal; menses resumed; last contact over 5 years after presentation (conclusion says more than 4 years)

Notes

Follow-up is stated as over 5 years in the case text and more than 4 years in the conclusion. Pregnancy outcomes in the infant were reported as healthy. Many review statements are drawn from textbooks and older series rather than original data.
