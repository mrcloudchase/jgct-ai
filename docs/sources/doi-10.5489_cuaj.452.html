Why it matters for JGCT

Infant testicular JGCT case with IHC profile and a literature-based summary of testicular JGCT features.

Key findings

  • A 3-month-old boy had a hard, mobile, painless right testicular mass enlarged for one month.
  • Ultrasound showed near-complete replacement of the right testis by a 3.7 x 2.2 x 2.5 cm multicystic mass without Doppler vascularization; AFP and beta-hCG were normal for age; karyotype 46,XY.
  • Right radical inguinal orchiectomy was performed; gross specimen was a 4.1 x 2.8 x 2.2 cm testis with a unilocular cystic mass containing pinkish fluid; mitoses were rare.
  • IHC positive for inhibin, vimentin, smooth muscle actin, S-100 and CD99; negative for PLAP and CD117.
  • Authors' review: testicular JGCT mostly occurs in the first 6 months of life (90% under 1 year), fewer than 50 cases reported all with benign course, only two bilateral cases (both intra-abdominal testes), chromosomal anomalies in 20% of reported cases.
  • Authors note the benign behavior suggests a role for testis-sparing surgery, but radical inguinal orchiectomy with surveillance remains usual practice at their centre. Karyotype and metastatic work-up can wait until after pathology.

Presentation

  • right testis enlarged for one month
  • hard, mobile, painless testicular mass
  • no endocrine manifestations

Genetic and molecular findings

  • karyotype: 46,XY (normal) (1/1)
    Review text: chromosomal anomalies (Y structural, XO/XY mosaicism) reported in ~20% of cases

Treatments reported

  • Right radical inguinal orchiectomy (primary; n = 1)
    Outcome: Pathology confirmed JGCT; no follow-up outcome reported

Notes

Case report published April 2012 (DOI indexed with year 2013 in the ledger). Literature figures are cited, not the authors' data. No follow-up interval reported.
