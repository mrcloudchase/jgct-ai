Ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors, pleuropulmonary blastoma and DICER1 mutations: a report from the International Pleuropulmonary Blastoma Registry.

Why it matters for JGCT

Early registry evidence linking jGCT (and gynandroblastoma) to germline DICER1 mutations in familial pleuropulmonary blastoma syndrome, though only two jGCT patients are reported.

Key findings

  • Search of the International PPB Registry (325 children with PPB from 296 kindreds) found 9 OSCST in PPB probands or relatives: 5 Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors, 2 Sertoli cell tumors, 1 juvenile granulosa cell tumor and 1 gynandroblastoma; plus 3 additional OSCST patients (SLCT, jGCT, gynandroblastoma) from families without PPB but with suspected genetic predisposition.
  • Only two jGCT patients are described: case 8, diagnosed at age 2 years, a second-degree relative of a PPB patient, treated by surgery, no evidence of disease at 33 years; and case 11, diagnosed at age 16 years, with nodular thyroid hyperplasia and a lung cyst, sister of a girl with gynandroblastoma (case 12, age 16), treated with surgery and chemotherapy, no evidence of disease at 1.5 years.
  • Germline DNA was available for 8 of 12 patients and 6 of 8 had a DICER1 mutation, including patients with Sertoli-Leydig tumor, juvenile granulosa cell tumor and gynandroblastoma; the jGCT mutation listed is c.3073G>T (p.Glu1025X), identical to one found in a gynandroblastoma (the two sisters).
  • Ages at diagnosis of Sertoli/SLCT tumors in PPB patients (6, 8, 13 years) and relatives (4, 7, 15, 32 years) were younger than sporadic SLCT (mean 25 years, as cited); ovarian tumor ages in family members were 2, 4, 7, 15, 18 and 32 years (median 11).
  • All family members with OSCST were alive without disease at median 20.5 years (range 13-45) after the ovarian diagnosis; numbers were too small to draw prognostic conclusions.
  • Four OSCST patients had nodular thyroid hyperplasia; the authors conclude that ovarian OSCST are a manifestation of the familial PPB/DICER1 syndrome (extending to jGCT and gynandroblastoma), may be the first presentation of DICER1 mutation in a family, and that a lung cyst or PPB-related tumor in an OSCST patient or family should prompt consideration of DICER1 testing.
  • The authors note that juvenile granulosa cell tumors have been described with Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome (citing literature) and that the prevalence of germline DICER1 mutations in OSCST in general cannot be determined from this group.

Presentation

  • jGCT age 2 years in a relative of a PPB patient
  • jGCT age 16 years with nodular thyroid hyperplasia and lung cyst on staging chest CT

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: germline heterozygous loss-of-function variants in OSCST patients (including c.3073G>T p.Glu1025X in a patient with jGCT; same variant in a gynandroblastoma patient) (6/8 OSCST patients with germline DNA tested (all OSCST types combined); 4/6 OSCST from PPB kindreds and 2/3 OSCST patients with no PPB history)
    Results aggregated by the authors to prevent disclosure; Table 2 lists one jGCT with p.Glu1025X, one gynandroblastoma with p.Glu1025X and another with p.V1260fs, and SLCTs with E503X, C748fs, a splice site variant; one SLCT had an intronic variant of uncertain significance. Which specific jGCT patient was tested is not stated.

Treatments reported

  • Surgery alone (jGCT age 2 years (second-degree relative of PPB patient, case 8); n = 1)
    Outcome: no evidence of disease, 33 years
  • Surgery and chemotherapy (jGCT age 16 years with nodular thyroid hyperplasia and lung cyst, no PPB family history (case 11); n = 1)
    Outcome: no evidence of disease, 1.5 years

Notes

Registry report; only 2 of 12 OSCST patients had jGCT, so findings are mainly about SLCT. Genetic results are aggregated by the authors so a patient-level jGCT genotype for case 8 is unknown. The same family/sisters (cases 11 and 12) may be reported again in later DICER1 or OSCST series.
