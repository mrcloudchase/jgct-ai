Why it matters for JGCT

Patient self-report survey of aGCT and jGCT; 52 jGCT respondents give a patient-reported view of age, stage, tumor size, surgery and early recurrence in jGCT. Not verified against medical records.

Key findings

  • Self-report survey (43 items; 2014-2019) of members of the closed Granulosa Cell Tumor-Survivor Sisters Facebook group: 743 analyzed respondents (691 adult-type, 52 juvenile-type); 11 surveys were completed for a person aged 16 or younger, generally by a family member.
  • Mean time since diagnosis 4.4 years (SD 5.9; median 2; range 0-38), with no significant difference between aGCT and jGCT.
  • jGCT age at diagnosis (Table 1): 15.4% aged 0-12, 17.3% 13-19, 21.2% 20-29, 23.1% 30-39, 9.6% 40-49, 1.9% 50-59, 11.5% missing; authors note 50% diagnosed before 30 but a number diagnosed later. All jGCT respondents were premenopausal at diagnosis.
  • jGCT stage at diagnosis (self-reported FIGO): IA 34.6%, IC 32.7%, IIA 1.9%, IIC 5.8%, III 9.6%, IV 0%, missing/unknown 15.3%. Tumors were larger in jGCT: 57.7% >=8.5 cm vs 19.2% <8.5 cm (p=.015 vs aGCT).
  • jGCT treatment: 56% open surgery; less hysterectomy (13% vs 42%) and bilateral oophorectomy (8% vs 43%) than aGCT; 13 jGCT respondents had chemotherapy.
  • Recurrence: 25% of jGCT (n=13) reported recurrent disease; 50% of recurrent jGCT recurred within 12 months of diagnosis versus 11% of recurrent aGCT (p<.01). The authors state there is little information on jGCT recurrence and that only about 50 jGCT respondents limit conclusions.
  • In aGCT (not jGCT-specific), recurrence was higher with tumor rupture (p<.001) or cutting (p=.01) than with an encapsulated tumor, and with later stage; 30% of aGCT reported recurrence overall.
  • Follow-up among non-recurrent respondents: inhibin A&B monitored in 60%, inhibin B 26%, CA-125 33%; scans 51%; few reported AMH monitoring.
  • Authors conclude this is one of the largest treatment surveys of GCT and that treatment patterns are in line with clinical audits; they caution about self-report, selection and survival bias.

Presentation

  • abdominal pain (42.2% of jGCT symptomatic respondents)
  • bloating (22.2%)
  • excessive bleeding (15.6%)
  • amenorrhea (11.1% of premenopausal jGCT)
  • increased sex drive (4.4%)
  • symptom onset before age 20 in 26.7% of jGCT

Treatments reported

  • Surgery (jGCT respondents) (initial treatment, self-reported; n = 52)
    Outcome: 56% open surgery; hysterectomy 13% (vs 42% aGCT, p<.01); bilateral oophorectomy 8% (vs 43% aGCT, p<.01); fertility-sparing treatment more common than in aGCT
  • Chemotherapy (jGCT respondents) (initial treatment, self-reported; n = 13)
    Outcome: 13 jGCT respondents reported chemotherapy; protocol not broken out for jGCT
  • Adult-type GCT chemotherapy (for comparison) (initial; n = 130)
    Outcome: 130/691 (18.8%) aGCT had chemotherapy; BEP 36.9%, carboplatin-paclitaxel 42.3% of those; BEP use 47% before 2015 vs 21% after

Notes

Social-media survey; no clinical verification. Text says 744 completed and 743 analyzed (one removed). The abstract's '25% jGCT recurrent' matches the text (n=13). Table 4 column values for the 'Type of disease' row appear mislabeled in the published table (aGCT row shows 691/483/208). Open-access copy hosted by University of Adelaide. Full-text rule: patients' own report, not forum content as a source.
