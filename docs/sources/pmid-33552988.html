Why it matters for JGCT

DICER1 syndrome review; JGCT appears only as a rare, weakly established DICER1-associated ovarian tumor. No JGCT patient counts or outcomes are given.

Key findings

  • Narrative review of DICER1 syndrome (autosomal dominant, reduced penetrance; DICER1 on 14q32.13); in tumors a germline loss-of-function variant is typically paired with a somatic hotspot variant in the second allele; the review cites a 207-carrier study giving neoplasm risk of 5.3% before age 10 and 31.5% before age 60.
  • In the ovary, Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor is the most frequent DICER1-related tumor, and the review states that juvenile granulosa cell tumor, gynandroblastoma and unclassified sex cord-stromal tumors have also been described; most such tumors are stage I.
  • Table 1 of the review (from Foulkes et al.) lists juvenile granulosa cell tumor with an asterisk and age of onset 'undetermined': the association with DICER1 variants 'may not be so strong to warrant testing in the absence of other features suggestive of DICER1 syndrome'.
  • JGCT is not among the major or minor criteria for DICER1 germline testing quoted from Schultz et al. (major criteria include ovarian Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor and gynandroblastoma).
  • Surveillance guidance quoted from Schultz et al.: pelvic ultrasound every 6 to 12 months from age 8 years until at least age 40 years for gynecologic tumors in carriers (general DICER1 recommendation, not JGCT-specific).
  • Most patients with germline DICER1 variants live healthy lives; tumor occurs in 19.3% of carriers by age 50, with females at risk of gynecologic neoplasms.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: germline loss-of-function variant plus tumor-specific somatic RNase IIIb hotspot variant (general DICER1 syndrome mechanism)
    JGCT is listed only as described in DICER1 syndrome, without numbers

Notes

JGCT content is limited to one sentence and one table row; the record covers only that. Review of other organs (PPB, thyroid, kidney, CNS) is out of scope.
