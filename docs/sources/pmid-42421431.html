Why it matters for JGCT

Formal expert-consensus nomenclature that separates benign infantile testicular JGCT from ovarian JGCT, relevant to counselling and to avoiding overtreatment of testicular cases.

Key findings

  • Consensus classification of testicular sex cord-stromal tumours (TSCSTs) by the 30-member TESST group (GUPS and ISUP), developed through consensus activities in 2023-2025 including meetings in Baltimore (March 2024) and Boston (March 2025); hierarchical tiers: group, category, entity, histological pattern.
  • 'Juvenile granulosa cell tumour' of the testis is renamed 'benign juvenile granulosa cell tumour' within the category granulosa cell tumours (defined as a benign, largely infantile testicular sex cord tumour closely resembling ovarian juvenile GCT).
  • Rationale: testicular JGCT is largely infantile (>90% in the first year of life), hormonally inactive, not reported in adults; molecular alterations of ovarian JGCT have not been identified in testicular counterparts, which show monosomy 10 (~60%); testicular JGCT appears invariably indolent and cured by surgical resection.
  • The descriptor 'benign' is meant to avoid overtreatment and excessive work-up because a subset of the more common ovarian JGCT shows malignant behaviour; authors state benign testicular JGCT does not require aggressive treatment or long-term follow-up after complete resection.
  • Other changes: Sertoli cell tumour NOS becomes 'usual type'; inflammatory and nested testicular sex cord tumour (EWSR1::ATF1) is a new malignant entity; FH-deficient Leydig cell tumour and spindle cell gonadal stromal tumour are new entities; testicular Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour provisionally included (a DICER1 RNase IIIb hotspot mutation reported in one case).
  • Limitations stated: relies on limited datasets, limited molecular validation for some entities, considerable interobserver variability, some entities provisional.

Presentation

  • largely infantile; hormonally inactive

Genetic and molecular findings

  • chromosome 10: monosomy 10 (~60% of testicular JGCT)
    stated as recurrent finding
  • AKT1/GNAS: alterations described in ovarian JGCT (not identified in testicular JGCT)
