Juvenile granulosa cell tumor with an unusual immunoprofile, presenting as precocious puberty.

Why it matters for JGCT

Documents a JGCT with precocious puberty that was inhibin-negative but calretinin-positive, a diagnostic pitfall for immunohistochemistry.

Key findings

  • A 4-year-old girl presented with 6 months of vomiting, abdominal distension and intermittent periumbilical pain, with significant breast development, pubic hair and an infraumbilical mass.
  • Hormones indicated peripheral precocious puberty: estradiol 234 pmol/L, LH <0.5 IU/L, FSH <0.5 IU/L, testosterone 0.5 ng/mL, DHEAS 46 ng/mL, 17-hydroxyprogesterone 0.3 ng/mL, AFP 1 ng/mL.
  • Ultrasound and CT showed a large cystic-solid septated lower abdominal mass; the resected left adnexal tumor measured 22 x 20 x 18 cm, tan, multilobulated, variegated with solid and cystic areas.
  • Histology showed a cellular tumor with irregular, variably sized follicles containing eosinophilic fluid, round hyperchromatic nuclei without grooves and no Call-Exner bodies.
  • IHC was strongly positive for calretinin and negative for inhibin, hCG, AFP, cytokeratin and EMA; the diagnosis of JGCT was made despite negative inhibin, which the authors present as an unusual immunoprofile.
  • The authors conclude that inhibin negativity does not exclude granulosa cell tumor and that calretinin is useful in inhibin-negative sex cord-stromal tumors; they note adjuvant treatment may be unnecessary when completely excised at an early stage.

Presentation

  • vomiting
  • abdominal distension
  • intermittent periumbilical pain for 6 months
  • breast development and pubic hair
  • palpable infraumbilical mass

Treatments reported

  • Laparotomy with resection of left adnexal mass (4-year-old girl with 22 x 20 x 18 cm tumor; n = 1)
    Outcome: no adjuvant therapy or follow-up reported in the text

Notes

Letter to the editor, very brief. Stage, postoperative course, follow-up and the estrogen-related changes after surgery are not reported. Whether inhibin was repeated or a panel including other markers (e.g. SF-1, FOXL2) was used is not stated, so the JGCT diagnosis rests on morphology plus calretinin.
