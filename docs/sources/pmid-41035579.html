Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor in an Adolescent With Xeroderma Pigmentosum: A Rare Case and Implications for Surveillance.

Why it matters for JGCT

Case of ovarian JGCT in a patient with xeroderma pigmentosum; suggests but does not prove an association with DNA-repair deficiency.

Key findings

  • A 14-year-old girl with xeroderma pigmentosum (multiple prior cutaneous squamous and basal cell carcinoma surgeries and a toe myeloma) had an incidental ovarian mass on ultrasound; she had no endocrine manifestations and no palpable mass.
  • Pelvic CT showed a 33 x 27 mm left latero-uterine mass; MRI showed a 31 x 21 mm lesion with restricted diffusion, central necrosis and O-RADS 4 classification.
  • Serum markers were normal: inhibin B 80 pg/mL, AFP 5 ng/mL, beta-hCG 2 mIU/mL, CA-125 20 U/L, LDH 180 U/L.
  • Laparoscopy found a well-circumscribed, predominantly solid 3.5 x 3.5 x 1 cm left ovarian mass without capsular invasion, normal contralateral ovary and uterus, no ascites or implants; left adnexectomy was performed.
  • Histology showed solid nodules of monotonous oval to spindle cells with eosinophilic or luteinized vacuolated cytoplasm, occasional coffee-bean nuclear grooves, 2 mitoses per 10 HPF, fibrous stroma and no Call-Exner bodies; tumor cells were positive for inhibin, calretinin and reticulin (nodule outlines), negative for GATA3.
  • The tumor was FIGO stage IA; a multidisciplinary team judged chemotherapy and radiotherapy unnecessary; follow-up showed no recurrence.
  • The authors link JGCT to xeroderma pigmentosum (especially group C, defective nucleotide excision repair) and recommend proactive prolonged gynecological surveillance in adolescents with XP; no germline or tumor genetic testing was reported in this patient, so the XP group was not stated.

Presentation

  • incidental left ovarian mass found on routine ultrasound during dermatological surveillance
  • no hormonal signs
  • no palpable mass, no ascites

Treatments reported

  • Laparoscopic exploration and left adnexectomy; no chemotherapy or radiotherapy (FIGO stage IA; n = 1)
    Outcome: Uneventful recovery; no recurrence on clinical, chest X-ray and abdominopelvic ultrasound follow-up (duration not stated)

Notes

The conclusion refers to XP type C, but the case itself does not report the complementation group or mutation. Histology noting 'coffee-bean' nuclei and no Call-Exner bodies is unusual for classic JGCT description; diagnosis rests on inhibin/calretinin positivity and morphology. Follow-up duration not stated. Literature in the discussion (Tunisian XP-C series, Bradford 2011, Kraemer 2003, Yurchenko 2023) is cited, not independently read.
