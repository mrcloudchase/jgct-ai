Why it matters for JGCT

General pediatric testicular tumor review with a short JGCT paragraph; JGCT statements are secondary citations without original patient data.

Key findings

  • Narrative review of pediatric testicular and paratesticular tumors; testicular tumors are about 1% of pediatric solid tumors with incidence 0.5-2 per 100,000, peaking before age 3 and after puberty.
  • Juvenile granulosa cell tumor is listed among gonadal stromal tumors (with Leydig and Sertoli cell tumors) and is described as a benign tumor that usually appears in the first year of life with a painless testicular mass.
  • The review states JGCT is associated with Y-chromosome structural anomalies, mosaicism and ambiguous genitalia; histology shows solid and cystic structures surrounded by granulosa-like cells, and inhibin-alpha staining separates it from yolk sac tumor.
  • Treatment of JGCT can be radical inguinal orchiectomy or testis-sparing surgery; the review states recurrence or metastasis is not expected.
  • General management: AFP is the main marker (elevated in about 90% of yolk sac tumors; may be physiologically high under 1 year); testis-sparing surgery with frozen section is increasingly used because most prepubertal tumors are benign; COG staging 1-4 used in prepubertal tumors.
  • Gonadal stromal tumors in this review: Leydig cell tumor is the most common (age 5-10); Sertoli cell tumor is second, benign under 5 years but can be malignant in older children.

Notes

Review; no patient counts. The JGCT claims cite Cortez & Kaplan 1993 and Shukla 2004. Page shows Marmara University authors; first author inferred as Dörücü D from the page header.
