Management of ovarian granulosa cell tumor in childhood: a case report and recommendations for a multidisciplinary approach.

Why it matters for JGCT

Pediatric-focused synthesis and 94-case literature review of juvenile granulosa cell tumor providing pooled presentation, stage, relapse and syndrome-association figures plus a multidisciplinary management flowchart.

Key findings

  • Index case: a 10-month-old girl with bilateral breast enlargement (Tanner B3PH1A1), estrogenized genitalia, clitoromegaly and macrocephaly; estradiol 195 pg/mL, testosterone 1.64 ng/mL with FSH and LH <0.3 mIU/mL; elevated inhibin B; ultrasound 48 x 38 mm and MRI 56 x 34 x 46 mm solid pelvic mass; histology jGCT, capsule intact, pT1apNx, FIGO/WHO IA; no adjuvant chemotherapy; no recurrence at 15 months; overgrowth NGS panel and PTEN MLPA negative.
  • The authors' PubMed review (search December 2024; 46 selected articles, 65 after reference screening) identified 94 pediatric jGCT cases (<18 years): mean age 7.2 years (range 0-17); 15 (15.9%) infants, 42 (44.7%) aged 1-8 years, 37 (39.4%) older than 8; 27 (28.7%) post-pubertal.
  • Presentation in 57 patients aged <=8 years: isosexual precocious puberty 43 (75.4%), vaginal bleeding 18 (31.6%), heterosexual precocious puberty 20 (35.1%; pubarche 29.8%, clitoromegaly 8.8%); in 27 post-pubertal patients menstrual irregularities 11 (40.7%). Among all 94: abdominal mass 32 (34.0%), pain 17 (18.1%), distension 15 (15.9%), ascites 8 (8.5%), acute abdomen from rupture 5 (5.3%), Meigs syndrome 2 (2.1%).
  • Inhibin A/B and AMH were reported as constantly elevated before surgery in previously published jGCT cases; estradiol and testosterone may serve as tumor markers when elevated preoperatively. Two ultrasound patterns are described: solid heterogeneous, and multilocular 'Swiss cheese'.
  • Stage: 71.2% of 66 patients with stage specified were FIGO/WHO I; 95.7% of 47 FIGO I patients were alive at last follow-up; 78.9% of 19 stage II-IV patients received adjuvant chemotherapy; overall 92.7% of 69 patients with outcome data alive (mean follow-up 4.8 years, range 0.2-18); 81.2% of 16 advanced-stage patients alive (mean 4.4 years); 24.6% of 69 had follow-up <1.5 years.
  • Three of five FIGO IC2 patients in the review received no adjuvant chemotherapy and were alive at 3-8 years follow-up, although EXPeRT/PARTNER recommends adjuvant chemotherapy for IC2 and IC3.
  • Relapse occurred in 17.0% of 94 patients (mean time to relapse 1.4 years, range 0.12-8) and 43.7% of those relapsing died of disease; 4.3% developed a contralateral ovarian tumor (median 2.3 years, range 0.12-7); the authors state up to 10% may develop a contralateral metachronous tumor.
  • Genetic or systemic conditions were found in 21 (22.3%) of 94: Ollier disease 14, Maffucci syndrome 1, ovotesticular DSD 1, DICER1 syndrome 1, Beckwith-Wiedemann 1, Li-Fraumeni plus PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome 1, NF1 1, tuberous sclerosis 1. Both the DICER1 and the LFS/PHTS patient died of tumor. Secondary malignancy occurred in 6 (6.4%), including 2 contralateral metachronous jGCT.
  • Recommendations summarized (as authors' recommendations from EXPeRT/PARTNER and ESGO/SIOPE sources): fertility-sparing unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy for stage I with staging inspection, tumorectomy not generally recommended, no debulking in disseminated disease except palliative, PEB or PEI 4 cycles (6 if metastatic) for advanced disease, follow-up every 3 months for the first 3 years, and genetic counselling; authors propose multidisciplinary flowcharts for diagnosis and follow-up.

Presentation

  • peripheral precocious puberty (thelarche, estrogenized genitalia, clitoromegaly)
  • abdominal pain, distension, mass
  • menstrual irregularities in post-pubertal patients
  • acute abdomen from rupture
  • ascites
  • Meigs syndrome

Genetic and molecular findings

  • (genetic testing): NGS overgrowth-syndrome panel and PTEN MLPA negative in the index case (0/1)
    Done because of macrocephaly; DICER1, PTEN and other overgrowth genes are part of such panels per the authors' context

Treatments reported

  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy with omental/peritoneal biopsies and washing; no adjuvant chemotherapy (index case, FIGO IA (pT1apNx); n = 1)
    Outcome: no evidence of recurrence 15 months after diagnosis; inhibin B normalized
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy (literature review, FIGO/WHO II-IV with stage reported; n = 19)
    Outcome: 78.9% of 19 received adjuvant chemotherapy; 81.2% of 16 with follow-up alive at mean 4.4 years (range 0.6-14)
  • Surgery, mostly without chemotherapy (literature review, FIGO/WHO I; n = 47)
    Outcome: 95.7% of 47 alive at last follow-up
  • Cisplatin-based PEB or PEI, 4 cycles (6 if metastatic) as recommended by EXPeRT/PARTNER (advanced/relapsed disease (cited recommendation))
    Outcome: no prospective data; authors note no superiority of either regimen in adults

Conflicts with

Notes

The index case report has an internal laterality inconsistency: MRI described a right iliac fossa mass 'originating from the contralateral ovary' and the figure legend says right pelvic mass originating from the left adnexa, while surgery was a left salpingo-oophorectomy. n_jgct_patients is the 94 literature cases (the index case is additional). Supplementary Tables S1-S5 and Supplementary Figure S1 were not in the text; figures were not available. Review percentages use varying denominators; its cases come from case reports (publication bias). Likely overlap with other case reports in the knowledge base cited in the review.
