Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor with Elevated Peripheral Interleukin-6 Level Shows Prolonged Fever and Delayed Puberty.

Why it matters for JGCT

Unusual non-estrogenic presentation of a pubertal-age ovarian JGCT with systemic inflammation, extreme CA-125 and delayed puberty that reversed after resection.

Key findings

  • A 13-year-old girl had 5 months of low-grade fever (38.6 C on exam), fatigue, a painless lower abdominal mass and Tanner stage 1 breasts and pubic hair; WBC 16,000/uL, platelets 723,000/uL, ESR 140 mm/h, CRP 18.7 mg/dL, SAA 1750 ug/mL, albumin 1.8 g/dL, with microcytic hypochromic anemia.
  • Serum IL-6 was 348 pg/mL (normal <4) and soluble IL-2 receptor 1,465 U/mL (145-519); CA-125 was 6,020 U/mL (<35); AFP, beta-hCG, CA19-9 and CEA were normal; estradiol 14.5 pg/mL, LH <0.1 and FSH 0.33 mIU/mL were prepubertal.
  • CT/MRI showed a 67 x 61 x 74 mm left pelvic tumor, iso-intense on T1 and high intensity on T2, with no metastasis; laparoscopic left salpingo-oophorectomy was done with piecemeal resection.
  • Histology showed sheet-like growth of cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, focal pseudopapillary pattern and follicles with eosinophilic secretion, no nuclear grooves; immunohistochemistry was focally positive for inhibin-alpha, CD56, CD99 and AE1/AE3, negative for calretinin, PAX8, CD10, ER, PR, chromogranin A, synaptophysin, c-kit and D2-40; Ki-67 about 3%.
  • IL-6 immunostaining showed weak cytoplasmic staining in a subset of tumor cells but also in non-neoplastic stroma, so tumor production of IL-6 could not be confirmed; no fresh or frozen tissue was available for gene expression.
  • After surgery fever resolved, IL-6 fell to 1.6 pg/mL by day 10 and inflammatory indices and CA-125 normalized; LH and FSH rose at 1 month (33.19 and 68.49 mIU/mL), first menses came at 2 months, by 6 months estradiol 23.85 pg/mL, LH 3.92, FSH 4.81, and by 1 year Tanner stage 4 breasts and 3 pubic hair; no recurrence at 3 years.
  • The authors speculate that tumor-associated IL-6 caused the prolonged fever and the delayed puberty (via inflammation-mediated GnRH suppression) and propose considering JGCT in prolonged fever.

Presentation

  • 5 months of low-grade fever and fatigue
  • lower abdominal mass
  • pallor
  • Tanner stage 1 breasts and pubic hair at age 13 (delayed puberty)
  • markedly raised inflammatory markers

Treatments reported

  • Laparoscopic left salpingo-oophorectomy with piecemeal tumor resection; no adjuvant therapy reported (Localized tumor, 67 x 61 x 74 mm, no metastasis; n = 1)
    Outcome: Fever resolved at once, IL-6 and inflammatory markers normalized, puberty began; no recurrence at 3 years

Notes

IL-6 source is unproven (authors say so). The mechanism for delayed puberty is speculative. Full text read from a J-STAGE PDF; no PMC ID recorded. Calretinin was negative in this tumor.
