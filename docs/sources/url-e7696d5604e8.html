Testicular juvenile granulosa cell tumour: a case report in a newborn (ECP 2019 abstract PS-16-002)

Why it matters for JGCT

Neonatal testicular JGCT with a slightly raised AFP and a full IHC panel (inhibin, WT1, calretinin positive; AFP, OCT3/4 negative).

Key findings

  • Neonate aged 10 days with an enlarged descended right testis, smooth and firm; alpha-fetoprotein slightly increased; scrotal ultrasound showed a multicystic mass with internal vascular flow replacing the testis.
  • Right radical inguinal orchiectomy without complications. Gross: 2.2 x 1.5 x 1.0 cm gray-white ovoid mass with multiple cysts of light yellow glistening fluid.
  • Histology: follicles of varying size lined by bland oval to round cells in single and multiple layers with basophilic luminal fluid; fibrous stroma with actin-positive spindle cells around follicles.
  • IHC: tumour cells positive for inhibin, WT1 and calretinin; negative for panCK, AFP, CD30, CD117, PLAP, D2-40 and OCT3/4; diagnosis juvenile granulosa cell tumour.
  • Authors state JGCT is the most common testicular tumour in infants younger than 6 months, with few reported cases; its importance lies in the differential with yolk sac tumour and other sex cord-stromal tumours.

Presentation

  • enlarged descended right testis, smooth and firm
  • slightly increased alpha-fetoprotein
  • multicystic mass with internal vascular flow replacing the testis on scrotal ultrasound

Treatments reported

  • Right radical inguinal orchiectomy (neonatal testicular JGCT; n = 1)
    Outcome: no complications; no follow-up reported
