The role of stathmin expression in the differential diagnosis, prognosis, and potential treatment of ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors.

Why it matters for JGCT

Immunohistochemistry of 5 JGCTs within a large sex cord-stromal series: uniform strong stathmin expression, so no discriminatory value against adult GCT; no clinical outcomes for JGCT.

Key findings

  • Tissue-microarray immunohistochemistry (clone EP247) in 390 ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors: 281 adult GCT, 5 JGCT, 33 Sertoli-Leydig tumors, 50 fibromas/thecomas, 11 Leydig/steroid cell tumors, 5 SCST-NOS, 3 Sertoli cell tumors, 2 sclerosing stromal tumors.
  • All 5 JGCTs expressed stathmin diffusely and strongly (>50% of cells in 5/5; overall positivity 90-100%, mean 98 +/- 4; H-score median 280, mean 257.8 +/- 42), the same as adult GCT (281/281).
  • JGCT patients: median age 26 years (range 21-43), mean 30 +/- 11.5; FIGO stage I in 2 cases and not available in 3; survival, disease status and recurrence data were not available for any of the 5 JGCTs.
  • Negative cases (<5% positive) occurred only in fibroma/thecoma (12/50, 24%) and Leydig/steroid cell tumors (7/11, 64%); expression did not correlate with recurrence or FIGO stage and was not prognostic in adult GCT (the only group with enough clinical data).
  • ROC analysis separated adult GCT from fibroma/thecoma (H-score cut-off 180, AUC 0.915, sensitivity 0.889, specificity 0.803), but the authors conclude stathmin has limited diagnostic value since morphology and molecular testing are more practical, and that it is not a prognostic marker; they speculate that lowering stathmin could increase taxane sensitivity (hypothesis, not tested).

Genetic and molecular findings

  • STMN1 (stathmin protein): immunohistochemical overexpression (cytoplasmic) (JGCT 5/5 positive, all strong (>50%))
    JGCT overall % positive range 90-100, mean 98 +/- 4; H-score mean 257.8 +/- 42, median 280

Notes

Series is from the same Prague pathology group as other FRalpha/sex cord-stromal TMA studies; JGCT cases likely overlap with those series. Only 5 JGCTs; the JGCT median age of 26 years suggests many are not pediatric cases. Journal, first author and DOI not stated in the fetched text.
