A rare congenital benign testis tumour in a newborn (ESPR 2013 abstract 076)

Why it matters for JGCT

Neonatal testicular JGCT case with imaging features (complex cystic mass with septal flow), normal markers and normal karyotype.

Key findings

  • Full-term newborn admitted for a left scrotal mass present from birth; testicular ultrasound on day 2 showed a well-defined, large, complex cystic mass with significant septal blood flow on Doppler.
  • Serum tumour markers and karyotype were examined; tumour markers were not elevated and the karyotype was normal.
  • After surgical resection, histology showed juvenile granulosa cell tumour (JGCT).
  • The authors state that testicular JGCT is a benign, rarely diagnosed subset of testicular sex-stromal tumours, most cases occurring in the first 6 months of life, typically as a painless unilateral scrotal mass, hormonally inactive without raised serum markers, and associated with chromosome X abnormalities (not found in this case).
  • The child stays under oncological observation; authors conclude JGCT of the testis should be considered in the differential of scrotal masses in newborns and note that 75% of prepubertal testis tumours are benign.

Presentation

  • left scrotal mass from the day of birth
  • well-defined large complex cystic mass with significant septal blood flow on Doppler

Treatments reported

  • Surgical resection; oncological observation afterwards (congenital testicular JGCT; n = 1)
    Outcome: child remains under oncological observation (no outcome detail given)
