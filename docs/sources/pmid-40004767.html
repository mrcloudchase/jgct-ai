Why it matters for JGCT

Marginal. A general review of giant ovarian tumors in young women; JGCT is reflected by one tabulated case and one general statement on outcome, with no original JGCT data.

Key findings

  • Two own cases (17-year-old with cystadenofibroma, 25-year-old with intestinal-endocervical mucinous borderline tumor) are not JGCT; the paper reviews 39 publications (2019-2024) on giant ovarian tumors (over 10 cm) in patients aged 10-25 years.
  • Among the 39 reviewed cases the authors list three granulosa cell tumors; the review table gives one as 'Granulosa cell tumor, juvenile type, FIGO IA' (age 12, 15.0 x 9.0 x 18.8 cm, laparotomy with left salpingo-oophorectomy, presenting with secondary amenorrhea, abdominal pain and galactorrhea with prolactin 70.5 ng/mL, no complications).
  • The other two granulosa cell tumors in the table are not typed as juvenile: age 15, 22.3 cm, stage 1C, left salpingo-oophorectomy with omentectomy, recurrent ovarian cyst six weeks postoperatively; and age 14, 15 x 10 x 9 cm, hirsutism, amenorrhea, testosterone elevated, oophorectomy after detorsion.
  • The text states that juvenile granulosa cell tumors, despite high mitotic activity, are typically confined to one ovary, and that patients with JGCT limited to one ovary generally have a favorable outcome after unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy.
  • The review also reports that, in a cited series of 63 patients with ovarian tumors over 20 cm, 66.67% were malignant, 26.98% benign and 6.35% borderline (not JGCT-specific).

Presentation

  • 12-year-old with secondary amenorrhea, intermittent abdominal pain, galactorrhea; prolactin 70.5 ng/mL; 15.0 x 9.0 x 18.8 cm left pelvic mass (tabulated case)

Treatments reported

  • Laparotomy, left salpingo-oophorectomy (surgery (single tabulated JGCT case, 12 years); n = 1)
    Outcome: Complications: none listed; FIGO IA
