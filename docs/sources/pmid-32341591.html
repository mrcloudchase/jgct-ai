Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor with an Unusual Clinical Course: A Late-onset and Late Recurrent Case.

Why it matters for JGCT

Documents a late (more than 5 years) JGCT recurrence in a woman first diagnosed at 35, FOXL2 wild-type, with fatal course.

Key findings

  • 40-year-old woman (G4P3) with a right ovarian tumor removed 5 years earlier (at age 35, originally diagnosed as serous cystadenoma, with torsion artifact) presented with secondary amenorrhea, appetite and weight loss; CA125 283 U/mL and estradiol 360 pg/mL pre-op, falling to 9.0 U/mL and <10 pg/mL 2 months post-op.
  • Imaging: 10.3 x 10.4 x 9.3 cm multicystic left ovarian mass with free fluid; surgery found the left ovarian mass plus an 8.0 cm pouch of Douglas mass and 2 small omental nodules.
  • Histology: multicystic with follicular and solid proliferation, irregular follicles with eosinophilic material, monomorphic hyperchromatic nuclei without grooves, pseudopapillary pattern, frequent mitoses (24/10 HPF), focal steroid cells; omental and POD lesions identical.
  • IHC positive for inhibin A, calretinin, CD56 and FOXL2; negative for WT1, p53, chromogranin A, synaptophysin, TTF-1 and OCT3/4. FOXL2 402C>G mutation absent, supporting JGCT over adult GCT.
  • Review of the original right ovarian tumor showed similar features (cystic, pseudopapillary, mitoses 12/10 HPF) and was reinterpreted as JGCT, so the later tumor was termed a late recurrence (more than 5 years).
  • Course was aggressive: further recurrences at 6 months despite anastrozole, patient declined chemotherapy and died 20 months after the operation.
  • Authors say this is the first comprehensive report of JGCT with late recurrence (>5 years), cite that almost all JGCT recurrences occur within 2 years and only 3% of JGCT present after age 30, and advise long-term surveillance; no Ollier or Maffucci features were found.

Presentation

  • secondary amenorrhea
  • loss of appetite and weight loss
  • 10.3 x 10.4 x 9.3 cm multicystic left ovarian mass with free fluid

Genetic and molecular findings

  • FOXL2: 402C>G (C134W) not detected (0/1 (this case); cited 86/89 adult GCTs (97%) carry it)
    Sequencing of tumor DNA with a typical adult GCT as positive control.

Treatments reported

  • Total abdominal hysterectomy, left salpingo-oophorectomy, omentectomy with resection of 8.0 cm pouch of Douglas mass and 2 omental nodules; anastrozole for recurrence (recurrence at 5 years after right ovarian tumor removal; n = 1)
    Outcome: Further recurrences on CT 6 months after surgery despite anastrozole; chemotherapy planned but declined; palliative care; died 20 months after the operation

Notes

The recurrence arose in the contralateral (left) ovary with peritoneal and omental disease, so the authors' 'recurrence' versus a metachronous contralateral primary is an interpretation (the paper itself notes that a 126-month event in another series was regarded as metachronous contralateral). Original diagnosis five years earlier was serous cystadenoma, reclassified on review. Age at presentation of 35 versus 40 at recurrence.
