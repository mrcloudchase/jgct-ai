Fertility sparing surgery in sex-cord stromal tumors: oncological and reproductive outcomes.

Why it matters for JGCT

Review pooling published JGCT fertility-sparing surgery and pregnancy data (26 JGCT with obstetrical data) and summarising ESGO/SIOPE guidance.

Key findings

  • Narrative literature review (PubMed 1994 to July 2021, English) on oncological and reproductive outcomes after fertility-sparing surgery in sex cord stromal tumors: obstetrical outcomes in 255 patients (132 granulosa cell tumors: 98 adult, 26 juvenile, 8 unspecified) and oncological outcomes in 900 patients from 12 publications.
  • Pregnancy after fertility-sparing surgery: 44 of 132 (33%) granulosa cell tumor patients; juvenile 11/26 (42.3%) versus adult 28/98 (28.6%); in articles stating patients tried to conceive the rate was 88.5% (23/26). The authors attribute the JGCT advantage to younger age and lower relapse and chemotherapy rates.
  • JGCT presentation per review: abdominal distension or pain, rarely torsion, hyperestrogenism (pseudo-precocious puberty with thelarche and vaginal bleeding; irregular menses in adolescents), rare virilization; JGCT is ~5% of granulosa cell tumors; most early stage with survival >90% with surgery alone, advanced stage 25-50% 5-year survival.
  • A multicenter study cited (Bergamini 2021, MITO-9) of 17 stage I JGCT: 2 radical, 15 conservative (11 unilateral adnexectomy, 2 cystectomy then adnexectomy, 2 cystectomy alone); no relapses; 6 pregnancies with a 100% live-birth rate in 15 conservatively treated patients.
  • Table 2 lists JGCT series comparing conservative and radical surgery: Wu 2016 (8 JGCT, all conservative, 74 months median follow-up, 75% survival), Zhao 2019 (6 JGCT, 100%), Bergamini 2021 (17 JGCT, 100%).
  • ESGO/SIOPE 2020 recommendations as summarised: unilateral oophorectomy preferred to cystectomy/tumorectomy in stage IA; surgery alone is an option in stage IC1; stage IC2/IC3 and advanced or high-risk (rupture, many mitoses) need adjuvant treatment, standard carboplatin plus paclitaxel for six cycles, BEP for four cycles an option with more toxicity; endometrial thickness to be assessed in asymptomatic patients.
  • Authors conclude that unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy with peritoneal staging is the preferred approach for early-stage sex cord stromal tumors; evidence for ultraconservative surgery (cystectomy) in JGCT and for advanced JGCT is limited, and transplantation of cryopreserved ovarian tissue is not recommended.

Treatments reported

  • Fertility-sparing surgery (unilateral adnexectomy preferred over cystectomy) (early-stage JGCT, literature pooled; n = 26)
    Outcome: 11/26 (42.3%) pregnancies among JGCT patients treated conservatively; 23/26 (88.5%) in articles specifying attempts to conceive (as stated for granulosa cell tumors)
  • Adjuvant carboplatin-paclitaxel x6 (standard per ESGO/SIOPE) or BEP x4 (JGCT stage IC2/IC3 and advanced)
    Outcome: Guideline recommendation as summarised by the review

Overlaps with

  • Lee IH 2011: Clinicopathologic characteristics of granulosa cell tumors of the ovary: a multicenter retrospective study.

Notes

Review pooling published series and case reports; patient overlap with the included primary reports (e.g. Lee 2011, Bergamini 2021 MITO-9, Wu 2016, Zhao 2019, Powell 2014) is certain. Internal inconsistency: Table 1 lists 10 JGCT patients from Lee 2011 with 4 pregnancies, whereas Lee et al. report 11 JGCT; the abstract gives adult-type pregnancy rate as 28.5% and the discussion as 28/98 (28.6%).
