Juvenile granulosa cell tumour of the ovary presenting with hyperprolactinaemic amenorrhoea and galactorrhoea.

Why it matters for JGCT

Documents an uncommon endocrine presentation of JGCT (estrogen-driven hyperprolactinemia mimicking prolactinoma) with fertility preserved after unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy.

Key findings

  • 16-year-old woman (menarche at 14) with 9 months of secondary amenorrhea and 2 months of bilateral galactorrhea; no headache or visual symptoms.
  • Labs: prolactin 7081 mIU/l (negative macroprolactin screen), LH and FSH <0.1 U/l, estradiol 7442 pmol/l, beta-hCG undetectable, testosterone 2.0 nmol/l (borderline), SHBG >200 nmol/l, IGF1 low 129 ug/l, inhibin B 2735 ng/l (ref <341), inhibin A 79.7 ng/l, CA125 20 U/ml and AFP 1.21 ug/l normal.
  • Imaging: pituitary MRI showed an enlarged anterior pituitary without micro- or macroadenoma; abdominal MRI showed a 4.8 cm right ovarian mass with no metastatic disease.
  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy; histology JGCT FIGO stage IA with mostly luteinised cells and scattered irregular follicular spaces; prolactin immunohistochemistry negative.
  • Estrogen and prolactin normalized after surgery; the patient conceived 6 months post-surgery and had a healthy child; estradiol 821 pmol/l and prolactin 327 mIU/l at 2 years.
  • Authors postulate hyperprolactinemia resulted from estrogenic stimulation of pituitary lactotrophs (state analogous to pregnancy) rather than tumor prolactin secretion, and claim this is the first report of such a mechanism in JGCT; they cite 5-year survival 90-95% in stage I and 25-50% in advanced stage.

Presentation

  • 9 months secondary amenorrhea
  • 2 months bilateral galactorrhea
  • hyperprolactinemia
  • no signs of androgen excess

Treatments reported

  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy (FIGO stage IA JGCT; n = 1)
    Outcome: estradiol (821 pmol/l) and prolactin (327 mIU/l) normal at 2 years; conceived 6 months after surgery, healthy child, second pregnancy at time of writing; defaulted from endocrine follow-up

Notes

Single case; prognosis figures and literature comparisons are quoted from cited sources. Patient lost to endocrine follow-up, so oncologic follow-up is limited (hormonal profile at 2 years via GP).
