Precocious Pseudo-Puberty Presenting with Bilateral Ovarian Involvement and Progressing to Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor in a 2-Year-Old Girl

Why it matters for JGCT

Case in which bilateral ovarian cysts with precocious puberty (MAS presumed) preceded a unilateral JGCT; GNAS negative in tumour.

Key findings

  • Girl aged 2 years 2 months with premature sexual development (Tanner B3P2A1, bone age 5 years, height +2.6 SD vs midparental -0.4 SD) and a single 3 cm cafe au lait patch; no bony lesion.
  • LHRH test: basal/peak LH 0.43/0.18 mIU/ml, FSH <0.1 mIU/ml; ultrasound showed enlarged cystic ovaries on both sides (left 63 mm, right 66 mm, largest cysts 38 mm).
  • McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS) was considered likely and tamoxifen 20 mg daily started; after 7 weeks there was rapid growth, breast development to B3-4, menorrhagia and bone age 6.5 years; repeat ultrasound showed a left-sided 10x8x6 cm vascular solid/cystic ovarian tumour with a normal right ovary.
  • The tumour torted within a week and was removed: 850 g, juvenile granulosa cell tumour, FIGO stage IA; GNAS mutational analysis negative; AKT1 testing planned.
  • Authors conclude the aetiology remains unclear, with MAS unproven and evolution towards JGCT, highlighting uncertainty about the relationship between MAS and JGCT.

Presentation

  • premature sexual development, Tanner B3P2A1, bone age 5 years
  • height +2.6 SD
  • single cafe au lait patch on left thigh
  • bilateral enlarged multicystic ovaries (63 mm and 66 mm) on ultrasound

Genetic and molecular findings

  • GNAS: mutation analysis of the tumour negative (0/1)
    AKT1 testing planned (not reported)

Treatments reported

  • Tamoxifen 20 mg daily started for presumed McCune-Albright syndrome (7 weeks), then surgical removal of torsed tumour (2-year-old with JGCT; n = 1)
    Outcome: tumour 850 g, FIGO IA; tamoxifen course followed by rapid height gain and breast progression to B3-4 with menorrhagia

Notes

Whole abstract read from the abstracts.eurospe.org page. Abstract states GNAS mutations have been described in some JGCTs; no outcome after surgery is reported.
