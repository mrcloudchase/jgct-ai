Survival benefit of lymphadenectomy in patients with early-stage juvenile granulosa cell tumor of the ovary: a single center retrospective cohort study

Tumor siteOvary
JGCT patients15

IdentifiersDOI 10.21037/gpm-22-45

Why it matters for JGCT

Directly addresses whether lymphadenectomy adds benefit in early-stage ovarian JGCT; small retrospective cohort with confounded groups (age).

Key findings

  • Single-center (West China Second Hospital) retrospective cohort, March 2013 to February 2022: 16 early-stage JGCT patients met criteria, one excluded (stage III by omental micrometastasis), 15 analysed: 8 with lymphadenectomy, 7 without.
  • Mean age 17.0 +/- 9.08 years (range 3-31); the non-lymphadenectomy group was much younger (9.33 vs 22.11 years, P=0.003); 5/15 (33.3%) pre-pubertal; mean tumor diameter 14.23 +/- 10.37 cm (range 4-35); rupture in 10/15 (66.7%); CA-125 >=35 IU/mL in 6/15.
  • No lymph node metastasis was found in any of the 8 patients who had lymphadenectomy.
  • Median follow-up 31.87 months (range 2-85): 3/15 (20.0%) relapsed (2 no-lymphadenectomy, 1 lymphadenectomy); 3-year DFS 80.0% and OS 93.3%; lymphadenectomy status not associated with DFS (P=0.40).
  • Relapses at median 12 months (range 6-20) in the abdomen/pelvis (2) and sigmoid colon (1), all treated with surgery plus chemotherapy; one patient died of disease (age 18, USO, TC x3 adjuvant, relapse at 6 months); CA-125 at relapse 89.7, 77.5 and 171.9 IU/mL.
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy in 8/15 (BEP 4, BVP 1, TC 3), seven with more than three cycles; no DFS difference between chemotherapy and none.
  • Authors conclude fertility-sparing surgery is the treatment of choice and prognosis good, and that lymphadenectomy showed no survival benefit in early-stage JGCT in their limited data; larger multicenter studies are needed.
  • Authors note 20 JGCT diagnosed at their hospital over 9 years, with peritoneal metastases in 5.

Presentation

  • abdominal distension and pain (about half)
  • menstrual irregularity in three post-pubertal patients
  • one found at cesarean section
  • one prepubertal with breast enlargement, one with vaginal bleeding
  • preoperative or intraoperative rupture in 10/15
  • CA-125 >=35 IU/mL in 6/15

Treatments reported

  • Fertility-preserving surgery with lymphadenectomy (primary, early stage; n = 8)
    Outcome: 1 relapse
  • Fertility-preserving surgery without lymphadenectomy (primary, early stage; n = 7)
    Outcome: 2 relapses; DFS not different (P=0.40)
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy: BEP 4, BVP 1, paclitaxel-carboplatin 3 (adjuvant; n = 8)
    Outcome: 7/8 had more than 3 cycles; no significant DFS difference vs no adjuvant therapy
  • Relapse: surgery plus chemotherapy (recurrence; n = 3)
    Outcome: 1 died of disease (no complete remission); 2 alive with no evidence of disease

Notes

Small n and age imbalance between groups; no multivariate analysis. Text says non-lymphadenectomy group younger. Relapse table lists case 2 DFS 24 months but text gives range of 6-20 months for time to relapse; the text 'Median time to relapse 12 months (range 6-20)' conflicts with Table 2 (6, 24, 12). The study excluded one stage III patient. Possible patient overlap with other West China Second Hospital JGCT reports is not stated. Bibliographic ledger year is 2023.
