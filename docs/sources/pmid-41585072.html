Minimally invasive surgery guidelines in paediatric surgical oncology - role of MIS in fertility preservation.

Why it matters for JGCT

Surgical guidance relevant to JGCT management only by implication: favours open, rupture-avoiding ovarian-sparing surgery for paediatric ovarian masses where JGCT is possible.

Key findings

  • Chapter on minimally invasive surgery (MIS) and fertility preservation in paediatric surgical oncology; JGCT is mentioned only once, as an example of rare malignancy that can occur even in cystic ovarian lesions.
  • Authors do not recommend laparoscopic ovarian tumour resection in children; laparotomy via supra-pubic approach remains preferred because malignancy may be present even in cystic lesions (e.g. rare cases of juvenile granulosa cell tumours) and intraoperative rupture would worsen prognosis.
  • In asymptomatic girls with an ovarian mass, work-up includes imaging and markers (alpha-fetoprotein, hCG, inhibin B, anti-Mullerian hormone, calcium); laparoscopic exploration is recommended as first step to assess the lesion and stage disease, followed by laparotomy for resection.
  • Benign tumours are ~90% of childhood ovarian lesions; ovarian-sparing surgery should always be considered; in acute torsion or rupture, laparoscopic exploration with ascites sampling and detorsion is advised, with oophorectomy discussed only if malignant features are seen.
  • Large tumours extending beyond the umbilicus are a contraindication to open-entry laparoscopy because of the rupture risk; laparotomy first.
  • Other fertility-preservation topics (ovarian transposition for pelvic radiotherapy, uterine transposition, ovarian tissue cryopreservation) are covered generally, not specific to JGCT.

Treatments reported

  • Open (supra-pubic laparotomy) ovarian-sparing resection after laparoscopic exploration/staging for paediatric ovarian tumours (paediatric ovarian tumours incl. rare cystic JGCT)
    Outcome: authors' recommendation; no JGCT outcome data

Notes

General paediatric fertility-preservation chapter; no JGCT patients or data. Recommendations are the chapter authors' practice, not JGCT-specific guideline.
