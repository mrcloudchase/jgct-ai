Rare virilizing granulosa cell tumor in an adolescent.

Why it matters for JGCT

Case report of a rare androgen-secreting JGCT in an adolescent, with IHC profile and rapid testosterone normalisation after oophorectomy; the epidemiologic statements are cited from other papers.

Key findings

  • 14-year-old girl (menarche at 10) with 18 months of secondary amenorrhea followed by virilization: Ferriman-Gallwey score 20, deepened voice, delayed thelarche and clitoromegaly of 5 cm; a 4 cm mass was palpable in the right ovarian area.
  • Total testosterone was 8.84 nmol/l (normal 0.17-2.81); DHEAS, FSH, LH, estradiol, progesterone, TSH, prolactin, AFP, CA-125 and CA 15-3 were normal.
  • Ultrasound showed a heterogeneous right ovarian mass; MRI showed a 36x42x45 mm solid right ovarian lesion with minor hepatosplenomegaly and ascites.
  • Laparoscopy found a 5x4 cm right ovarian tumour with abnormal vascularisation and a normal uterus, left ovary and tubes; right oophorectomy was done with bagging of the specimen.
  • Histology: 5.5x4x3 cm yellow-tan lobulated tumour with solid and cystic areas, lobules, nests and perivascular palisades of cells with scant cytoplasm, ovoid nuclei and grooving; no lymphovascular invasion and intact serosa.
  • IHC positive for inhibin, Melan-A, WT1, calretinin and CD99; negative for EMA, CD117, PLAP, AFP and CD30; diagnosis juvenile-type granulosa cell tumour.
  • Testosterone normalised after surgery and menses returned; follow-up reported at 4 months (testosterone <0.17 nmol/l).
  • The authors state that about 95% of granulosa cell tumours are adult type and 5% juvenile, that hyperestrogenism occurs in 97-98% and hyperandrogenism in 2-3% of cases, and that long-term follow-up is advised because late recurrences are reported.

Presentation

  • secondary amenorrhea for 18 months then masculinization
  • hirsutism (Ferriman-Gallwey 20)
  • deepened voice
  • clitoromegaly 5 cm
  • delayed thelarche
  • palpable 4 cm right ovarian mass

Treatments reported

  • Laparoscopic right oophorectomy (specimen removed in an Endobag), no adjuvant therapy reported (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: Testosterone fell from 8.84 to 0.497 nmol/l two days after surgery, then 0.50, 0.41 and <0.17 nmol/l at 1 week, 1 month and 4 months; normal menses resumed

Notes

Source text is a PDF extraction. The ultrasound text reports a 12x12 mm mass within a 23x16 mm ovary but MRI and surgery found 36x42x45 mm and 5x4 cm masses, a size discrepancy within the paper. The paper terms the tumour 'juvenile-type GCT' while citing the adult/juvenile split; follow-up was only 4 months. Author name from the PDF header (Dorottya Bus).
