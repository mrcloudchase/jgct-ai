Why it matters for JGCT

Radiology pictorial essay that places testicular JGCT in the neonatal scrotal-mass differential with one illustrated case.

Key findings

  • Pictorial review of neonatal scrotal masses and mimics; JGCT is a small part. The authors state JGCTs are very rare but the most common testicular tumors in infants younger than 6 months, and are uniformly benign.
  • JGCTs lack accompanying endocrine abnormalities; a minority of cases are associated with abnormal external genitalia and an abnormal karyotype.
  • Sonography: multiseptated cystic appearance with a hypervascular rim on Doppler; differential includes cystic dysplasia of the testis, teratoma, yolk sac tumor, Sertoli cell tumor and paratesticular sarcoma.
  • Preoperative diagnosis is difficult; a multiseptated cystic and solid mass with negative AFP and beta-hCG is suggestive (AFP is physiologically high in infants under 6 months).
  • Illustrative case (Figure 6): a 3-day-old boy with a left testicular mass seen on day 2 of life; 1.6 x 1.9 x 1.9 cm solid-cystic hypervascular mass replacing most of the testis; beta-hCG < 1 IU/L and AFP 17,260.2 ug/L; radical left orchiectomy on day 6 with JGCT on pathology.
  • Management view: testis-sparing surgery (enucleation or partial orchiectomy with frozen section) is attempted in suspected neonatal neoplasms because benign histology is likely; complete orchiectomy if frozen section is not reassuring; no clear long-term follow-up guidelines, with follow-up advised particularly for tumors with high mitotic figures or poor differentiation.

Presentation

  • scrotal swelling or testicular mass in a neonate
  • no endocrine abnormalities typically
  • minority with abnormal external genitalia and abnormal karyotype

Treatments reported

  • Radical left orchiectomy on day 6 of life (illustrative case) (primary; n = 1)
    Outcome: Pathology: juvenile granulosa cell tumor; no follow-up given

Notes

Review/pictorial essay, not a series. Journal and first author not in fetched text. The illustrative case shows AFP markedly elevated (17,260.2 ug/L) in a neonate with JGCT, consistent with physiologic neonatal AFP elevation noted by the authors.
