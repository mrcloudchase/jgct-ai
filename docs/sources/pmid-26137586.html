A Hot-spot of In-frame Duplications Activates the Oncoprotein AKT1 in Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumors.

Why it matters for JGCT

Identifies recurrent activating AKT1 pleckstrin-homology-domain duplications in about 60% of pediatric JGCTs, one of the main known somatic drivers and a candidate therapeutic target.

Key findings

  • Cohort of 16 histologically proven JGCTs in girls under 15 years (collected 1994-2014, Necker-Enfants Malades Paris and Montpellier); 12 FFPE and 4 frozen samples; peritumoral DNA was available for only 8.
  • In-frame tandem duplications of AKT1 exon 3 were found in 10 of 16 JGCTs (more than 60%); the 10 duplications had not been reported before and all but two were different, which the authors attribute to a dynamic mutational process (DNA-polymerase errors).
  • The two duplications tested against peritumoral DNA were somatic. No duplications were found in 10 adult-type GCTs, 15 colorectal carcinomas, 59 NCI cell lines, or in AKT2 exon 3.
  • Full AKT1 sequencing found conserved-residue point mutations in 10 tumors, confirmed somatic in 7 with peritumoral DNA; in total 14 of 16 JGCTs carried putative AKT1 driver alterations. GNAS R201C/H mutations were absent in all 16 and PIK3CA exons 9, 18, 20 had no mutations in the 7 tumors tested.
  • In HeLa cells the duplicated AKT1 proteins localized almost exclusively under the plasma membrane (with filopodia-like processes), were strongly phosphorylated at S473 compared with wild-type and E17K, and were hyperactive and serum-independent in a FOXO3a luciferase reporter; the Q79K-W80R double mutant (and each mutation alone) was also hyperactive.
  • Phospho-AKT immunostaining confirmed phosphorylated AKT1 in a tumor section (T5) bearing a duplication.
  • The authors conclude that somatic AKT1 mutations are a major driver event in JGCT, that the duplications could facilitate molecular diagnosis, and that PI3K-AKT-mTOR inhibitors in clinical trials open targeted-therapy perspectives; functional work was in HeLa cells, not patient-derived cells.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • AKT1: in-frame tandem duplications in exon 3 affecting the pleckstrin-homology domain (6th beta strand) (10/16 JGCTs)
    All but two duplications were different; somatic status confirmed in the 2 tumors with peritumoral DNA available; none found in 10 adult GCTs, 15 colorectal carcinomas or 59 NCI cell lines; no duplications in AKT2 exon 3
  • AKT1: point mutations at highly conserved residues (e.g. Q79K and W80R on the same allele in T2; E91N, M458I, T21I, P348S; P24L, G232W, D274H, S378F in T4) (10/16 tumors carried point mutations; 14/16 JGCTs had putative driver AKT1 alterations)
    Somatic status confirmed for 7 where peritumoral DNA was available; several tumors without duplications carried two or more potentially damaging variants; two tumors had homozygous/hemizygous mutations
  • GNAS (Galphas): R201C/R201H activating mutations (exon 8 sequenced) (0/16)
    Absence suggested these tumors might harbor drivers elsewhere; previous study from the same group found them in about 30%
  • PIK3CA: exons 9, 18, 20 sequenced (0/7 tumors)
    No mutations found

Notes

Table 1 (clinical features) was not present in the extracted text, so per-patient clinical data (stage, age, outcome) are not captured. Abstract says 'more than 60%', consistent with 10/16 in the text. Text states duplications in 9 of 16 by gel plus T1 (slow band only) = 10. Same group previously reported GNAS mutations in about one third of JGCTs (older cohort), so cohort overlap with earlier Kalfa papers is possible but not stated.
