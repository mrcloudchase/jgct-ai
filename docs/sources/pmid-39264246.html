Why it matters for JGCT

Points to biallelic MAD2L1B-related mosaic variegated aneuploidy as a possible rare predisposition associated with JGCT in two siblings (ovary and testis); evidence is a single sibling pair, and the review makes no JGCT-specific surveillance recommendation.

Key findings

  • This is a review of the 2023 AACR Childhood Cancer Predisposition Workshop update on cancer screening and surveillance for children with recessive genomic instability disorders (ataxia telangiectasia, Nijmegen breakage syndrome, Bloom syndrome, Rothmund-Thomson syndrome, telomere biology disorders, Fanconi anemia, xeroderma pigmentosum, mosaic variegated aneuploidy); it is not specific to JGCT.
  • The only JGCT-specific statement is in the mosaic variegated aneuploidy section: two siblings of consanguineous parents with biallelic mutations in MAD2L1B (as printed in the text) developed juvenile granulosa cell tumors of the ovary and testis, respectively, while a third unrelated affected individual did not develop cancer and other modifying genetic cancer risk factors in the siblings cannot be excluded.
  • For MVA the review reports a convincing childhood cancer risk only for MVA1 (BUB1B; Wilms tumor, rhabdomyosarcoma, MDS/AML/ALL) and MVA3 (TRIP13; predominantly Wilms tumor); none of 15 individuals with MVA2 (CEP57) described in the literature developed cancer.
  • The recommendation for MVA is awareness of tumor signs and renal ultrasound every 3 months from birth to age 7 (supporting SIOP-Europe Host Genome Working Group recommendations) with regular clinical review for rhabdomyosarcoma; no JGCT-specific surveillance is recommended.
  • For xeroderma pigmentosum the review lists skin, oral, ocular, central nervous system and hematological malignancies (AML risk with XPC variants) and recommends dermatological exam every 3 months and ophthalmologic and oral exam at least twice yearly; it does not mention JGCT in the XP section.
  • The review states that heterozygous carriers of recessive genomic-instability genes are not recommended for childhood surveillance.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • MAD2L1B (as printed; spindle assembly checkpoint gene): biallelic germline variants causing mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA) in two consanguineous siblings (2 siblings with JGCT (ovary and testis); a third unrelated affected individual had no cancer)
    Review states other genetic risk factors in the siblings cannot be excluded (cited report, ref 84)

Notes

JGCT is mentioned once in the full text. The gene is printed 'MAD2L1B' in the text (MAD2L1BP is the official symbol of the gene encoding p31comet; this is a point for the reviewer to verify against the cited source, not stated in this review). Related XP-associated JGCT case: pmid-41035579 (the XP section of this review does not mention it).
