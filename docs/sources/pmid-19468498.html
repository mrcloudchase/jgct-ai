Why it matters for JGCT

Secondary (review) source for a single cited case of testicular JGCT in a newborn managed with testis-sparing surgery and no recurrence at 5 years; not an independent patient series.

Key findings

  • Narrative review (Medline to December 2007; 49 full papers plus one abstract) of testis-sparing surgery; highest evidence level 2c, no randomized trials.
  • In the pediatric series table, one Shukla 2004 series of 3 patients (1 testis-sparing, 2 radical orchidectomy) is footnoted as juvenile granulosa cell tumor on final histology, with no local recurrence at 5-14 years follow-up.
  • The text cites Shukla et al. as reporting the first case of a seven-day-old boy with a testicular juvenile granulosa cell tumor treated with testis-sparing surgery, free of recurrence at 5 years.
  • Pediatric testicular tumors are more often benign than adult ones and rarely have concomitant testicular intratubular germ cell neoplasia; testis-sparing surgery is described as most suitable for prepubertal teratoma, epidermoid cysts and Leydig cell tumors.
  • Authors report many pediatric surgery centers now apply testis-sparing surgery first for elective management when serum tumor markers are normal, but call for prospective multicenter studies.

Treatments reported

  • Testis-sparing surgery (juvenile granulosa cell tumor of the testis in a 7-day-old boy (cited from Shukla et al.); n = 1)
    Outcome: Free of recurrence at 5-year follow-up

Notes

Review-level evidence only; the JGCT information is second-hand from Shukla et al. and is limited to one infant. Table footnote labels the 3-patient Shukla series as juvenile granulosa cell tumor while the text refers to a single case.
