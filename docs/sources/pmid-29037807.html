DICER1-related Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor and gynandroblastoma: Clinical and genetic findings from the International Ovarian and Testicular Stromal Tumor Registry.

Why it matters for JGCT

Registry-derived JGCT cohort (n=25) with stage-specific outcomes and DICER1 results showing JGCT is not DICER1-related, in contrast to SLCT/GAB. Short follow-up limits survival inference.

Key findings

  • International Ovarian and Testicular Stromal Tumor (OTST) Registry report on the first 107 enrolled individuals (December 2011 to March 2016) with ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors: 49 SLCT, 25 JGCT, 5 gynandroblastoma, 10 adult granulosa cell tumors and others; pathology centrally reviewed in 98/107 (local and central diagnoses agreed in 80/98, 82%).
  • JGCT (n=25): median age at presentation 9 years (range 1 month to 28 years); 40% Caucasian; most had hormonal symptoms; median time from diagnosis 15 months (0-415); status NED 21 (84%), in recurrence treatment 1 (4%), deceased 3 (12%).
  • JGCT staging and outcome (Table 4): stage IA 7, IB 1, IC 4, IC2 4, above IC 3, unknown 6; 5 received adjuvant chemotherapy and 20 surgery only; 5 relapses and 3 deaths overall; stage IA/IB patients (8) all treated with surgery alone and free of disease at median 9 months (1-77).
  • Of 11 JGCT patients with stage IC or greater, 3 (27%) died of disease at median 17 months (10-44); by the table, IC: 2 NED, 2 relapse, 1 death of 4; IC2: 4 of 4 NED (2 chemotherapy); above IC: 1 NED, 2 relapse, 2 deaths of 3.
  • Intraoperatively ruptured JGCT was generally treated with surgery alone (14 of 24, 58.3%); the most common chemotherapy was cisplatin-etoposide-bleomycin (JGCT=4); one JGCT patient received carboplatin-etoposide-bleomycin.
  • Germline DICER1 testing was negative in all 19 JGCT tested and tumor sequencing found no DICER1 mutation in the 2 JGCT sequenced; no DICER1 mutation was found in any centrally reviewed non-SLCT/GAB sex cord-stromal type.
  • Two patients with DICER1 mutations diagnosed locally as JGCT were reclassified as gynandroblastoma on central review, which the authors cite as showing DICER1 tumor testing can help in difficult-to-classify cases.
  • By contrast, 97% (36/37) of sequenced SLCTs and 4/4 gynandroblastomas had DICER1 RNase IIIb hotspot mutations, about half with predisposing germline or mosaic mutations; authors recommend central pathology review and DICER1 testing for SLCT/GAB, and consideration in other sex cord-stromal tumors if history suggests DICER1 predisposition.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: germline mutation (0/19 JGCT tested)
    versus 25/41 SLCT and 3/5 gynandroblastoma
  • DICER1: tumor sequencing (RNase IIIb hotspot or other) (0/2 JGCT sequenced)
    versus 36/37 SLCT and 4/4 gynandroblastoma

Treatments reported

  • Surgery alone (JGCT stage Ia (n=7) and Ib (n=1), plus 3 of 4 stage IC and 2 of 4 stage IC2 and 1 of 3 >IC and the 6 unknown stage; n = 20)
    Outcome: All 8 stage Ia/Ib patients free of disease at median follow-up 9 months (1-77)
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy (BEP/PEB given to 4 JGCT; carboplatin-etoposide-bleomycin to 1 JGCT) (JGCT stage IC or higher; n = 5)
    Outcome: Of 11 patients with stage IC or greater, 3 (27%) died of disease at median 17 months (10-44)

Notes

Registry enrollment may favor referral of difficult or advanced cases; for 5 JGCT no pathologic material was available and the local diagnosis was used. Stage counts in Table 4 (7+1+4+4+3+6) sum to 25; status NED 21, relapse 5 (some relapsed patients later NED). Likely overlap with other OTST Registry papers.
