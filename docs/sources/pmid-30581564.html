Why it matters for JGCT

Narrative case series where one of five cases is a JGCT in a girl with Ollier's disease; useful for the Ollier/Maffucci association and rupture outcome, but with sparse pathology and follow-up.

Key findings

  • Series of five unrelated pediatric tumors from one surgeon's practice (two ovarian, one gastric teratoma, one mesenteric teratoma, one metastatic choriocarcinoma); only case 2 is a JGCT, summarized as stage II JGCT of the right ovary with tumor spillage and Ollier's syndrome.
  • Case 2: a 13-year-old pubertal girl presented with abdominal distension, discomfort and isosexual precocious puberty; the tumor was 33 x 15.5 x 33 cm and adherent to surrounding structures with omental neovascularity; dissection tore the capsule with torrential hemorrhage that was controlled at the ovarian pedicle.
  • She was followed with tumor markers and imaging and the tumor never recurred; no chemotherapy is reported. The author takes this as consistent with the observation that syndromic patients, even with rupture, have an excellent prognosis.
  • The text calls case 2 a 'juvenile germ cell tumor' in one heading although the series table and glossary state JGCT (juvenile granulosa cell tumor); no histology details, IHC or marker values are given for this case.
  • The author's background statements (not data): JGCT incidence peaks in childhood, tumors grow large but have moderate to low virulence; complete resection gives overall survival 95% in children under 10 years even if the tumor ruptures; JGCT arise from uncommitted mesenchymal stem cells beneath the urogenital ridge; inhibin B and AMH are useful markers; cisplatin, etoposide and bleomycin are used for advanced disease (unresectable, recurrent, high mitotic activity or nuclear atypia, or ascites).
  • Table 7 lists syndromes associated with sex cord stromal tumors: Peutz-Jeghers, Maffucci and Ollier's disease are associated with JGCT; the author states syndromic sex cord stroma tumors usually occur in the first decade and are benign.

Presentation

  • abdominal distension and discomfort
  • isosexual precocious puberty
  • huge 33 x 15.5 x 33 cm ovarian tumor adherent to surrounding structures and omentum
  • Ollier's disease (enchondromatosis)

Treatments reported

  • Surgical resection (ovarian tumor with torn capsule and tumor spillage); no chemotherapy given (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: tumor never recurred (follow-up duration not stated)

Notes

Heading error: 'Juvenile Germ Cell Tumor' in the case 2 title versus juvenile granulosa cell tumor elsewhere. Case 2 is described as 13 years old yet 'Stage II' only in the summary table. Follow-up duration not given. Other four cases are not JGCT and omitted.
