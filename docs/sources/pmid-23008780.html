Why it matters for JGCT

Mixed adult and juvenile GCT case series and narrative review. JGCT-specific content: one infantile testicular JGCT case (20-year follow-up), a neonatal ovarian GCT with hybrid histology, a count of 4 juvenile-type tumors among 37, and cited statements on JGCT age, hormones and prognosis; most review content concerns adult-type GCT.

Key findings

  • Five unusual GCT index cases from one Saskatchewan hospital plus a 14-year (1997-2011) pathology review of 37 GCTs (overall prevalence 0.0041%, 37/902,100), used to discuss 'myths and realities' of granulosa cell tumors.
  • Index case 1: a 5-month-old boy with a 2 cm left testicular mass treated by orchiectomy; histology solid with cystic spaces, extensive fibrocollagenous stroma, inhibin positive; the only male GCT, disease-free 20 years later.
  • Index case 2: a 7-day-old girl with a 10 cm multiseptated cystic left ovarian mass with torsion, estradiol 310, prolactin 34.9, TSH 11.05, normal LH, FSH, AFP and beta-hCG; histology was intermediate between adult and juvenile GCT (macrofollicular pattern with small solid insular areas, no theca interna); cured by oophorectomy, well at 18 years.
  • In the 37-case pathology review (36 female, 1 male), female age ranged 7 days to 85 years (mean 52, median 48); only 4 (10.8%) GCTs were of the juvenile type; sites included 17 right ovary, 15 left ovary and one each bowel, abdominal wall and omentum.
  • Review statements (cited literature): about 95% of GCT are adult-type; up to 90% of JGCT occur under 30 years of age, half under 10 years and 10% in infants under 1 year; estradiol-producing cells are present in 70% of JGCT; isosexual precocious pseudopuberty occurs in 80-90% of JGCT patients under 8 years; average tumor size is 12 cm; few Call-Exner bodies; JGCT is considered by many to be relatively benign with survival as high as 97% at 3.5-year follow-up in one study; advanced JGCT can be aggressive with short interval to relapse and responds to platinum combination chemotherapy.
  • Testicular JGCT (cited): sex cord-stromal tumors are 4% of testicular tumors; JGCT is far more common than adult GCT in the testis; about half are diagnosed in the first month and over 95% in the first year of life; 25% have gynaecomastia; usually benign, treated by radical orchiectomy; stage-matched testicular GCT has a better prognosis than ovarian.
  • Rare JGCT presentations cited: paraneoplastic hypercalcemia and Meigs-type pleural effusion with ascites; tumor rupture mimicking ectopic pregnancy in 10-15% of GCT; inhibin sensitivity/specificity in 30 women with GCT (Mom et al): inhibin A 67%/100%, inhibin B 89%/100%.
  • Adult-type GCT dominates the review's data: late recurrence (quarter recur, mean 5-10 years, 10-20% as late as 20-40 years), BEP as standard for advanced/recurrent disease, lifelong follow-up recommended; the authors conclude GCT is an indolent neoplasm of low malignant potential with unpredictable behaviour and late relapses.

Presentation

  • testicular JGCT: left scrotal mass in a 5-month-old boy
  • neonatal ovarian GCT: abdominal distension, constipation, 10 cm multiseptated cystic mass, elevated estradiol (310), prolactin (34.9), TSH (11.05)

Genetic and molecular findings

  • FOXL2: C134W missense somatic mutation (>90% of adult GCT (cited))
    adult-type; not tested in the series
  • chromosome: trisomy 12/14, monosomy 22; Y-chromosome defects in male GCT
    cited literature

Treatments reported

  • Orchiectomy (5-month-old boy, testicular JGCT, 2 cm; n = 1)
    Outcome: no recurrence 20 years postoperatively
  • Left oophorectomy preserving fallopian tube (7-day-old girl, GCT with mixed adult/juvenile features, torsion; n = 1)
    Outcome: no residual disease on CT; estradiol and TSH normalized; well at 18 years

Notes

The text says the male patient was 'Index case 4' in the surgical review, but the only male described is index case 1 (a 5-month-old boy; case 4 is a 64-year-old woman), an internal inconsistency. Unclear whether the 4 juvenile-type cases include index case 1 or 2. Index case 2 is described as histologically between adult and juvenile GCT, not definitively JGCT; n_jgct_patients=4 follows the survey count. Review literature claims (percentages, survival) are cited secondary data, not original.
