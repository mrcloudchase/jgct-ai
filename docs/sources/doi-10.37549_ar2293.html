Why it matters for JGCT

Neonatal testicular JGCT treated with partial orchiectomy; background statements summarize benign behavior and testis-sparing options.

Key findings

  • Newborn boy (39 weeks gestation) with an enlarged right testicle at birth; ultrasound showed a 7 mm mixed solid and cystic mass at the inferior pole of the right testicle (testis 0.8 x 0.7 x 1.3 cm) with internal color Doppler flow; left testis normal; AFP and other tumor markers normal for age.
  • Partial orchiectomy was performed; histology showed a mucinous lesion with cystic regions surrounded by granulosa cells; final diagnosis juvenile granulosa cell tumor; he recovered uneventfully and was discharged on day 8.
  • Discussion (from cited literature): testicular JGCT is benign, about 3% of primary testicular tumors in boys under 12, the most common testicular sex cord-stromal tumor in infancy with fewer than 100 reported cases; about half present in the first days of life and over 90% by 6 months.
  • Cited features: normal AFP for age (excludes yolk sac tumor), inhibin-alpha positivity, no precocious puberty, association with Y-chromosome structural abnormalities, ultrasound showing cystic or partly cystic mass 0.8-5.0 cm; no recurrence or metastasis reported after orchiectomy or testis-sparing enucleation, and enucleation is advocated when AFP is normal.

Presentation

  • enlarged right testicle on newborn exam
  • bilateral moderate hydroceles

Treatments reported

  • Partial orchiectomy (primary; n = 1)
    Outcome: Uneventful recovery, discharged day of life 8; follow-up with urology and pediatrician

Overlaps with

Notes

Short imaging case summary; follow-up beyond discharge is not reported and no immunohistochemistry or karyotype is given for the patient. Literature statements are citations (Ross 2002, Shukla 2004, Lawrence 1985, Harms 1997 and others).
