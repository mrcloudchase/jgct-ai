Why it matters for JGCT

Epidemiology only: JGCT is reported as the leading sex cord-stromal tumor and tied for most common primary malignant ovarian tumor in a small Nigerian pediatric series; no JGCT-level data.

Key findings

  • Single-institution retrospective review (University College Hospital, Ibadan, 1991-June 2013) of 24 ovarian neoplasms in children under 15 years, 2.8% of all ovarian tumors seen.
  • 14 (58.3%) were benign and 10 (41.7%) malignant; germ cell tumors made up 16 of 20 primary tumors; 4 secondary tumors were all Burkitt lymphoma.
  • Sex cord-stromal tumors were 4 of 20 primary tumors (16.7% of all childhood ovarian neoplasms); the authors state juvenile granulosa cell tumor was the most common of these.
  • The Discussion states yolk sac tumor and juvenile granulosa cell tumor were equally the most common primary malignant childhood tumors; the number of JGCT cases, ages, treatment and outcomes are not given in the text.
  • No surface epithelial tumors and no malignant ovarian tumor under 5 years were seen.

Notes

JGCT case count not stated in the text (Table 1 is an image/not extracted). Statement about JGCT being 'equally common' with yolk sac tumor is only in the Discussion; the abstract says Burkitt lymphoma was the most common malignant tumor.
