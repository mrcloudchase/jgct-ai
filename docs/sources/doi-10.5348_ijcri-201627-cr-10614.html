Why it matters for JGCT

Single case labeled juvenile androgen-secreting granulosa cell tumor with persistent virilization after resection and a false-positive suspected recurrence in pregnancy (fibroid).

Key findings

  • 30-year-old Jamaican woman presented in 2005 with one year of hirsutism, 3 cm clitoromegaly and acne; total testosterone 365 ng/dl and 17-OH progesterone 253 ng/dl; ACTH stimulation excluded late-onset 21-hydroxylase deficiency.
  • Pelvic ultrasound showed an 11 x 7 x 10 cm adnexal solid mass; endometrial biopsy was benign. She had laparotomy, right salpingo-oophorectomy, lymph node sampling, pelvic washings and partial omentectomy; pathology was androgen-secreting granulosa cell tumor of the ovary, stage IA. Testosterone normalized after surgery.
  • Surveillance with examination, testosterone, inhibin A and B and imaging was normal, but hirsutism and clitoromegaly persisted for years despite medical treatment.
  • In 2014 CT showed a 7 cm adnexal mass (7.3 x 5 x 5.4 cm) and MRI an 8 x 8 x 7 cm (8.9 x 7.1 cm in the figure legend) pelvic mass with cystic changes, separate from the uterus, in a patient who had become pregnant; testosterone and inhibin were normal. The tumor board decided on surgery for suspected recurrence.
  • Laparotomy at 16 weeks of pregnancy found an 8 cm pedunculated subserosal fibroid of the lower uterine segment, excised and confirmed on frozen section and pathology; fetal monitoring showed no complications.
  • Author conclusion: recurrent androgen-secreting JGCT is rare, can be confused with a pedunculated fibroid even on MRI, and laparoscopy before laparotomy should be considered.

Presentation

  • hirsutism, clitoromegaly of 3 cm and acne for one year
  • total testosterone 365 ng/dl and 17-OH progesterone 253 ng/dl
  • 11 x 7 x 10 cm solid adnexal mass on ultrasound

Treatments reported

  • Exploratory laparotomy, right salpingo-oophorectomy, lymph node sampling, pelvic washings, partial omentectomy (primary, stage IA (2005); n = 1)
    Outcome: testosterone normalized; surveillance (exam, testosterone, inhibin A/B, imaging) normal; hirsutism and clitoromegaly persisted
  • Exploratory laparotomy at 16 weeks of pregnancy for suspected recurrence (2014 pelvic mass; n = 1)
    Outcome: 8 cm pedunculated subserosal fibroid of lower uterine segment excised (frozen section and pathology); no fetal complications

Notes

Labeling inconsistency: the abstract and conclusion call the tumor a juvenile granulosa cell tumor, but the case pathology text says only 'androgen secreting granulosa cell tumor, stage IA'; the patient was 30 at diagnosis, which is atypical for juvenile type and no histologic or IHC detail is given for the primary tumor. Laterality inconsistency: text says right salpingo-oophorectomy for a left adnexal mass, while Figure 1 legend says right adnexal mass. Pelvic mass in 2014 was not a recurrence. Single author.
