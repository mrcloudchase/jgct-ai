Massive ascites as the only sign of ovarian juvenile granulosa cell tumor in an adolescent: a case report and a review of the literature.

Why it matters for JGCT

Atypical JGCT presentation (massive ascites without hormonal signs) in an adolescent, with inhibin as a marker and 18-month remission after surgery plus 4 cycles of chemotherapy.

Key findings

  • 13-year-old premenarchal girl with 2 months of progressive painless abdominal distension and massive ascites as the only manifestation; no signs of hormonal excess.
  • Ultrasound: cystic lesion with few septations 70 x 64 mm arising from the left adnexa, right ovary also enlarged with a 36 mm cystic lesion; CT: multilocular thick-walled cystic mass from left ovary with large ascites.
  • beta-hCG, AFP, CEA, CA 19-9 and CA-125 were normal; inhibin was not measured preoperatively but was increased immediately after surgery.
  • Surgery: left salpingo-oophorectomy and bilateral omentectomy; about 10 L of transudate ascites evacuated, ascitic cytology negative. Tumor 9 x 5 x 3.5 cm, multilocular cystic with papillary projections.
  • Histology: JGCT with ovoid and polygonal pale cells, irregular follicles, pseudopapillary projections; IHC positive for calretinin, inhibin and CD99. Chest CT and bone survey unremarkable. Staged FIGO IC (surface projections; negative ascitic cytology).
  • Because of residual tumor on postoperative imaging and inhibin level, 4 cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy were given; patient was in complete remission at 18 months. Menarche began about 10 days after surgery.
  • Authors state that to their knowledge massive ascites as the only clinical manifestation of JGCT had not been reported. They cite platinum-based chemotherapy response rates of 63% to 80% in advanced or recurrent disease and 5-year survival for stage I GCT above 90% (as low as 75% in some reports).

Presentation

  • progressive painless abdominal distension for about 2 months
  • massive ascites (about 10 L transudate evacuated)
  • premenarchal; no precocious puberty

Treatments reported

  • Left salpingo-oophorectomy and bilateral omentectomy, then 4 cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy (text names carboplatin, bleomycin, etoposide, vinblastine; discussion says '4 courses of ICE') (primary surgery plus adjuvant for residual tumor on postoperative imaging and raised postoperative inhibin; n = 1)
    Outcome: Complete remission without residual tumor at 18-month follow-up; normal imaging after chemotherapy started

Notes

Internal inconsistency: the case text names carboplatin, bleomycin, etoposide, vinblastine whereas the discussion says 'ICE' (4 courses). Stage IC assigned on surface projections although ascitic cytology was negative. Right ovary had a 36 mm cyst on ultrasound; its fate is not described.
