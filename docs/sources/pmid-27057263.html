Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumour of the Ovary with Unilocular Pure Cystic Presentation: A Case Report and Review of Literature.

Why it matters for JGCT

Unusual purely cystic unilocular JGCT in an 18-year-old, older than typical, with elevated estradiol and virilization features.

Key findings

  • 18-year-old unmarried girl with abdominal pain and distension for 2 months and facial hair for 1 year; no precocious puberty on retrospective evaluation.
  • Ultrasound showed a large anechoic cystic lesion with posterior acoustic enhancement and no internal echoes or septations; CT showed a large unilocular fluid-density lesion (reported as 85x110x125 cm AP x TR x CC, as printed) without enhancing soft tissue or septae; left ovary not seen separately.
  • Imaging diagnosis was a benign large left ovarian cyst. Estradiol was elevated at 450 pg/mL (reference 15-350); CA-125 and beta-hCG were normal.
  • Left ovarian cystectomy found a cyst wall with a few intervening solid areas; histology showed round to oval cells with moderately pleomorphic nuclei, prominent nucleoli, scanty cytoplasm, irregular nuclear margins, some grooves, and Call-Exner bodies, diagnosed as granulosa cell tumor (juvenile type per title and discussion).
  • The authors propose that a malignant ovarian lesion be included in the differential diagnosis of a unilocular, purely cystic ovarian lesion even when no malignant imaging criteria are present.
  • Discussion (literature-based statements by authors): juvenile form is about 5% of granulosa cell tumors, survival up to 97% cited, and about 70% of GCTs secrete estrogen; morphology and imaging cannot reliably separate adult from juvenile types.

Presentation

  • chronic abdominal pain and distension for 2 months
  • facial hair for 1 year (virilization features)
  • large pelvic cystic mass reaching xiphisternum

Treatments reported

  • Left ovarian cystectomy (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: Diagnosis of granulosa cell tumor on histology; no further treatment or follow-up reported

Notes

CT dimensions printed as 85x110x125 cm, evidently a unit error (likely mm). Case was treated by cystectomy only; no follow-up, staging or IHC reported. JGCT subtype is asserted in title/abstract; the case-description text says 'Granulosa cell tumour'.
