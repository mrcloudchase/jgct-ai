Why it matters for JGCT

One JGCT patient with ruptured tumor treated with fertility-sparing surgery plus BEP and two later pregnancies; chemotherapy choice follows a germ cell regimen.

Key findings

  • Three-case report (immature teratoma, juvenile cystic granulosa cell tumor, dysgerminoma) of fertility-sparing surgery followed by chemotherapy in young women.
  • JGCT case (Mrs. C, 25 years, married): acute abdominal pain with ascites; ultrasound suggested ruptured ovarian cyst (2009); laparotomy and left ovariotomy; histopathology juvenile cystic granulosa cell tumor; capsule rupture led to upstaging.
  • She received four cycles of BEP (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin), did not conceive for 4 years (husband had oligospermia), conceived on the second clomiphene cycle with antioxidants, delivered by cesarean for fetal distress, and had a second spontaneous pregnancy 2 years later.
  • All three women achieved pregnancy and had normal children; there was no recurrence to date and the authors conclude tumor spill or capsule rupture should not prompt radical surgery in women wishing to preserve fertility.
  • Authors state juvenile GCT is rarer than adult GCT and usually presents as a predominantly solid mass; BEP is the most widely used regimen for germ cell tumors, whose duration has no consensus.

Presentation

  • acute abdominal pain
  • ascites
  • ruptured ovarian cyst on ultrasound

Treatments reported

  • Left ovariotomy (unilateral oophorectomy) then 4 cycles of BEP (fertility-sparing surgery, upstaged because of rupture; n = 1)
    Outcome: Two live births (one after infertility treatment, one spontaneous), no recurrence to date
