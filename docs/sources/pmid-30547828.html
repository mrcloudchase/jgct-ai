Rapidly growing juvenile granulosa cell tumor of the ovary arising in adult: a case report and review of the literature.

Why it matters for JGCT

Documents a histologically diagnosed JGCT in an adult (38 years), a diagnostic pitfall versus adult GCT, with possible rapid growth and a high mitotic index.

Key findings

  • A 38-year-old Japanese woman presented with abdominal distension and frequent urination; a laparoscopic myomectomy about one year earlier had shown macroscopically normal ovaries, suggesting rapid tumor growth.
  • Estradiol was 214.5 pg/mL (normal 70-160), FSH <0.1 mIU/mL and LH 2.0 mIU/mL (suppressed by feedback), testosterone normal, CA-125 39.2 U/mL (slightly elevated); no menstrual irregularity.
  • MRI showed a 10 x 10 x 12 cm multiloculated cystic right ovarian mass with fluid-fluid levels, intracystic hemorrhage, strongly enhancing septa with high diffusion signal, small ascites, a normal-size uterus without endometrial thickening and an atrophic left ovary (1.7 cm).
  • Frozen section could not exclude malignancy (nuclear atypia; differential yolk sac tumor, malignant mucinous tumor, AGCT) and lacked coffee-bean nuclei and Call-Exner bodies; hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, partial omentectomy and appendectomy were done.
  • Final pathology: 13 cm multiloculated cystic tumor with hemorrhage, macrofollicles, brisk focal mitoses (average 10 per 10 HPF), MIB-1 index about 30%; positive for vimentin, calretinin, CD99 and alpha-inhibin; diagnosis JGCT, FIGO IA, no further treatment.
  • The authors conclude that JGCT can occur in adults, that intraoperative diagnosis may be difficult, that MRI helps, and that the tumor appeared to grow rapidly, possibly related to nuclear atypia and high mitotic rate; the atrophic contralateral ovary may be a clue to hormonal activity.
  • The authors cite a similar adult JGCT in a 43-year-old woman, and cite JGCT as accounting for under 5% of GCTs, mean age about 13 years, 97% before age 30 in one series.

Presentation

  • abdominal distension
  • frequent urination
  • pelvic mass about 12 cm
  • no menstrual irregularity despite elevated estradiol
  • atrophic contralateral ovary

Treatments reported

  • Total abdominal hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, partial omentectomy and appendectomy; no additional treatment (FIGO IA JGCT in adult; n = 1)
    Outcome: no follow-up outcome reported in the text

Notes

Rapid growth is inferred from normal-appearing ovaries at myomectomy one year earlier, not from serial imaging. The 'review of the literature' is brief narrative discussion. No postoperative follow-up duration is given. The abstract text says right ovarian mass; consistent with full text.
