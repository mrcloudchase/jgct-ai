Why it matters for JGCT

Epidemiology and pathology series of ovarian granulosa cell tumors in Lebanon; JGCT is a 6-patient subgroup with age, size, histology and immunohistochemistry compared against adult-type tumors.

Key findings

  • Retrospective single-center (National Institute of Pathology, Beirut) study of 65 consecutive ovarian granulosa cell tumors (GCT) referred from Lebanese hospitals January 2000 to January 2020; 6 (9.2%) juvenile and 59 (90.8%) adult type. Calculated GCT incidence 16.2 per million per year (authors describe this as upper end of international range).
  • JGCT group (n=6): mean age 21 years (adult group 60); high tumor size >10 cm in 4/6 (66.7%) versus 20/59 (33.9%) adults; high mitotic index 1/6; grooved nuclei 1/6; Call-Exner bodies 1/6; microfollicular 3/6, macrofollicular 1/6, trabecular 1/6, insular 1/6. Authors report significant association of JGCT with young age and large size (p<0.05).
  • Recurrence: 13/65 (20%) overall, 12 adult and 1 JGCT (1/6, 16.7%); recurrence not significantly related to type (p=0.09); recurrent cases were stage 3 (6) or 4 (6) except one stage 2, and had high mitotic index (10/13) and tumor size >10 cm (12/13).
  • Stage was known for only 35 patients because of loss to follow-up; stage was not reported separately for JGCT.
  • Immunohistochemistry (tests performed varied): inhibin positive 30/40 overall with 3 JGCT positive and 2 negative; calretinin 18/23 overall, JGCT 2 positive; cytokeratin 10/20, JGCT 1 positive and 1 negative; CD99 19/23, JGCT 2 positive; vimentin 18/18, JGCT 2 positive.
  • FOXL2 testing was not performed because it is not covered by payers in Lebanon; authors suggest some tumors may be classified as GCT by default and possibly over-diagnosed.
  • Authors conclude older age, high mitotic index, large size and advanced stage predict recurrence, and call for FOXL2 access and further Mediterranean epidemiology; JGCT conclusions rest on 6 patients.

Presentation

  • abdominal pain, postmenopausal or intermenstrual bleeding in GCT overall (not broken down for JGCT)

Notes

Internal inconsistencies: abstract states 91% adult versus 19% juvenile (sums above 100%; text and Table 1 give 6/65 = 9.2% JGCT); JGCT mean age is 21 years in Table 1 but 13 in the Discussion; the p-value cited for recurrent-case predictors is given as 0.09 in the same sentence as 'significant'. Table 3 inhibin counts imply 3 JGCT positive and 2 negative (the percent columns in the paper are mislabeled). No survival data. No FOXL2 testing. Journal and first author not stated in the retrieved full text.
