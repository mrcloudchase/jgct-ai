Why it matters for JGCT

Marginal. Diagnostic-accuracy study of ovarian cysts in young women in which one JGCT is counted among 48 complex cysts; no patient-level JGCT data.

Key findings

  • Prospective cohort of 300 patients (described as adolescents; mean age 18.9 +/- 1.3 years, 83.3% married) with ovarian cysts at Al-Azhar University Hospital (Assiut); 252 (84%) simple and 48 (16%) complex cysts on ultrasound, mean size 8.42 +/- 2.87 cm.
  • Histopathology of the complex cysts included 27 dermoid cysts, 7 endometriomas, 8 serous cystadenomas, 3 mucinous cystadenomas, 2 immature teratomas and 1 juvenile granulosa cell tumor (2% of the complex cysts).
  • The single JGCT was detected by ultrasound and by tumor markers (reported detection 1/1, 100%); no clinical details, stage, treatment or outcome of this patient are given.
  • Overall ultrasound sensitivity and specificity were 87% and 84% in the text (Table 6: 87% and 79.5%), tumor markers 84% and 80%, combined ultrasound plus markers sensitivity 89.6% and specificity 83.3%.
  • Markers measured included beta-hCG, LDH, CA-125, AFP, inhibin, estrogen and testosterone; the authors conclude that ultrasound is the first-line tool and that adding tumor markers helps exclude malignancy.

Notes

Internal inconsistencies: specificity of ultrasound 84% (text/abstract) vs 79.5% (Table 6); symptom table is for N=48 not 300; the cohort is called adolescent but mean age is 18.9 years with 83.3% married. The paper's background states juvenile granulosa cell tumor is about 50% of malignant sex cord-stromal cancers in this age group (cited, not derived from the study data).
