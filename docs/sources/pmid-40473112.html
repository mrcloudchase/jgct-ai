Why it matters for JGCT

Testicular tumor pathology consensus exercise; JGCT is reported as 3 of 44 cases. Supports that testicular JGCT is reproducibly recognizable.

Key findings

  • TEsticular Sex cord-Stromal Tumor (TESST) group, cosponsored by ISUP and GUPS: 44 scanned H&E slides of testicular sex cord-stromal tumors reviewed online by 17 of 18 invited expert pathologists; consensus defined as 70% or more agreement.
  • Consensus was reached in 26/44 cases (59%); a majority (over 50%) diagnosis was reached in 75% of cases.
  • All 3 juvenile granulosa cell tumors reached consensus (100% for cases 35 and 36; 70%-79% for case 4); the authors conclude JGCT of the testis can be reliably identified on morphology with minimal workup.
  • Only the single adult granulosa cell tumor case (with FOXL2 p.C134W, 14/17 = 82%) reached consensus; adult GCT was frequently diagnosed by some experts in other tumor types, suggesting overdiagnosis, and a cited analysis found FOXL2 pathogenic variant in only 1/13 testicular adult GCT.
  • Table 2 states that because testicular JGCT is very different from its ovarian counterpart, a renaming emphasizing its benign nature is suggested.
  • Consensus statements: WHO 2022 classification is not aligned with best evidence; terminology for gonadal stromal tumors, Sertoli, granulosa and mixed tumors needs standardization; referral to a specialist genitourinary pathologist is recommended; recommendations on diagnostic workup in pediatric sex cord-stromal tumors are needed.
  • Other categories: Sertoli cell tumor consensus 8/12, sex cord-stromal NOS 2/8, Leydig cell tumor 6/6, large cell calcifying Sertoli cell tumor 2/4.

Overlaps with

Notes

Peer-reviewed expert-panel study rather than a formal guideline; tier set to guideline because it issues consensus statements and recommendations for a classification. Genetic results were withheld from participants. Supplementary tables not read.
