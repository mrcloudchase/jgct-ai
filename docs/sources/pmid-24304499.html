Why it matters for JGCT

Large histopathology series of ovarian SCSTs that contains 24 JGCTs with age, size, surgery type, cytokeratin IHC and limited follow-up (11 patients).

Key findings

  • Single-center retrospective study (Aga Khan University Hospital, Karachi, 1992-2012) of 480 ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors (SCST); median age 45 years (range 1-92); 24 were juvenile granulosa cell tumors (5%) and 211 (43.9%) adult GCT.
  • JGCT: median age 19 years and mean tumor size 15 cm (Table 3); the authors state JGCT was the most frequent SCST in children and that JGCT were around 10% of all GCT in this series, higher than in other large series.
  • IHC was done in 20 JGCTs and showed negative cytokeratins; across all GCT inhibin was positive in every case tested, calretinin about 90% and CD99 68% of the 163 tested adult/granulosa cases (JGCT split not given).
  • Follow-up of only 11 JGCT cases was available: 2 recurred after 8 and 17 months and one died of disease after 7 months.
  • Authors note JGCT shows greater nuclear pleomorphism and mitotic activity than AGCT, and that cytokeratin negativity helps exclude epithelial malignancy.
  • In comparison, for adult GCT (follow-up available for 86): 5 late recurrences (18-52 months), 4 with metastasis, 2 deaths from AGCT (11 and 18 months); 13 AGCT had spread at presentation.
  • Authors conclude SCSTs are uncommon with good prognosis in the Pakistani population, that IHC is needed to exclude epithelial tumors, and that follow-up data were missing for about half the patients (FOXL2 and SF-1 not available).

Presentation

  • in the whole SCST series: 7 patients with precocious puberty; JGCT-specific presentation not separated
  • children: JGCT was the most frequent SCST

Treatments reported

  • Surgical specimens for JGCT (n=24): TAH-BSO 7, unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy 5, BSO 3, TAH-USO 5; no chemotherapy details given (surgery; n = 24)
    Outcome: follow-up available for 11: 2 recurred (at 8 and 17 months) and 1 died of disease (at 7 months)

Notes

Sample is referral/second-opinion biased (the authors say). JGCT percent of GCT: 24/235 is about 10% as the authors write; that percentage was computed by the authors. JGCT follow-up is available for under half of patients so recurrence and death counts cannot be read as rates. Stage not given for JGCT.
