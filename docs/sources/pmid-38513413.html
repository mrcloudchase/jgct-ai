Vaginal bleeding imitated rape in a 6-year old girl, a case report about granulosa cell tumor as a reason of peripheral precocious puberty.

Why it matters for JGCT

Prepubertal granulosa cell tumor with peripheral precocious puberty and markedly elevated AMH and inhibins that normalized after surgery; histologic subtype is ambiguous.

Key findings

  • A 6-year-old girl admitted with 5 days of abdominal distension, lower abdominal pain, nausea and mild fever; blood stains in underwear prompted a forensic consultation for suspected abuse, but thelarche pointed to an endocrine cause.
  • Ultrasound: multilocular-solid right-sided mass about 63 x 40 mm; CT: solid-cystic pelvic mass 122 x 106 mm.
  • Hormones: estradiol 115 pg/mL (normal under 9), FSH under 0.55, LH under 0.10, androstenedione 5.8, AMH 1322 pmol/L (normal 0.1-38.6), inhibin A 291 pg/mL (normal under 4.7), inhibin B 956 pg/mL (normal under 111), AFP and beta-hCG normal.
  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy; the opposite ovary and uterus were preserved. Specimen 11.5 x 8 x 2 cm multilocular cyst with thin septa and intact capsule; microscopy showed granulosa cells lining the cyst with microfollicles resembling Call-Exner bodies; inhibin and calretinin positive.
  • All hormones returned to normal prepubertal levels two weeks after surgery; the authors suggest inhibin and hormone monitoring as tumor markers, with follow-up every 3 months in an endocrine clinic and pelvic ultrasound and labs at least every 6 months.
  • The authors write that the microscopic characteristics were highly compatible with adult-type GCT and that assigning type by age range is not reliable; no association syndromes were present.

Presentation

  • abdominal distension and lower abdominal pain for 5 days with nausea and mild fever
  • vaginal bleeding
  • thelarche
  • multilocular-solid ovarian mass

Treatments reported

  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: All hormones returned to normal prepubertal levels at 2 weeks; follow-up beyond that not reported

Notes

The abstract and title call it juvenile granulosa cell tumor in the abstract, but the Discussion says the histology was too compatible with adult GCT; the subtype is therefore uncertain. Stage and follow-up duration are not reported. The tumor was an almost entirely cystic variant. Journal and DOI not stated in the retrieved text.
