Why it matters for JGCT

Single-centre histopathology frequency study; its only JGCT content is the count of 3 juvenile granulosa cell tumours among 390 ovarian neoplasms (and 27 sex cord-stromal tumours) in a Pakistani laboratory.

Key findings

  • Cross-sectional histopathology study of 390 ovarian neoplasms examined November 2020 to October 2021 at the Chughtai Institute of Pathology, Lahore, Pakistan, classified by WHO; ages 10-80 years.
  • Of 390 tumours, 320 (82.05%) were benign, 11 (2.82%) borderline, 57 (14.61%) malignant and 2 (0.52%) metastatic.
  • Surface epithelial tumours were 246 (63.08%), germ cell tumours 115 (29.48%) and sex cord-stromal tumours 27 (6.92%).
  • Among the 27 sex cord-stromal tumours: fibroma 10, adult granulosa cell tumour 7, thecoma 5, juvenile granulosa cell tumour 3 (0.76% of all tumours), Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour 2.
  • Sex cord-stromal tumours had no cases in the 11-20 year age group and peaked at 41-50 years (9 cases); the age of the 3 JGCT cases is not given separately.
  • No clinical follow-up, treatment, marker or outcome data are reported; the authors list lack of follow-up and single-centre design as limitations.

Notes

First author not stated in the extracted text. Percentages are of all 390 neoplasms. No age, stage or IHC detail for the JGCT cases; the discussion text does not mention JGCT separately. Not a pediatric series (ages 10-80).
