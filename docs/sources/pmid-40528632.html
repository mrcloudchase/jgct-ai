Why it matters for JGCT

Guideline-level statement that testicular JGCT is benign and genetically distinct from ovarian JGCT, with a proposed rename; also summarizes ovarian JGCT features for contrast.

Key findings

  • Consensus statement of 20 genitourinary pathologists (modified Delphi, three questionnaires, live vote 3/23/2024 at Johns Hopkins) with 70% agreement threshold; testicular sex cord-stromal tumors are about 5% of testicular neoplasms, about 90% are indolent and about 10% aggressive.
  • Recommendation: juvenile granulosa cell tumor of the testis should be renamed 'benign juvenile granulosa cell tumour' (consensus on need to rename 12/15, 80%; on the name 15/15, 100%), with a comment that it differs clinically and molecularly from ovarian JGCT, to avoid overtreatment.
  • Panel states testicular JGCT is largely an infantile entity with more than 90% of cases in the first year of life, mostly hormonally inactive, a subset arising in dysgenetic gonads, and apparently without malignant potential; a genomic study of a limited number of cases showed they lack the alterations of ovarian JGCT.
  • Panel states ovarian JGCT mostly affects children (median age about 8 years) with a subset in post-pubertal children and adults including during pregnancy, characteristically produces sex steroids, and about 10% behave aggressively with abdominal/pelvic recurrence or distant metastasis; ovarian tumors carry AKT1 pleckstrin-homology-domain internal tandem duplications and hotspot GNAS gain-of-function variants.
  • Brisk mitotic activity predicts aggressive potential in Leydig and Sertoli cell tumors but not in juvenile granulosa cell tumor; JGCT, myoid gonadal stromal tumor, fibroma/thecoma are described as invariably indolent in published data.
  • Testicular adult granulosa cell tumor should be diagnosed only sparingly (consensus 16/20, 80%); FOXL2 p.C134W (present in more than 90-95% of ovarian adult GCT) is found only in a small subset of testicular homonyms.
  • Other recommendations: FH-deficient Leydig cell tumor (14/18, 78%), inflammatory and nested testicular sex cord tumor with EWSR1::ATF1 as a distinct entity (15/18, 83%), 'gonadal stromal tumour' umbrella for pure spindle cell tumors (16/20, 80%), testicular Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor as a rare distinct entity (15/15), do not use pTNM staging for these tumors (14/15, 93%), and classify post-pubertal tumors without metastasis as low or high risk (13/13).
  • No consensus on whether myoid gonadal stromal tumor is a distinct entity (12/20, 60%), FH IHC in Leydig tumors without worrisome features (12/20), a mixed germ cell/sex cord-stromal category (13/20) or Ki-67 for risk (9/18).

Genetic and molecular findings

  • AKT1 / GNAS: AKT1 internal tandem duplications (pleckstrin homology domain) and hotspot gain-of-function GNAS variants described in ovarian JGCT; not found in the limited number of testicular JGCT studied
    Statement by the panel citing prior studies; no new data in this paper.
