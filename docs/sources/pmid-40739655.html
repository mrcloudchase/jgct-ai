Why it matters for JGCT

Minimal but useful for molecular pathology: a 2025 expert review stating that JGCT lacks a recurrent diagnostic molecular event (unlike FOXL2 in adult granulosa cell tumor), with only candidate prognostic genes and Ollier/Maffucci association listed.

Key findings

  • Narrative pathology review of recent developments in gynecological tumor classification (2020 WHO 5th edition and later); no new data; 6th WHO edition expected 2026.
  • Table 1 (molecular events in ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors): for juvenile granulosa cell tumor (JGCT) there are no recurrent molecular events useful in diagnosis; other molecular events that may be useful for prognostication are TERT, AKT1 (printed 'AKTC' in the table), GNAS and KMT2C; associated tumor syndromes are Ollier disease and Maffucci syndrome.
  • By contrast, adult granulosa cell tumors contain somatic FOXL2 (C134W missense) mutations in well over 90% of cases, and moderately/poorly differentiated Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors very commonly contain DICER1 mutations (somatic or germline); these are the molecular events the author highlights as diagnostically useful.
  • The author notes immunohistochemistry confirms a sex cord-stromal tumor but is of minimal value in distinguishing between tumor types, and that rarity and morphologic overlap mean many cases benefit from specialist pathology referral, which also facilitates case-series accrual.
  • The review otherwise covers endometrial TCGA classification, LVSI, mesonephric-like adenocarcinoma, uterine mesenchymal tumors, HPV-associated/independent cervical, vulvar and vaginal carcinomas and STK11 adnexal tumors, with no further JGCT content.

Genetic and molecular findings

  • TERT, AKT1 (printed 'AKTC'), GNAS, KMT2C: listed as 'other molecular events (may be useful in prognostication)' in JGCT
    Table 1 gives no frequencies; no recurrent diagnostic molecular event is listed for JGCT

Notes

JGCT content is one table row and no narrative discussion; the table's gene list (AKTC, probably AKT1) has no cited primary data or frequencies in the text, and frequencies of the other events are not given.
