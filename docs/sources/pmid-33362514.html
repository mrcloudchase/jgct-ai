Gender Dysphoria Disrupting the Course of Treatment of a Recurrent Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor in an Adolescent Female: A Case Report.

Why it matters for JGCT

Rare case of recurrent androgen- and insulin-secreting JGCT after enucleation alone in an adolescent, illustrating recurrence after non-radical surgery and ethical/fertility issues in management.

Key findings

  • A 17-year-old girl had menarche at 12, irregular menses and hormonal/dermatological problems from age 15 (hypothyroidism, insulin resistance, psoriasis, hirsutism), and underwent laparoscopic enucleation of a left ovarian tumor in 2018, diagnosed as FIGO IA folliculoma.
  • At 9 months, ultrasound and MRI showed a 7 cm left ovarian tumor suggesting recurrence; she was painless with markers in normal range (AFP 3.2 kU/L, CA-125 27.96 U/mL, HE4 36.7 pmol/L, hCG 2 mIU/mL, estradiol 750 pg/mL, FSH 10.7, LH 12.9 mIU/mL) except testosterone 14.96 nmol/L (reference 0.17-2.81) and insulin 19.7 uU/mL.
  • Histology showed granular cells with central nuclear atypia, common mitoses, large eosinophilic cytoplasm, follicular structures and no Call-Exner bodies; IHC positive for alpha-inhibin, CD99 and calretinin.
  • The patient requested hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy and disclosed gender dysphoria; parents refused consent; after psychological, psychiatric and endocrine consultations she accepted unilateral removal of the adnexa with staging, confirming a unilateral FIGO IA relapse of JGCT.
  • Testosterone declined to 4.87 nmol/L two days post-operatively; the patient declined adjuvant chemotherapy that had been recommended and scheduled because of recurrent disease; gender dysphoria diagnostics were ongoing and oocyte cryopreservation was being considered.
  • The authors state that most JGCTs secrete estradiol and only several androgen-secreting cases are reported; they suggest the androgen-secreting tumor may have affected gender dysphoria, say the recommended treatment for recurrent JGCT is hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy but fertility-sparing surgery was chosen in this young patient, and suggest postponing any sex-change process 5 years after surgery.

Presentation

  • irregular menses from age 15
  • hirsutism
  • insulin resistance
  • hypothyroidism
  • psoriasis
  • abdominal obesity
  • left ovarian mass 7 cm at recurrence

Treatments reported

  • Laparoscopic enucleation of left ovarian tumor (2018) (initial treatment, FIGO IA; n = 1)
    Outcome: recurrence 7 cm in left ovary at 9 months
  • Unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy with staging (recurrence, FIGO IA relapse; n = 1)
    Outcome: testosterone fell to 4.87 nmol/L two days after surgery; patient declined recommended adjuvant chemotherapy; no later follow-up reported

Notes

Tumor called 'folliculoma' in the text; histology and IHC match JGCT per authors. Initial enucleation (tumorectomy) preceded recurrence at 9 months, consistent with the general concern about ovarian-sparing excision. Estradiol is listed as 750 pg/mL, within the quoted reference range. Insulin units in Table 1 are garbled in the source. Staging 'FIGO IA' for the recurrence is the authors' wording. Authors' statement about the recommended treatment and 5-year delay cites other sources.
