Why it matters for JGCT

Compares adolescent ovarian cancer guidelines; gives the stage-based chemotherapy thresholds for JGCT and sex cord-stromal management, but contains no primary JGCT patient data.

Key findings

  • Narrative review (search September 2022) comparing five guidelines on malignant ovarian tumors in children and adolescents: Centres Experts TMRG 2022 (France), BritSPAG 2018, ESMO 2018, ESGO-SIOPE 2020, EXPeRT/PARTNER 2021; 96 items screened, 5 guidelines included.
  • Cited epidemiology: about 2.6 cases per 100,000 girls per year; 10-30% of ovarian masses in girls up to 17 are malignant; about 85% of preadolescent malignant ovarian masses are germ cell tumors, 8% epithelial, 5% sex cord-stromal and steroid cell tumors; adolescents mostly have juvenile granulosa cell, Sertoli and Sertoli-Leydig tumors.
  • Diagnostics: ultrasound first, serum markers (beta-hCG, AFP, LDH, CA125) in all suspicious masses, hormonal profile if hormone production or precocious puberty; AMH and inhibin B may indicate granulosa cell tumors; immunohistochemistry (inhibin A, calretinin, SF1, FOXL2, WT1, CD99 and others) and DICER1 testing in suspected sex cord-stromal tumors; EXPeRT/PARTNER recommends FOXL2 to distinguish adult from juvenile granulosa cell tumors.
  • Prognosis per ESMO: sex cord-stromal tumors generally have a better prognosis, with 20% relapsing or dying of metastatic cancer; advanced FIGO stage, intraperitoneal rupture and tumor size above 5 cm are adverse factors; only ESMO and ESGO-SIOPE give prognostic factor detail.
  • Staging by FIGO 2014 with complete surgical staging; systematic lymphadenectomy not recommended in early sex cord-stromal tumors; total hysterectomy only in stage II+ (Centres Experts TMRG); omentectomy only if adhesions (EXPeRT/PARTNER); open approach preferred to avoid rupture.
  • JGCT-specific chemotherapy recommendations: stage IA-IC1 with complete resection may avoid chemotherapy and IC2-IC3 requires it (Centres Experts TMRG, ESGO-SIOPE; text says potentially IC1 per ESMO; ESMO table entry lists stage IC chemotherapy recommended); EXPeRT/PARTNER recommends 3-4 cycles in stage IC.
  • For advanced stages debulking is recommended by ESMO and Centres Experts TMRG but EXPeRT/PARTNER states it has no role apart from palliation and advises biopsy and upfront chemotherapy followed by delayed resection; 4 cycles in stages II-IV with second-look surgery if incomplete resection.
  • Fertility: ovarian stimulation allowed only for stage IA granulosa-type tumors after multidisciplinary discussion and contraindicated above IA (Centres Experts TMRG); hormone replacement generally avoided in sex cord-stromal tumors except stage IA-IB granulosa-type; estrogen-containing contraceptives contraindicated for these tumors.
  • Authors conclude there is substantial agreement among the guidelines but call for an international consensus.

Presentation

  • non-specific abdominal/pelvic pain, distension, rapid increase in abdominal volume
  • vaginal bleeding and menstrual irregularity rarer in early puberty
  • mass usually large at diagnosis

Genetic and molecular findings

  • DICER1: Mutation testing recommended for suspected sex cord-stromal tumors and gynandroblastoma (not specific to JGCT)
    Guideline recommendation summarized by the review.

Treatments reported

  • Sex cord-stromal early stage IA: surgery alone with fertility preservation if all macroscopic lesions excised; JGCT stage IA-IC1 with complete resection may avoid chemotherapy, stage IC2-IC3 chemotherapy recommended (guideline summary (Centres Experts TMRG 2022, ESGO-SIOPE 2020, ESMO 2018, EXPeRT/PARTNER 2021))
    Outcome: No patient outcome data (review of guidelines)
  • Advanced or higher-stage sex cord-stromal tumors: BEP 3-4 cycles; alternatives cisplatin-etoposide-ifosfamide (at least 4 cycles) or carboplatin-paclitaxel (6 cycles in some guidelines) (guideline summary; EXPeRT/PARTNER recommends 3-4 cycles in stage IC and 4 cycles in stages II-IV)
    Outcome: No patient outcome data
  • Recurrent sex cord-stromal tumors: cytoreductive surgery followed by platinum-based chemotherapy; trial options include hormone therapy (GnRH agonists, tamoxifen, progestin, aromatase inhibitors), antiangiogenic and targeted drugs; bevacizumab, HIPEC, high-dose chemotherapy with stem cell transplant or radiotherapy individually (guideline summary)
    Outcome: No patient outcome data

Notes

Review of guidelines for all adolescent malignant ovarian tumors (germ cell, sex cord-stromal, SCCOHT); JGCT content is limited to a few paragraphs and rows of Table 2. n_jgct_patients is null since no patients are reported. Table 2 columns for JGCT are partly misaligned in the extracted text, so the guideline-to-recommendation mapping for ESMO versus EXPeRT/PARTNER was read from the narrative where possible (the narrative says BEP for stage IC2-IC3 in the guideline quartet and 'potentially IC1 per ESMO'; the table lists ESMO stage IC chemotherapy recommended). The review itself is secondary (guidelines are not independently verified).
