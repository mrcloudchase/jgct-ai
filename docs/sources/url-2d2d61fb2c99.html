Why it matters for JGCT

Contributes one testicular JGCT in a 2-month-old with the characteristic multicystic ultrasound pattern and mildly raised AFP in a 118-lesion pediatric testicular tumor series.

Key findings

  • Single-center retrospective study (Capital Center for Children's Health, Beijing; Nov 2016 to Jun 2024) of pathologically confirmed prepubertal testicular tumors aged 0-14 years: 125 identified, 8 excluded for incomplete US, 117 patients included (median age 27 months, IQR 8.5-83.5), 118 lesions.
  • Histology: mature teratoma prepubertal type 62 (52.5%), yolk sac tumor 20 (16.9%), epidermoid cyst 12 (10.2%), immature teratoma 6 (5.1%), dermoid cyst 4 (3.4%), Leydig cell tumor 2, hemangioma 2, rhabdomyosarcoma 2, juvenile granulosa cell tumor 1 (0.8%), Sertoli-Leydig cell tumor 1, mixed teratoma and yolk sac tumor 1, seminoma 1, secondary tumors 4 (2 ALL, 1 Burkitt lymphoma, 1 neuroblastoma).
  • The single JGCT patient was 2 months old with a 3.9 cm tumor; AFP was 244.19 ng/mL (all other non-yolk-sac, non-mixed tumors had normal AFP), and preoperative testosterone was elevated in this patient (also in 2 Leydig cell tumors and 6 teratomas); beta-hCG normal in all.
  • US of the JGCT was predominantly cystic ('Swiss cheese' multilocular pattern, hypoechoic or isoechoic solid components with increased vascularity); the authors describe JGCT as showing pathognomonic 'swiss-cheese' morphology and classify it among benign tumors with cystic or cystic-dominant pattern.
  • Cystic or cystic-dominant US pattern: PPV 96.1% (95% CI 85.4-99.3) for benignity, NPV 47.8%; solid or solid-dominant: NPV 91.5% for malignancy, PPV 49.2%; solid pattern plus AFP >300 ng/mL for yolk sac tumors: PPV 94.7% (95% CI 71.9-99.7), NPV 97.0% (95% CI 90.8-99.2).
  • Discussion (authors, citing literature): testicular JGCT is the most common congenital testicular tumor, typically within the first 6 months of life, benign and hormonally inactive, rarely recurring after testis-sparing surgery; normal or mildly elevated AFP differentiates it from yolk sac tumor. Authors conclude combined US and pathology offers a diagnostic framework for risk stratification and testis-sparing decisions.
  • No treatment or outcome data are reported for the JGCT patient.

Presentation

  • JGCT: 2-month-old, 3.9 cm predominantly cystic multilocular 'Swiss cheese' testicular mass with increased vascularity in solid components
  • AFP 244.19 ng/mL and elevated preoperative testosterone in the JGCT patient

Notes

Preprint, not peer reviewed. Internal inconsistencies: abstract says benign 71.2% while discussion says 71.8%; text lists 62 mature teratomas while Table 1 gives 68 teratomas (62 mature + 6 immature); Table 1 describes JGCT as 'usually without hormonal activity' while the text reports elevated testosterone in the JGCT patient. Table 2 places the JGCT among 'predominantly cystic' lesions. Whole PDF (18 pages) read.
