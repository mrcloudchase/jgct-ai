GATA-4 and FOG-2 expression in pediatric ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors replicates embryonal gonadal phenotype: results from the TREP project.

Why it matters for JGCT

Small Italian TREP series of pediatric ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors with a 6-patient JGCT subgroup reporting stage, size, mitoses, chemotherapy, outcome and FOG-2/GATA-4/inhibin immunohistochemistry.

Key findings

  • Italian TREP project (registry of very rare pediatric tumors; patients 18 years or younger): 15 girls with ovarian sex cord-stromal tumors: 6 JGCT, 6 Sertoli-Leydig cell tumors (SLCT), 1 fibrothecoma, 1 fibroma (bilateral, Gorlin syndrome), 1 sclerosing stromal tumor. COG/POG staging: stage I 12, II 2, IV 1 in the whole series.
  • JGCT patients (ages 31, 7, 40, 73, 97 and 118 months): stage I in 5 and stage II in 1 (case 3, age 40 months); tumor size 3 to 22 cm (case 1 3 cm, case 2 8 cm, case 3 10 cm, case 5 9 cm, case 6 22 cm); mitotic rate <1, >10, >10, 21, 6, >10 per 10 HPF; cysts in 5/6.
  • JGCT outcome: all six in complete remission, with follow-up of 67, 57, 36, 72, 52 and 15 months; only case 6 (stage I, 22 cm) received PEB chemotherapy. No JGCT was clinically malignant despite frequently high mitotic rates.
  • JGCT immunohistochemistry: inhibin and vimentin positive in all evaluable JGCT (inhibin not available in 1), pancytokeratin focally positive in 3/6; FOG-2 strongly expressed in 5/6; GATA-4 negative in 4 of 5 evaluable cases and weakly positive in 1 (the case with 21 mitoses/10 HPF).
  • In the whole series inhibin was positive in 11/15, vimentin 13/15, pancytokeratin 6/15, GATA-4 5/13 and FOG-2 11/15. SLCT expressed FOG-2 in 4/6 and GATA-4 in 3/5; fibroma/thecoma expressed both, sclerosing stromal tumor was FOG-2 negative.
  • Whole-series outcome: 13 in first complete remission, 1 second remission after a metachronous contralateral SLCT, 1 death of disease (a stage II SLCT with retiform pattern and heterologous elements, relapsed pelvis with rhabdomyosarcoma-like component, died at 33 months). PEB was given in 3/15 (stage I, II, IV).
  • Authors conclude pediatric JGCT show a FOG-2/GATA-4 pattern resembling primordial ovarian follicles, that low GATA-4 may relate to the good JGCT outcome, and that FOG-2 may have prognostic value; they stress the very small numbers and that histology and complete excision remain the main prognostic factors.

Presentation

  • JGCT diameter 3 to 22 cm, cysts present in 5 of 6
  • mitotic rate 1 to 21/10 HPF in JGCT, generally high

Genetic and molecular findings

  • FOG-2 (ZFPM2): protein expression by IHC (nuclear) (strong in 5/6 JGCT (negative in case 3))
    expression, not mutation
  • GATA-4: protein expression by IHC (nuclear) (negative in 4/5 evaluable JGCT, weak (+) in 1 (case 4, 21 mitoses/10 HPF); not assessable in 1)
    expression, not mutation

Treatments reported

  • Surgery alone (JGCT stage I (cases 1,2,4,5) and stage II (case 3); n = 5)
    Outcome: all in complete remission, follow-up 36 to 72 months
  • Surgery plus cisplatin, etoposide, bleomycin (PEB) (JGCT stage I (case 6); n = 1)
    Outcome: complete remission, follow-up 15 months

Notes

Ages in Table 2 are in months. The TREP project is a collaborative registry; patients may overlap with other TREP-derived Italian papers (not checked). IHC data not available for every case (n.a.). Mitotic cut-offs: high is 10 or more per 10 HPF. Journal and first author not stated in the retrieved full text.
