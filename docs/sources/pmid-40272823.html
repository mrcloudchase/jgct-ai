Testicular and ovarian Juvenile granulosa cell tumors in children and adolescents: Analysis of 113 patients registered to the German Registry for Rare Pediatric Tumors (STEP).

Why it matters for JGCT

Largest prospective pediatric JGCT cohort (113, testis and ovary): defines the stage-based chemotherapy stratification (chemotherapy for IC2 and higher), high mitotic rate as a risk factor, and testicular JGCT as benign with no relapses.

Key findings

  • Between 2001 and 2019, 119 patients with JGCT were prospectively registered in the German STEP/MAKEI registries (centers in Germany, Austria, Switzerland); after excluding those older than 18 years, 113 were analyzed: 24 testicular and 89 ovarian; reference pathology and central therapy recommendations were used.
  • Testis (n=24): all neonates or infants, median age 16 days (day 1 to 12 months), 17 diagnosed in the first month and 7 at 6-12 months; 13 left, 10 right, 1 bilateral; painless scrotal swelling, no hormone signs, AFP and beta-hCG within age-related reference ranges; size 2-6 cm (median 2); all stage I; no relapse after median follow-up 95 months (1-155).
  • One neonate with diffuse bilateral testicular JGCT was treated with buserelin (LH-RH blockage) and was histologically tumor free after 14 months; the authors suggest testicular JGCT may be a developmental tumor and recommend considering organ-sparing surgery, but not when AFP is above age-related levels or rising (to avoid missing yolk sac tumor).
  • Ovary (n=89): median age 9.8 years (birth to 18), 7 in the first year, 44% under 8 years; median largest dimension 11 cm (2-30), smaller in prepubertal (7.8 cm) than pubertal (12.6 cm); AFP and beta-hCG not secreted; inhibin B measured in 11 patients (10-2284 ng/L), >1000 ng/L in 5 (3 with precocity) and normalizing after resection.
  • Ovarian FIGO stage: IA 52 (58%), IC1 14, IC2 14, IC3 4, II 4, III 1 (II/III 5, 6%); no distant or lymph node metastases at diagnosis; mitotic rate low (<10/10 HPF) 38%, moderate (10-19) 26%, high (>19) 36%, median 14 (range 1-72).
  • Treatment: laparotomy 86%, laparoscopy 14%; adnectomy 64%, ovariectomy 27%, organ-sparing resection 9%; R0 58%, R1 41%; adjuvant chemotherapy in 21 patients (24%): 2/52 stage IA, 1/14 IC1, 13/18 IC2/IC3, 5/5 II/III, mostly PEI (cisplatin 100 mg/m2, etoposide 300 mg/m2, ifosfamide 7500 mg/m2 per cycle), 3-6 cycles.
  • Outcome (ovary): median follow-up 53 months (1-179); 4 relapses and 2 deaths; 5-year EFS 0.95 +/- 0.03 (85/89), OS 0.97 +/- 0.02 (87/89). Three relapses were stage IA (2 on watch-and-wait, 1 after 4 PEI) and one was stage IIB; all four tumors had high mitotic counts (20-67/10 HPF) or high Ki-67; three had organ-sparing surgery; no relapses in IC2/IC3 (which had unfavorable prognosis in the historical MAKEI cohort).
  • Prognostic factor: EFS was 1.0 (61/61) for low or moderate mitotic rate (<20/10 HPF) versus 0.85 +/- 0.07 (24/28) for high (>19/10 HPF), log-rank p=.032; stage was not significantly correlated with outcome; the authors recommend considering mitotic rate and incomplete surgery in risk stratification and second-look surgery after organ-sparing resection with high mitotic activity.
  • Comparison with the historical MAKEI cohort (45 patients, 27% chemotherapy, 6 relapses, 5 deaths; updated 5-year survival 50% for IC2 and 88% overall): current OS by stage IA 98%, IC1 100%, IC2/IC3 100%, II/III 80%, overall 97%; the authors conclude that restricting chemotherapy to IC2 and above and central reference review improved outcomes.
  • The authors conclude testicular and ovarian JGCT are clinically distinct entities: testicular JGCT presents only in infancy with excellent prognosis, while ovarian JGCT can arise at any age and be aggressive.

Presentation

  • testis: painless scrotal swelling in neonates/infants without hormone signs
  • ovary: abdominal pain 56%, abdominal swelling 42%
  • ovary: isosexual precocity in 71% of prepubertal patients (27/38 under 8 years)
  • ovary: torsion 6%, abnormal vaginal bleeding 28%, secondary amenorrhea 8%, hirsutism 4%

Genetic and molecular findings

  • chromosome 10: monosomy 10 (high proportion in testicular, not ovarian, JGCT (cited from another study))
    Discussion statement citing a recent molecular analysis; not measured in this registry

Treatments reported

  • Inguinal orchiectomy (one organ-sparing partial orchiectomy; one bilateral case treated with buserelin LH-RH blockage) (24 testicular JGCT, all stage I; n = 24)
    Outcome: No relapse at median follow-up 95 months (range 1-155)
  • Resection, watch-and-wait (Ovarian FIGO IA; n = 50)
    Outcome: 52 IA total; 48 first CR, 2 relapsed among W&W
  • Resection, watch-and-wait (13/14) or chemotherapy (1/14) (Ovarian FIGO IC1 (intraoperative rupture); n = 14)
    Outcome: No relapses; OS 100%
  • Resection plus adjuvant PEI (cisplatin, etoposide, ifosfamide), up to 4 cycles, in 13/18; 4 watch-and-wait; 1 incomplete data (Ovarian FIGO IC2/IC3 (preoperative rupture or microscopic metastases); n = 18)
    Outcome: No relapses; OS 100% (table lists 17)
  • Resection plus adjuvant chemotherapy (PEI in 4, cisplatin/etoposide/cyclophosphamide in 1); second-look surgery recommended if residual (Ovarian FIGO II/III; n = 5)
    Outcome: 1 relapse (stage IIB, died); OS 80%
  • Salvage: resection with PEI, BEP, carboplatin/etoposide/bleomycin, irradiation 30.6 Gy, stem cell transplantation, paclitaxel, bevacizumab, hyperthermia (4 relapsed ovarian patients; n = 4)
    Outcome: 2 alive in second/third CR (survival after relapse 32.2 and 126.9 months); 2 died of disease (19.5 and 2.5 months after relapse)
