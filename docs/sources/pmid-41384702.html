Why it matters for JGCT

Contrasts testicular JGCT (benign infantile tumour, monosomy 10, no AKT1/GNAS) with ovarian JGCT and endorses re-labelling it as benign juvenile-type granulosa cell tumour.

Key findings

  • Narrative review of testicular sex cord-stromal tumours (TSCSTs, 4%-8% of testicular neoplasms; ~90%-95% benign, 5%-10% malignant and unresponsive to chemo- and radiotherapy), summarizing molecular findings and TESST group classification recommendations.
  • Testicular juvenile granulosa cell tumour (JGCT) is described as an infantile disease that appears invariably benign, presenting largely in the first year of life (90% in infants 6 months or younger), mostly as a testicular mass; gynecomastia in rare patients; some associated with gonadal dysgenesis.
  • Pathology: well-circumscribed multicystic or solid and cystic tumours with follicles containing eosinophilic or basophilic secretions, small round cells with scant eosinophilic cytoplasm, fibrous/myxoid stroma bands; solid areas may have spindle cells or reticular pattern mimicking yolk sac tumour. Mitotic activity may be prominent (>5-10 per 10 HPF) without aggressive behaviour; necrosis, lymphovascular invasion and rete testis involvement described in rare cases.
  • Molecular: ovarian JGCT show AKT1 PH-domain internal tandem duplications and codon 201 GNAS mutations, which have not been identified in testicular JGCT; testicular JGCT shows monosomy 10 in ~60%.
  • Immunohistochemistry: beta-catenin membranous (no nuclear), SF1, inhibin, calretinin, SOX9 and FOXL2 variably positive; classic sex cord markers are non-specific.
  • TESST recommendation 8: testicular JGCT should be re-classified as 'benign juvenile-type granulosa cell tumour' to avoid confusion with ovarian JGCT, which has malignant potential (~10%), to prevent over-treatment and unnecessary work-ups; Table 4 lists JGCT as an example of invariably benign TSCST.
  • Testicular adult-type granulosa cell tumour is a heterogeneous diagnostic category; FOXL2 p.C134W occurs only anecdotally in testicular cases (versus >90% of ovarian adult GCT).
  • Therapeutic section is about malignant TSCSTs generally (CDK4, mTOR, PARP, Wnt and CD30-directed options experimental); none JGCT-specific.

Presentation

  • testicular mass (mostly)
  • gynecomastia in rare patients
  • presentation largely in first year of life

Genetic and molecular findings

  • chromosome 10: monosomy/loss of chromosome 10 (~60% of testicular JGCT)
    cited recurrent finding
  • AKT1: internal tandem duplications in PH domain (recurrent in ovarian JGCT; not identified in testicular JGCT)
  • GNAS: codon 201 hotspot mutations (recurrent in ovarian JGCT; not identified in testicular JGCT)
