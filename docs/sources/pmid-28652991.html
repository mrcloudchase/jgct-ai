Why it matters for JGCT

Case report of congenital testicular JGCT with IHC profile, marker values and 4-year follow-up after orchiectomy alone.

Key findings

  • A term (40 weeks, 3040 g) newborn boy had an enlarged right testis at the first post-delivery examination; ultrasound showed a heterogeneous solid mass without retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy.
  • Serum AFP was 97,819 ng/mL (considered within the normal range for gestational age and birth weight) and total hCG was 31.4 mIU/mL (reference <5), the mild hCG elevation being interpreted by the authors as a false positive.
  • Right inguinal orchiectomy at 21 hours of age: a 3.0 x 2.0 x 1.5 cm solid, light-brown, sharply defined tumor replacing most of the testicular parenchyma with focal follicle-like cysts; epididymis and rete testis displaced but not involved; no necrosis, no invasion of tunica albuginea, nerves or vessels; margins free.
  • Histology showed lobulated solid areas of large cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and visible nucleoli, with frequent mitoses and apoptoses; IHC: diffuse vimentin, focal calretinin and inhibin positive; negative for beta-hCG, AFP and cytokeratins, which excluded yolk sac tumor (the main differential).
  • Follow-up at 4 years showed a healthy child with no testicular or scrotal complaints; no adjuvant therapy was given.
  • Literature statements cited by the authors: JGCT is 1.2% of prepubertal testis tumors and the most common testicular neoplasm in the first 6 months of life; 48 cases had been compiled up to 2002; Y-chromosome abnormalities in about 20% of cases; clinical features are an asymptomatic mass without endocrine signs; orchiectomy is the treatment of choice and testis-sparing enucleation is possible after frozen section.
  • The authors regard testicular JGCT as universally benign with an excellent prognosis and consider AFP and hCG useless as tumor markers for it.

Presentation

  • enlarged, irregular, asymmetric right testis found at first neonatal examination
  • no endocrine features
  • left testis descended, normal external genitalia

Treatments reported

  • Right inguinal orchiectomy at 21 hours of age (primary surgery; n = 1)
    Outcome: no evidence of disease or testicular/scrotal complaints at 4 years

Notes

Authors call JGCT 'universally benign' (their statement, supported only by one case plus literature). Stage and karyotype not reported.
