An Unusual Case of Juvenile Granulosa Cell Tumor of the Ovary.

Why it matters for JGCT

Example of a large stage IC JGCT in a young adult that metastasized within 3 months despite negative surgical staging, illustrating aggressive behavior of ruptured stage I disease.

Key findings

  • A 19-year-old nulligravid woman presented with right abdominal pain, a lower abdominal mass, hirsutism with male-pattern pubic hair and menstrual irregularity since age 14; no hormone levels were obtained.
  • CT showed a multilobulated, septated, predominantly low-attenuation abdominopelvic mass of about 27 x 22 x 15 cm with moderate ascites and non-enlarged periaortic nodes; ultrasound showed a complex cystic-solid mass.
  • Laparotomy found a 27 x 21 x 15 cm right ovarian cystic and solid mass with some ruptured cysts; right salpingo-oophorectomy and staging (nodes, omentum, ascites negative) were performed; specimen 3482 g, 60% solid, 40% cystic with hemorrhage and necrosis.
  • Histology showed frequent mitoses, some nuclear anaplasia, luteinized irregular follicles and no Call-Exner bodies; inhibin positive and focally AE1/AE3 positive; diagnosis JGCT, FIGO IC.
  • No further treatment was given initially; CT at 3 months showed two new liver lesions (3.4 and 4 cm) and splenic hilum soft tissue masses (metastases). Despite numerous cycles of varying chemotherapy regimens she progressed, with new right adnexal collections and persistently elevated inhibin, less than 1 year after diagnosis.
  • The authors present the case as an aggressive stage IC JGCT and cite that relapses of juvenile tumors tend to be rapid and aggressive, that relapse occurred in 2 of 4 stage IC patients receiving chemotherapy in one cited series, and that cisplatin-based multidrug regimens have been suggested for advanced or recurrent disease.

Presentation

  • worsening right-sided abdominal pain
  • lower abdominal mass
  • menstrual irregularity and heavy bleeding since age 14
  • hirsutism with male-pattern pubic hair
  • ascites

Treatments reported

  • Right salpingo-oophorectomy with staging (pelvic/periaortic nodes, omentum, ascites negative); no adjuvant therapy (FIGO IC (ruptured cysts, 27 cm tumor); n = 1)
    Outcome: metastases to liver and splenic hilum on 3-month CT
  • Numerous cycles of varying chemotherapy regimens (not specified) (metastatic recurrence; n = 1)
    Outcome: progression: enlarging liver lesions and peritoneal implants, new right adnexal collections; inhibin still elevated, less than 1 year after diagnosis

Notes

Specific chemotherapy regimens are not named. Tumor size is given as 27 x 22 x 15 cm on CT and 27 x 21 x 15 cm at surgery (figure legend says 21 x 27 x 15). The abstract says the patient 'returned 3 months later with metastatic disease', whereas the text says metastases were found on the 3-month surveillance CT. Review statements in the Discussion are cited from other literature.
